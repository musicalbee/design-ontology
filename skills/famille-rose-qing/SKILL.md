# Famille rose (Qing)

> A white porcelain glaze takes opaque enamel like a canvas, and a pink from gold lets the painter model soft flesh, petals and clouds.

Qing porcelain painted in opaque enamels over a fired glaze. A pink made from gold gives the style its name. The imperial kilns of Jingdezhen made the finest vases and bowls.

**Period:** 1720–1800 · **Era:** The porcelain race · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Fencai, Yangcai, Rose family porcelain

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Famille rose (Qing), you MUST:

1. Fire the white porcelain and its clear glaze first, at about 1300 °C, and paint only on the finished surface.
2. Mix the pink enamel with colloidal gold, in a ratio of about 1 part gold salt to 1000 parts glass.
3. Lay a white enamel under each pink, so that the pink reads in graded tints from deep rose to white.
4. Outline each shape in fine black or iron red, 0.3 to 0.5 mm wide, before the fill.
5. Cover the whole wall with one scene: flowering branches, birds, peaches and bats, or figures in a garden.
6. Fire the enamels again at 750 to 800 °C in a small muffle kiln, one colour group at a time.
7. Add gilt lines at the neck, the foot and the handles; keep the gold to 10 per cent of the surface.
8. Keep the inside and the base of an imperial piece white, or turquoise, with a six-character reign mark.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Baluster and pear-shaped vases, double-gourd bottle vases, lidded jars, deep bowls, and large dishes with a wide rim.
- **Profile:** A full, smooth curve from the foot to a narrow neck; bowls have a thin, flared wall and a plain rim.
- **Foot and rim:** A low, neat foot ring that is glazed white or turquoise inside and carries a reign mark; a fine rim with a line of gold or iron red.
- **Proportion:** Vase height 25 to 45 cm; the wall is 2 to 4 mm thick, and the neck is about one third of the width of the belly.
- **Rules:**
  - Make the form simple and balanced, so that the painting is the whole display.
  - Throw the piece in sections and join them when they are leather-hard.
  - Pare the wall thin until it is translucent at the rim.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** porcelain
- **Secondary:** ormolu
- **Avoid:** stoneware · earthenware · bone-china
- **Rule:** Use a white kaolin and petuntse body that fires hard and translucent at 1300 °C. Western mounts of ormolu are the only metal that is added after the firing.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing in sections and joining at leather-hard · paring the wall on the wheel · press moulding of lobed bowls and dishes · painting of enamels in the palace workshops of Beijing
- **Joins:** Neck and belly are luted with slip and then pared flush, so that the join does not show under the enamel.
- **Details:** A bare ring on the foot, a clear six-character mark in blue or in red enamel, and a fine glass-like enamel surface that stands a little above the glaze.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** clear-feldspathic · celadon · crackle-glaze
- **Decorations:** famille-rose · overglaze-enamel · gilding · underglaze-cobalt
- **Pattern:** Flowering branches, peaches, birds, bats and clouds across the whole wall, with a ground of gold, turquoise or coral, and a border of lotus scrolls.
- **Gloss:** High gloss, about 85 to 100 GU on the glaze; the enamel stands as a soft raised film on top.
- **Wear:** The enamel can flake at the rim and leave bare spots; the pink keeps its tint, but old gilding rubs off.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A soft rose pink at the centre of a wide range of pastel tints, with green, yellow and blue as support, on a white or coloured ground.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A Jingdezhen egg-shaped kiln, wood-fired, for the body and glaze; a small muffle kiln of charcoal for the enamels.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** first firing of the body and clear glaze at about 1300 °C · painting of the white enamel and the pink in graded tints · second firing of the enamels at 760 to 800 °C · third firing for the gold and some of the reds
- **Scale:** Imperial work for the Yongzheng and Qianlong courts: small runs, with one painter for each colour group.
- **Signs:** A raised glassy enamel, small pin-holes in the pink, and a pale ring at the foot where the enamel stops.

### Gold pink enamel (material)

A soft rose-pink overglaze enamel that colloidal gold makes. Painters mix it with white enamel to make a range of tints from deep rose to white. Origin: ceramics, Jingdezhen, China, c. 1720.

- Dissolve gold in acid and precipitate it with tin to make a purple powder.
- Mix the powder with a lead glass frit and grind it to a fine paste.
- Mix the pink with white enamel in different ratios to make tints.
- Paint the tints on the fired glaze and fire again at 760 to 800 °C.
