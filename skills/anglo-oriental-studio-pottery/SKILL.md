# Anglo-Oriental studio pottery

> A good pot joins the plain beauty of East Asian stoneware with the English craft of one person who makes, glazes and fires it.

British studio pottery that Bernard Leach started at St Ives in 1920. A potter made high-fired stoneware by hand, with ash and iron glazes after Chinese, Korean and Japanese pots.

**Period:** 1920–1975 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Leach tradition, St Ives pottery, Anglo-Japanese studio ware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Anglo-Oriental studio pottery, you MUST:

1. Make each pot by one potter from clay to kiln, and sign it with a seal.
2. Throw the form on the wheel in a plain shape: a round belly, a firm foot and a thick rim.
3. Cut the foot with a clear bevel of 3 to 5 mm and leave part of it bare.
4. Use ash, tenmoku and celadon glazes, fired high in reduction at 1280 to 1300 °C.
5. Paint the decoration with iron or cobalt in a few brush strokes: a leaf, a fish, a bird.
6. Fire in a wood-fired climbing kiln with a long reduction; accept the variation that results.
7. Keep the colour to browns, greens and greys of the earth.
8. Make useful pots first: bowls, bottles and jars for the table.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Bowls, tall bottles, round lidded jars, vases and large dishes with a strong, plain shape.
- **Profile:** A round, full belly with a firm shoulder; a bottle has a narrow neck, and a bowl has a thick rim and a steep wall.
- **Foot and rim:** A trimmed foot ring cut with a bevel and left bare, so that the clay shows; the rim is thick and slightly uneven.
- **Proportion:** Height 10 to 40 cm; the wall is 4 to 8 mm and the foot one third of the width of the pot.
- **Rules:**
  - Throw with a firm hand and leave the finger marks on the belly.
  - Trim the foot cleanly and keep it bare of glaze.
  - Let the glaze run thin on the shoulders and pool in the hollows.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stoneware
- **Secondary:** earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · jasperware
- **Rule:** Use a grey or buff English stoneware that fires at 1280 °C and takes ash glaze well. Use earthenware only for slip-trailed pieces of the older English kind.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the kick wheel · trimming the foot when leather-hard · brush decoration with iron and cobalt · wax resist and slip trailing · wood-ash glaze mixing by the potter
- **Joins:** Handles are pulled and fixed with slip; lids are thrown from the same clay as the jar.
- **Details:** Throwing rings, a brush stroke of iron, a bare bevelled foot, spur marks of the kiln and a seal mark of the potter.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** ash-glaze · tenmoku · celadon · lead-glaze
- **Decorations:** iron-painting · wax-resist · incised · slip-trailing · undecorated
- **Pattern:** A few quick brush strokes of iron under an ash glaze: a leaf, a fish or a bird; a plain tenmoku with a rust-brown rim.
- **Gloss:** Satin to gloss, 20 to 60 GU; tenmoku is a deep gloss at 70 GU.
- **Wear:** Ash glaze stays hard and keeps its colour; iron decoration and bare feet darken with use.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** The colours of earth and plants: ash green, iron brown and slate grey, with a little cobalt.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A wood-fired climbing kiln of Japanese type, with several chambers and a long reduction.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 950 °C · dipping and pouring of the ash glaze · brush painting of iron and cobalt · high reduction firing at 1280 to 1300 °C · slow cooling over 24 hours
- **Scale:** A small pottery of 5 to 10 workers; each firing holds about 2 000 pots.
- **Signs:** Spur marks, an uneven glaze with runs and pools, a bare bevelled foot and a potter's seal.

### Reduction firing (process)

A kiln fires with too little air, so that the flames take oxygen from the glaze and the clay. Iron turns green or black, and copper turns red. Origin: ceramics, Zhejiang, China, c. 200.

- Close the damper and the air inlets in the high part of the firing.
- Let the flames fill the kiln with carbon monoxide.
- Keep the reduction for 1 to 3 hours at about 1000 to 1300 °C.
- Open the air and cool the kiln slowly.
