# Muscle and Pony Car

> A fast car is for the young and must cost little. It has a long bonnet over a big engine, a short tail and a few bold marks, on a small base.

The young, fast American coupe. A long bonnet, a short rear deck and a fastback or notch roof. It has a bold stripe, a bonnet scoop, vinyl bucket seats and a woodgrain dash.

**Period:** 1964–1974 · **Era:** Youthquake · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** American muscle car, Pony car, Detroit performance coupe

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A long-bonnet, short-deck coupe: a low body with a flat bonnet, a roof that sits back and a tail that rises in a short kick.
- **Line:** A body line that rises slightly from the front wheel to the rear wing. A bold curve over the rear wheel is known as the hip.
- **Fit:** Four seats, but the rear seats are tight; the front bucket seats sit low and close to the wheel.
- **Proportion:** Length 4.6 to 4.9 m; height 1.3 m; the bonnet is about 40 percent of the length; the rear deck about 15 percent.
- **Rules:**
  - Show the long bonnet with a scoop, a bulge or a wide stripe.
  - Set a small round or square lamp in each end of the grille.
  - Give the rear wing a strong hip line over a wide tyre.
  - Set the tail lamps in three vertical bars or in a full-width panel.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Automotive clay modelling (process)

A designer shapes a full-size car in warm industrial clay over a frame. The clay is cut, smoothed and checked in light before any metal is made. Origin: fashion, Detroit, United States, c. 1930.

- Build a wood and foam frame in the size of the car.
- Warm the clay and press it onto the frame, then cut it with rakes and scrapers.
- Check the highlights in light and measure the form for the dies.

### Chrome plating (process)

A thin layer of chromium is put on steel by electrolysis. The layer gives a hard, bright surface that does not tarnish. Origin: fashion, New York, United States, c. 1924.

- Clean the steel part and plate it with copper and nickel.
- Put the part in a chromic acid bath and pass a current through it.
- Buff the part to a mirror finish.

### Injection moulding (process)

Hot plastic is pushed into a steel mould under high pressure. The part cools, and the mould opens to release it. Origin: fashion, Newark, United States, 1872.

- Melt the plastic pellets in a heated barrel.
- Press the melt into the closed mould.
- Cool the part, open the mould and push the part out.

### Sheet-metal pressing (process)

A press forms a flat steel sheet into a body panel in one stroke. The panels are then welded into a body. Origin: fashion, Philadelphia, United States, c. 1914.

- Cut the steel sheet to size and load it into the die.
- Close the press so that the sheet takes the shape of the die.
- Weld the panels into one shell and grind the seams smooth.
