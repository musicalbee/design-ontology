# Whalebone Stays

> A lady stands straight because her stays hold her straight, and the gown above shows the cone that they build.

The rigid bodice of the long eighteenth century. It was linen and silk stiffened with strips of whalebone and laced at the back. It pressed the torso into an upright cone under the gown.

**Period:** 1700–1790 · **Era:** Court and guild · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Boned stays, Pair of stays, Eighteenth-century stays

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Whalebone Stays, you MUST:

1. Cut the stays as a cone: wide at the shoulder, narrow at the waist, with a point at the front.
2. Stiffen the body with whalebone strips 3 to 6 mm wide, set 1 to 2 cm apart in sewn channels.
3. Join two or three layers of linen with a paste so that the cloth is stiff before any bone goes in.
4. Set the straps wide and low on the shoulder, so that the shoulders pull back and the chest lifts.
5. Lace the stays at the back with a spiral cord, and lace a second line at the front if the wearer has no help.
6. Cover the stays with silk, wool or leather, and bind each edge with a narrow strip of leather.
7. Wear the stays over a linen chemise, and never let them touch the skin.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** An inverted cone with a flat, upright front, a narrow waist and a point at the lower front.
- **Line:** A straight, tall line from the shoulder to the point; the back is flat and the shoulders sit far apart.
- **Fit:** Hard and close on the ribs, with the weight of the garment on the hip bone, not on the shoulder.
- **Proportion:** Length 38 to 45 cm at the front; waist 55 to 65 cm for a young woman; tabs below the waist 8 to 12 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the front flat from the neck to the point, with no curve at the stomach.
  - Cut the neck low and square, so that the chemise shows at the edge.
  - Cut the back narrow and pull it together with lacing.
  - Cut tabs below the waist so that the stays lie flat over the hip.
  - Leave a gap at the front for a decorated stomacher.

### Boning (process)

A way to stiffen a bodice with thin strips of whalebone, cane or steel set in narrow channels, so that the cloth holds the shape of the torso. Origin: fashion, Europe, c. 1550.

- Sew two or three layers of strong cloth together.
- Stitch narrow channels, 6 to 10 mm wide, from the waist to the chest.
- Push one strip of bone or steel into each channel and close the end.
- Bind each edge of the garment in leather or tape.
