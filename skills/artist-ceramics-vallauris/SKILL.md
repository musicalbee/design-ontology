# Artist ceramics of Vallauris

> A painter treats the potter's vase as a body and the plate as a canvas, and plays with the craft in a joyful, quick and plain way.

Ceramics by painters and sculptors at the Provençal potteries of Vallauris. Picasso made thrown vases into owls, bulls and women, and painted plates in engobe and wax resist, in editions of a few hundred.

**Period:** 1947–1973 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Picasso ceramics, Madoura ceramics, Vallauris pottery

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Artist ceramics of Vallauris, you MUST:

1. Start from a thrown form: a bottle, a jug or a plate by the potter's wheel.
2. Alter the form by hand while it is wet: pinch, bend, cut and join to make an owl, a woman or a bull.
3. Draw on the leather-hard clay with a knife, and cut lines through a coat of slip.
4. Paint with engobes in black, white, ochre and green, and use wax to resist the glaze.
5. Keep the line quick and plain, as in a drawing: a few strokes make a face, a fish or a faun.
6. Use a white or red earthenware, and leave parts of the clay bare.
7. Make an edition of 25 to 500, with a stamp or an incised mark of the pottery and the artist.
8. Choose Mediterranean subjects: fauns, fish, bulls, owls, doves and women.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Altered vases that become owls, doves, bulls, fauns and women; plates and dishes with a drawn face; plaques and tiles with a single image.
- **Profile:** A thrown body that the artist pinches, bends and joins: a vase that becomes an owl with raised ears and round eyes.
- **Foot and rim:** A thick, uneven foot or a plain flat base; the rim of a plate is cut or pinched, and a bottle neck is bent into a beak or a horn.
- **Proportion:** Height 15 to 45 cm; the wall is 6 to 10 mm thick, and the form keeps the round proportions of the thrown vessel.
- **Rules:** Begin with a thrown form, then alter it by hand. · Draw the main subject with a few quick lines. · Keep a part of the red or white clay bare. · Use two or three engobes and one glaze. · Stamp the base with the pottery mark and the edition number.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Secondary:** terracotta
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · parian · european-hard-paste
- **Rule:** Use a local red clay or a white earthenware body that is soft, porous and warm in colour. Keep a part of the clay bare, and keep porcelain out.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the wheel by the potter · altering of the thrown form by hand · slab building of plaques and tiles · moulding of plates in plaster · incising of the leather-hard surface
- **Joins:** Handles, beaks, ears and legs are pinched out or added with slip. The joins are left visible, and the thumb marks stay in the clay.
- **Details:** Finger and knife marks, a stamped pottery mark, an edition number incised in the base and a slightly uneven glaze.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** tin-glaze · majolica-glaze · bare-clay · lead-glaze
- **Decorations:** sgraffito · wax-resist · incised · underglaze-polychrome
- **Pattern:** Quick drawn lines in black and white engobe on red clay: a fish, an owl's face, a faun with pipes, a bull. A single line makes the shape of a face.
- **Gloss:** Matt to gloss, 20 to 80 GU; the bare clay is matt and the glaze on the white engobe is bright.
- **Wear:** The glaze crazes in a fine net, and the engobe scratches. The bare clay takes oil from the hand and darkens.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Red or white clay with black, white and ochre engobes, and a clear or white glaze; green and blue appear in small areas.

### Thrown-form alteration (skill)

A potter throws a plain vessel on the wheel and then bends, pinches, cuts and joins it while it is wet, so that the vessel becomes an animal or a figure. Origin: ceramics, Vallauris, France, c. 1947.

- Throw a bottle, a vase or a jug on the wheel.
- Close the neck, or pinch out ears and a beak.
- Press the belly and add arms or legs of slip.
- Draw the face with a knife or a brush.

## Organisations

- **Madoura pottery** (company, Vallauris, France, 1938–now), launched the style. A pottery in Vallauris that Suzanne and Georges Ramié ran. Picasso worked there from 1947, and the pottery fired and sold his editions.
