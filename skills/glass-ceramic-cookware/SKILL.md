# Glass-ceramic cookware

> A cook wants one pot that goes from the freezer to the flame to the table, so the body is a glass that heat turns into a fine crystal ceramic.

Oven and flame ware made of a white glass-ceramic, a glass that is crystallised by heat. The body takes the gas flame and the freezer, and a blue cornflower decal marks the early range.

**Period:** 1958–1990 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Corning Ware, Pyroceram cookware, Freezer-to-oven ware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Glass-ceramic cookware, you MUST:

1. Melt a lithium-aluminosilicate glass with a nucleating agent, and form it as glass at about 1500 °C.
2. Heat the formed glass to about 750 to 1100 °C in a second step, called ceramming, so that fine crystals grow in it.
3. Leave the surface smooth, glossy and white; no separate glaze is added.
4. Use a wall 3 to 6 mm thick; keep the thickness even, so that the heat spreads.
5. Print a blue cornflower on the side with a decal in a low firing.
6. Fit a plastic handle or a glass lid with a rim that sits in the wall.
7. Make the shapes plain and round: a casserole, a saucepan, a skillet and a baking dish.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Round and oval casseroles, saucepans with handles, skillets, loaf pans and covered baking dishes, made in matched sets.
- **Profile:** A straight wall that flares a little on a flat base; the lid is a glass dome with a knob.
- **Foot and rim:** A flat base that sits on a gas ring; a thick rolled rim that takes a lid and resists chips.
- **Proportion:** A 1.5-litre casserole is 20 cm wide and 9 cm high; wall 3 to 6 mm, rim 8 mm.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the wall even, so that the heat spreads and the pot does not crack.
  - Print the decal in the middle of the wall, in a band 4 to 6 cm high.
  - Match the lid to the pot in size and colour.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** glass-ceramic
- **Secondary:** glass · plastic
- **Avoid:** earthenware · terracotta · stoneware
- **Rule:** Use the white glass-ceramic body, a crystal-in-glass material with almost no thermal expansion. Glass is for the lid and plastic for the handle. Porous clay bodies do not give a pan that goes from the freezer to the flame.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pressing and blowing the glass in a mould · ceramming the formed glass in a controlled kiln · fire-polishing the surface · applying a decal to the side · fitting a handle or a lid
- **Joins:** A handle of plastic or metal is bolted through two holes cast in the wall; a glass lid sits on a ledge.
- **Details:** A mould seam on the base, a fine matte surface on the underside, and a smooth glassy face on the inside.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** bare-clay
- **Decorations:** decal · undecorated · screen-print
- **Pattern:** A blue cornflower in a band on the side, a plain white pot for the clean range, and in later years brown or green floral prints.
- **Gloss:** A smooth glassy gloss of about 80 to 95 GU; the surface is the body itself, with no added glaze.
- **Wear:** The decal fades after many washes; the white body stays clean and does not craze.

## Palette

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A tank furnace for the glass and a controlled heat treatment lehr for the ceramming.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** melt the glass at about 1500 °C · form the shape by pressing in a mould · hold the glass at about 750 °C so that crystals form · raise the heat to about 1100 °C to grow the crystals · fire the decal at a low heat
- **Scale:** A factory line that made several million pieces a year; the pans went from the freezer to a gas flame and back.
- **Signs:** A mould seam on the base, a smooth glassy surface with no glaze runs, and a decal band.

### Ceramming (process)

A formed piece of glass is heated in two steps. Fine crystals grow in the glass and make it a glass-ceramic that does not break in a heat shock. Origin: ceramics, Corning, New York, 1953.

- Melt a glass of lithium, aluminium and silica with a nucleating agent such as titanium dioxide.
- Form the piece as glass and cool it.
- Hold the piece at about 750 °C, so that nuclei form.
- Raise the heat to about 1100 °C, so that the crystals grow and fill the glass.
