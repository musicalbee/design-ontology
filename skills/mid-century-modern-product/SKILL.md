# Mid-century modern product

> New materials from the war economy make a new kind of object: light, curved, bright and cheap enough for the new suburban home.

Post-war household objects of moulded plywood, fibreglass, steel rod and enamel, in organic curves and bright accents, made by American furniture firms and small Italian workshops.

**Period:** 1945–1975 · **Era:** Post-war modern · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Atomic-age design, American modernism, Italian post-war design

- The brief asks for hand-made craft with a visible tool mark.

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Mid-century modern product, you MUST:

1. Mould plywood, fibreglass or plastic in a compound curve that fits the body.
2. Put each shell on a slim base of steel rod, dowel or tapered wood, 10 to 25 mm in section.
3. Use organic outlines: the kidney, the boomerang, the egg and the disc.
4. Combine warm walnut or teak with a cool part of chrome, brass or enamel.
5. Add one saturated colour, in orange, turquoise, mustard or red, to each room set.
6. Make each object a module of a system that a customer can combine and extend.
7. Design the object for series production in a factory, and show the production method in the form.
8. Use a gloss finish of 40 to 80 GU on moulded and enamelled parts.

## Form

- **Geometry:** Compound curves and plane forms: the shell, the kidney, the boomerang and the saucer, on thin straight or splayed legs.
- **Silhouette:** An organic body that floats on a thin, spidery base; the leg angle is 5 to 15 degrees from the vertical.
- **Edges:** Soft radius of 10 to 40 mm on shells and tops; thin steel and wood parts keep a small radius of 1 to 3 mm.
- **Proportion:** A lounge seat is 38 to 42 cm high; a coffee table is 38 to 45 cm high with a top of 90 to 150 cm; a sideboard is 70 to 80 cm high on legs of 15 to 20 cm.
- **Ornament:** Pure shape and colour. The only repeated detail is a row of balls, a rod grid or a perforated pattern.
- **Rules:**
  - Each shell or top has a curve in two directions or a free-form outline.
  - Each base is a rod, a dowel or a tapered leg of 10 to 25 mm section.
  - Each room set has one saturated colour that covers about 10% to 20% of the objects.
  - Wood is walnut, teak or birch with an oiled or lacquered finish.
  - A moulded shell is of one material in one piece.

## Finish & texture

- **Finishes:** oiled · lacquered · moulded-gloss · moulded-matte · polished · gloss-enamel · natural-fibre · piece-dyed
- **Textures:** figured-grain · fine-grain · satin-smooth · mirror-smooth · plain-weave · orange-peel · pile
- **Gloss:** 40–80 GU on shells and enamel; 5–15 GU on oiled wood
- **Patina:** The style wants clean, fresh surfaces; light wear on wood and leather is fine, but a chipped enamel or a dull shell is out of character.

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** chrome
- **Section:** Rod 6–12 mm for bases and frames; brass or steel legs 12–25 mm
- **Joints:** Welded steel rod, rubber shock mounts and visible bolts between shell and base.
- **Handles:** A routed finger pull, a round wooden knob of 30 mm, or a thin brass rod.
- **Switches and taps:** A plain ivory rocker switch; a chrome lever mixer with a slim, curved spout.

## Manufacture

- **Processes:** moulding of plywood under heat and pressure · hand lay-up of fibreglass shells · steel rod bending and welding · spray enamelling · spinning and brazing of brass

## Techniques

### Laminated timber (material)

Thin boards or veneers glued in layers, so that wood forms long spans, bent shapes and large panels. Origin: architecture, Weimar, 1906.

- Glue boards of 20 to 45mm for glulam; set the grain of all layers in one direction.
- Cross the layers at 90° for a panel (CLT); use 3, 5 or 7 layers.
- Bend veneers of 1 to 3mm in a mould for furniture; glue 5 to 9 layers.
- Leave the layered edge in view as a line pattern.
