# Pop screen print

> A mechanical image, repeated and coloured by hand in loud inks, makes the familiar new.

A photograph or a found image pulled through a silk screen in flat, bright inks, often with the black photo layer off register. Warhol made it a studio process in 1962; Corita Kent printed text and colour; the look stands for pop art.

**Period:** 1962–1985 · **Era:** Studio press · **Domain:** Print · **Also called:** Silkscreen, Serigraph, Pop art screen print, Warhol print

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "screen print", "silkscreen", "warhol style", "pop art portrait", "serigraph", "off-register print", "pop art print".

Good for: posters and gig prints, album covers, portrait merchandise, brand campaigns with irony.

Do not use it when:
- The subject needs a sober or a respectful treatment.
- The photo has too little contrast to cut.

## System directives

When specifying, prompting or art-directing a printed piece or its look on screen: a poster, a plate, a book page, a zine, a label or a print-look illustration in Pop screen print, you MUST:

1. Start from a found photograph, cut to high contrast or a coarse halftone, in a black ink.
2. Put 2–5 flat, opaque, bright colours under the black, cut by hand and a little loose from the photo.
3. Let the black layer miss the colour by 2–5 mm, so the hand shows.
4. Repeat the same image in a grid with different colour sets.
5. Use colours from advertising: hot pink, lemon, turquoise, orange.
6. Keep the ink films thick and the edges a little uneven where the squeegee dragged.

## Process

- **Family:** stencil
- **Matrix:** A silk or nylon mesh on a wood frame, with a photo stencil for the image and cut stencils for the colours.
- **Press:** A flat bed with a hinged frame and a rubber squeegee, pulled by hand.
- **Image making:** The artist picks the photo and the colours; studio assistants burn the screens and pull the prints.
- **Edition:** A portfolio of 10 prints in 250 copies, or unique canvases.

## Inks

- **Strategy:** Thick, opaque screen inks: flat colour areas first, the black photo layer last.
- **Ink count min:** 2
- **Ink count max:** 8
- **Overprint:** opaque

## Techniques

### Halftone (process)

Tone printed as dots of different size or spacing; at a distance the dots merge into grey or colour. Origin: print, New York, 1879.

- Set the screen at 10 to 30 lines per cm for a print grain, or enlarge the dots to 4 to 8mm for a Pop effect.
- Turn each colour screen to its own angle: cyan 15°, magenta 75°, yellow 0°, black 45°.

### Screen printing (process)

Ink pushed through a fine mesh with a squeegee; a stencil on the mesh blocks the parts that do not print. Origin: print, Manchester, 1907.

- Stretch a silk or polyester mesh of 40–120 threads a centimetre on a frame.
- Make the stencil by hand cut film or by a photo emulsion exposed through a film positive.
- Pull thick, opaque ink across the screen with a rubber squeegee in one even stroke.
- Print each colour from its own screen and let each layer dry before the next.
