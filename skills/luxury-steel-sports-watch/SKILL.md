# Luxury steel sports watch

> Steel can be luxury if the maker finishes it like gold, so the price comes from the hand work, not from the metal.

A sports watch that costs more than a gold dress watch. It has a steel case with exposed screws, a patterned dial and a bracelet that grows from the case. The Royal Oak of 1972 and the Nautilus of 1976 set the type.

**Period:** 1972–now · **Era:** Youthquake · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Integrated bracelet sports watch, Steel luxury watch, Octagonal steel watch

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Luxury steel sports watch, you MUST:

1. Fuse the case and the bracelet into one flowing line, with no lug and no gap at the join.
2. Fix the bezel with visible screws or with a porthole shape, so that the case looks like a ship's window.
3. Alternate brushed and polished surfaces on every link, bevel and edge.
4. Cut a pattern in the dial, for example a tapisserie grid with 1 mm squares or horizontal lines.
5. Keep the case 7 to 10 mm thick and 39 to 42 mm wide, to wear like a dress watch.
6. Make the watch water resistant to 50 or 100 m, as a sports watch is.
7. Print baton markers and hands in the same tone as the case, with luminous fill.
8. Make the bracelet taper from 28 mm at the case to 18 mm at the clasp.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** An octagon or a rounded cushion in steel, with an integrated bracelet that flows out of the case.
- **Line:** One sloping line from the bezel to the bracelet; sharp bevels on each edge.
- **Fit:** The bracelet wraps the wrist with a snug fit and the case lies flat.
- **Proportion:** Case thickness to width about 1 to 4.5; the dial fills about 75 per cent of the face; the bracelet tapers by 10 mm.
- **Rules:**
  - Make the bezel and the bracelet links one design, with the same bevel on both.
  - Use a pattern on the dial and keep the markers simple.
  - Make the case and bracelet in one metal, with no two-tone trim except on request.
  - Hide the clasp under the line of the bracelet.

### Engine turning (skill)

A lathe with a cutting tool cuts a regular pattern of fine lines in a flat metal face. The pattern catches the light and shows no tool marks. Origin: fashion, Europe, c. 1600.

- Fix the metal blank on the rose engine, and set the cam that gives the pattern.
- Cut each line at a depth of 0.05 to 0.1 mm, and keep the pitch the same.
- Polish the cut lines, or fill them with clear enamel.
