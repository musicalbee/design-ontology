# Humanist photography

> Ordinary people share a human nature, and a photograph of everyday life can show their dignity, their humour and their need for each other.

A warm and tender form of documentary photography from France. It shows the everyday life of ordinary people in streets and bistros. It joins sympathy with wit, and it hopes for a world of mutual care.

**Period:** 1930–1960 · **Era:** The small camera · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** School of Humanist Photography, Photographie humaniste

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "humanist photography", "photographie humaniste", "doisneau", "willy ronis", "paris 1950s street", "tender street scene", "picture essay of everyday life", "belleville".

Good for: editorial city stories, community and care campaigns, photo books of everyday life, image prompts for warm street scenes.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a harsh, raw or edgy mood.
- The brief needs a news event or a staged glossy ideal.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in Humanist photography, you MUST:

1. Photograph ordinary life in the street, the bistro, the market and the working-class district, and not news events.
2. Use available light and a small camera, a 35mm lens or a waist-level Rolleiflex, so that people do not notice you.
3. Stand 1.5 to 4m away, so that the face and the setting share the frame.
4. Wait for a gesture of tenderness or wit: a child at play, a couple, a shared joke.
5. Print in black and white with deep mid-tones and a soft contrast.
6. Work in series for the picture essay, and publish it in a magazine or a book.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Cold, distant views of people as types.
- **NEVER:** Cruel or mocking close-ups.
- **NEVER:** Harsh flash that flattens the face.
- **NEVER:** Heavy blur or grain that hides the person.
- **NEVER:** Staged scenes with hired models.
- **NEVER:** Pictures of news events or battles with no human warmth.

## Composition

- **Framing:** Loose and readable; the frame holds the person and the room or street around them.
- **Viewpoint:** Eye or waist height, handheld, 1.5 to 4m from the subject.
- **Geometry:** Gentle diagonals, doorways and window light; a small human drama sits inside the setting.
- **Subject placement:** Off centre, with the gesture or the gaze of the person in a pocket of light.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| focal-point | Give 1 human gesture as the point of the picture and let the setting support it. |
| layering-depth | Put 2 or 3 planes in the frame: a foreground detail at 1.5m, the person at 3m and the street beyond. |
| balance | Balance the frame by a second figure or a shop sign within 0.8 to 1.25 of the visual weight. |
| rule-of-thirds | Put the main figure within 0.05 of 0.33 or 0.67 of the width. |

## Light

- **Quality:** Available light: soft daylight, a café window or the street lamp at dusk.
- **Direction:** Side light from windows and low sun; gentle backlight.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 3:1 to 5:1 (about 2 stops)
- **Colour temperature:** 5000–5500K daylight; 3200K for tungsten rooms
- **Time of day:** Morning or late afternoon; early evening in town

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** 35mm rangefinder or a 6×6 Rolleiflex
- **Focal length:** 35mm to 50mm (35mm format)
- **Aperture:** f/4 to f/8
- **Shutter:** 1/60s to 1/250s
- **Film speed:** ISO 100–400
- **Focus:** Set by scale or on the ground glass; the subject stays sharp with softly blurred surroundings.
- **Support:** Handheld, or from the waist
- **Equipment:** 35mm rangefinder or Rolleiflex · 35mm or 50mm prime · black and white film

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Black and white with rich mid-tones and a gentle S-curve; the tone is warm and human.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Convert to monochrome with a yellow-orange filter mix (R 45, G 45, B 10).
  - Set the black point at 2% and the white point at 96%.
  - Apply +15% midtone contrast with a soft S-curve.
  - Dodge the faces by +0.3 stop and burn the corners by −0.3 stop.
  - Add film grain, amount 15%, size 30%, as for ISO 400 film.
  - Keep the full frame; crop by no more than 5%.
- **Grain:** ISO 400 film: fine and even, visible at A4

## Subject & staging

- **Subjects:** children at play · couples · workers and bistro guests · market and street scenes · working-class districts · dancers and street musicians
- **Staging:** Little or none. The photographer waits, and may speak to the person first.
- **Direction:** People are at ease; they ignore the camera or give a short, natural smile.

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:**
  - Develop in a standard developer at ISO 400, with no push.
  - Make a contact sheet of the whole series and choose the frames that tell a story.
  - Print on grade 2 to 3 paper with soft burning at the edges.
  - Order the prints as a picture essay for a magazine or a book.
- **Output:** A warm black and white 8×10 in print, or a series of 6 to 12 prints for a picture essay.

## Techniques

### 35 mm camera (technology)

A small, quiet camera that takes 36 frames of 24 x 36 mm on cinema film, so that the photographer can work by hand in the street. Origin: photography, Wetzlar, Germany, 1925.

- Use a 28 to 50 mm lens and focus by scale, not through the finder.
- Shoot at 1/125 to 1/500 s, from the hand.
- Show the full frame; keep the 2:3 format.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Le Groupe des XV** (collective, Paris, 1946–1957), exhibited the style. A group of fifteen French photographers who showed together in Paris each year until 1957. It wanted photography to count as an art and wanted to keep French photographic heritage. Its members rejected pictorialism and shot street life. Most of them helped French humanist photography.
