# New Hollywood

> The studio picture is used for a director's personal, sceptical view, so the heroes are flawed, the places are real and the endings stay open.

A director's cinema inside the studios: location shooting, long lenses, a zoom, a wide anamorphic frame and flawed heroes. A story that ends without a lesson. The style looks lived in and tough.

**Period:** 1967–1980 · **Era:** Portable cameras and the new waves · **Domain:** Film & Video · **Also called:** Hollywood Renaissance, American New Wave

- The piece must be a short vertical clip with a fast hook.

## System directives

When planning, shooting, prompting, editing or grading moving images in New Hollywood, you MUST:

1. Shoot on real streets, roads and rooms, with practical lamps in the frame, and let the light fall off to dark in the corners.
2. Frame in 2.35:1 anamorphic or 1.85:1 and use a long lens of 85mm to 200mm to compress a street into a flat, crowded plane.
3. Use a slow zoom of 2 to 5 s into or out of a face, and a handheld follow of 1 to 1.5 m/s at a key moment.
4. Cut at an average shot length of 5 to 8 s and let a dialogue scene run in a single take of 30 to 90 s.
5. Print with a low-key grade, a lifted contrast of +20% and a desaturated palette of earth and brown.
6. Use source music from a car radio or a jukebox as the score, at −18 to −20 dB under the dialogue.
7. End on a freeze, an unresolved image or a bleak close, with no moral summing up.

## Composition

- **Framing:** A wide 2.35:1 frame in which the character sits small in a compressed, crowded street or a long, empty road.
- **Staging in depth:** A long-lens stack of 3 to 5 planes, compressed; practical lamps and signs fill the back, and a foreground edge cuts into the frame.
- **Subject placement:** Off centre by 20% to 30%, with the car, the door or the window as a frame inside the frame.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule | Value |
|---|---|---|
| framing | Frame in a wide anamorphic ratio and use the door, the window or the car as a second frame inside it. | inner frame 10% to 35% of the view, 1 to 2 frame planes |
| layering-depth | Use a long lens to stack the planes of a street into a crowded, compressed picture. | 85mm to 200mm lens, 3 to 5 planes |
| negative-space | Leave the road, the desert or the dark room as a large empty mass beside the figure. | 40% to 60% of the frame empty |
| contrast | Let the practical lamps be the brightest points and let the rest fall to a low key. | key to fill 4:1, lamps at +3 stops over the room |
| rule-of-thirds | Put the character on a third line and leave the other two thirds to the place. | figure at 25% to 33% from the edge |

## Camera & movement

- **Movement:** A zoom, a handheld follow in the street and a locked-off wide for a long dialogue. Moves are motivated and a little rough.
- **Support:** Handheld Panaflex or Arriflex, a dolly and a car mount
- **Camera height:** 1.3 to 1.7 m, eye level; a low angle from a car seat at 0.8 m
- **Move speed:** Handheld follow 1.0 to 1.5 m/s; a zoom over 2 to 5 s

## Lens & format

- **Aspect ratio:** 2.35:1 (anamorphic) or 1.85:1
- **Frame rate:** 24 fps
- **Shutter angle:** 180° (1/48 s)
- **Focal length:** 40mm to 200mm (35mm format); a zoom of 25mm to 250mm
- **Aperture:** f/2 to f/4 in low light; f/5.6 outdoors
- **Resolution:** 35mm negative, grain and halation; scan at 2K or 4K
- **Focus:** Selective focus on the character; a rack focus from a foreground to a face over 1 to 2 s.
- **Equipment:** Panavision anamorphic lenses · a 25–250mm zoom · handheld Arriflex · fast stock, pushed 1 stop

## Light

- **Quality:** Low, practical and often underexposed, with sodium and tungsten lamps; the shadows are black and the highlights burn.
- **Direction:** From a window or a lamp in the frame; a bounce from the ceiling for the face.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 4:1 to 6:1 (2 to 2.6 stops)
- **Colour temperature:** Tungsten 3200K lamps with daylight 5600K windows, left uncorrected
- **Source:** Practical lamps, windows and a bounced photoflood

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** A desaturated palette of brown, olive and ochre, with deep blacks and a grainy, warm print.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Set the black point at 3% and let the shadows crush below 8%.
  - Desaturate to 70% of the original and push the midtones to warm brown by +8%.
  - Add a contrast curve of +20% and let the practical lamps burn to white.
  - Add grain at 15% to 20% and a halation of 3% around the lamps.
  - Do not add a teal cast; keep a single warm and earthy grade.
- **Texture:** Heavy grain at 15% to 20%, with halation around highlights

## Editing & rhythm

- **Average shot length:** 5 to 8 s (estimate; check Cinemetrics for each title)
- **Shot length range:** 1 s for a shock cut to 90 s for a long dialogue take
- **Cutting logic:** Cut on the rhythm of the talk, and let a scene run past its point. Cut to a hard shock at the break of violence, and cut away to the road.
- **Transitions:** Hard cut, a freeze frame of 2 to 4 s at the close, a dissolve of 1 to 2 s, and a smash cut to black.
- **Runtime:** 100 to 150 min

## Sound

- **Approach:** Location sound with room tone, traffic and a car radio; a layered track in which voices overlap.
- **Dialogue:** Naturalistic, overlapping, with a street idiom, and a mumble that the mix does not fix.
- **Music:** Source music from a jukebox, a radio or a rock record; a score that is spare, with a solo guitar or a synthesizer.
- **Loudness:** −23 LUFS integrated for a restoration, with dialogue peaks at −6 dBFS

## Titles & graphics

- **Treatment:** A plain title card over a location shot or black, with a pop song; credits in a plain list.
- **Type:** A bold sans-serif or a grotesque in white upper case, 2 to 4 s for the title and 1 to 2 s for a credit.

## Techniques

### Handheld camera (tool)

A camera that the operator carries on the body, with no tripod or dolly; the shake of the body shows in the frame. Origin: film & video, Paris, c. 1960.

- Use a light camera and a wide lens of 18mm to 35mm so that the shake stays small in the frame.
- Follow the action at the pace of a walking person and let the frame move 2% to 5% off the subject.
- Do not stabilise in post; accept a late refocus and a missed frame as part of the take.
- Shoot long takes of 30 seconds or more, so that the camera seems to live in the room.
