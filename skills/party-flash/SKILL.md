# Party flash

> The harsh direct flash is the honest light of a real night, and an image that looks unplanned and unflattering reads as truthful and as cool.

A night-out picture lit by one hard flash on the camera. The subject glows against a falling-off, dark background, skin shines and colour is bold; the image looks candid, tipsy and slightly rude.

**Period:** 2008–today · **Era:** Digital and the grade · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** Flash snapshot, Direct flash, On-camera flash

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "party flash", "flash photography", "direct flash", "on-camera flash", "snapshot", "nightlife photo", "indie sleaze", "disposable camera look".

Good for: fashion and music campaigns, nightlife and event coverage, social media content, image prompts for night scenes.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a flattering or premium product image.
- Faces must look soft, calm or formal.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in Party flash, you MUST:

1. Put one flash on the camera axis, at 1.5 to 3m from the subject, with the flash at full or one stop under.
2. Let the background fall off by 2 to 3 stops, so that it drops to black or to a dark ambient colour.
3. Shoot at 28mm to 35mm (35mm format), f/2.8 to f/4, 1/60s or 1/30s to let some ambient light in (slow sync).
4. Keep the flash at 5500K against a warm tungsten ambient of 2800 to 3200K, and let the orange and blue split stay.
5. Frame the moment loosely: tilted, cut-off, with a friend half in and red eyes allowed.
6. Grade for punch: clip the highlights at 100%, crush the shadows to 0–5% and raise contrast by +30%.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Bounce flash, softboxes and diffused fill.
- **NEVER:** A clean, even exposure of the room.
- **NEVER:** Perfect skin, retouched shine and corrected red eye.
- **NEVER:** Careful composition with a clear subject on a third.
- **NEVER:** Pastel or low-contrast grading.
- **NEVER:** A tripod or a posed group in rows.

## Composition

- **Framing:** Tight, tilted, in a 3:2 or 4:5 frame; people crowd the edges.
- **Viewpoint:** Handheld at chest or face height, 1.5 to 3m, sometimes from the hip.
- **Geometry:** Flat plane of lit faces against a dark void; a hard shadow on the wall behind the subject.
- **Subject placement:** Centred or off centre by chance; faces take 30–60% of the frame.

## Light

- **Quality:** Hard direct flash, small source, strong specular shine on skin and glass.
- **Direction:** From the camera axis, slightly above the lens; the shadow falls behind the subject.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 8:1 or more (3 stops or more), flash to ambient
- **Colour temperature:** Flash 5500K; ambient 2800–3200K (tungsten) or coloured club light
- **Time of day:** Night

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** 35mm film compact or a digital compact or phone with a flash
- **Focal length:** 28mm to 35mm (35mm format)
- **Aperture:** f/2.8 to f/4
- **Shutter:** 1/60s to 1/30s (slow sync)
- **Film speed:** ISO 400 (film); 200 to 800 (digital)
- **Focus:** Autofocus on the nearest face; the depth of field is 1 to 4m and the rest falls into the dark.
- **Support:** Handheld
- **Equipment:** 35mm point-and-shoot with flash · digital compact · phone with flash

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** High-contrast, warm-skin, with a black background and bold colours of clothes and lights; some blue-orange split from mixed light.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Set the white point at 100% with 3% of the highlights clipped on skin.
  - Crush the black point to 3% and let the background fall to #0B0A10.
  - Add +30% contrast with a hard S-curve.
  - Raise saturation by +10% and keep the flash at 5500K against warm ambient.
  - Add film grain, amount 20%, size 30%, or JPEG noise of 8%.
  - Add a soft red-eye glow only when it is in the original.
- **Grain:** ISO 400 colour film or sensor noise at ISO 800

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:** Shoot the whole night; take 300 frames and keep 10. · Scan the film or import the JPEG; make no skin retouching. · Apply a single contrast and saturation preset to the set. · Post as an unedited carousel with the original crop.
- **Output:** A square or 4:5 image on a screen, or a 4×6 in lab print with a white border.

### Direct flash (tool)

A flash on the camera that fires straight at the subject, so that faces are bright, shadows are hard and the background goes dark. Origin: photography, Germany, 1929.

- Put the flash on the camera axis; do not bounce it.
- Stand 1 to 3 m from the subject; let the background fall 2 to 4 stops darker.
- Keep the red eye, the shine on skin and the hard shadow behind the subject.
