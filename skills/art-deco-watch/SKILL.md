# Art Deco watch

> A watch is a piece of jewellery with a plan. It has straight lines, black and white, and a rich metal, with no curve that has no job.

The rectangular watch that Louis Cartier drew in 1917 after the plan of a tank seen from above. It has two side bars, a railway minute track, blued sword hands and a cabochon on the crown. The case is platinum or gold.

**Period:** 1917–1939 · **Era:** Belle Époque and the Great War · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Tank-form watch, Deco dress watch, Cartier Tank style

1. Draw the case as a rectangle about 23 mm wide and 30 mm long, with two parallel side bars that run into the lugs.
2. Print Roman numerals inside a railway minute track that forms a rectangle.
3. Cut the hands as blued steel swords.
4. Set a cabochon of sapphire or ruby on the crown.
5. Use black, white and one precious metal; add colour with enamel or gems only.
6. Keep the face flat and the lines straight; round only the crown and the strap.
7. Mount diamonds in a platinum frame with milled edges, in straight rows of baguettes.
8. Fit a strap of black or brown crocodile with a gold or platinum buckle.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A rectangle about 23 mm by 30 mm with two straight side bars that form the lugs, on a narrow strap.
- **Line:** Straight and parallel lines; the only curve is the crown and the bend of the strap.
- **Fit:** The case sits flat on the top of the wrist, and the strap curves round it.
- **Proportion:** Case width to length is about 3 to 4. The dial fills about 75 per cent of the case. Strap width equals the width between the lugs.
- **Rules:**
  - Make the side bars as long as the case and as wide as 3 mm.
  - Place the crown in the middle of the short side, at 3 o'clock.
  - Keep the numerals to 12 Roman numerals and draw the track as a rectangle.
  - Match the strap width to the lug gap, so that case and strap read as one line.

## Construction

- **Methods:** case cut and finished in gold or platinum · baguette diamond setting in rows · black enamel ring fired on the dial · engine-turned dial centre · railway minute track printed in black · crocodile strap sewn by hand
- **Closures:** A gold or platinum folding clasp, or a buckle, closes the strap; a cabochon crown winds and sets the watch.
- **Details:** Sword hands of blued steel, a cabochon crown, Roman numerals and the side bars as lugs.

### Engine turning (skill)

A lathe with a cutting tool cuts a regular pattern of fine lines in a flat metal face. The pattern catches the light and shows no tool marks. Origin: fashion, Europe, c. 1600.

- Fix the metal blank on the rose engine, and set the cam that gives the pattern.
- Cut each line at a depth of 0.05 to 0.1 mm, and keep the pitch the same.
- Polish the cut lines, or fill them with clear enamel.
