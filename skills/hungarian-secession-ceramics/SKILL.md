# Hungarian Secession ceramics

> A national style needs a national material: frost-proof, bright clay tiles with Hungarian folk flowers and Eastern patterns make a roof into the face of a building.

Colourful architectural ceramics of the Hungarian Secession. Ödön Lechner and the Zsolnay factory made frost-proof pyrogranite tiles and iridescent eosin glazes for roofs, domes and facades.

**Period:** 1893–1914 · **Era:** Art pottery and reform · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Zsolnay architectural ceramics, Lechner ceramic roofs, Hungarian national romantic ceramics

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Hungarian Secession ceramics, you MUST:

1. Cover the roof with glazed tiles in 3 or 4 colours, laid in zigzag and diamond patterns.
2. Mould the tiles from pyrogranite, a clay fired at a very high heat that resists frost.
3. Draw the ornament from Hungarian folk art: tulips, pomegranates, peacocks and stylised leaves.
4. Use the iridescent eosin glaze on the finials, the crown pieces and the wall panels.
5. Frame the arches and the gates with bands of relief and a ceramic mosaic.
6. Fire the tiles in a single, hot firing so that the colours do not fade.
7. Set a bright ridge, a finial and a tower top on the roof line.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Roof tiles, ridge pieces, finials, facade panels, window frames and moulded relief tiles for gates and towers.
- **Profile:** A roof of steep pitch; the tile is flat or scale-shaped and sits in rows; the finial rises in a tulip or a pointed form.
- **Foot and rim:** The tile has a nib at the back for the batten and a smooth, even edge; the finial has a socket for the ridge.
- **Proportion:** A roof tile is about 20 to 40 cm long and 12 to 15 mm thick; a finial stands 40 to 100 cm high.
- **Rules:** Use bright colour in a repeated pattern on the roof. · Make each tile hard and frost-proof. · Use folk flowers and Eastern shapes for the relief. · Give the ridge a bright piece of eosin lustre.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** pyrogranite · earthenware
- **Secondary:** terracotta · stoneware
- **Avoid:** bone-china · parian · jasperware · paper-clay · technical-porcelain
- **Rule:** Use a hard pyrogranite body for tile and relief, and a finer faience body for the small ornamental pieces. Both must resist frost.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pressing in plaster moulds · extrusion of tile blanks · hand modelling of finials · slip casting of small parts
- **Joins:** Roof tiles hang on battens with a nib; panels are set in mortar on a backing wall, and the finial is fixed on the ridge with an iron pin.
- **Details:** A bright, uneven glaze at the edge, a stamp of the factory on the back and a fine web of crackle on some pieces.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** lead-glaze · eosin-glaze · coloured-gloss
- **Decorations:** relief-moulding · underglaze-polychrome · lustre · undecorated
- **Pattern:** Tulips, peacocks and folk flowers in relief; zigzag and diamond roof patterns in green, yellow, orange and blue; eosin lustre in red, purple and gold.
- **Gloss:** Gloss, about 70 to 95 GU; the eosin glaze has an oily metallic sheen that shifts with the light.
- **Wear:** The frost-proof tile keeps its colour for more than a century; the eosin lustre can lose a little shine, and an edge may chip.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Strong colours on a roof: green, orange-yellow, cobalt and a deep red, with the eosin lustre in purple and gold as the final accent.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A large coal-fired kiln of a ceramics factory; the tile stands in stacks, and a high firing brings the body to a hard, dense state.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** pressing and drying of the body · a bisque firing · spraying or brushing the glaze · a glaze firing at about 1250 °C · a reduction pass for the eosin lustre
- **Scale:** A factory of several hundred workers in Pécs, which supplied roofs and facades across Hungary and the Austro-Hungarian empire.
- **Signs:** A factory stamp on the back, strong colour and gloss on the roof tile, and a lustre that changes colour from one angle to the next.

### Pyrogranite tile (material)

A hard, frost-proof ceramic that the Zsolnay factory made from 1886. It is fired at a very high heat and carries a strong glaze, so that it can face roofs and walls. Origin: ceramics, Pécs, Hungary, 1886.

- Mix a stoneware clay with grog and press it into a mould.
- Fire the body at 1200 to 1300 °C until it is dense.
- Apply a coloured glaze and fire it again.
- Hang the tile on battens or set it in mortar on the wall.
