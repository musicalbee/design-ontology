# Hispano-Moresque lustreware

> A plain earthenware dish can serve a king when a white glaze, a gold lustre and a heraldic crest cover it in one dense pattern.

Spanish tin-glazed earthenware with a gold-copper lustre, made first by Muslim potters and later by Christian ones. Heraldic shields, plant scrolls and cobalt blue cover large dishes and tall jars.

**Period:** 1300–1550 · **Era:** Medieval glaze · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Valencian lustre, Manises lustreware, Málaga lustre

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Hispano-Moresque lustreware, you MUST:

1. Dip the earthenware in a white tin glaze and fire it at about 1000 °C.
2. Paint cobalt blue on the glaze first, for the outlines and the large forms.
3. Paint the lustre pigment over the glaze and fire a second time in smoke at 600 to 700 °C.
4. Fill the whole dish with scrolls, ivy, bryony leaves and a central emblem or a coat of arms.
5. Use radial bands that turn from the centre to the rim, and a raised boss at the centre.
6. Add a small raised relief to the rim or the cavetto, for the finest large dishes.
7. Keep the pattern dense and flat, with fine dots and hatching as background.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Large circular dishes, deep bowls, albarelli, jugs and tall winged vases (Alhambra vases).
- **Profile:** A dish has a flat well, a rounded cavetto and a wide rim; a vase has a swelling body and a tall neck.
- **Foot and rim:** A low foot ring with a hole for a hanging cord; a wide, flat rim of 3 to 5 cm.
- **Proportion:** Dish diameter 35 to 50 cm and a wall 6 to 10 mm thick; a vase up to 1.3 m high.
- **Rules:**
  - Pierce the foot ring for a cord, as the dish hangs on a wall.
  - Divide the dish into a centre emblem and one to three rings.
  - Keep the rim wide, to carry the scrolls or an inscription.
  - Place any relief at the centre or the rim, not across the field.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · bone-china
- **Rule:** Use a pale pink to buff earthenware that fires hard enough to carry a thick tin glaze. Keep the body free from fine grey or translucent clays.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the wheel · press moulding of relief · turning the foot · pierced foot ring
- **Joins:** Large vases are thrown in sections and joined at the neck and the foot; handles are pulled and luted with slip.
- **Details:** Three stilt marks inside the dish, a small hole in the foot ring, and a copper-gold sheen that goes pink or green at an angle.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** tin-glaze
- **Decorations:** lustre · underglaze-cobalt · relief-moulding
- **Pattern:** Ivy and bryony scrolls, coats of arms, Arabic-like script and radial bands, in lustre with fine blue outlines.
- **Gloss:** Glossy tin glaze, about 65 to 80 GU, with a metallic lustre.
- **Wear:** The lustre fades or flakes at the rim; the glaze may craze, and the lustre stays bright in sheltered parts.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A white glaze, a gold-copper lustre and a deep cobalt blue, with a green or a red lustre as a rare variant.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A wood-fired updraught kiln for the glaze and a smaller, smoky muffle kiln for the lustre firing.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:** biscuit firing · dipping in tin glaze · painting cobalt blue · glaze firing at about 1000 °C · painting with lustre · second firing in smoke at 600 to 700 °C
- **Scale:** In the 15th century about 20 workshops at Manises sold dishes to the courts of Spain, Italy and France.
- **Signs:** A metallic sheen with a brown or green shift, a stilt mark inside the dish and a cord hole in the foot.

### Lustre painting (process)

A paint with copper and silver salts is brushed on a glazed pot. A second firing in a smoky kiln leaves a thin layer of metal with a gold or copper shine. Origin: ceramics, Basra, Iraq, c. 800.

- Fire the pot with a tin glaze and let it cool.
- Paint the pattern with a mix of copper or silver salts, ochre and vinegar.
- Fire the pot again at about 600 °C in a smoky kiln.
- Wash off the ochre to show the metallic film.
