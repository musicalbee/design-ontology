# Victorian Corsetry

> The dress is only as good as the foundation: a firm, shaped corset gives the hourglass that the outer cloth then covers.

The factory-made corset of the Victorian age. It has coutil cloth, a steel busk, metal eyelets and spring-steel bones, shaped with gussets into an hourglass. It was worn under the crinoline and the bustle.

**Period:** 1840–1899 · **Era:** Victorian dress · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Victorian corset, Steel-busk corset, Hourglass corset

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Victorian Corsetry, you MUST:

1. Cut the corset in 8 to 12 panels, and add a gusset at the bust and at the hip to give the curve.
2. Close the front with a steel busk of two flat plates, and lace the back through metal eyelets.
3. Set a bone or a steel strip in each seam, and one at each side of the busk and of the eyelets.
4. Make the body of coutil, a tight cotton herringbone twill that does not stretch.
5. Reduce the waist 5 to 10 cm below the natural waist; use a long busk of 30 to 35 cm.
6. Add a satin or a broché cover for show, with a narrow lace edge at the top.
7. Keep a plain cotton chemise under the corset and a corset cover over it.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** An hourglass: a high bust, a small waist and a full hip, with a long, hard front.
- **Line:** A smooth curve from the bust to the hip; the busk keeps the front flat and the back is laced close.
- **Fit:** Close and firm on the waist and the ribs; the gussets give room at the bust and the hip.
- **Proportion:** Waist 50 to 58 cm for fashion; length 30 to 40 cm at the front; the busk 30 to 35 cm long.
- **Rules:**
  - Make the waist 15 to 20 cm smaller than the bust and the hip.
  - Run a flat steel busk from the bust to the stomach.
  - Add a gusset at the bust and at the hip so that the cloth lies smooth.
  - Cut the top edge low, so that the corset does not show under a day bodice.
  - Lengthen the corset below the waist in the 1870s, so that it holds the hip down to the thigh.

## Techniques

### Boning (process)

A way to stiffen a bodice with thin strips of whalebone, cane or steel set in narrow channels, so that the cloth holds the shape of the torso. Origin: fashion, Europe, c. 1550.

- Sew two or three layers of strong cloth together.
- Stitch narrow channels, 6 to 10 mm wide, from the waist to the chest.
- Push one strip of bone or steel into each channel and close the end.
- Bind each edge of the garment in leather or tape.

### Metal eyelet (technology)

A ring of steel that is pressed into the lacing hole of a corset, so that the cord can pull hard and the hole does not tear. Origin: fashion, Europe, 1827.

- Punch a small hole in the cloth.
- Press a steel ring through the hole and fold the rim over the cloth.
- Lace a cord through the rings in a cross pattern.
