# Memphis interior

> Function is not the only meaning of an object; colour, pattern and irony are functions too.

The rooms of the Milan design group around Ettore Sottsass. Furniture is a stack of primary solids in plastic laminate, printed with squiggles and terrazzo chips in clashing colours; each piece stands alone like a sculpture, and no axis or rule of good taste orders the room.

**Period:** 1981–1988 · **Era:** Post-war modern · **Domain:** Interior · **Also called:** Memphis Milano, Memphis Group interior, 1980s postmodern interior

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "memphis", "memphis milano", "sottsass", "1980s postmodern", "squiggle pattern", "laminate furniture", "postmodern interior", "80s design".

Good for: retail and pop-up stores, creative offices, bars and cafés, exhibition design, music video sets.

Do not use it when:
- The brief asks for rest, calm or a long-lived neutral room.
- The users need a quiet room for work or sleep.

## System directives

When planning, describing or rendering a room in Memphis interior, you MUST:

1. Build each piece from primary solids, such as a cylinder, a cone, a cube and a sphere, at clashing scales of 1:3 or more.
2. Cover 30–50% of the furniture surface with printed plastic laminate in squiggle, terrazzo or grid patterns at a 10–20 cm repeat.
3. Use 4–6 saturated hues and set complementary hues side by side with no neutral border.
4. Rotate pieces 15–45 degrees to the wall lines, and keep 60 cm or more of free floor around each piece.
5. Make one totem, such as a room divider or a bookcase 180 cm or taller, the focal point; keep the rest below 100 cm.
6. Treat each lamp as a sculpture with a visible bulb, neon tube or coloured glass.
7. Show no wood grain: lacquer, laminate and terrazzo cover every surface.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** A mirrored layout about a centre line.
- **NEVER:** Natural wood grain and oiled finishes.
- **NEVER:** Matching sets of furniture in one colour.
- **NEVER:** Beige, grey or tonal palettes with no saturated hue.
- **NEVER:** Historical ornament used without irony.
- **NEVER:** Hidden light sources and recessed lamps only.

### Terrazzo (material)

Chips of marble or glass set in a binder and ground flat, so that each chip shows as a cut section. Origin: architecture, Venice, c. 1500.

- Set chips of 5 to 25mm in a binder; the chips cover 40% to 60% of the surface.
- Use 2 to 4 chip colours on one ground colour.
- Grind and polish the surface flat, so that the chip edges show as cut sections.
