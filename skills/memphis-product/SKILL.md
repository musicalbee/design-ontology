# Memphis product

> An object is a toy for the mind: it joins cheap, bright materials with symbols and jokes, and it refuses the rule that good taste is quiet.

The objects of the Memphis group: bookcases, tables, lamps and ceramics built from bright laminate, terrazzo and coloured glass, in stacked geometric shapes with loud patterns.

**Period:** 1981–1990 · **Era:** Postmodern plural · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Memphis Milano objects, Memphis design furniture, Postmodern laminate furniture

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Memphis product, you MUST:

1. Build each piece from 3 to 7 simple solids: a cube, a cone, a sphere, a slab and a bar.
2. Cover a flat plane with plastic laminate in a bold pattern of dots, stripes, squiggles or speckles.
3. Use 4 or more contrasting colours on one object; set a pastel next to a saturated hue.
4. Make the form asymmetrical: a bookcase has arms, steps and angled shelves.
5. Join cheap and rich materials: laminate with terrazzo, or enamel with coloured glass.
6. Put a visible joint or a colour change on every junction where two solids meet.
7. Give each object a name and a joke; use it as a sign more than as a tool.

## Form

- **Geometry:** Stacked cubes, cones, spheres, slabs and bars, joined at angles that break the right angle.
- **Silhouette:** An asymmetrical totem or a tilted stack with arms, steps and a small top element.
- **Edges:** Sharp edges on slabs and planes with a radius of 0 to 3 mm; spheres and cones give the only curves.
- **Proportion:** A bookcase is about 190 cm high and 190 cm wide; a table top is 4 to 6 cm thick on short legs; a lamp has a base of 15 to 25 cm.
- **Ornament:** All over. The surface is the main part of the design: dots, stripes, squiggles, terrazzo chips and a bold black and white grid.
- **Rules:**
  - Each object has 3 to 7 primary solids.
  - Each large plane carries one pattern.
  - Each object uses 4 or more colours from 4 different hue groups.
  - The top outline is asymmetrical.
  - At least 50% of the visible surface carries a printed or pressed pattern.

## Finish & texture

- **Finishes:** lacquered · vitreous-glaze · honed · clear-gloss · printed · painted-matte · gloss-enamel
- **Textures:** satin-smooth · mirror-smooth · aggregate · twill
- **Gloss:** 30–70 GU on laminate; 80–95 GU on glaze and glass; 5–15 GU on terrazzo
- **Patina:** The style wants a new, flat and clean surface; a worn pattern or a chip is a fault.

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** chrome
- **Section:** Rods and legs 12–25 mm; a tube leg or a stem 20–30 mm
- **Joints:** Visible planes joined at angles; a colour change marks each joint.
- **Handles:** A round or a cone knob in a bright colour, or a plain slot cut in the laminate.
- **Switches and taps:** A flat square rocker in white or a primary colour; a plain round chrome tap.

## Palette

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Terrazzo (material)

Chips of marble or glass set in a binder and ground flat, so that each chip shows as a cut section. Origin: architecture, Venice, c. 1500.

- Set chips of 5 to 25mm in a binder; the chips cover 40% to 60% of the surface.
- Use 2 to 4 chip colours on one ground colour.
- Grind and polish the surface flat, so that the chip edges show as cut sections.
