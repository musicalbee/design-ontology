# Architectural photography

> A building is judged in a picture, so the picture must keep its lines true and choose the light that shows its form.

Photographs of buildings with upright lines, clean light and a chosen hour. The picture shows the form, the material and the way a building sits in its place.

**Period:** 1930s–today · **Era:** Constructed images · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** Building photography, Modernist architecture photography

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "architectural photography", "tilt-shift", "building photograph", "facade shot", "julius shulman", "ezra stoller", "blue hour building".

Good for: practice portfolios and monographs, magazine features on buildings, real-estate and development marketing, image prompts for true-line building views.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs candid street life around the building.
- The only lens is a wide-angle phone camera with no correction.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in Architectural photography, you MUST:

1. Use a tilt-shift lens of 24mm to 45mm, or a view camera, and keep the camera back parallel to the facade, so that verticals stay at 0°.
2. Stop down to f/11 to f/16 at ISO 100, on a tripod, with a shutter of 1/15s to 2s.
3. Choose the hour: low sun at 15° to 30° for a facade, or blue hour with interior lamps lit for a glow.
4. Shoot at 1.5m height for a plan view of the facade, or raise to 3m to 6m for a clean top line.
5. Keep people small or absent; use a figure at 1/20 of the frame height for scale.
6. Grade to neutral colour: keep the true tone of the material, and keep shadow detail.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Converging verticals from a tilted camera.
- **NEVER:** Wide-angle distortion below 17mm.
- **NEVER:** Flat grey light with no shadow modelling.
- **NEVER:** Heavy HDR halos.
- **NEVER:** Cars and signs that block the form.
- **NEVER:** Colour casts that change the material.

## Composition

- **Framing:** Complete and level; the building sits whole or in a bold crop with true verticals.
- **Viewpoint:** Level at 1.5m to 3m, 10m to 60m from the facade; the camera is square or at a 30° to 45° corner view.
- **Geometry:** Verticals, horizontals and receding diagonals; the grid of the facade sets the rhythm.
- **Subject placement:** Centred for a frontal view, or on a third for a corner view; sky takes 15–30%.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| symmetry-axis | Place the axis of a frontal facade at 0.5 of the width, within 0.02. |
| leading-lines | Use 2 to 4 receding lines from a wall or a path that meet at one vanishing point. |
| rhythm | Repeat the bay or the window at a constant pitch, with 6 to 30 repeats across the frame. |
| negative-space | Leave 15–30% of the frame as sky and 10–20% as ground. |
| proportion | Keep the 4:5 or 3:2 frame and a facade that fills 70–85% of the height. |

## Light

- **Quality:** Clear and directional for form; soft and even for a dusk glow.
- **Direction:** Low sun from the side at 15° to 30° elevation; at blue hour, interior lamps at 3000K against a sky at 7000K.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 3:1 to 4:1 on a facade; 1:1 at blue hour
- **Colour temperature:** 5500K (sun); mixed 3000K and 7000K at dusk
- **Time of day:** One to two hours after sunrise, one hour before sunset, or blue hour

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** View camera 4×5 in, or digital full frame or medium format with tilt-shift
- **Focal length:** 24mm to 45mm (tilt-shift); 90mm to 135mm (4×5 in)
- **Aperture:** f/11 to f/16
- **Shutter:** 1/15s to 2s
- **Film speed:** ISO 100
- **Focus:** Focus one third into the scene; the depth of field covers 10m to infinity.
- **Support:** Heavy tripod with a geared head and a spirit level
- **Equipment:** tilt-shift lens · tripod with geared head · spirit level · polarising filter · wireless release

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Neutral colour with true material tone and open shadows; a quiet blue sky.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Set the white balance at 5500K, tint 0.
  - Set the black point at 2% and the white point at 97%.
  - Lift shadows by +20 and lower highlights by −15.
  - Correct verticals and horizontals to 0° and keep the original crop at 95% or more.
  - Lower saturation by −5% and keep the material hue true.
  - Blend 2 or 3 exposures by hand for the windows; no automatic HDR.
- **Grain:** None: ISO 100, clean

## Subject & staging

- **Subjects:** facades · stairs and atria · bridges and towers · courtyards · details of material and joint · buildings at blue hour
- **Staging:** Prepared: clear cars and bins, switch on interior lights, and wait for a clear sky and the hour.
- **Direction:** Place a single person at 1/20 of the frame height on a route line for scale.

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:** Shoot a bracket of 3 to 5 frames at ±1 stop. · Blend windows and sky by hand. · Correct perspective to 0° and clean up distractions. · Export at 4:5 and 3:2, sRGB, 3000px on the long edge.
- **Output:** A true-line, evenly graded building image for a magazine, a monograph or a practice site.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Tilt-shift (tool)

Lens or camera movements that keep vertical lines parallel and move the plane of focus; a building stands upright and sharp. Origin: photography, Vienna, 1904.

- Keep the back of the camera vertical and shift the lens up by 10 to 20 mm, so that vertical lines stay parallel.
- Tilt the lens by 2° to 8° to turn the plane of focus along the ground for depth at a small stop.
- Close the lens to f/11 to f/22 for full depth of field.
- In an editor, use a narrow band of focus and a strong blur outside it to fake a miniature.
