# Symbolism

> A picture should suggest an inner idea by symbol and mood, and should turn from the plain surface of the visible world toward dream and the spirit.

Pictures of dream, myth, death and desire, in which a figure or a form stands for an idea. The mood is quiet, strange and inward; line and colour are chosen to suggest, never to explain.

**Period:** 1886–1910 · **Era:** Nineteenth century · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Symbolist painting, Fin de siècle dream

- The brief needs a bright, clear, literal or technical image.
- The picture must be loud, fast or playful.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Symbolism, you MUST:

1. Show an idea by an image: a sphinx, a flower with a face, a floating head, a veiled woman, a dark pool.
2. Keep the space shallow and still, with a flat, decorative field of 40–60% behind the main figure.
3. Set the figure in the centre or on a clear axis, and hold it motionless, with a closed or far-off gaze.
4. Use a limited, dim, jewel-like palette: violet, deep blue, gold and green, with pale flesh and a dark ground.
5. Draw a long, soft, flowing line of 2–6mm and let the contour curve like a stem or a hair.
6. Blur the edges in mist and dusk, and blend the light into a glow without a source.
7. Leave one thing unexplained: a title, a gaze, or an object that has no place in the scene.

## Composition

- **Layout:** A still, frontal figure or head on a central axis, against a shallow, flat or misty field, with a curving contour and a few symbolic objects.
- **Focal point:** The face or the eye, in the upper half of the picture at 33–45% from the top, on the central axis or one third from an edge.
- **Format:** A vertical canvas or panel at 3:4 to 1:2; a small to medium size of 0.3 to 1.2m; a tall format gives a frieze-like calm.

**Principles**

| colour-distribution | Spend 70–80% of the area on 2 dim hues of violet, blue or green, and 5–10% on a gold or pale accent. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A dim, jewel-like key: violet, deep blue and green, lit by pale flesh and a small amount of gold; the ground is dark or misted.
- **Distribution:** Dim hue ground 50–60%, mid-tone dress and plants 20–30%, pale flesh and light 10–15%, gold accent 5%.
- **Ground:** A dark, warm ground or a gold leaf field; thin glazes over it.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil and tempera in thin, matt layers; pastel and watercolour for dream scenes.
- **Support:** Fine canvas, panel or paper with a smooth ground.
- **Brushwork:** Fine, soft strokes of 1–3mm, blended; a flowing contour line of 2–6mm; no loud touch.
- **Paint handling:** 4–8 thin glazes; the surface is smooth and the colour glows from below; gold leaf in places.
- **Edges:** Soft and lost at the hair, the mist and the far field; a clear, curving contour at the face and the hand.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** A dim, even glow with no clear source, like dusk, a lamp or moonlight; the face holds the highest value.
- **Modelling:** Gentle, in 4–6 steps; the face is smooth and pale, with a dark eye.
- **Shadow:** Deep and soft, 40–60% of the area, in violet or blue; no hard cast edge.
- **Temperature:** A cool, dim key with a small warm gold or rose glow around the face.

## Subject

- **Typical:** A sphinx, a muse, a head on a plate, a veiled woman, a dream flower, a dark lake, a myth, a vision and a death-watch.
- **Figure scale:** A head or a figure fills 40–70% of the picture height, close and still.
- **Treatment:** Hushed, strange and inward; the image suggests a meaning and gives no key.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Matt and soft, with an even, velvet look.
- **Texture:** Smooth, with a faint grain; gold leaf or a thin gilt line in places.

### Lithography (process)

A print from a flat stone or plate on which a greasy drawing takes ink and water repels it; the artist draws as on paper. Origin: graphic movements, Munich, c. 1796.

- Draw on the stone with a greasy crayon or a tusche wash; the drawing takes ink and the rest stays wet.
- Pull the print in a press at a run of 20 to 200 copies for an artist print, or more for a poster.
- Use one stone for each colour; 3 to 5 stones give a poster.
- Keep the soft crayon grain and the dark, velvet black.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Le Figaro** (magazine, Paris, 1826–now), published the style. A French daily newspaper. It printed the Symbolist manifesto in 1886 and the Futurist manifesto in 1909.
