# Regency gilded bone china

> Bone ash gives English potters a strong, white and warm body at a fair price, and they cover it in colour and gold to match the rich rooms of the Regency.

Bone china with rich painting and heavy gilding for the houses of the Regency. The body has about 40 per cent bone ash, so it is strong, white and translucent, and it carries bold Imari patterns, botanical flowers and wide gold bands.

**Period:** 1800–1837 · **Era:** Industrial pottery · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** English bone china, Spode bone china, Regency porcelain

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Regency gilded bone china, you MUST:

1. Mix about 40 per cent calcined bone ash with china stone and kaolin for the body.
2. Fire the biscuit at 1250 °C and the glaze at about 1050 °C, so that the ware turns white and translucent.
3. Paint patterns in the Imari manner: dark blue under the glaze, then red and gold over it.
4. Paint flowers from botanical plates in the well of a dessert plate, one flower for each plate.
5. Gild the rim with a band 5 to 10 mm wide, and add a gold line round the foot.
6. Model the handles as leaves, scrolls or animal heads, and gild them.
7. Make a dessert service of 40 or more pieces, each with its own painted scene.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Dessert plates, dinner plates with a gilt rim, covered tureens, sauceboats, and footed dishes for fruit.
- **Profile:** A wide, round plate with a shaped or scalloped edge; the well is shallow, and the rim is broad for gold and colour.
- **Foot and rim:** A low foot with a glazed base; the rim is gilt, with a moulded scallop or a plain edge.
- **Proportion:** Plate diameter 21 to 24 cm and wall 2 to 3 mm thick; the gilt rim is up to 10 mm wide.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the wall thin, so that the plate shows light through it.
  - Divide the plate into a painted well and a gilt or patterned rim.
  - Gild all raised parts: the rim, the handles and the knop.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** bone-china
- **Avoid:** creamware · earthenware · stoneware
- **Rule:** Use a bone china body that is strong, white and translucent. Do not use earthenware: the style needs light to pass through the wall.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing and turning · press moulding in plaster moulds · slip casting of handles and knops · painting in overglaze enamel · gilding with gold in honey or oil · burnishing the gold with a stone
- **Joins:** Cast handles and knops are luted with slip; a lid sits in a rebated seat.
- **Details:** Fine gilt lines, a pattern number painted in red in the base, and a polished gold band.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** clear-feldspathic · coloured-gloss
- **Decorations:** gilding · overglaze-enamel · hand-banding · relief-moulding · liquid-gold
- **Pattern:** Imari patterns of blue, red and gold, botanical flower paintings, landscapes, and wide gold bands on the rim.
- **Gloss:** Bright gloss, about 85 to 95 GU, on a warm white ground.
- **Wear:** The gold wears at the rim and the handles; the glaze stays hard and keeps its shine.

## Palette

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A coal-fired bottle kiln; the biscuit is fired at the highest heat, then the glost, then the enamel and the gold.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 1250 °C · glost firing at about 1050 °C · enamel firing at about 800 °C · gold firing at about 750 °C · burnishing the gold
- **Scale:** Factories at Stoke, Derby, Worcester and Rockingham made services for the court and the new rich, and sold them to the world.
- **Signs:** A pattern number in the base, a heavy gold rim, and a warm, translucent white.

### Bone-ash body (material)

A porcelain body that holds calcined bone ash, about 40 to 50 per cent, with china clay and china stone. It fires white and strong at a lower heat. Origin: ceramics, Bow, London, England, c. 1748.

- Burn animal bone to white ash and grind it fine.
- Mix the ash with china clay and china stone.
- Form the ware and fire the biscuit at about 1250 °C.
- Glaze and fire again at a lower heat.
