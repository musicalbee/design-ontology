# Space Age couture

> Dress for the future shows clean, geometric lines and new materials. The body moves free in a short, flat, hard-edged shape of white, silver and plastic.

The Paris couture of 1964 to 1970 that took its look from the space race. It uses white and silver, short A-line dresses and flat trousers. It also has vinyl, plastic and metal discs, and low boots with a visor or helmet hat.

**Period:** 1964–1970 · **Era:** Youthquake · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Space Age fashion, Courrèges look, Cosmos line, Moon Girl look

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Space Age couture, you MUST:

1. Cut the hem 10 to 15 cm above the knee on a day dress, and keep the skirt in a plain A-line.
2. Make each seam a visible line, with a flat welt or a top-stitched seam in a contrast colour.
3. Choose a stiff cloth with body, such as heavy gabardine, that holds the A-line away from the leg.
4. Use white, silver and one bright colour, and keep the plain areas wide.
5. Cut a window, a circle or a square into the dress, so that the shape is geometric.
6. Join plastic or metal discs of 5 to 8 cm with small rings in place of thread.
7. Finish with flat boots, a visor or a helmet cap, and tinted glasses.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A short A-line or a straight tunic with a flat bust and no waist, worn with straight trousers and flat boots.
- **Line:** A straight shoulder, a high empire or no waist, and a hem 10 to 15 cm above the knee.
- **Fit:** Free of the body at the waist and hip; the stiff cloth stands away and does not cling.
- **Proportion:** Hem 10 to 15 cm above the knee. Trouser leg straight and ankle-length. Boot heel 2 to 3 cm. Hat brim or visor 4 to 6 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Cut the dress in a trapeze that opens from the shoulder to the hem.
  - Set a seam or a welt in a different colour so that it draws the shape.
  - Pair a short dress with flat white boots and tinted glasses.
  - Keep a plain neck and no collar, or a small round collar.
  - Make a metal-disc dress as a short, sleeveless shift.

### Linked disc assembly (process)

A dress made of plastic or metal discs that small rings link together, with no cloth and no thread. Origin: fashion, Paris, France, 1966.

- Cut or stamp discs of plastic or metal, 5 to 8 cm across, each with holes.
- Join each disc to its neighbours with small metal rings, using pliers.
- Shape the sheet to the body with a few darts of rings, and add a plain lining at the neck and the armhole.
