# Art Deco ceramic figures

> The modern woman, the dancer and the fast animal are stylised into long lines and flat planes, then cast in clay and painted in hard, bright colours.

Slip-cast figures of dancers, bathers, birds and gazelles with long limbs, sharp planes and bold colour. Makers in Vienna, Turin and Bohemia made them in earthenware for the cabinet and the cocktail room.

**Period:** 1925–1939 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Art Deco pottery figures, Goldscheider figures, Lenci figures, 1920s ceramic dancers

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Art Deco ceramic figures, you MUST:

1. Stylise the body into long, thin limbs and a small head, with a total height of 20 to 45 cm.
2. Cut the face and the hair into sharp planes, and give the figure a bob or a short cap of hair.
3. Take the pose from the dance, the swim or the sport: a Charleston step, a diver, a bow with a long scarf.
4. Cast the figure in earthenware from a plaster mould, and hold the wall at 4 to 6 mm.
5. Cover the figure with a smooth matt or satin glaze in a few flat colours.
6. Set two colours in hard contrast: black and ivory, orange and turquoise, silver and red.
7. Add geometric patterns on the dress: zigzags, triangles, circles and stripes.
8. Mount a figure on a stepped, geometric base, in black, marble or a glazed block.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Dancers, bathers, harlequins, women with a dog, birds, deer and gazelles, in thin, tall, stylised forms on geometric bases.
- **Profile:** A long, sharp line from head to foot, with arms and a scarf that cut sharp angles; animals are drawn as streamlined curves.
- **Foot and rim:** A stepped or wedge-shaped base, flat and heavy, often in black or a bold colour; the underside is flat, glazed and has a mark.
- **Proportion:** Height 20 to 45 cm; the body is stretched, and the head is about one ninth of the height; the wall is 4 to 6 mm thick.
- **Rules:**
  - Stretch the limbs and shrink the head.
  - Cut the face and the hair in sharp planes.
  - Use two or three hard colours in flat areas.
  - Put the figure on a stepped geometric base.
  - Show a diagonal in the pose: a lean, a step or a flying scarf.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Secondary:** porcelain
- **Avoid:** terracotta · stoneware · parian · raku-clay
- **Rule:** Use a fine, white to cream earthenware that casts thin and takes a smooth glaze. A hard-paste porcelain serves for the finest figures.

## Forming

- **Methods:** modelling of a master by a sculptor · slip casting in plaster moulds · joining of cast parts with slip · fettling of seams · hand painting and spraying of glaze
- **Joins:** Arms, scarves and head are cast in separate moulds and fixed with slip. The seams are cut and sponged until the surface is smooth.
- **Details:** A hand-painted face with a small red mouth, a vent hole in the base, and a mark of the maker and the model number under the base.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** matte-glaze · satin-glaze · coloured-gloss · clear-feldspathic
- **Decorations:** overglaze-enamel · underglaze-polychrome · gilding · relief-moulding
- **Pattern:** Flat colour blocks and geometric patterns on the dress: zigzags, bands, triangles and dots. A face has a painted mouth and a dark brow.
- **Gloss:** Matt to satin, 10 to 50 GU for most figures; a bright colour or a gilt area is higher.
- **Wear:** The matt glaze marks easily with grease, and the gilt wears at the hem. Thin arms, scarves and ears chip.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A few hard, flat colours that clash on purpose: black and ivory, with orange, turquoise, red and a touch of silver or gold.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A gas- or coal-fired tunnel or chamber kiln, with a biscuit firing and a glaze firing.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at 1000 to 1100 °C · spraying or dipping in glaze · glost firing at 1000 to 1150 °C · enamel or gilding firing at 700 to 800 °C
- **Scale:** A factory with sculptors and decorators, and series of 100 to 1000 figures a model; some figures were numbered limited editions.
- **Signs:** A smooth thin glaze, a hand-painted face, a vent hole in the base and a model number with the maker's name.

### Slip casting (process)

Liquid clay is poured into a plaster mould. The plaster takes water from the clay and a wall forms on the inside of the mould. Origin: ceramics, Staffordshire, England, c. 1745.

- Make a plaster mould in two or more parts.
- Pour slip into the mould and wait 10 to 20 minutes for a wall of 3 to 5 mm.
- Pour out the rest of the slip.
- Open the mould when the clay is leather-hard and fettle the seam.
