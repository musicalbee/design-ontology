# Technical shell alpinism

> A shell must stop rain and let sweat out. A membrane with pores that block water and pass vapour, seams that are sealed, and a cut that moves with the climber.

The alpine kit built around a waterproof, breathable membrane shell with taped seams, over a fleece mid layer. A synthetic base, made for speed and low weight.

**Period:** 1976–now · **Era:** Youthquake · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Hardshell alpine wear, Membrane shell, Gore-Tex era alpine kit

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Technical shell alpinism, you MUST:

1. Laminate a membrane with pores of about 0.2 micron between two nylon layers.
2. Tape every seam with a heat-sealed strip, so that water cannot pass the needle holes.
3. Weigh a full hard shell at 400 to 600 g, and a light shell at 250 g or less.
4. Cut the hood to fit over a helmet and move with the head.
5. Add a water-resistant zip with a storm flap on the front and a vent zip under the arm.
6. Cut the arm with an articulated elbow and a long back, so that the hem stays down when the arm is up.
7. Wear a base layer, a fleece and the shell, and no cotton.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Cotton next to the skin that holds sweat.
- **NEVER:** Seams without tape that leak.
- **NEVER:** A heavy, stiff shell that blocks the arm.
- **NEVER:** A hood too small for a helmet.
- **NEVER:** A shell with no vent for body heat.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A close, articulated hooded shell with a long back, sealed zips and a snug cuff, worn over a fleece.
- **Line:** A sculpted shoulder and arm that follow a reach, and a longer back hem that drops below the seat.
- **Fit:** Trim, with room for a mid layer and a harness; the articulation lets the arm rise without a lift of the hem.
- **Proportion:** Jacket to the hip, longer at the back; roughly 1 to 1.2 chest to hip over the layers.
- **Rules:**
  - Make the hood adjustable at three points, to fit a helmet.
  - Tape each seam and seal each zip against rain.
  - Add a pit zip for vents and a chest pocket above the harness line.
  - Cut the hem long at the back, with a drawcord at each side.
  - Fit a stiff peak to the hood for rain and snow.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Membrane lamination (material, invented here)

A thin membrane with pores that block water drops. Pass water vapour, bonded between a face cloth and a liner to make a shell cloth. Origin: fashion, Newark, Delaware, 1976.

- Stretch the membrane of PTFE until it holds billions of pores in each square centimetre.
- Bond the membrane between a nylon face cloth and a liner.
- Tape each seam of the finished garment, so that no water enters at a stitch.

### Polar fleece knit (material, invented here)

A napped polyester knit that is light, dries fast and keeps its loft when wet; it replaced wool pile as the mid layer. Origin: fashion, Lawrence, Massachusetts, 1979.

- Knit a polyester fabric with a loop face.
- Raise and shear the loops to form a soft, even nap.
- Cut the cloth into a jacket with a close collar and a zip.
