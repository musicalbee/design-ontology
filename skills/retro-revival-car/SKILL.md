# Retro Revival Car

> A new car can borrow the face of a loved old one. It must keep the key shapes and add the comfort and safety of today.

A new car with the shape of an old one: a round Beetle, a small Mini or a long-bonnet Mustang. It has chrome trim, round lamps and a painted dashboard.

**Period:** 1997–now · **Era:** Power and subculture · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Retro car, Neo-classic car, Heritage-inspired car

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "retro car", "retro revival car", "new beetle", "new mini", "heritage car", "neo-classic car", "retro-inspired coupe", "round headlamps".

Good for: a lifestyle advertisement for a heritage-inspired car, a car interior concept with a painted dash, an image prompt for a retro new car, a design history lesson on nostalgia.

Do not use it when:

- the brief wants a real period car
- the brief wants a sharp sports car

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Retro Revival Car, you MUST:

1. Copy the key shapes of the old car: the roof, the round lamps and the grille, at 90 to 110 percent of the old size.
2. Make the body bigger than the old one by 5 to 15 percent, to meet crash rules.
3. Fit round lamps, a chrome ring on each, and a grille in the old pattern.
4. Paint a panel of the dashboard in the body colour, with chrome rings on the dials.
5. Add chrome trim as a bright accent on the door handles, the mirrors and the grille.
6. Offer two-tone paint, a stripe, a roof in a second colour and a retro badge.
7. Hide the modern parts: airbags, screens and sensors, behind the retro surface.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A body with the key outline of a past car: a round bubble roof, a long-bonnet coupe or a short box. It is now bigger and taller, with a modern stance.
- **Line:** The old line, repeated with a higher waist, a deeper wing and larger wheels.
- **Fit:** A cabin with the space and safety of a new car, but with the dials and the dash of the old one.
- **Proportion:** Length 3.6 to 4.8 m; the body is 5 to 15 percent bigger than the original; the wheels are 16 to 19 inches.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the original face: the lamp shape, the grille and the badge.
  - Raise the waist line to meet today's crash rules.
  - Offer a roof in a second colour or a bonnet stripe.
  - Set a dash panel in the body colour, with round dials and a chrome ring.

## Techniques

### Automotive clay modelling (process)

A designer shapes a full-size car in warm industrial clay over a frame. The clay is cut, smoothed and checked in light before any metal is made. Origin: fashion, Detroit, United States, c. 1930.

- Build a wood and foam frame in the size of the car.
- Warm the clay and press it onto the frame, then cut it with rakes and scrapers.
- Check the highlights in light and measure the form for the dies.

### Chrome plating (process)

A thin layer of chromium is put on steel by electrolysis. The layer gives a hard, bright surface that does not tarnish. Origin: fashion, New York, United States, c. 1924.

- Clean the steel part and plate it with copper and nickel.
- Put the part in a chromic acid bath and pass a current through it.
- Buff the part to a mirror finish.
