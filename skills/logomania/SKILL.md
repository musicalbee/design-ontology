# Logomania

> The mark of the maker is the ornament, and the wearer shows a place in the world by it.

Accessories that carry a brand mark on the whole surface. Examples are a monogram repeat on coated canvas, large metal letters on a belt and a logo on a scarf. The mark is the design, and it shows status.

**Period:** 1985–2005 · **Era:** Power and subculture · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Designer logo fashion, Monogram bags and belts

## When to load this skill

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Logomania, you MUST:

1. Repeat the mark across the whole surface in rows, 2 to 4 cm tall.
2. Print or weave the mark in a tone that contrasts with the ground.
3. Use a coated canvas that shows the print and wears well.
4. Set a large metal letter or a double mark as a buckle or a clasp, 4 to 8 cm wide.
5. Carry the mark from the bag to the belt, the wallet and the scarf, so that the set matches.
6. Keep the leather trim in a plain tan or black to frame the print.
7. Place the mark so that it reads at a distance of 5 m.
8. Make a copy, a remix or a custom piece in the same logic, in the street.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Coated canvas (material)

Cotton or linen canvas covered with lacquer, paint or resin, so that it sheds rain, wipes clean and takes a printed pattern. Origin: fashion, Paris, France, 1854.

- Stretch the canvas flat and apply the coat in thin layers, 3 or more.
- Dry each layer, then stove or press the surface to a smooth finish.
- Print or stripe the pattern before the last coat, so that the coat protects it.

### Screen printing (process)

Ink pushed through a fine mesh with a squeegee; a stencil on the mesh blocks the parts that do not print. Origin: print, Manchester, 1907.

- Stretch a silk or polyester mesh of 40–120 threads a centimetre on a frame.
- Make the stencil by hand cut film or by a photo emulsion exposed through a film positive.
- Pull thick, opaque ink across the screen with a rubber squeegee in one even stroke.
- Print each colour from its own screen and let each layer dry before the next.
