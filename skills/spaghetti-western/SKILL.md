# Spaghetti western

> An extreme close-up and an extreme wide shot, with a long wait between them, make a duel into a ritual and the land into a stage.

A western shot in Spain and Italy on a wide 2.35:1 frame. A face fills the screen in an extreme close-up of the eyes, then the frame opens to a vast desert. Time stretches, the cut is slow, and the score is a part of the scene.

**Period:** 1964–1975 · **Era:** Portable cameras and the new waves · **Domain:** Film & Video · **Also called:** Italian western, Euro-western, Leone western

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "spaghetti western", "leone", "italian western", "extreme close-up eyes", "duel scene", "techniscope".

Good for: western and duel video prompts, trailer and title sequences, music video with a desert setting, fashion and spirits films.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a fast, modern action cut.
- The setting is a clean, indoor or urban place without a link to the myth.

## System directives

When planning, shooting, prompting, editing or grading moving images in Spaghetti western, you MUST:

1. Shoot in Techniscope at 2.35:1 with a 2-perf frame and shoot in colour on a hot, dry location.
2. Cut between an extreme close-up of the eyes (a 75mm to 150mm lens) and an extreme wide shot (a 20mm to 28mm lens).
3. Hold the build to a duel for 30 to 90 s before the first shot is fired.
4. Use a stylised, sparse set with sun, dust, stubble and sweat on every face.
5. Light with hard sun at a high angle, and keep the faces in deep, dark eye shadow.
6. Dub the sound after the shoot: loud boots, spurs, wind, one shot and a long silence.
7. Write the score first for the key scenes, and let the music set the length of the take.

## Composition

- **Framing:** A 2.35:1 frame with a split: a face at the edge and a far figure at the other edge, or one eye strip that fills the full width.
- **Staging in depth:** 2 to 4 planes: a foreground edge or a gun, a face or a body, and a far figure on the horizon.
- **Subject placement:** Faces at the edge and the horizon at 1/3 from the bottom; the centre stays empty.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule | Value |
|---|---|---|
| scale | Cut from an eye strip to a lone figure on the horizon. | eyes 100% of frame height, figure 5% to 10% |
| framing | Fill the wide frame with an eye strip, and place two duellists at the opposite edges. | 2 subjects at 5% and 95% of the frame width |
| layering-depth | Stage a duel in depth with a foreground object, a face and a far figure. | 3 planes, 1 close object at 10% of the frame |
| rhythm | Slow the cut to a crawl before a shot, and then break it with a fast burst of close-ups. | 30 to 90 s build, then 0.5 to 1 s cuts |
| negative-space | Leave the sky and the ground empty so that the figures stand alone. | 40% to 60% empty frame |

## Camera & movement

- **Movement:** A slow zoom or a dolly into the eyes; a crane that rises to reveal the town; short static wide shots.
- **Support:** Dolly, crane and tripod; a zoom lens for the push-in
- **Camera height:** 1.0 to 1.7 m; low angle for the gunmen
- **Move speed:** Zoom over 5 to 15 s; dolly 0.2 to 0.5 m/s

## Lens & format

- **Aspect ratio:** 2.35:1 (Techniscope, 2-perf)
- **Frame rate:** 24 fps
- **Shutter angle:** 180° (1/48 s)
- **Focal length:** 20mm to 28mm for the wide shot; 75mm to 150mm for the eye close-up
- **Aperture:** f/8 to f/11 in hard sun
- **Resolution:** 35mm 2-perf negative; scan at 2K or 4K
- **Focus:** Deep focus in the wide, a shallow focus of 0.1 to 0.3 m on the eyes
- **Equipment:** Techniscope 2-perf 35mm camera · a long telephoto lens · a zoom lens · reflectors for sun fill

## Light

- **Quality:** Hard desert sun, high contrast and burned skin; in the town a low lamp or a fire.
- **Direction:** From the top at 60° to 80°, so the eyes sit in shadow under the hat.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 4:1 to 6:1 in the sun
- **Colour temperature:** 5600K daylight
- **Source:** Sun, reflectors and a low fire or lamp at night

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** A warm, dry colour print with a dust-yellow base and a deep brown shadow.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Set the white balance at 5600K and push the highlights 300K warmer.
  - Lift the midtone saturation by 10% to 15% in the yellow and orange range.
  - Add a contrast curve of +25% with the black point at 4%.
  - Add grain at 20% to 25% and a vignette of 15%.
  - Keep the blue in the sky desaturated by 20%.
- **Texture:** 35mm grain at 20% to 25%, with dust and heat shimmer

## Editing & rhythm

- **Average shot length:** 5 to 8 s outside a duel, 0.5 to 2 s in a duel (estimate)
- **Shot length range:** 0.3 s for an eye flash to 60 s for a long build
- **Cutting logic:** Cut to the music; hold the build to a duel with a rising count of close-ups, then fire and cut out.
- **Transitions:** Hard cut, a slow dissolve for the time of a ride and a snap cut on a shot.
- **Runtime:** 100 to 175 min

## Sound

- **Approach:** Post-synchronised sound with a loud, simple mix: spurs, wind, flies and a creak.
- **Dialogue:** Sparse and dry; the actors speak in several languages and the voices are dubbed.
- **Music:** A score with a whistle, a guitar, a choir and a bell, written to lead the scene.
- **Loudness:** −23 LUFS integrated, with a wide dynamic range for the silence

## Titles & graphics

- **Treatment:** A stylised title over a painted or an animated sequence; the credits in a bold western face.
- **Type:** A heavy slab-serif in warm white or orange, 3 to 5 s per card.
