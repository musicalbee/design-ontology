# Meissen baroque porcelain

> Once Europe can make porcelain, it shows the fact: a white body that is modelled in a sculptural mass and painted in every enamel colour.

The first true European hard-paste porcelain. The factory at Meissen paints it with a bright enamel palette, models it in full baroque relief, and gilds the edge, for the court of Saxony.

**Period:** 1720–1756 · **Era:** The porcelain race · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Meissen porcelain, Böttger and Kändler porcelain, Saxon court porcelain

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Meissen baroque porcelain, you MUST:

1. Fire the hard paste at 1400 °C in reduction so that it turns dense and translucent.
2. Paint with a palette of 10 or more overglaze enamel colours, and fire them at 800 °C.
3. Model the form in relief: swans, shells, reeds, and applied flowers, as in a sculpture.
4. Divide a plate into a moulded rim and a painted well; add gold on the edge.
5. Paint harbour scenes, Chinese figures and flowers in panels called reserves, framed by gold scroll work.
6. Mark the base with the crossed swords, painted in cobalt under the glaze.
7. Make the whole service for one patron, with his arms or his initials on each piece.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Tureens, plates with a moulded rim, sauceboats, covered bowls, and large dishes, with modelled handles and knops.
- **Profile:** A full, curved body with a moulded border; the rim carries relief of shells, scrolls or basket weave in a regular pattern.
- **Foot and rim:** A low ring foot with a glazed base and the crossed swords; the rim is moulded, with a gilt edge.
- **Proportion:** Plate diameter 22 to 26 cm and wall 3 to 5 mm thick; a tureen may be 35 cm long.
- **Rules:**
  - Mould the border in relief and keep the well flat for the painting.
  - Model each knop and handle as a figure: a swan, a fruit or a flower.
  - Gild the edge of each rim and each handle.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** european-hard-paste
- **Secondary:** silver
- **Avoid:** earthenware · stoneware · bone-china
- **Rule:** Use a hard paste of kaolin, feldspar and quartz, which fires dense and white. Do not use earthenware, and do not use bone china: it did not exist.

## Forming

- **Methods:** press moulding in plaster moulds · slip casting of figures and handles · modelling by hand for the master models · luting of cast and applied parts · painting in enamel colours · gilding with gold in a binder
- **Joins:** Cast handles, knops and flowers are luted with slip on the leather-hard body; silver mounts are fixed after the firing.
- **Details:** A fine slip seam on a cast part, a crossed-swords mark in blue under the glaze, and a number cut in the base.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** clear-feldspathic
- **Decorations:** overglaze-enamel · gilding · relief-moulding · underglaze-cobalt · sprigging
- **Pattern:** Chinoiserie scenes, harbour views, flowers and birds in reserves, with gold scroll work and applied flowers.
- **Gloss:** Bright gloss, about 80 to 95 GU, on a very white ground.
- **Wear:** The gold wears at the rim and handles; the enamel colours stay fresh on the hard glaze.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A pure white ground with the full enamel palette: strong red, yellow and green, a purple, a turquoise, and gold.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A wood-fired kiln, with saggars, with a first firing for the biscuit and a second for the glaze; the enamel goes in a small muffle kiln.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 900 °C · dipping in glaze · glaze firing at about 1400 °C in reduction · enamel firing at about 800 °C · gold firing at a lower heat
- **Scale:** A manufactory of hundreds of painters, modellers and gilders under a royal director.
- **Signs:** The crossed swords in blue under the glaze, an incised model number, and a fine slip seam on cast parts.

### Overglaze enamelling (process)

Coloured glass enamels are painted on a glazed and fired piece. A third firing at a low heat fixes them on the glaze. Origin: ceramics, Jingdezhen, Jiangxi, China, c. 1465.

- Fire the piece with a clear glaze.
- Paint the design with enamels of red, green, yellow and blue.
- Fire it again in a small muffle kiln at 750 to 850 °C.
- Add gold in a last low firing, when the design needs it.
