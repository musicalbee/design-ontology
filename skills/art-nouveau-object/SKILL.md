# Art Nouveau object

> An object grows like a plant: its structure and its ornament are one curved line, made by a craftsman from the best material.

Lamps, glass, furniture and fixtures drawn from the line of a plant stem: cast bronze, art glass, carved fruit wood and porcelain, with one long curve that joins structure and ornament.

**Period:** 1893–1914 · **Era:** Reform and craft · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Jugendstil object, Modern Style object, Liberty-style object

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Art Nouveau object, you MUST:

1. Draw each support as a plant stem that is thin at the root of the curve and thick where the load meets it.
2. Join structure and ornament in one S-shaped line; a leg, a handle and a leaf are one stroke.
3. Use coloured art glass in two to four layers for a shade or a vase, and let light pass through it.
4. Cast the metal parts in bronze or brass and give them a brown or green patina.
5. Carve fruit wood with a curved edge; leave 3 to 5 mm of soft radius on every corner.
6. Take the motif from nature: the iris, the orchid, the water lily, the dragonfly and the thistle.
7. Make each piece a unit with its room: the same curve runs on the lamp, the tap, the chair and the handle.

## Form

- **Geometry:** Whiplash curves, ellipses and drops; each form swells and narrows along one curved axis, as a stem or a bud does.
- **Silhouette:** An asymmetric outline with a long S-curve: a lamp stem bends to one side and ends in a bud-shaped shade.
- **Edges:** Soft edges with a radius of 3 to 10 mm; the rim of a vase and the edge of a table top flow in a wave, not a straight line.
- **Proportion:** A table lamp is about 45 cm high; the shade is 1 to 1.2 times the width of the base, and the stem is slim, about 1 part to 4 parts of height.
- **Ornament:** Plant and insect motifs in relief, in inlay or in the glass layers; they cover 30 to 60% of the surface and follow the structure.
- **Rules:**
  - Each piece has one dominant curve that runs from the base to the top.
  - No part is a straight line longer than 15 cm.
  - Legs and stems are thin at the join and thick at the load, in a ratio of about 1 to 2.
  - Each motif follows the structure; no motif is a flat pattern that ignores the form.
  - Glass has 2 to 4 layers of colour; the outer layer is cut or etched to show the inner layer.

## Materials

- **Primary:** coloured-glass · bronze · walnut · mahogany · porcelain
- **Secondary:** solid-brass · silver-plate · pressed-glass · opal-glass · oak · cherry · ash · copper · stoneware · velvet · leather · wrought-iron · silk
- **Avoid:** chrome-plated-steel · stainless-steel · abs-plastic · plastic-laminate · acrylic · concrete · polypropylene
- **Rule:** Glass and metal carry the light and the line; wood and textile are warm and dark, and each wood piece is about 40% of the visible surface.

## Finish & texture

- **Finishes:** patinated · acid-etched · french-polished · waxed · gilded · vitreous-glaze · clear-gloss
- **Textures:** cast-relief · etched · figured-grain · fluted · satin-smooth · mirror-smooth · open-grain
- **Gloss:** 30–70 GU on wood and patinated metal; 80–95 GU on glaze and glass
- **Patina:** The style wants a brown or green patina on bronze and a deep, warm shine on wood; a bright new surface looks wrong.

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** bronze
- **Section:** Stems and rods 10–18 mm, narrowing to 6 mm; handles 12–20 mm
- **Joints:** Cast in one piece, or joined by a hidden dovetail or a tenon; a screw is hidden under a bud, a leaf or a cap.
- **Handles:** A cast bronze pull in the form of a stem, a tendril or a leaf, with a long curve that fits the hand.
- **Switches and taps:** A tap with a swan-neck spout and a lever in the form of a stem; a switch in porcelain or brass on a carved wood block.

## Techniques

### Whiplash line (motif)

A long S-shaped line whose curve changes along its length, drawn from a plant stem; it joins structure and ornament in one stroke. Origin: architecture, Brussels, c. 1893.

- Draw each line as an S-curve with a radius that changes along its length.
- Swell the stroke from 1 to 4 units along the curve, as a stem thickens at the base.
- Join letters, frame and plant forms with the same line; leave no straight edge to break the flow.
- Use few colours, 2 to 4, in muted tones.
