# Realism

> A picture should record the real world of its own time, and ordinary work and ordinary people are as worthy a subject as a king or a god.

Ordinary people, work and country in plain light and earth colour. The painter shows life as it is, at life size, with no myth, no ideal and no sweetness.

**Period:** 1848–1880 · **Era:** Nineteenth century · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Courbet realism, Naturalism

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "realism", "realist painting", "courbet", "millet", "daumier", "peasant painting", "stonebreakers", "social realism", "plain life".

Good for: farm, craft and food provenance identity, editorial on work, labour and the land, documentary book and film cover, portrait with a plain, honest tone.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs glamour, fantasy or a bright, playful mood.
- The picture must be abstract, stylised or ornamental.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Realism, you MUST:

1. Choose a subject from daily life: a peasant, a worker, a stonebreaker, a farm, a road.
2. Paint the figures at life size, or large against the picture, at 60–100% of its height.
3. Keep the light plain and from one side: an overcast day or a window, with no divine beam.
4. Hold the palette to earth colour: brown, ochre, grey, olive and a dull black, with one muted red or blue.
5. Build the form with a palette knife or a heavy brush in thick, matt paint, and leave rough edges.
6. Compose with the figures in a plain, near-frieze arrangement, off-centre, and cut by the edge as in life.
7. Add no moral, no idealised body and no story; show weight, dirt, wear and posture.

## Composition

- **Layout:** A plain, frieze-like arrangement of life-size figures on a shallow stage, with a low horizon at 25–35% of the height and no central axis.
- **Focal point:** The main figure or tool, set off-centre at one third from an edge, in the lightest value of the picture.
- **Format:** A large, wide canvas at 4:3 to 2:1 for a group scene, or a vertical canvas for a single figure; the edge cuts the figures and the land as in a snapshot.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| scale | Paint the figures at 60–100% of the picture height, close to life size, so that the viewer stands among them. |
| grouping | Gather 2–5 figures into one tight cluster of 30–40% of the picture width, and leave a plain ground around it. |
| balance | Place the heavy figure mass off-centre and balance it with the large, plain land or sky at 40–50% of the area. |
| colour-distribution | Spend 70–80% of the area on earth browns, ochres and greys, and keep a muted accent of red or blue at 5–10%. |
| lighting-layers | Use one plain light from one side with a pale fill, and keep the shadow at value 25–40% with soft edges. |
| hierarchy | Make 2 levels: one lit figure or tool as the focus, and the rest in half-tone at 15–20% darker. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Earth colour in a low key: brown, ochre, olive and grey, with a dull black and one muted accent.
- **Distribution:** Brown and ochre ground 40–50%, olive and grey land and sky 30–40%, black 10%, muted accent 5–10%.
- **Ground:** A mid-brown or red-brown ground, painted over in thick, matt colour.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint, thick and stiff, bound with little oil.
- **Support:** Large canvas on a brown or red-brown ground.
- **Brushwork:** Broad, flat strokes of 10–30mm and a palette knife for land and rock; the touch is heavy and plain.
- **Paint handling:** 2–3 layers of opaque, thick paint of 1–3mm, with dark glazes only in the deepest shadows.
- **Edges:** Soft to middling edges; a hard edge only on a tool and a face, and the land has a rough, torn edge.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** Plain daylight from one side or a window: overcast and even, with no beam and no halo.
- **Modelling:** Solid and weighty, with 4–5 steps; the form is built by value and not by line.
- **Shadow:** Dark brown and soft, at value 25–40%, on one side of the form, with a pale reflected light.
- **Temperature:** A neutral to warm-brown key with a cool grey sky; no strong warm and cool split.

## Subject

- **Typical:** Peasants at work in the field, stonebreakers, washerwomen, a country burial, a farm, a road and a bourgeois portrait.
- **Figure scale:** Life size or large: 60–100% of the picture height.
- **Treatment:** Plain, direct and unflattering; the work, the weight and the wear of the body are shown.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Matt to satin, with no gloss.
- **Texture:** Rough and thick: knife marks, ridges and a coarse canvas grain stay in view.

## Techniques

### Plein air (process, invented here)

Work made outside in front of the subject, in the light of the place and the hour. Origin: painting, Rome and France, c. 1780.

- Work in front of the motif in one session of 1 to 3 hours, before the light moves.
- Take the colour of shadows from the sky and the ground around them; do not paint them black.
- Use the light that is there; add no lamp.
