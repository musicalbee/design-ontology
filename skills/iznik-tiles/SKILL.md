# Iznik tiles

> A court tile is a garden in glaze: the painter shows tulips, carnations and leaves in clear colours on a white ground, and the glaze gives them depth.

Painted wall tiles that the Iznik kilns made for the Ottoman court. A white quartz body takes a white slip, a free painting of flowers in cobalt, turquoise, green and raised red, and a clear glaze.

**Period:** 1550–1700 · **Era:** Blue and white · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Ottoman court tiles, İznik çini, Iznik wall tiles

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Iznik tiles, you MUST:

1. Cover the quartz body with a white slip, then paint the design on the slip under a clear glaze.
2. Draw the outline in black or cobalt first, then fill the shapes with flat colour.
3. Paint the flowers of the court garden: tulip, carnation, hyacinth, rose and the long saz leaf.
4. Put the raised red, made of bole clay, on a flower only after about 1560; keep it 0.1 to 0.3 mm thick.
5. Add emerald green after 1567; before that use only cobalt, turquoise, purple and black.
6. Set the tiles in a square repeat of 25 cm and fit a border and a corner tile to each panel.
7. Cover a whole wall or lunette over a window, with the design running over the joints.
8. Leave a white ground between the shapes; let the white carry about one third of the surface.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Square wall tiles in sets, tile panels and lunettes for mosques and palaces, border tiles with a saz-leaf frieze, and tile friezes for tombs.
- **Profile:** A flat plane with a very slight relief where the red bole stands above the glaze; the tile is thin and light for a wall.
- **Foot and rim:** The back is rough and unglazed with a mortar key; edges are square and cut after the biscuit firing to fit tight joints.
- **Proportion:** A wall tile is about 25 cm square and 10 to 15 mm thick; panels cover many square metres.
- **Rules:**
  - Paint the picture across several tiles and cut the tiles to the pattern.
  - Keep the white slip and the clear glaze thin, about 0.5 mm each.
  - Fit the repeat of the flowers to the tile joints.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stonepaste
- **Secondary:** earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · creamware
- **Rule:** Use a white stonepaste of ground quartz, glass frit and a little clay. It is hard and white like porcelain, but it contains no kaolin.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pressing the tile in a flat mould · drying the tile in the shade · painting from a pricked paper cartoon · applying a thin white slip · dipping in a clear glaze
- **Joins:** Tiles are set in lime mortar on the wall, with joints of 1 to 2 mm.
- **Details:** Pinprick marks of the cartoon, a clear brush line, a fine crackle in the glaze and a slight blur at the edge of the colours.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** alkaline-glaze
- **Decorations:** underglaze-polychrome · underglaze-cobalt
- **Pattern:** Tulips, carnations, hyacinths and saz leaves in cobalt, turquoise, green and raised red on a white slip ground.
- **Gloss:** Bright and glassy, about 80 to 95 GU.
- **Wear:** The glaze keeps its shine for centuries; the red crazes in time, and old tiles show fine crackle.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A white ground with cobalt and turquoise; the tomato red from 1560 and the emerald green from 1567 give the peak colours.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A kiln with saggars in the town of Iznik, fired with wood.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing of the pressed tile · slip and painting on the biscuit · glaze firing in one pass
- **Scale:** Iznik kilns made large sets of tiles for imperial buildings in Istanbul, often to one order of the court.
- **Signs:** Raised red under the glaze, a fine crackle, a blur of the blue at the outline, and stilt marks on the back.

### Raised bole red (material)

A thick slip of red clay that stands in low relief on the tile and fires to a bright tomato red under a clear glaze. It is the signature colour of Iznik tiles. Origin: ceramics, Iznik, Anatolia, c. 1560.

- Make a slip of red bole clay, rich in iron.
- Apply it thick with a brush, so that it stands 0.5 to 1 mm above the white ground.
- Cover it with a clear glaze and fire it in an oxidising kiln.
- Use it for flowers, so that red joins blue, turquoise and green.
