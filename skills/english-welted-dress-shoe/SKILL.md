# English welted dress shoe

> A dress shoe lasts decades when its sole is welted and the upper is fine calf, so the shoe is a quiet sign of care.

The lace-up dress shoe of the English gentleman. It is a closed-lacing Oxford or an open-lacing Derby in polished calf, sewn to a leather sole with a welt that a cobbler can replace.

**Period:** 1871–present · **Era:** Victorian dress · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Goodyear-welted Oxford, Bespoke Northampton shoe, Gentleman's dress shoe

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in English welted dress shoe, you MUST:

1. Stitch the leather sole to the upper through a welt, so that a cobbler can resole it four to six times.
2. Close the throat of an Oxford with a lace tab that is sewn under the vamp.
3. Keep the toe round or slightly chiselled, and the toe cap plain.
4. Cut the heel stack at 2.5 cm and the waist of the sole narrow and close.
5. Burnish the toe and the heel to a dark shine in two coats of polish.
6. Use 5 to 6 pairs of eyelets, with a flat waxed lace.
7. Choose a last with a quiet shape: no sharp toe, no square toe.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A long, low shoe with a round, slightly chiselled toe and a waisted sole.
- **Line:** One clean line from the toe cap along the vamp to the lace, with a low arch.
- **Fit:** Close at the heel and the instep; the lace tightens the quarters around the foot.
- **Proportion:** Heel 2.5 cm; vamp length about 40 percent of the shoe; five or six eyelet pairs.
- **Rules:**
  - Hold the line plain: a toe cap or a wing tip, but not both.
  - Stitch the welt at the edge of the sole in fine, even stitches.
  - Match the belt to the shoe in colour and finish.
  - Wear black for the city, brown for the country.

## Construction

- **Methods:** Goodyear welt stitched around the insole rib · hand lasting over a wooden last · channelled insole · closed-throat Oxford quarters · leather and cork filler under the insole · cut and stitched toe cap
- **Closures:** Flat waxed laces through five or six pairs of eyelets; closed lacing for an Oxford, open lacing for a Derby.
- **Details:** A straight toe cap, a perforated medallion on a brogue, a leather-lined heel, a stacked leather heel with a rubber top piece.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** burnished · matte · patent-lacquered · woven-leather
- **Pattern:** Plain, or fine perforations (brogueing) along the seams on a country shoe.
- **Gloss:** Polished calf at 30–60 GU; suede at 2–5 GU; patent for evening at 80–95 GU.
- **Wear:** The style wants a patina: the leather darkens, creases at the flex and takes a deeper shine each year.

### Goodyear welting (process)

A way to sew a strip of leather, the welt, to the upper and the insole rib, and then to stitch the sole to the welt. Origin: fashion, United States, c. 1869.

- Sew the welt to the upper and to the rib of the insole.
- Fill the gap under the insole with cork.
- Stitch the outsole to the welt, so that a cobbler can replace it.
