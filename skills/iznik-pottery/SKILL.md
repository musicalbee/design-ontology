# Iznik pottery

> A bright garden of flowers can be painted flat and sharp on a white body, and it stays fresh under a clear glass-like glaze.

Ottoman court pottery with a white quartz body, a white slip and a clear glaze. Painters used cobalt, turquoise, green and a thick tomato red for tulips, carnations and saz leaves.

**Period:** 1480–1700 · **Era:** Blue and white · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Iznik ware, Ottoman fritware, Turkish court pottery

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Iznik pottery, you MUST:

1. Make the body of ground quartz, glass frit and a little clay, and cover it with a fine white slip.
2. Paint under a clear alkaline glaze, so that the colours stay bright after the firing.
3. Use a limited range of colours: cobalt blue, turquoise, then green, and after 1550 a raised tomato red.
4. Outline the motifs in a dark line and fill them in flat, with no shading.
5. Draw tulips, carnations, hyacinths, roses and saz leaves, and arrange them in a flowing, balanced garden.
6. Set an even border of waves and rocks, or a scroll, round the rim of a dish.
7. Fire at 900 to 1050 °C in an oxidising kiln.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** A buff clay body without a white slip.
- **NEVER:** Soft blended shading in the petals.
- **NEVER:** Overglaze enamel or gilding on top of the glaze.
- **NEVER:** A thick glossy glaze that blurs the line.
- **NEVER:** Naturalistic perspective and shadows.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Large dishes, bowls on a foot, jugs, mosque lamps, flasks and tall flat-sided bottles.
- **Profile:** A dish has a shallow well and a broad, flat rim; a jug has a round body and a short, strong neck.
- **Foot and rim:** A low foot ring with a pierced hole, a flat or slightly flared rim, and a lip of dark blue or turquoise.
- **Proportion:** Dish diameter 25 to 40 cm and a wall 4 to 7 mm thick; a jug height 20 to 30 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Divide a dish into a central design and a border of repeated waves.
  - Keep the body thin and hard, with a very white surface.
  - Pierce the foot ring for a cord to hang the dish.
  - Align the motifs with the symmetry of the form.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stonepaste
- **Secondary:** earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · creamware
- **Rule:** Use a stonepaste of ground quartz, glass frit and white clay. It is hard and white, but it is not a porcelain.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the wheel · press moulding of tiles and flasks · applying white slip · painting with a brush on the slip · dipping in glaze
- **Joins:** Handles and spouts are luted with slip; mosque lamps are thrown in two parts and joined.
- **Details:** A white slip that shows a dark line at the edge, a hole in the foot ring, and a red that stands up slightly from the surface.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** alkaline-glaze
- **Decorations:** underglaze-polychrome · underglaze-cobalt
- **Pattern:** Tulips, carnations, hyacinths and saz leaves in flowing scrolls, with a border of rocks and waves.
- **Gloss:** Glassy, about 80 to 95 GU, with a clear, thin alkaline glaze.
- **Wear:** The tomato red may flake or raise a little; the glaze may craze and show a faint green tint at the thicker places.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A white ground with cobalt blue, turquoise and a thick tomato red in the second half; green and black outlines add depth.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A wood-fired kiln with saggars; the potters made the glaze and the body with fine quartz.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** throw the body · dip in white slip · paint with colours · dip in clear glaze · single glaze firing in saggars
- **Scale:** Dozens of potteries in Iznik worked for the Ottoman court and for export, in the 16th century.
- **Signs:** A bright thin glaze, raised red, a cord hole in the foot, and sharp black outlines on a pure white ground.

### Cobalt underglaze painting (process)

Cobalt oxide is painted on the raw or bisque body. A clear glaze covers it, and one firing turns the paint a deep blue. Origin: ceramics, Jingdezhen, Jiangxi, China, c. 1330.

- Grind cobalt ore and mix it with water.
- Paint the design on the dry body with a brush.
- Dip the piece in a clear glaze.
- Fire it once at 1250 to 1300 °C.

### Stonepaste body (material)

A body of ground quartz, glass frit and a little white clay. It is white and hard, and it takes a thin glaze, but it is not as clear as porcelain. Origin: ceramics, Egypt, Iraq and Iran, c. 1000.

- Grind quartz pebbles to a fine powder.
- Mix with glass frit and about 10 per cent of white clay.
- Shape the body on the wheel or in a mould.
- Glaze and fire at about 1000 °C.
