# Garçonne and flapper dress

> After the war, a woman's dress drops the corset and the waist, and a straight, short, easy line lets her work and dance.

The short, straight dress of the 1920s: a loose chemise with a low waist and a knee hem, in jersey or beaded chiffon.

**Period:** 1920s · **Era:** Interwar modern · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Flapper dress, Chemise dress, 1920s drop-waist dress, La Garçonne

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Garçonne and flapper dress, you MUST:

1. Cut the dress as a straight tube with the waist seam at the hip, about 20 cm below the natural waist.
2. Set the hem at the knee, between 25 and 40 cm above the floor.
3. Skip the corset; use a flat bandeau so that the chest looks level.
4. Use jersey or crêpe de Chine for day, and silk chiffon or georgette for the evening.
5. Cover an evening dress in glass beads in rows, so that it weighs 3 kg or more and swings with each step.
6. Keep the neckline round or V-shaped and the arm bare for evening.
7. Wear a cloche hat, long beads and a low-heeled shoe with a strap.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A straight tube from the shoulder to the knee with a low waist at the hip.
- **Line:** A level bust, a drop waist at the hip, a hem at the knee and a bare or short sleeve.
- **Fit:** Loose on the body; the dress hangs from the shoulder and does not follow the waist.
- **Proportion:** Waist seam about 20 cm below the natural waist; hem 25 to 40 cm above the floor; bust, waist and hip nearly equal.
- **Rules:**
  - Place the waist seam at the hip, and use a sash or a belt there.
  - Hang the dress from the shoulder in straight panels with no darts.
  - Add movement with fringe, pleats or a hem in points, not with volume.
  - Match the beads to the cloth, so that the weight keeps the line straight.
  - Pair the dress with a cloche hat and a long string of beads.

## Construction

- **Methods:** straight panels with a low waist seam · bias-cut godets or pleats in the skirt · glass beads sewn on by tambour hook · pleats set by heat · silk slip cut in the same line
- **Closures:** A side placket with snaps or hooks, or no closure at all when the dress pulls on over the head.
- **Details:** A round or V neckline, a bare arm, a low sash, a fringe or a hem with points, and long beads.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** beaded · matte · embroidered · heat-set-pleated
- **Pattern:** Plain jersey for day; for evening, beads in Art Deco bands and fans, or all-over sequins and fringe.
- **Gloss:** Jersey and crêpe at 2–6 GU; beaded chiffon at 20–50 GU where the glass catches light.
- **Wear:** Glass beads pull the silk and the thread rots, so old beaded dresses shed beads and tear at the shoulder.

### Tambour embroidery (skill)

A fast chain stitch made with a fine hook on cloth that is stretched tight in a frame; later the same hook sews beads on silk. Origin: fashion, France and Britain, c. 1760.

- Stretch the cloth tight in a round frame.
- Pull a loop of thread up through the cloth with the hook, and chain the loops along the pattern.
- Thread beads on the yarn first, and chain them into the cloth to make a beaded surface.
