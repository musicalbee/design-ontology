# Pointillism

> Colour is a science: pure dots set side by side mix in the eye with more light than a mix on the palette, and a picture can be built by rule.

A picture made of small, separate dots of pure colour that the eye mixes at a distance. The scene is still and ordered, built by colour theory and not by blending.

**Period:** 1886–1891 · **Era:** Nineteenth century · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Divisionism, Neo-Impressionism, Chromoluminarism

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "pointillism", "pointillist", "seurat", "signac", "divisionism", "neo-impressionism", "dots of colour", "la grande jatte", "optical mixing".

Good for: poster and cover with a luminous, dotted texture, print and halftone-inspired illustration, leisure, travel and park identity, educational material on colour theory.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a fast, loose or spontaneous mood.
- The picture must show sharp fine detail, such as a face or a product label.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Pointillism, you MUST:

1. Lay each dot as a separate touch of pure colour, 2–5mm across, with a ground that shows between them.
2. Mix by juxtaposition: set complementary pairs, such as orange and blue, next to each other, so that the eye fuses them.
3. Build each area from 3 elements: local colour, light (warm) and shadow (cool complement), plus a reflected colour.
4. Hold the scene in still, simple masses: a frieze of figures in profile and in full face, in a clear, ordered space.
5. Plan the picture on a grid or a golden-section armature, with strong verticals and horizontals.
6. Add a painted border in the colour that contrasts with the edge of the scene.
7. Use a matt, chalky surface so that the dots stay pure and bright.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Blended or glazed colour that hides the dot.
- **NEVER:** Spontaneous, quick and uneven strokes.
- **NEVER:** Dark, black or brown shadows.
- **NEVER:** Soft, lost edges that run into the next form.
- **NEVER:** Movement, blur and a fleeting moment.
- **NEVER:** Large flat areas of one mixed colour.

## Composition

- **Layout:** A still, ordered arrangement of simple masses on a hidden grid of verticals and horizontals, with a frieze of figures at the front and a horizon at 40–50% of the height.
- **Focal point:** A figure or a mass at one third from an edge, set by the golden section at 0.62 of the width or the height.
- **Format:** A wide canvas at 3:2 to 2:1; a painted border of 20–60mm in a dot-built contrast colour closes the picture.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| proportion | Divide the canvas by the golden section at 0.62, and set the main vertical and the horizon on those lines. |
| rhythm | Repeat a small unit, such as a figure, a tree or a parasol, 6–10 times at a regular step of 8–12% of the picture width. |
| symmetry-axis | Use a quiet, formal pose: figures in strict profile or full face, with a vertical axis in each figure and none for the whole picture. |
| colour-distribution | Split every area into 3 dot colours: a local hue at 60%, a warm light hue at 25%, and a cool shadow hue at 15%. |
| unity-variety | Hold the dot size to 2–5mm across the whole picture, and vary only the hue and value of the dot. |
| framing | Close the picture with a painted border of 20–60mm, in a hue that is the complement of the nearby edge. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Pure spectral hues set in dots, with complementary pairs in contact; no black and no earth, and the mix happens in the eye.
- **Distribution:** A broad light and sky area 30–40%, local colour of the subject 30–40%, a cool shadow colour 15–20%, and a warm light colour 10–15%.
- **Ground:** A white or very pale ground that shows between the dots and lifts the colour.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint, thick and matt, used straight from the tube with little medium.
- **Support:** A fine canvas with a white ground.
- **Brushwork:** Small, round dots or short touches of 2–5mm, laid in regular rows, one dab at a time; 10 000 or more dots on a large canvas.
- **Paint handling:** One layer of dots, with a second pass of 1–2 corrections; no blending and no glaze.
- **Edges:** A hard form edge made by a change in the dot hue; a slight vibration, with no line drawn.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** A bright, even daylight, shown by warm yellow-orange dots in the lit areas and cool blue-violet dots in the shadow.
- **Modelling:** By the change of the dot hue and value in 3–4 steps; the form is built by colour and not by line.
- **Shadow:** A cool, coloured shadow of blue, violet or green dots at value 40–60%; no black.
- **Temperature:** A warm lit area against a cool shadow, with the complement set side by side so that the edge vibrates.

## Subject

- **Typical:** Leisure in a park or on a river, a harbour, a bather, a circus, a seaside and a town scene.
- **Figure scale:** Small to medium figures at 15–45% of the picture height, seen in profile or full face.
- **Treatment:** Calm, frozen and modern; people stand and sit as in a frieze, with no story and no sign of emotion.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Matt and chalky.
- **Texture:** A fine, regular grain of dots across the whole picture, with the canvas weave in view.

## Lineage fallbacks

When the user asks for a variation, do not improvise. Blend toward the neighbour named here, and load its skill.

- If the user asks for **looser and more spontaneous**: move toward [`impressionism`](../impressionism/SKILL.md) (Impressionism). Keep the bright colour and the open-air light; make the touch a quick dab or dash of 10–20mm, allow blending at the edges, and drop the grid.
- If the user asks for **bolder and wilder colour**: move toward [`fauvism`](../fauvism/SKILL.md) (Fauvism). Keep the pure hue; enlarge the dot to a patch of 20–60mm, allow a hue that is not true to nature, and add a dark contour.
