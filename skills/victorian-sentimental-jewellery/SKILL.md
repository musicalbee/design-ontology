# Victorian Sentimental Jewellery

> A jewel carries a memory: it holds a lock of hair, a name or a likeness close to the body.

Jewels that hold a person or a feeling: lockets, hair work, cameos, snakes, hearts, and black jet for mourning. They were made in gold, jet and pearl in Britain after 1837.

**Period:** 1837–1901 · **Era:** Victorian dress · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Victorian mourning and love jewellery, Sentimental jewels of the Victorian era

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Victorian Sentimental Jewellery, you MUST:

1. Give each piece a hollow or a glass front for a lock of hair, a portrait or a monogram.
2. Carve jet or conch shell by hand into a flower, a hand, a cross or a head in relief.
3. Make mourning jewellery in black jet for the first year and the first two years of widowhood, with pearls as tears.
4. Shape the piece as a symbol: a heart, a snake with a tail in its mouth, a clasped hand, an anchor or a forget-me-not.
5. Engrave a date, a name or a motto on the back of the piece.
6. Wear a locket on a long chain or a short velvet ribbon, at the centre of the chest or the throat.
7. Set small stones in a ring so that the first letters of the stones spell a word, such as REGARD.
8. Fit a brooch with a safety catch and a hinged back, for a pin that holds a shawl or a collar.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A medium, hand-sized jewel with a message: an oval locket, a cameo brooch or a bracelet of hair with a gold clasp.
- **Line:** Oval, round and heart outlines with a bead or a rope edge, kept simple so that the symbol reads.
- **Fit:** Worn at the throat or the chest on a chain or a ribbon, pinned at the collar, or on the wrist.
- **Proportion:** A locket is 3 to 5 cm tall. A cameo brooch is 4 to 6 cm wide. A mourning chain is 40 to 90 cm long.
- **Rules:**
  - Place a locket on a long chain at the centre of the chest.
  - Pin a cameo or a jet brooch at the high neck of a dark dress.
  - Make a bracelet of a gold band with a hinged snake head and a small stone for an eye.
  - Pair a black jet brooch, a bracelet and a drop earring as a mourning set.

## Construction

- **Methods:** hand-carved jet in relief · conch-shell cameo cut in layers · hair woven or coiled under a glass front · hollow locket with a hinged back · gold repoussé and engraved back · black enamel borders for a mourning ring
- **Closures:** A hinged back and a pin with a safety catch on a brooch; a spring ring on a chain; a bolt ring on a bracelet.
- **Details:** A glass or crystal cover over hair, a pearl border, a gold rope or bead edge, a black enamel band and an engraved date.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** carved · engraved · enamelled · polished · cabochon · faceted
- **Pattern:** Relief of flowers, hands and heads; engraved script and borders of beads or rope.
- **Gloss:** Jet and polished gold take a glass-like shine of 60 to 80 GU; mourning pieces keep black and bright.
- **Wear:** Jet chips and gold rubs on the edge; a worn locket shows the owner's hand and keeps its meaning.

### Jet carving (skill)

The cutting and polishing of jet, a hard black fossil wood, into beads, brooches and cameos. Origin: fashion, Whitby, England, c. 1808.

- Cut the rough jet to a block with a saw.
- Carve the block with a knife and a file into the form of the piece.
- Polish the piece with fine powder until it shines black.
