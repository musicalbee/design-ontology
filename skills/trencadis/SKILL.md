# Trencadís

> A broken tile is not waste: a wall of shards takes any curve, and the glaze colour of each piece joins into one bright surface.

A surface of broken tile and china that is set in mortar on a curved wall, bench or roof. Antoni Gaudí and Josep Maria Jujol made it a free, colourful skin for Barcelona buildings.

**Period:** 1900 to the present · **Era:** Art pottery and reform · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Broken tile mosaic, Pique assiette on architecture, Shard mosaic

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Trencadís, you MUST:

1. Break glazed tiles and plates with a hammer into pieces of 2 to 10 cm.
2. Set the pieces in a bed of wet mortar on a curved wall, a bench or a chimney.
3. Mix many colours in one field; place the pieces at random and keep the pattern loose.
4. Add shards of plates, bottle glass and cups, so that a few printed motifs appear.
5. Keep the glaze face outward; the sharp edges of the shards stay below the grout line.
6. Fill the gaps with a thin mortar joint of 3 to 5 mm and wipe the face clean.
7. Use the mosaic on a form that has a curve: a serpentine bench, a lizard, a dome or a chimney.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Curved benches, ridge ornaments, chimneys, finials, domed roofs, columns and sculptural animals that carry a skin of shards.
- **Profile:** A soft, flowing curve with no sharp corner; the surface is rough and bright, with small variations of 1 to 3 mm in the height of the shards.
- **Foot and rim:** Edges are rounded with mortar and the shards are cut to follow the curve; the top of a bench ends in a smooth lip.
- **Proportion:** Shards are 2 to 10 cm across; the mortar bed is 2 to 3 cm thick and the mosaic skin adds 10 to 15 mm to the wall.
- **Rules:** Apply the shards on a curve. · Use free colour in each field. · Break the tile by hand and keep the pieces large. · Do not place the shards in rows.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Secondary:** porcelain · stoneware · terracotta · glass · concrete
- **Avoid:** paper-clay · technical-porcelain · vitreous-china · porcelain-stoneware
- **Rule:** Use the waste of tile factories and household china: glazed earthenware first, with porcelain and glass as a bright accent. The mortar base must be strong and curved.

## Forming

- **Methods:** breaking glazed tile by hand · setting shards in wet mortar · cutting the shards with nippers · wiping and polishing the surface
- **Joins:** Each shard is pressed into a bed of lime or cement mortar on the wall; the joint is thin and the same colour as the mortar.
- **Details:** A small chip on the edge of a shard, a part of a printed flower or a letter on a plate, and a thin uneven mortar line.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** lead-glaze · tin-glaze · coloured-gloss
- **Decorations:** tile-mosaic · underglaze-polychrome · transfer-print
- **Pattern:** Free fields of broken colour: blue, white, green, yellow and orange, with small parts of plate patterns, and some bright whole discs.
- **Gloss:** Gloss, about 70 to 95 GU; each shard gives its own sheen, and the whole surface glitters in the sun.
- **Wear:** The glaze keeps its colour in the sun and rain; a mortar joint may crack, and a loose shard falls off the edge.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Many saturated colours in small pieces; each field has a leading colour, for example blue and white on one bench and orange and green on a roof.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** The shards come from many kilns: tile factories fire earthenware tile, and potters fire plate and cup shards at higher heat.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** firing of the tile at the factory · breaking the tile into shards · setting in mortar · wiping and curing of the mortar
- **Scale:** The benches and walls of Park Güell run for hundreds of metres; the work used teams of masons and many tonnes of waste tile.
- **Signs:** Uneven shard edges, a patch of a plate motif, a thin mortar line and a bright, glittering face.

### Broken tile mosaic (process)

A mosaic of shards of glazed tile and china set in mortar. A mason can cover a curved surface with it, and the pieces give free, bright colour. Origin: ceramics, Barcelona, Catalonia, c. 1900.

- Break glazed tile and china by hand into pieces of 2 to 10 cm.
- Spread a bed of wet mortar, 2 to 3 cm thick, on the curved surface.
- Press the shards into the bed with the glaze outward and keep a joint of 3 to 5 mm.
- Wipe the face clean before the mortar sets.
