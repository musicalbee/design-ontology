# Organic modern tableware

> A table set is easy to live with when its forms flow like pebbles and its colours are the quiet colours of stone, leaf and sand.

Soft, curved dinnerware for the postwar home. The designers shape plates, shakers and pitchers like pebbles and leaves, and glaze them in muted, matt or satin colours of the earth.

**Period:** 1939–1960 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Soft modern dinnerware, American Modern, Biomorphic tableware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Organic modern tableware, you MUST:

1. Shape each piece in a soft curve, like a pebble, a leaf or a bird, with no sharp corner.
2. Make the rim a free curve, and let the foot rise from the same line.
3. Glaze in a muted, matt or satin colour: grey, sand, sea green, chartreuse or cinnamon.
4. Let the glaze pool a little in the curves, so that the tone changes.
5. Design the service as a family: each piece shares one curve in its handle and its lip.
6. Pair the shakers as a mated couple, with one curve that fits the other.
7. Test each piece in the hand; the grip and the balance decide the shape.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Plates with a soft oval rim, free-form platters, curved pitchers, sauceboats, and mated salt and pepper shakers.
- **Profile:** A soft, flowing line from foot to rim; the plate has no break between the well and the rim.
- **Foot and rim:** A low foot, with a thin rim that curves with the shape; the lip of a pitcher is a soft spout.
- **Proportion:** Plate diameter 20 to 26 cm and wall 4 to 6 mm thick; a platter may be 40 cm long.
- **Rules:**
  - Use one soft curve for the whole line, and repeat it in each handle and lip.
  - Keep the wall even and the edge round.
  - Pair the shakers so that one curve fits the other.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware · vitreous-china
- **Avoid:** bone-china · european-hard-paste · terracotta
- **Rule:** Use a smooth, light earthenware for the cheap line and a vitreous china for the hard-wearing line. Do not use a decorated porcelain: the style relies on form and tone.

## Forming

- **Methods:** slip casting of curved hollow ware · jiggering of oval plates · press moulding of platters · glaze spraying · sponging of the foot
- **Joins:** Handles and spouts are cast with the body in one piece, so that the line does not break.
- **Details:** A fine mould seam that is fettled soft, a coloured glaze that thins at the edge, and a maker mark in the base.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** satin-glaze · matte-glaze · coloured-gloss
- **Decorations:** undecorated · relief-moulding
- **Pattern:** No pattern, or a faint relief of ribs or leaves; the interest is the shape and the tone of the glaze.
- **Gloss:** Matt to satin, about 20 to 55 GU.
- **Wear:** The matt glaze shows knife marks, and the colour stays even; the thin glaze at the rim shows the body.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Muted earth colours in a matt or satin finish: grey, sand, green and a warm brown, with one lively accent.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A tunnel kiln with saggars; a biscuit firing, then a glost firing for the matt or satin glaze.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** slip casting and jiggering · biscuit firing · spraying or dipping in glaze · glost firing in oxidation · sorting by tone
- **Scale:** A big factory that made the line in large numbers for department stores in the United States.
- **Signs:** A mould seam, a soft glaze edge, and a maker mark with the name of the designer in the base.

### Matt glazing (material)

A thick, dry-looking glaze with a velvet surface. The potter adds alumina or a silica surplus, so that fine crystals make the surface dull. Origin: ceramics, Boston, United States, c. 1898.

- Add alumina or a surplus of silica to the glaze, so that it does not melt to glass.
- Apply the glaze thick, 1 to 2 mm, by dipping or by brush.
- Fire it to the full heat and cool it slowly.
- Wipe the shoulder clean of drips to show the form.
