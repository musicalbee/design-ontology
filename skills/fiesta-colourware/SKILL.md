# Fiesta colourware

> One simple, ringed shape in a set of strong glaze colours lets every buyer build a table of their own, one piece at a time.

Solid-colour dinnerware for the American home. A round Art Deco shape with concentric rings is glazed in one strong colour, and the buyer mixes the colours of the open stock. The first red glaze held uranium.

**Period:** 1936–1973 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** American solid-colour dinnerware, Mix-and-match colour tableware, Fiesta

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Fiesta colourware, you MUST:

1. Use one shape family with concentric rings in relief on the rim and the foot.
2. Glaze each piece in one solid colour, and offer 5 or more colours in the first year.
3. Sell the line as open stock: each piece is sold by itself, and the buyer picks the colour.
4. Make the glaze thick and semi-opaque so that the colour covers the body.
5. Fire a red glaze with uranium oxide to get an orange-red.
6. Leave the base unglazed in a ring where the piece stood in the kiln.
7. Add a new colour from time to time to keep the line new.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Round plates, bowls, covered casseroles, pitchers, tumblers and shakers, all with the same concentric rings.
- **Profile:** A round, simple form; the plate has a flat well and a low wall, and a bowl has a rounded body.
- **Foot and rim:** A narrow foot ring, unglazed at the base; the rim is round, with 2 to 3 raised rings.
- **Proportion:** Plate diameter 15 to 28 cm and wall 5 to 7 mm thick; the rings are about 1 mm high and 6 mm apart.
- **Rules:**
  - Put the rings on the rim of every plate and bowl in the line.
  - Keep the form round and simple, with no pattern on the face.
  - Glaze the whole visible surface in one colour.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · stoneware
- **Rule:** Use a semi-vitreous white earthenware that takes a thick, bright glaze. Do not use porcelain: the line has to be cheap and strong.

## Forming

- **Methods:** jiggering of plates and bowls · press moulding of dishes · slip casting of pitchers and shakers · glaze dipping and spraying · stilt-free stacking in saggars
- **Joins:** Handles are cast or pressed and luted with slip; a lid sits on a plain rebated seat.
- **Details:** Three small marks of the kiln stilts on the base, a ring of bare body at the foot, and a mould seam on cast parts.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** uranium-glaze · coloured-gloss
- **Decorations:** undecorated · relief-moulding
- **Pattern:** No pattern: solid glaze colour on a shape with concentric rings in relief.
- **Gloss:** Bright gloss, about 85 to 95 GU, with a semi-opaque colour.
- **Wear:** The glaze crazes in a fine net, and the cutlery marks the well in grey; the colour stays strong.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A set of strong solid colours, each in its own tone: red, cobalt blue, green, yellow and ivory, then turquoise.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A tunnel kiln with saggars; a biscuit firing at high heat and a glost firing at a lower heat.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 1150 °C · dipping or spraying in coloured glaze · glost firing at about 1050 °C · sorting by colour and grade
- **Scale:** A large factory that made tens of thousands of pieces a day, in a single open-stock line.
- **Signs:** Stilt marks on the base, a ring of bare body at the foot, and an even colour from piece to piece.

### Uranium oxide glazing (material)

A lead glaze that holds uranium oxide fires to a bright orange-red. The red cannot be made with the common metal oxides at that heat. Origin: ceramics, Newell, West Virginia, USA, c. 1936.

- Mix uranium oxide into a lead glaze.
- Dip the biscuit piece in the glaze.
- Fire in clean air at the glost heat.
- Check each piece for an even red.
