# Joseon moon jar

> A form that is round and nearly empty, white and warm, can hold more than a painted jar because it shows the breath of its making.

A large round jar of white porcelain from Joseon Korea. Two bowls are thrown, joined at the waist and glazed milk-white, so the form is full, a little off-round and quite plain.

**Period:** 1650–1800 · **Era:** The porcelain race · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Dalhangari, Korean white porcelain moon jar, Baekja daeho

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Joseon moon jar, you MUST:

1. Throw the upper and the lower half as two large bowls and join them at the waist.
2. Leave the waist join and the slight flat or lean in the shape; it is the mark of the hand.
3. Cover the whole jar with a clear, soft white glaze, with a faint warm grey-blue cast.
4. Fire at 1250 to 1300 °C with an even, moderate reduction.
5. Keep the surface bare of ornament; the colour is a milk-white, not a snow-white.
6. Give the jar a tall, round form of 40 to 50 cm and a narrow foot ring about half of the belly.
7. Keep the mouth a plain, short, straight neck without a lip.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** A tall, full, round jar (daeho) with a short neck, a plain mouth and a narrow foot ring.
- **Profile:** A full sphere that is slightly flat or leaning at the join; the upper half is a little smaller than the lower.
- **Foot and rim:** A low, plain foot ring that is unglazed or lightly glazed, and a short straight mouth rim.
- **Proportion:** Height 40 to 50 cm, belly diameter 40 to 45 cm, mouth diameter 16 to 20 cm and a wall 6 to 10 mm thick.
- **Rules:** Join two thrown halves at the waist and leave the join soft. · Allow the shape to lean a little off-round. · Keep the neck short and the mouth plain. · Leave the surface free of any pattern.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** porcelain
- **Avoid:** earthenware · bone-china · creamware
- **Rule:** Use a white, hard kaolin porcelain with a slight warm cast. It carries the large, thick form without slump.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing two halves on the wheel · joining at the waist · turning the foot · dipping in glaze
- **Joins:** The two bowls are joined with slip at the leather-hard stage and the join is smoothed from the inside by hand.
- **Details:** A faint line at the waist, a slight lean in the shape, sand or spur marks on the foot ring, and a warm milk-white tint.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** clear-feldspathic
- **Decorations:** undecorated
- **Pattern:** None; the white surface is the pattern. Rare pieces carry a faint underglaze iron or cobalt painting.
- **Gloss:** Soft gloss, about 55 to 75 GU, with a milk-white, slightly warm tone.
- **Wear:** Fine crackle may show in the glaze; the glaze may show a small cream or grey-blue tint at the thick base.

## Palette

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A wood-fired climbing kiln at the royal workshop of Bunwon; the large jars were fired in the middle of the chamber.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:** throw two halves · join and smooth · biscuit firing · dip in glaze · single glaze firing in moderate reduction · slow cooling
- **Scale:** The royal kilns at Gwangju made a small number of large jars, and a failed firing lost all of the piece.
- **Signs:** A faint waist join, a warm white colour, a slight lean, and spur marks at the foot.

### Joined-hemisphere throwing (process)

Two large bowls are thrown on the wheel and joined at the rim when leather-hard. The seam is smoothed, and the sphere closes at the top with a collar. Origin: ceramics, Gwangju, Korea, c. 1650.

- Throw two bowls of the same width on the wheel.
- Dry both until they are leather-hard.
- Join the rims with slip and smooth the seam inside and outside.
- Add a neck and a foot ring, then glaze and fire.
