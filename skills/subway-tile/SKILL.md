# Subway tile

> A white, shiny, small tile makes a wall that is clean, bright and easy to wash.

A small white rectangular wall tile with a glossy glaze and a bevelled edge, laid in a brick bond. It began as a clean, washable lining for tunnels and stations. It is now the default tile of the kitchen and the bathroom.

**Period:** 1904–present · **Era:** Art pottery and reform · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Metro tile, White brick tile, Bevelled white tile

Good for: a kitchen or bathroom wall, a station or tunnel lining, a plain white wall with a strong grid.

Do not use it when:

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Subway tile, you MUST:

1. Make the tile a 2:1 rectangle, 3 by 6 inches, or about 7.5 by 15 cm.
2. Use a white glaze with a high gloss, 85 to 100 GU, so that the wall reflects light.
3. Bevel the edge by 1 to 2 mm, so that each tile has a soft line and the grout is not flat.
4. Lay the tiles in a running bond with a half-tile offset, and keep the joints to 2 to 3 mm.
5. Fill the joint with a light or a dark grout in one colour to set the strength of the grid.
6. Add a trim set: a bullnose, a cap, a base and a corner piece in the same glaze.
7. Keep the wall plain; use one band of colour or one border at most.
8. Fire the tile hard enough that the glaze does not craze and stains do not enter.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Small rectangular wall tiles in white, with trim pieces: bullnose, cap, base and corner.
- **Profile:** A flat face with a slight bevel on all four edges; a bullnose has a rounded edge.
- **Foot and rim:** The back has a grid of small ribs for the mortar; the edge has a small lug on early tiles to hold the joint.
- **Proportion:** A standard tile is 3 by 6 inches and 6 to 8 mm thick; a wall uses 140 tiles in each square metre.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the 2:1 ratio so that the running bond lines up.
  - Make every tile the same size to 0.5 mm, so that the joints stay straight.
  - Include a trim piece for each edge and corner.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Secondary:** vitreous-china
- **Avoid:** porcelain · jasperware · creamware
- **Rule:** Use a white or buff pressed earthenware for the wall. Use vitreous china for the tile that stays wet, as in a bath or a pool. Keep fine porcelain out.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pressing the tile from dust in a steel die · drying in a tunnel dryer · dipping or spraying in a white glaze · setting the tile in a glaze kiln on a roller · bevelling the edge in the die
- **Joins:** The tile is set in a cement bed or in adhesive, and the joints are filled with grout.
- **Details:** A slight bevel, small ribs on the back, a clean edge and a mark of the maker.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** coloured-gloss · sanitary-glaze · lead-glaze
- **Decorations:** undecorated · relief-moulding
- **Pattern:** No pattern. A plain white gloss, or one colour in the same gloss; a moulded bullnose or cap in the same glaze.
- **Gloss:** High gloss, 85 to 100 GU.
- **Wear:** The glaze wipes clean for decades; fine cracks in the glaze let in dirt, and the edge chips in a wet room.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** White first; one or two soft colours from the same glaze family; the grout sets the line.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A tunnel kiln or a roller kiln.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** pressing in a steel die · drying · biscuit firing · glazing · glost firing at about 1050 °C
- **Scale:** A line makes tens of thousands of tiles in a day and covers the interior of whole stations.
- **Signs:** A uniform gloss, small bevels, a very slight wave in the glaze and ribs on the back.

### Dust pressing (process)

Nearly dry clay powder is pressed in a steel die under high pressure to form a tile in one step. The tile needs little drying and keeps its size. Origin: ceramics, Staffordshire, England, 1840.

- Grind the clay and sieve it to a fine powder with about 6 per cent water.
- Fill the steel die and press the powder under a screw or hydraulic press.
- Remove the tile and fire it with no long drying.
- Keep the size of each tile within 0.5 mm.
