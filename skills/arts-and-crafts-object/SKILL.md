# Arts and Crafts object

> A good object shows the hand and the material that made it: the joint, the hammer mark and the grain are the ornament, and the maker is a free craftsman.

The handmade household goods of the reform movement: oak furniture with exposed joints, hammered copper and iron, stoneware and wool, made in small workshops against the machine ornament of the age.

**Period:** 1880–1918 · **Era:** Reform and craft · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Arts and Crafts household goods, Craftsman object, Cotswold craft

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Arts and Crafts object, you MUST:

1. Leave each joint in view: a through tenon, a peg, a dovetail or a wedge.
2. Use oak, ash and elm in the solid, with a straight grain and a wax or oil finish.
3. Hammer metal by hand, and leave the hammer marks; copper and iron are the main metals.
4. Use hand-forged hinges and straps of 3 to 6 mm iron on doors and chests.
5. Put 1 stylised plant motif on a surface, such as a leaf, a tulip or a pomegranate, in flat colour.
6. Use earth colours: moss green, madder red, indigo blue and the brown of oak.
7. Make each piece in a small shop, and let a part differ a little from the next.

## Form

- **Geometry:** Straight, square sections with a gentle taper; a slat, a post and a rail, with a round copper or iron part for contrast.
- **Silhouette:** A sturdy, upright outline with a broad top rail and a visible frame; a rectangular case on square legs, with a flat lid.
- **Edges:** A plain chamfer or a soft hand-planed edge of 2 to 5 mm; the hammered metal has a rolled rim.
- **Proportion:** A settle is about 110 cm high; a slat is about 8 cm wide and 2 cm thick; a post is a square of 5 to 7 cm.
- **Ornament:** A flat, stylised plant motif in inlay, stencil, enamel or print, and the rhythm of the joint and the hammer mark.
- **Rules:** Each frame shows at least 1 through tenon, peg or dovetail. · Each metal part has a hand-worked surface. · A motif is a stylised plant in 1 to 3 flat colours. · Oak and copper cover more than 50% of the visible surface. · A part of one object does not match another part exactly.

## Materials

- **Secondary:** ash · walnut · pine · beech · leather · cotton · solid-brass · bronze · ceramic-tile · clear-glass · coloured-glass · fireclay · plaster · cane · mild-steel · jute
- **Avoid:** abs-plastic · chrome-plated-steel · chrome-plated-brass · plastic-laminate · acrylic · pvc · mirror-glass · fibreglass

## Finish & texture

- **Finishes:** waxed · oiled · unlacquered · patinated · matte-glaze · piece-dyed · printed · natural-fibre
- **Textures:** open-grain · fine-grain · hammered · tool-marked · satin-smooth · speckled-clay · twill · knotted-pile
- **Gloss:** 5–25 GU on wax and oil; 5–40 GU on hammered metal; 3–20 GU on glaze
- **Patina:** The style wants a living surface: copper and brass darken in use, wax builds up and a hand mark stays in view.

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** copper
- **Section:** Straps 20–40 mm wide and 3–6 mm thick; rods 8–16 mm
- **Joints:** Through tenons, pegs and wedges in view; hand-forged strap hinges with round-headed rivets.
- **Handles:** A hand-hammered copper or iron ring, a plain wooden pull or a leather loop, fixed with a visible rivet.
- **Switches and taps:** A small switch of brass or copper with a hammered plate and a plain turn knob. A tap is a plain brass cock with a hammered copper basin.

## Techniques

### Exposed joinery (process)

A joint that stays in view, so that the way of making is part of the look. Origin: interior, Giza, c. 2500 BCE.

- Cut the joint in the main wood: a through-tenon of 6 to 12mm shows beyond the face.
- Peg the joint with a wooden pin of 8 to 12mm; do not hide it with filler or paint.
- Use one joint type in a room or a building; repeat it at each corner.
