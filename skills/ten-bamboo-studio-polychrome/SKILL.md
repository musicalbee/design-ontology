# Ten Bamboo Studio polychrome block print

> A page of a painting manual can carry the soft colour of a scroll when the printer splits the picture into small blocks that fit like a jigsaw.

Hu Zhengyan's workshop cut many small blocks for one picture (douban) and printed them in turn on one sheet. Dry blocks pressed the paper (gonghua) and raised the outline of a petal or a cloud.

**Period:** c.1619–1644 · **Era:** Hand press · **Domain:** Print · **Also called:** Shizhuzhai, 十竹斋, Doubanh jigsaw printing, 饾版, Gonghua embossing, 拱花

## System directives

When specifying, prompting or art-directing a printed piece or its look on screen: a poster, a plate, a book page, a zine, a label or a print-look illustration in Ten Bamboo Studio polychrome block print, you MUST:

1. Split each picture by colour into small blocks, up to dozens for one page, and print them one by one on the same sheet.
2. Grade the ink on one block (a wet-brush effect) so one petal or leaf shades from pale to deep.
3. Print the key outline in light ink, with a thin line, and let the colour carry the form.
4. Press a dry block into the sheet (gonghua) to raise the outline of a petal, a cloud or a wave, with no ink on it.
5. Choose a quiet subject of the literati: a plum, bamboo, a rock, a bird or an orchid.
6. Use pale, clear colours: rose, moss green, water blue and ink grey.
7. Add a short poem in calligraphy and a red seal.

## Process

- **Family:** relief
- **Matrix:** Pearwood blocks, cut small, up to dozens for one image, and dry blocks for the embossed lines.
- **Press:** No press. A printer rubs the back of the sheet with a pad by hand.
- **Image making:** A painter makes the design. A carver splits it by colour into jigsaw blocks, and a printer sets them in order.
- **Edition:** Book editions, in sets of albums.

## Inks

- **Strategy:** Water-based colour and ink on small blocks, with graded ink on a single block. The outline prints in pale ink, then the colour blocks, then the dry emboss.
- **Ink count min:** 6
- **Ink count max:** 12
- **Overprint:** transparent

## Tone and screen

- **Method:** solid
- **Ruling lpi:** 0
- **Rule:** Make a tone with a graded wash of ink on one block, with a soft edge of about 5–20 mm. The style has no screen.

## Registration

- **Tolerance mm:** 0.4
- **Rule:** The small blocks must fit within 0.4 mm. The jigsaw join shows only as a hair line of white.

## Stock

- **Stock:** Thin xuan-type paper of bark and straw fibre.
- **Weight gsm min:** 30
- **Weight gsm max:** 60
- **Ground:** #EFE4C9
- **Finish:** Soft and absorbent, so the ink sinks in and the paper takes the emboss.

## Surface

- **Impression:** Medium. The dry blocks raise the outline by 0.1–0.3 mm.
- **Sheen:** Matt. The raised lines show only in raking light.
- **Texture:** Soft colour with a feathered edge, a raised line without ink, and a fine paper fibre.

### Woodcut (process)

A print from a block of wood with the white parts cut away; the result is flat colour, hard edges and visible grain. Origin: print, Dunhuang, 868.

- Cut the block along the grain with a knife and a gouge; leave a line of 1mm to 3mm for the black.
- Use one block for each colour and register them within 1 mm.
- Print with a flat, opaque ink and leave the grain and the knife marks visible.
- Keep the shapes flat, with no more than 3 to 6 tones in a print.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Ten Bamboo Studio** (publisher, Nanjing, China, 1619–now), made the style. The workshop of Hu Zhengyan in Nanjing. It made colour albums and embossed letter paper from small jigsaw blocks. Hu ran it from about 1619, and it went on into the Qing period.
