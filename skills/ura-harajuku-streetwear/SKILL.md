# Ura-Harajuku streetwear

> A small shop on a backstreet is the centre of the style: a few friends make graphic clothes for their own scene, and the mark on the chest says who belongs.

Streetwear from the small shops on the backstreets of Harajuku in Tokyo, from 1993. It has the camouflage-print hoodie, the printed T-shirt and the branded sneaker, sold in small stores and tied to friends in music and design.

**Period:** 1993–present · **Era:** Power and subculture · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Ura-Hara, Tokyo backstreet streetwear

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Ura-Harajuku streetwear, you MUST:

1. Print a mascot, a pattern or a name on the T-shirt and the hoodie in flat colours.
2. Cover a hoodie or a jacket in a camouflage print in two to four colours.
3. Cut the T-shirt and the hoodie slightly boxy, 4 to 8 cm wider than the body.
4. Choose a sneaker with a clean low court shape and a small logo on the side.
5. Add a small trim detail: a woven label, a snap or a full zip.
6. Sell the piece in a small shop and let friends and artists add their own marks to it.
7. Pair the garment with a cap and a plain, washed jean.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A slightly boxy top over a straight or slim jean, finished by a cap and a clean low sneaker.
- **Line:** A soft shoulder, a hoodie hem at the hip, and a jean hem that rests on the sneaker.
- **Fit:** Relaxed on top and close on the leg; the garment is easy to move in but not as wide as the parent style.
- **Proportion:** T-shirt 4 to 8 cm wider than the chest. Hoodie hem at the hip. Jean leg 18 to 22 cm at the hem. Cap crown 9 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Drop the shoulder seam 2 to 5 cm below the natural line.
  - Keep the hoodie hem and cuff in a rib knit.
  - Cut the jean straight or slim, and let the hem rest on the shoe.
  - Place the main print on the chest or the back, at 15 to 30 cm in width.
  - Add a cap and a low sneaker.

### Camouflage print (motif)

A pattern of irregular patches in a few related colours, taken from military camouflage and printed on cloth for its strong design and its symbolism. Origin: fashion, London, United Kingdom, 1919.

- Take a pattern of irregular patches from military camouflage, or draw a new one.
- Give the patches a small set of colours, often olive, tan, brown and black.
- Print the pattern over the full cloth, and cut the garment from it.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **A Bathing Ape** (company, Ura-Harajuku, Tokyo, 1993–now), made the style. A Japanese fashion brand that Nigo founded in Ura-Harajuku in 1993. It first sold hoodies, T-shirts and camouflage-print hoodies in a small store. Nigo left the brand in 2013.
- **Nowhere** (company, Ura-Harajuku, Tokyo, 1993–now), sold the style. A store and retail venture in Ura-Harajuku that Nigo and Jun Takahashi opened on 1 April 1993. It was the first store of Nigo.
