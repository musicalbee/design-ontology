# Mannerism

> A picture is most beautiful when it shows skill and invention, so the painter stretches, twists and crowds the ideal body past nature.

Elongated, twisting figures in crowded, unstable space, in acid colour. The painters took the Renaissance rules and bent them on purpose: grace and artifice replace calm balance.

**Period:** 1520–1600 · **Era:** Early modern · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Late Renaissance, Maniera

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "mannerism", "mannerist", "el greco", "parmigianino", "pontormo", "bronzino", "elongated figures", "serpentine figure", "late renaissance".

Good for: fashion editorial and costume art, book and album covers with a strange, cold grace, fantasy and myth illustration, portrait with a stylised, courtly look.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs calm, plain clarity or a friendly mood.
- The picture needs natural body proportion or a clear, open space.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Mannerism, you MUST:

1. Draw the figure 9 to 10 heads tall, with a small head and long limbs and neck.
2. Twist each figure in a serpentine line (figura serpentinata) that spirals up through 180° or more.
3. Fill the picture to the edges with 5–15 figures, and leave no calm floor to stand on.
4. Tilt the space: raise the horizon and push the ground up the picture plane so that figures climb and stack.
5. Use acid, shifting colour: a cold pink, lemon, green and blue that change hue inside one drape (cangiante).
6. Light the figure from a cold, unclear source, and keep the faces smooth and enamel-like.
7. Put the main action off to one side or far back, and place a large decorative figure in front.

## Composition

- **Layout:** A crowded, vertical stack of twisting figures that climb the picture plane, with a raised horizon and a small, tilted window of deep space far behind.
- **Focal point:** A group of figures off-centre, at about one third from an edge; the eye is led there by a spiral of limbs and drapery.
- **Format:** A tall panel or canvas at ratios of 2:3 to 1:2; the figures reach and are cut by the top and side edges.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| proportion | Draw figures 9 to 10 heads tall, with the head at one tenth of the height, against 7 to 8 heads in the earlier ideal. |
| rhythm | Repeat a spiral of limb and drapery 3–5 times from the base to the top, each turn 90° to 180° from the last. |
| layering-depth | Compress the space to 3 tight layers with a high horizon at 20–30% from the top, and a small deep gap behind. |
| balance | Hold an unstable balance: put 60–70% of the figure mass on one side, and counter it with a small, far, bright figure. |
| colour-distribution | Spread 4–5 acid hues across the figures in patches of 5–15% each, and keep no hue as a dominant field. |
| focal-point | Place the main group at one third from the edge, and make it the only area where three or more diagonals meet. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Cold, acid and shifting colour: a pink, lemon, sea-green and lapis blue, with a changing hue inside one fold of cloth, on a dark, cool ground.
- **Distribution:** Flesh and pale drapery 35–45%, acid colour patches 30–40%, dark ground and shadow 20–30%.
- **Ground:** A dark grey-green or brown ground, glazed with thin colour over a light underpainting.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint with glazes over a pale tempera underpainting; in a wall picture, fresco.
- **Support:** Panel or fine-weave canvas with a smooth, pale ground.
- **Brushwork:** Fine strokes of 1–3mm, blended away; the hand does not show.
- **Paint handling:** 4–8 thin layers with smooth glazes; the flesh is polished and the drapery is crisp and sharp.
- **Edges:** Hard, clean contours on the figures, with a soft edge only inside the hair and the far haze.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** A cool, even or unclear light from a high side; it shows no warm sun and no strong beam.
- **Modelling:** Smooth, enamel-like skin with 4–6 steps from light to dark; the limbs read as cool marble.
- **Shadow:** Dark, cool and clean, kept to 15–25% of a figure; it does not blur the contour.
- **Temperature:** A cool key overall, with the warm tones kept to a small flesh or a red accent.

## Subject

- **Typical:** Religious scenes, myth and allegory, and long-necked portraits of court figures in stiff, rich dress.
- **Figure scale:** The figures fill 70–90% of the picture height and press the edges.
- **Treatment:** Elegant, cool and artificial; the sitter or saint is poised, with no plain emotion on the face.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Smooth and glossy, polished like enamel.
- **Texture:** No visible brush texture; a fine, even surface.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Figura serpentinata (motif, invented here)

A figure that twists along its height like a snake, with hips, shoulders and head turned in different directions. Origin: painting, Italy, c. 1520.

- Turn the hips, the shoulders and the head each 30 to 90 degrees from the one below.
- Stretch the body to 9 or 10 heads tall.
- Put the weight on one leg, so that the figure seems to spiral up.

### Trompe-l'oeil (process)

Painted perspective that continues the real room, so that a flat wall or ceiling seems to open into space. Origin: painting, Mantua, Italy, c. 1465.

- Set the vanishing point at the eye of a viewer who stands at one marked spot in the room.
- Continue the real cornice, columns and arches in paint for 1 to 3 storeys of false height.
- Paint the sky or the open part with the lightest value; keep the painted frame at the value of the real stone.
