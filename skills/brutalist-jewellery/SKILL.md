# Brutalist Jewellery

> A jewel is a raw object: a rough surface and an uncut stone show the real material and refuse the polish of the old shop.

Rough, heavy jewels of textured gold and silver that look cast from rock or bark. They are set with uncut crystals and raw stones, and were made in London from the 1960s.

**Period:** 1960–1979 · **Era:** Youthquake · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Textured gold jewellery, 1960s British studio jewellery, Nugget and uncut-stone jewels

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Brutalist Jewellery, you MUST:

1. Give the metal a rough, deep texture, as if it came from rock, bark or coral. Make it with a torch, a hammer or a cast.
2. Use a thick piece of metal, at least 1.5 mm thick, and keep the mass heavy and clear.
3. Set uncut or rough stones: tourmaline, citrine, quartz crystal and baroque pearl, with the natural faces left on.
4. Make the form irregular and asymmetrical, as a natural lump, with no repeat.
5. Use yellow gold with a dull surface in the recesses and a bright surface on the high points.
6. Choose a material for its honesty: let the cast, the weld and the hammer show.
7. Make each piece unique, and sign it with a stamp on the back.
8. Design the piece as a large object on the body: a cuff, a ring or a brooch of 5 to 8 cm.

### Lost-wax casting (process)

A method that melts a wax model out of a mould and fills the empty space with molten metal. Origin: industrial design, Near East and South Asia, c. 4000 BCE.

- Carve or form the model in wax.
- Cover the model with clay and bake it, so that the wax melts out.
- Pour molten metal into the empty mould and break the mould.

### Welding (process)

Two parts of metal or thermoplastic are joined: heat melts the edges, and the melt sets into one piece. The seam can show, or be ground away. Origin: industrial design, Paris, France, 1881.

- Clean the joint to bare metal and fit the parts with a gap of 0 to 3 mm.
- Melt the edges with an electric arc or a gas flame, and add filler rod of the same metal.
- Keep the bead even, with a width of 1 to 2 times the thickness of the sheet.
- Grind the bead flush for a hidden joint, or leave the bead raw for a visible joint.
