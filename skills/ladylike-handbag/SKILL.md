# Ladylike handbag

> A lady's bag completes her outfit, and it is small, firm and kept in order.

The neat, matched handbag of the 1950s and early 1960s. It is a rigid calfskin box with a top handle and a turn-lock, or a quilted flap bag on a chain. The wearer carries it on the arm with gloves and a small hat.

**Period:** 1955–1969 · **Era:** War and the New Look · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** 1950s structured handbag, Top-handle and chain-strap bag

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Ladylike handbag, you MUST:

1. Build a firm box on a frame, 20 to 30 cm wide, that stands on its own.
2. Hold the bag by a short top handle, 8 to 12 cm tall, or on the forearm.
3. Close the flap with a brass turn-lock or a kiss clasp.
4. Quilt a soft skin in diamond rows of 3 to 4 cm, and hang it on a chain strap.
5. Match the bag to the shoes and the gloves.
6. Line the inside in silk twill, with a pocket for a mirror and a purse.
7. Make the stitches small and even, 8 to 10 to the cm, by hand.
8. Keep the hardware small, gold in tone and plain.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A small, firm box or a flat flap bag with a clean outline and square or softly rounded corners.
- **Line:** A level top edge, a straight flap and a short handle that stands up.
- **Fit:** The bag is carried on the forearm or held in the gloved hand; the chain version hangs from the shoulder at the hip.
- **Proportion:** Box bag 20–30 cm wide, 15–20 cm high and 8–12 cm deep; handle drop 8–12 cm; chain strap 55–60 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the shape rigid with a frame and a stiff lining.
  - Set one lock or one clasp at the centre of the flap.
  - Match the colour of the bag to the shoes, the gloves or the hat.
  - Carry no more on the surface than a quilted pattern or a clasp.

## Construction

- **Methods:** wood and card frame · hand-cut and hand-sewn calfskin panels · diamond quilting on a padded skin · chain strap threaded with leather · silk twill lining · edges painted and burnished
- **Closures:** A brass turn-lock on the flap, a kiss clasp on a frame bag, or a clasp with a metal bar.
- **Details:** A short top handle, an inner pocket for a mirror, a small foot at each corner and a stamp of the house inside the flap.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** burnished · quilted · patent-lacquered · matte · gilded · woven-leather
- **Pattern:** Plain leather in one colour; a diamond quilt on the flap; a thin line of stitching at the edge.
- **Gloss:** Calfskin at 20–35 GU; patent leather at 85–100 GU; gilt hardware at 80–90 GU.
- **Wear:** The bag wants to look new and pressed: scuffs and bent corners spoil the shape, and the maker re-dyes and re-forms it.

### Saddle stitch (skill)

A hand stitch in which one thread with a needle at each end passes twice through each hole; if one stitch breaks, the seam holds. Origin: fashion, Paris, France, c. 1837.

- Mark the holes at 3 to 4 mm spacing with a stitching chisel.
- Pass the two needles through each hole from opposite sides, and pull each stitch tight.
- Finish the seam with a double stitch back, and burnish the edge.
