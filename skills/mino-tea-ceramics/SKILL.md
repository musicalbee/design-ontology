# Mino tea ceramics

> Japanese potters of the Momoyama period make their own tea ware: thick glaze, free brush and a form that is bent on purpose.

Japanese stoneware for the tea room from the kilns of Mino. The first white Shino glaze, the green Oribe glaze and the black Setoguro glaze go on bold forms with bold painted lines.

**Period:** 1580–1650 · **Era:** Blue and white · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Mino ware, Shino and Oribe ware, Momoyama tea ceramics

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Mino tea ceramics, you MUST:

1. Cover the body with a thick feldspar glaze, 1 to 2 mm, so that the glaze shows pinholes and crawling.
2. Paint with iron oxide in a few quick strokes: grass, bridges, fences and plain lines.
3. Bend the form on purpose: press the rim out of round and let one side lean.
4. Divide the surface: one part glazed in green, one part in white or bare.
5. Fire in a climbing kiln in reduction at 1200 to 1250 °C.
6. Leave a fire-orange blush where the white glaze is thin and the clay shows.
7. Choose one colour in the glaze and one tone in the painting for each piece.
8. Match each form to a use in the tea room: a bowl, a water jar or a tea jar.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Tea bowls, water jars and tea jars in thick stoneware, with square, round or bent forms. The potters of Mino also made dishes for the tea meal, but the tea forms lead.
- **Profile:** A bowl has a wall that stands straight or leans, a thick rim that the potter presses out of round, and a heavy base.
- **Foot and rim:** A high, cut foot ring that is left bare and shows the red clay; the rim is thick, rounded and uneven, and a glaze drip may run over it.
- **Proportion:** A bowl is 11 to 13 cm wide and 8 to 10 cm high; the wall is 5 to 9 mm thick and the glaze is up to 2 mm thick.
- **Rules:** Throw a heavy form and bend it when the clay is soft. · Paint one bold theme with a few lines of iron oxide. · Leave the foot bare and cut it by hand. · Make the glaze thick and let it gather in places.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stoneware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · raku-clay
- **Rule:** Use a coarse local stoneware that fires to buff or pink-red where the glaze is thin. Keep a white porcelain body out: the red edge is part of the style.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the wheel · bending and cutting the form when leather-hard · press moulding of squares and slabs for dishes · carving the foot ring by hand · painting with an iron slip under the glaze
- **Joins:** Handles and spouts are rare; where a lug or a lid is needed, the potter lutes it with slip.
- **Details:** Knife marks on the foot, a thumb dent in the rim, and kiln stilt marks on the foot where the dishes stood in the kiln.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** shino-glaze · oribe-glaze · tenmoku · ash-glaze
- **Decorations:** iron-painting · incised · undecorated
- **Pattern:** Iron-brown grass, bridge, fence and line patterns under a thick white Shino glaze or a green Oribe glaze; a plain black bowl for the contrast.
- **Gloss:** Satin to soft gloss, about 25 to 75 GU; the Shino glaze is thick and milky, the Oribe glaze is brighter.
- **Wear:** The glaze crawls and cracks at the rim, shows pinholes and shows the red clay where it is thin. Tea stains the crackle.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Milky white, copper green and iron black on a warm red-buff clay. One piece uses two or three of these tones.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A climbing kiln (noborigama) or a single-chamber kiln (ōgama), fired with pine wood; the pieces stand on stilts.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:** painting the iron slip on the dry body · dipping in glaze · reduction firing for several days · pulling a test piece to see the glaze · slow cooling in the closed kiln
- **Scale:** Many kilns at Mino; the local lords backed them, and tea masters such as Furuta Oribe ordered special forms.
- **Signs:** Stilt marks on the foot, pinholes in the glaze, a pink or orange blush on thin edges and a drip of glaze at the base.

### Deliberate distortion (skill)

The potter bends a thrown form when the clay is soft, so that the piece leans, the rim is oval and the base is not level. Origin: ceramics, Mino province (Gifu), Japan, c. 1600.

- Throw the form with a thick wall.
- Press or cut the wall when the clay is soft.
- Keep one side higher than the other by 5 to 15 mm.
- Add a glaze that gathers in the dents.
