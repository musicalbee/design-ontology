# West German fat lava

> A thick glaze that is allowed to run, bubble and crater gives a mass-made vase the look of cooled volcanic rock.

Vases and bowls from West German factories between 1960 and 1980. A thick, bubbled and cratered glaze runs over a moulded earthenware body, in orange, brown, green or yellow.

**Period:** 1960–1980 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Fat lava ceramics, West German pottery, WGP vases

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in West German fat lava, you MUST:

1. Mould the earthenware body in a bold, round or flared shape with a wide opening.
2. Apply the lava glaze in a layer of 1 to 3 mm, and add a second thinner glaze over it.
3. Let the glaze run and crater, so that no two vases are the same.
4. Choose a bright ground: orange, yellow, green or brown, with a white or black drip.
5. Make large floor vases of 40 to 60 cm and table vases of 15 to 30 cm.
6. Add a relief of rings, bands or bubbles in the mould, to give the glaze a place to pool.
7. Fire at 1000 to 1100 °C in a tunnel kiln.
8. Mark the base with the factory name, a model number and a height in cm.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Floor vases, bottle vases, wide bowls, planters and baluster vases with a bold, round or flared outline.
- **Profile:** A heavy, swelling belly, a wide flared mouth or a bulb on a short neck; relief rings or wave bands often run round the form.
- **Foot and rim:** A wide, flat base with a stamped mark; the rim is thick and rounded and may be wavy.
- **Proportion:** Height 15 to 60 cm; the wall is 6 to 12 mm and the weight is high for the size.
- **Rules:**
  - Mould the form with a thick wall to carry a heavy glaze.
  - Keep the base flat and stamped.
  - Place the lava glaze on the upper two thirds and keep the base plain.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Secondary:** stoneware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · jasperware
- **Rule:** Use a red or white earthenware that is cast or pressed in a mould. A thick body keeps the glaze from warping the piece.

## Forming

- **Methods:** slip casting in plaster moulds · press moulding · jiggering of bowls · spraying and dipping of the glaze · hand application of the second glaze
- **Joins:** A vase is cast in two halves and joined; the seam is fettled and hidden under the glaze.
- **Details:** A stamped factory mark, a model number and a glaze that is thick, crusted and uneven.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** lava-glaze · coloured-gloss · matte-glaze
- **Decorations:** relief-moulding · undecorated · hand-banding · incised
- **Pattern:** A thick, bubbled and cratered lava glaze in orange, brown or green, with a drip of a second colour over the shoulder.
- **Gloss:** Satin to gloss in the thin parts, matt in the crater; about 10 to 60 GU.
- **Wear:** The crust of the lava glaze is hard but can chip at the rim, and the body may show through at the edge.

### Lava glazing (material)

A thick glaze that blisters and runs in the kiln. It leaves a rough, cratered surface like cooled lava, often in two or more colours. Origin: ceramics, West Germany, c. 1960.

- Mix a glaze with a high content of frit and a gas-forming additive.
- Apply it in a layer 2 to 4 mm thick over a base glaze.
- Fire it so that the layer blisters and runs.
- Cool the kiln slowly so that the crater surface sets.
