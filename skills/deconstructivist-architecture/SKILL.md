# Deconstructivism

> The pure form is already flawed: distort the grid, collide the volumes and let the building show that order is unstable.

Skewed, fractured volumes that collide at sharp angles, with a facade that seems to tear itself apart. Gehry, Hadid, Libeskind and Coop Himmelb(l)au used distortion to disturb the pure box of the Modern Movement from inside.

**Period:** 1980s–2000s · **Era:** Postmodern and After · **Domain:** Architecture · **Also called:** Deconstruction, Deconstructivist architecture

Good for: museum and gallery renders, landmark concept art, film and game environments, exhibition pavilions.

Do not use it when:
- The brief asks for a low-cost, repeatable or fast-to-build building.
- The programme needs clear, regular rooms, as in housing or a hospital.

## System directives

When designing, describing or rendering buildings and interiors in Deconstructivism, you MUST:

1. Collide two or more orthogonal volumes at 10 to 40° skews, so that no wall is parallel to its neighbour.
2. Fragment the form into 5 to 15 shards or bars that overlap, tilt and cantilever.
3. Tilt walls and floors off the vertical by 3 to 15°, and let beams and columns slope.
4. Cut slot windows and sharp gashes at angles that do not follow the floor levels.
5. Show the stress: bracing, folded planes and structure that seems to carry the load without a clear path.
6. Wrap the shards in one material, usually metal sheet, zinc or titanium, and let the joints draw the geometry.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Regular orthogonal grids and repeated bays.
- **NEVER:** Symmetric compositions around an axis.
- **NEVER:** Soft rounded blob forms with no hard edges.
- **NEVER:** Historical quotation or applied ornament.
- **NEVER:** A plain box with one decorative sloped wall.
- **NEVER:** Windows aligned with floor levels and a clear horizontal order.

## Massing & form

- **Form:** Shards, bars and planes that cross and cantilever 3 to 12m; two or more volumes at skewed angles in one composition.
- **Proportion:** Long thin elements, with a ratio of 1:6 or more, crossing short stout ones; the silhouette has no clear centre.
- **Roof:** A tilted, folded or broken roof that continues the wall; no flat roof with a clear edge line.
- **Relation to ground:** The mass crashes into or lifts off the ground: a splayed base, a cut-in plaza or a slab that is propped on one point.

## Structure

- **System:** A steel frame or space-frame, often designed with 3D software, with a concrete core and a sheet-metal skin.
- **Span and module:** No regular module: spans vary from 4 to 30m and members are cut to a unique length and angle.
- **Expression:** The structure is shown as a tangle: raking columns, trusses and braces cross the interior and the skin.

## Materials

- **Primary:** zinc and titanium sheet · stainless steel · glass · exposed steel
- **Secondary:** raw concrete · plywood · perforated metal · plaster
- **Finish:** Mixed: bright metal on the shards, raw or plain finish on the inside, and joints that are visible and unaligned.

## Facade

- **Composition:** Overlapping planes at different angles; the window pattern ignores floor levels and the front has no clear base, middle and top.
- **Openings:** Slots, triangles and wedges cut at skewed angles, 3 to 20% of a plane at a time; the window-to-wall ratio is 20 to 50%.
- **Solid to void:** Solid leads at 50–80%, cut by sharp voids.

## Light & space

- **Daylight:** Light enters through slashes, skylights and tilted glass; it makes sharp, moving bars and wedges across the floor.
- **Spatial sequence:** A route with no straight line: slanted halls, split stairs and bridges lead through the shards and show a new view at each turn.
- **Interior character:** Tilted walls, sloped floors, exposed bracing and few right angles; the room has no clear centre.

## Ornament

- **Attitude:** No ornament in the classical sense; the distortion and the fracture are the only expression.
- **Details:** skewed slot windows · folded metal panels · cantilevered shards · exposed raking columns · sloped walls
