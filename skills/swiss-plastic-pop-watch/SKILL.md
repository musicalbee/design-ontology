# Swiss plastic pop watch

> A watch can be a fashion accessory that one wears for a season. The maker sells colour and a pattern at a price that allows many watches.

A cheap Swiss quartz watch in a plastic case with a bright pattern. The maker sells it as an accessory and changes it each season. The Swatch line of 1983 set it.

**Period:** 1983–1999 · **Era:** Power and subculture · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Plastic fashion watch, Swatch style, Fashion watch of the 1980s

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Swiss plastic pop watch, you MUST:

1. Mould the case from plastic in one piece with the strap lugs, so that it needs few parts.
2. Limit the number of parts to about 51, with a quartz module that the maker welds into the case.
3. Print a bold pattern in two to four colours on the dial, the strap or the case.
4. Release two collections each year, so that the buyer returns each season.
5. Make the dial large and flat with simple hands, so that the time is clear.
6. Make the strap from the same plastic as the case, in a vivid colour.
7. Price each watch at the level of a pair of shoes, so that one can own many.
8. Invite artists and designers to draw the face for limited editions.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A thin round plastic case with a flat glass, a one-piece strap and a bold printed dial.
- **Line:** A flat disc with a smooth side, a plain bezel and a strap that clips straight into the case.
- **Fit:** The strap is soft and light, and the case lies flat on the wrist.
- **Proportion:** Case thickness to width about 1 to 4; the dial fills about 80 per cent of the face; the strap is 17 mm wide.
- **Rules:**
  - Make the dial a flat field for graphics, with hands that stay clear of the print.
  - Use bright primary and neon colours on the case, the strap and the dial together.
  - Keep the case a single colour and print the pattern on the dial only.
  - Use bold simple numerals or no numerals at all.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Injection moulding (process)

Hot plastic is pushed into a steel mould under high pressure. The part cools, and the mould opens to release it. Origin: fashion, Newark, United States, 1872.

- Melt the plastic pellets in a heated barrel.
- Press the melt into the closed mould.
- Cool the part, open the mould and push the part out.

### Quartz movement (technology)

A timekeeper in which a quartz crystal vibrates at a fixed rate, and a circuit counts the vibrations to move the hands or to show digits. Origin: industrial design, New York, United States, 1927.

- Cut a quartz crystal to vibrate at 32,768 Hz.
- Divide that rate in a circuit down to one pulse each second.
- Drive a stepping motor or a display with each pulse.

### Welding (process)

Two parts of metal or thermoplastic are joined: heat melts the edges, and the melt sets into one piece. The seam can show, or be ground away. Origin: industrial design, Paris, France, 1881.

- Clean the joint to bare metal and fit the parts with a gap of 0 to 3 mm.
- Melt the edges with an electric arc or a gas flame, and add filler rod of the same metal.
- Keep the bead even, with a width of 1 to 2 times the thickness of the sheet.
- Grind the bead flush for a hidden joint, or leave the bead raw for a visible joint.
