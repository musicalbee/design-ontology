# Fauvism

> Colour is free from the object: the painter chooses a hue for its force and its relations, and so builds the picture and the feeling by colour alone.

Colour at full strength and not true to nature: a green face, an orange sky. Flat patches, bold contours and quick strokes make the picture an arrangement of colour first.

**Period:** 1905–1908 · **Era:** Avant-garde · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Les Fauves, The wild beasts

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "fauvism", "fauve", "matisse", "derain", "vlaminck", "wild colour", "expressive colour", "pure colour", "non-naturalistic colour".

Good for: poster, festival and summer campaign art, packaging for drinks, food and sport, children and creative-learning identity, editorial illustration with a bold palette.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a muted, serious or luxury calm.
- The picture must show true product colour.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Fauvism, you MUST:

1. Choose each hue for its force and not for its likeness: a green shadow, a red tree, a blue face.
2. Apply the colour pure, straight from the tube, in large flat patches of 30–200mm.
3. Set strong contrasts of complementary pairs at full saturation: red against green, orange against blue.
4. Outline the forms in a dark or coloured contour of 3–10mm, and leave the area inside flat.
5. Flatten the space: reduce the depth to 1–2 layers and let the colour carry the distance.
6. Paint fast, with a visible brush stroke, and leave bare ground between patches.
7. Take plain subjects, such as a landscape, a port, a portrait and a bather, and show them in bold, simple shapes.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** True-to-life local colour and tone.
- **NEVER:** Blended, graded modelling that makes a round form.
- **NEVER:** Deep perspective and atmospheric haze.
- **NEVER:** Muted, grey and brown palettes.
- **NEVER:** Fine, fussy detail and a polished surface.
- **NEVER:** Dots or a scientific system of colour.

## Composition

- **Layout:** Large flat colour shapes with a bold contour, in a shallow space of 1–2 layers; the shapes divide the picture into 5–12 patches.
- **Focal point:** The strongest colour contrast, a complementary pair, at about one third from an edge, in a patch at 10–20% of the area.
- **Format:** A canvas at 4:5 to 3:2, with the subject cut by the edge; a figure fills 50–80% of the height.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| contrast | Set one complementary pair, such as red against green, at full saturation in the main patch, with a hue shift of 150° or more. |
| colour-distribution | Divide the picture in 4–6 pure hue patches of 10–25% each, with no hue lower than 5% and none above 30%. |
| layering-depth | Reduce the depth to 1–2 layers, and show distance by colour temperature, not by size or haze. |
| rhythm | Repeat a stroke or a curve 8–15 times at 20–60mm, so that the colour patches move across the picture. |
| unity-variety | Hold one contour weight of 3–10mm and one stroke width, and let the hue vary from patch to patch. |
| focal-point | Make the face or the main shape the largest, brightest patch at one third from an edge, and keep the rest 10–15% lower in value. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Pure, saturated hues used for force and not for likeness, in strong complementary pairs, with a dark contour and bare ground.
- **Distribution:** 4–6 hue patches at 10–25% each, bare or white ground 5–15%, dark contour under 10%.
- **Ground:** A white ground that is left bare in places and lifts the colour.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint straight from the tube, thick, with little or no thinner.
- **Support:** Canvas with a white ground, in small and medium sizes.
- **Brushwork:** Broad, quick strokes of 15–40mm, short, straight or curved, with a few thin lines of 3–10mm for contour.
- **Paint handling:** One layer, rarely two; the patches are flat and opaque, with a gap of bare ground between them.
- **Edges:** A hard, dark or coloured contour at 3–10mm on the main forms; the colour patches meet with a ragged edge.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** No single source: the light is in the colour, and a patch of high-key colour is the light.
- **Modelling:** Little or none; a form turns by a change of hue from warm to cool, in 1–2 steps.
- **Shadow:** A colour and not a dark: green, violet or blue patch at the same value as its neighbour.
- **Temperature:** Chosen for feeling and for contrast; warm patches next to cool ones, with a hue shift of 150° or more.

## Subject

- **Typical:** Port and harbour scenes, a sunlit landscape, a portrait, a bather, a still life and an interior with a window.
- **Figure scale:** A figure fills 50–80% of the picture height, or a landscape fills the whole canvas.
- **Treatment:** Direct, joyful and simple; the subject is a reason for colour, and the face is a patch like any other.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Matt to semi-gloss with a bare, raw look.
- **Texture:** Flat patches with a visible brush ridge, and bare canvas that shows between.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Woodcut (process)

A print from a block of wood with the white parts cut away; the result is flat colour, hard edges and visible grain. Origin: internet, Dunhuang, 868.

- Cut the block along the grain with a knife and a gouge; leave a line of 1mm to 3mm for the black.
- Use one block for each colour and register them within 1 mm.
- Print with a flat, opaque ink and leave the grain and the knife marks visible.
- Keep the shapes flat, with no more than 3 to 6 tones in a print.

## Lineage fallbacks

When the user asks for a variation, do not improvise. Blend toward the neighbour named here, and load its skill.

- If the user asks for **more controlled and scientific**: move toward [`pointillism`](../pointillism/SKILL.md) (Pointillism). Keep the pure hue and the complementary pairs; break each patch into dots of 2–5mm, add a still frieze layout and a painted border.
- If the user asks for **more abstract and structural**: move toward [`cubism-painting`](../cubism-painting/SKILL.md) (Cubism). Keep the flat patches; cut the palette to 3–4 earth hues, break the subject into tilted planes, and show more than one viewpoint.
