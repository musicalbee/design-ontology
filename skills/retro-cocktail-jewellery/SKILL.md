# Retro Cocktail Jewellery

> A jewel is a bold, warm sculpture in gold: large, curved forms and bright coloured stones that gain from the dress and the cocktail hour.

Big, curved jewels in warm yellow and rose gold, set with large coloured stones. The forms are bows, ribbons, flowers and bracelets of links. They were made in the years of war and wartime metal rules.

**Period:** 1935–1950 · **Era:** Interwar modern · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Retro Modern jewellery, 1940s gold jewellery, Cocktail jewels

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Retro Cocktail Jewellery, you MUST:

1. Use gold, yellow and rose, as the main metal; platinum is scarce in the war years.
2. Sculpt the metal into bows, ribbons, scrolls, flowers and folds that look like cloth.
3. Set large coloured stones, such as aquamarine, citrine, amethyst and topaz, in a simple frame.
4. Add small rubies, synthetic rubies or sapphires as a point of colour.
5. Make the piece big. A cocktail ring is 2 to 3 cm, a brooch is 6 to 9 cm and a bracelet is 2 to 4 cm wide.
6. Build a bracelet of curved links like a tank track, a gas pipe or a woven strap.
7. Make a clip brooch in pairs that can be worn as one piece on the lapel or the neckline.
8. Polish the gold bright, and set it in contrast to a few matte or woven areas.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A large, sculpted jewel in gold: a ribbon bow, a flower clip, a cocktail ring or a thick bracelet of links.
- **Line:** Curved and rounded, with ribbons, scrolls and folds; the shape is thick and soft.
- **Fit:** A clip sits on the lapel or the shoulder, a bracelet lies loose on the wrist, a ring sits high on the finger.
- **Proportion:** A cocktail ring is 2 to 3 cm across. A clip is 6 to 9 cm wide. A link bracelet is 2 to 4 cm wide and 15 to 18 cm long.
- **Rules:**
  - Wear a pair of clips at the neckline of a dark dress.
  - Set one large stone in a ring with a gold ribbon shank.
  - Link a bracelet in thick, curved gold in the style of a tank track.
  - Add a spray of small rubies to the centre of a gold bow.

### Mystery setting (skill)

A method that sets cut stones on a hidden rail so that no metal shows between them and the stones form one surface. Origin: fashion, Paris, France, 1933.

- Cut each stone with a groove on its sides.
- Slide the stones on thin metal rails inside a frame.
- Close the frame so that no claw or prong shows.
