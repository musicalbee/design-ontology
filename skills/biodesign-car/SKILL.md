# Biodesign Car

> A car for everyday life has the forms of a plant or an animal. It is round, smooth and friendly, with no hard edge and no sign of a machine.

The soft, organic car of the 1990s. A rounded one-box body with an oval face, body-colour bumpers and large clear lamps. The cabin is soft cloth and curved plastic.

**Period:** 1990–2002 · **Era:** Power and subculture · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Bio-design, Jellybean car, Organic car

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Biodesign Car, you MUST:

1. Round each corner with a radius of at least 15 cm, and merge the bumpers into the body.
2. Paint the bumpers and the door mirrors in the body colour.
3. Shape the face as an oval or a smile, with large, clear, curved lamps.
4. Raise the roof into a bubble to give a tall, open cabin.
5. Cut the number of body panels, and keep the shut lines thin and even.
6. Shape the dashboard in soft curves, in two-tone plastic, with round vents and a single curved binnacle.
7. Trim the seats with a bright, soft knit or velour in a playful pattern.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A compact one-box or two-box body with a high, rounded roof, a short sloped bonnet and a rear that ends in a smooth curve.
- **Line:** One smooth line from the bonnet over the roof to the tail, with no break at the screen or the lamp.
- **Fit:** A tall, open cabin with a high seat position and a lot of glass; the body is short but roomy.
- **Proportion:** Length 3.4 to 4.3 m; height 1.4 to 1.7 m; wheels at the corners; overhangs short.
- **Rules:**
  - Round the front in one smooth curve with a small, oval grille.
  - Set large lamps with clear lenses into the curve of the wings.
  - Paint the bumpers, the mirrors and the door handles in the body colour.
  - Use a rear window and a tail panel that flow in a single arc.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Automotive clay modelling (process)

A designer shapes a full-size car in warm industrial clay over a frame. The clay is cut, smoothed and checked in light before any metal is made. Origin: fashion, Detroit, United States, c. 1930.

- Build a wood and foam frame in the size of the car.
- Warm the clay and press it onto the frame, then cut it with rakes and scrapers.
- Check the highlights in light and measure the form for the dies.

### Injection moulding (process)

Hot plastic is pushed into a steel mould under high pressure. The part cools, and the mould opens to release it. Origin: fashion, Newark, United States, 1872.

- Melt the plastic pellets in a heated barrel.
- Press the melt into the closed mould.
- Cool the part, open the mould and push the part out.

### Sheet-metal pressing (process)

A press forms a flat steel sheet into a body panel in one stroke. The panels are then welded into a body. Origin: fashion, Philadelphia, United States, c. 1914.

- Cut the steel sheet to size and load it into the die.
- Close the press so that the sheet takes the shape of the die.
- Weld the panels into one shell and grind the seams smooth.

### Wind-tunnel testing (tool)

A model or a car stands in a tunnel with a strong air flow. The test shows how the body pushes the air and how much drag it makes. Origin: fashion, London, United Kingdom, 1871.

- Fix the model to the balance in the tunnel.
- Run the fan at a set speed and measure the drag and the lift.
- Change the body form and test again.
