# Cornishware

> A kitchen set looks right when every piece carries the same four blue stripes on white, so the shelf reads as one plain family.

White earthenware kitchen ware with four blue hand-banded stripes. It started as a set of storage jars and grew to jugs, mugs and egg cups, in a pattern that a lathe repeats by hand.

**Period:** 1924–1985 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Cornish Ware, Blue and white banded kitchenware, Kitchen storage jars

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Cornishware, you MUST:

1. Make every piece in white earthenware with a wall 3 to 5 mm thick and a plain, round shape.
2. Band the piece on a banding wheel with four blue stripes: two thick and two thin.
3. Use one blue, a deep cobalt, in the stripes, and keep the rest white.
4. Mark the side of a canister with its name in black lettering.
5. Put a plain lid with a knob on each jar; make the size steps 1, 2 and 3.
6. Glaze the piece in a clear, bright, white-looking glaze and fire it at about 1060 °C.
7. Add a pale blue or a cream in later pieces only after 1960.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Storage jars and canisters in graduated sizes, jugs, mugs, bowls, egg cups, rolling pins and teapots.
- **Profile:** A straight or barrel-shaped wall; a jug has a rounded belly and a short neck; a lid is a flat disc with a knob.
- **Foot and rim:** A flat, glazed foot; a plain, rounded rim; a flat lid that sits in a ledge.
- **Proportion:** A canister stands 9 to 20 cm high; wall 3 to 5 mm; the stripes are 4 to 15 mm wide.
- **Rules:**
  - Put four stripes on every piece, in the same order and widths.
  - Letter the contents on the front in black capitals.
  - Keep the forms plain and round.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · terracotta
- **Rule:** Use a white earthenware that takes a bright clear glaze. The body must stay white so that the blue stripes stand out.

## Forming

- **Methods:** jolleying and jiggering · slip casting for jugs and teapots · hand banding on a banding wheel · stamping or lettering the name · dipping in clear glaze
- **Joins:** Cast handles are fettled and fixed with slip; lids are cast in a plaster mould to fit the ledge.
- **Details:** The stripes are slightly uneven where the hand runs the brush; the lettering is in black stamp type.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** lead-glaze · coloured-gloss
- **Decorations:** hand-banding · decal · undecorated
- **Pattern:** Four horizontal blue stripes in groups of two thick and two thin, with the name of the contents in black capitals on the front of a canister.
- **Gloss:** A bright gloss of about 80 to 95 GU.
- **Wear:** The blue stripes sit under the glaze and stay clear; the glaze crazes in a fine net on old pieces.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A gas or coal-fired tunnel kiln; biscuit firing, banding and a clear glaze firing.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 1100 °C · banding the stripes by hand on the wheel · lettering the name · dipping in clear glaze · glaze firing at about 1060 °C
- **Scale:** A factory of several hundred workers; the range reached more than 30 shapes and sold in the millions.
- **Signs:** Four blue stripes that vary a little from piece to piece, and black lettering on the front.

### Slip casting (process)

Liquid clay is poured into a plaster mould. The plaster takes water from the clay and a wall forms on the inside of the mould. Origin: ceramics, Staffordshire, England, c. 1745.

- Make a plaster mould in two or more parts.
- Pour slip into the mould and wait 10 to 20 minutes for a wall of 3 to 5 mm.
- Pour out the rest of the slip.
- Open the mould when the clay is leather-hard and fettle the seam.
