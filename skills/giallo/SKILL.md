# Giallo

> Saturated gel light, a killer's-eye camera and a stylised set make the murder a piece of design, so fear comes from the look more than from the plot.

An Italian murder film in saturated colour. A killer in black gloves is seen only as a hand and a blade. The camera moves as the killer sees, and the set glows in red, blue and green gels.

**Period:** 1963–1985 · **Era:** Portable cameras and the new waves · **Domain:** Film & Video · **Also called:** Italian thriller, Italian murder mystery

## System directives

When planning, shooting, prompting, editing or grading moving images in Giallo, you MUST:

1. Light with coloured gels in red, blue and green, and mix 2 saturated colours in one frame.
2. Show the killer as a gloved hand, a coat and a blade, and keep the face off screen until the last reel.
3. Put the camera at the killer's point of view, and move it by 0.3 to 1 m/s through a door or a window.
4. Shoot each murder as a set piece of 60 to 180 s, with a wide lens of 18mm to 24mm and a slow build.
5. Use a bold set, with a wallpaper, a statue, a mirror or a painting in the frame.
6. Hold a close-up of an eye, a key or a glass for 1 to 3 s as a clue.
7. Dub all sound after the shoot and let the score run loud over the murder.

## Composition

- **Framing:** A wide 2.35:1 or 1.85:1 frame with a saturated wall, a mirror or a painting that sets a second frame; a black glove at the frame edge.
- **Staging in depth:** 3 planes: a foreground object or a glove, the victim, and a coloured wall or window at the back.
- **Subject placement:** The victim off the centre at 1/3; the killer's hand enters from the frame edge.

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** A saturated colour print where red, blue and green wash a whole wall, with black shadow.
- **Grade recipe:** Raise the saturation by 25% to 40% in red, blue and green. · Set the black point at 3% and use a contrast curve of +25%. · Keep the whites tinted by the gel; do not neutralise them. · Add grain at 15% to 20%. · Do not add a teal and orange split.

### Coloured gel lighting (process)

A white lamp covered with a sheet of coloured film, so that a set or a face is lit in red, blue or green with no natural cause. Origin: film & video, Rome, c. 1964.

- Put a saturated gel (red, blue or green) over a hard source; use one colour for each zone of the set.
- Light the face from the side in one colour and the wall behind it in a second colour from the opposite side.
- Keep the key light at one stop or more above the fill so that the colour reads as shadow and as light.
- Do not justify the colour with a lamp in the frame; the colour is the mood.
