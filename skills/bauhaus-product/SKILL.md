# Bauhaus product

> An everyday object is a model for the factory: its form comes from plain geometry, its material and its use, and ornament has no place on it.

The workshop objects of the Bauhaus: lamps, tea sets and chairs built from the sphere, the cylinder and the cone in nickel, chrome, opal glass and bent steel tube, as models for mass production.

**Period:** 1923–1933 · **Era:** Machine modern · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Bauhaus metalwork, Bauhaus industrial design, Machine-age functionalism

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Bauhaus product, you MUST:

1. Build each body from a sphere, a cylinder, a cone or a cube; show the solid, not a style.
2. Make each part do one job, and let the eye read the job: a stem carries, a shade spreads light, a handle holds.
3. Use bent steel tube of 20 to 25 mm for the frame of a seat or a table, and leave it bare and chromed.
4. Use opal glass for a diffuser, and nickel or chrome for the metal parts.
5. Keep the surfaces smooth and free of ornament; a screw or a joint stays in view.
6. Keep colour to the colour of the material, with black, white and one primary colour at most.
7. Draw each object so that a factory can make it in series from standard parts.

## Form

- **Geometry:** The sphere, the hemisphere, the cylinder, the cone and the disc, joined on one vertical or horizontal axis.
- **Silhouette:** A clear outline of two or three solids: a disc base, a straight stem and a dome or a sphere on top.
- **Edges:** Sharp edges on metal discs and rims, with a radius of 0 to 2 mm; tube bends have a radius of 2 to 3 times the tube diameter.
- **Proportion:** A table lamp is about 36 cm high with a shade of 18 cm; a base disc is about equal to the shade diameter.
- **Ornament:** None. The only pattern is the repeat of standard parts, such as a row of tubes or a band of rivets.
- **Rules:**
  - Each object has 2 to 4 primary solids.
  - Each metal tube is 20 to 25 mm in diameter.
  - A shade, a bowl or a vessel is a part of a sphere or a cylinder.
  - No surface carries a motif; 0% of the surface is ornament.
  - A colour other than black, white, grey or the metal covers less than 10% of the object.

## Finish & texture

- **Finishes:** polished · acid-etched · lacquered · moulded-gloss · natural-fibre · painted-matte
- **Textures:** mirror-smooth · satin-smooth · etched · plain-weave · fine-grain
- **Gloss:** 70–95 GU on metal, 5–15 GU on glass and wood
- **Patina:** The style wants a new, clean surface; chrome and nickel stay bright, and a scratched object is out of character.

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** chrome
- **Section:** Tube 20–25 mm, rods and stems 8–12 mm
- **Joints:** Screwed or riveted joints in view; bent tube instead of a welded corner where possible.
- **Handles:** A straight bar or a ring of round section, or a disc knob of black Bakelite or wood.
- **Switches and taps:** A round rotary switch or a small pull cord; a cross-head or lever tap on a cylinder body.

## Manufacture

- **Processes:** metal spinning · tube bending · glass blowing in a mould · nickel and chrome plating · riveting

### Cantilever (process)

A beam or slab fixed at one end that carries load beyond its support, so that it seems to hang in the air. Origin: architecture, Northern Europe, c. 1300.

- Fix the beam at one end and anchor it with a back span of at least 1 part to every 2 parts of the overhang.
- Keep the free end thin; a depth of 1/8 to 1/10 of the span suits steel and concrete.
- Repeat the horizontal line in a run of 2 or 3 projecting planes to give a strong shadow.
