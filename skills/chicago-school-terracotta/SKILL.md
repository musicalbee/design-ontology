# Chicago School terracotta

> When a steel frame carries the load, the wall becomes a skin: a thin clay tile protects the steel from fire and carries the ornament.

Thin, fireproof terracotta cladding on the steel frames of early skyscrapers. Architects in Chicago used it for flat, white or cream walls and for flowing plant ornament.

**Period:** 1885–1930 · **Era:** Art pottery and reform · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Chicago terracotta cladding, Sullivanesque terracotta, Skyscraper terracotta

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Chicago School terracotta, you MUST:

1. Hang a thin hollow terracotta tile on the steel frame; the tile carries no load.
2. Keep the wall flat and divided in a grid that shows the frame behind it.
3. Use a cream, buff or white glaze, so that the clay shows the same colour from the street.
4. Model the ornament as flowing plants and geometric knots, in low relief, over the piers and the spandrels.
5. Fire the tile hard so that it protects the steel from fire and rain.
6. Make each piece in a mould, and fit it to a number on the plan.
7. Cover the whole facade with one material, from the base to the cornice.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Hollow cladding blocks for piers and spandrels, window surrounds, cornices, capitals and large relief panels.
- **Profile:** A flat face with a low, crisp relief; the ornament rises 1 to 3 cm and flows over the whole panel.
- **Foot and rim:** Each tile has square edges with a small lip for the joint and an open back with webs, which hold the anchor.
- **Proportion:** A tile is about 30 to 60 cm wide and 5 to 10 cm thick; a spandrel panel can be 1 m wide.
- **Rules:** Make the tile hollow and thin. · Show the grid of the frame on the wall. · Use low relief that flows across tiles. · Keep the colour in one pale family.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** terracotta
- **Secondary:** steel-frame · fired-brick
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · stoneware · jasperware · parian · paper-clay
- **Rule:** Use a fine, even terracotta that fires pale, so that a thin glaze or a clear slip gives a cream or white surface. The tile must be hard and without large pores.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pressing in plaster moulds · hollow casting with webs · drying in a heated drying room · firing in a downdraught kiln
- **Joins:** Tiles are hung on steel anchors and rods on the frame, with thin joints of cement mortar between them.
- **Details:** A mould seam on the back, an anchor slot, a number or mark on the edge, and a thin, bright glaze line at the joint.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** bare-clay · satin-glaze · matte-glaze
- **Decorations:** relief-moulding · incised · undecorated
- **Pattern:** Plant and geometric ornament in low relief on piers, spandrels and cornices; the wide fields between are plain, in cream or white.
- **Gloss:** Satin to matt, about 10 to 50 GU; some tall buildings used a bright white glaze of about 70 GU.
- **Wear:** The tile weathers well; the glaze crazes with age, and a few tiles lose a corner at the edge.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A pale, clean colour on the whole wall: white, cream or light buff, with a little green or red in the ornament of the base.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A large downdraught kiln, often in Chicago or on the east coast, with saggars for glazed work.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** pressing in plaster moulds · drying for several days · a bisque firing at about 1100 °C · a glaze firing at about 1100 °C
- **Scale:** Several large companies made the tile; a single tower used tens of thousands of blocks.
- **Signs:** A numbered back, a thin mould seam and a small mark of the maker on the edge.

### Hollow terracotta block (process)

A building block of clay that has webs inside. A maker presses it in a mould, so that it dries and fires without a crack and weighs little. Origin: ceramics, England, c. 1850.

- Press the clay in a plaster mould to the shape and the relief of the block.
- Leave the back open and add webs inside, with a wall of 25 to 50 mm.
- Dry the block slowly, then fire it hard at about 1100 °C.
- Bed the block in mortar and tie it to the backing wall or the steel frame.
