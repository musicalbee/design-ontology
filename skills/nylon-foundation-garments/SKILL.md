# Nylon Foundation Garments

> The post-war dress needs a built body under it, and nylon and elastic make that build light, washable and soft to wear.

The structured underwear of the New Look years: circle-stitched cups, a waist cincher, an elastic girdle and a suspender belt. They were knitted or woven in nylon, satin and rubber-core elastic.

**Period:** 1947–1962 · **Era:** War and the New Look · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Bullet bra and girdle, Waist cincher, Post-war foundations

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Nylon Foundation Garments, you MUST:

1. Cut each cup as a cone, and stitch it in circles from the tip, so that the point holds its shape.
2. Narrow the waist by 5 to 8 cm with a waist cincher or a long-line bra with boning.
3. Knit the body in nylon tricot, so that it dries fast after washing.
4. Set a panel of elastic in the girdle, and fit four suspenders to the lower edge.
5. Fasten the bra with three hooks at the back and the girdle with a side zip.
6. Trim the cups with a nylon lace and a small satin bow.
7. Match the foundation to the dress: a strapless basque for a full skirt, a girdle for a narrow skirt.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A pointed, lifted bust, a very small waist and a smooth, firm hip: an hourglass built under the dress.
- **Line:** A high bust point, a long waist line and a smooth hip line down to the thigh.
- **Fit:** Close and firm; the stitched cup holds the bust and the girdle holds the hip and the stomach.
- **Proportion:** Waist 5 to 8 cm below the natural waist; cup point 3 to 5 cm out from the body; girdle length 35 to 45 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Stitch each cup in circles from the tip to the edge.
  - Run the waist cincher from the bust to the hip, with boning at the sides.
  - Set an elastic panel at the front and the back of the girdle.
  - Hang four suspenders from the lower edge for the nylon stockings.
  - Fasten the back with hooks and eyes in three rows.

### Lastex yarn (material)

An elastic yarn with a rubber core wrapped in wool, rayon, silk or cotton thread; it let a knitted garment stretch and return. Origin: fashion, United States, 1931.

- Wrap a rubber thread in one or two layers of cotton, silk or rayon.
- Knit or weave the yarn into a band or a panel.
- Cut the band to length and finish the edge.

### Raschel warp knitting (technology)

A flat warp-knitting machine that makes open nets and fine elastic meshes, and that could knit the new synthetic fibres at speed. Origin: fashion, Europe, 1859.

- Feed a beam of parallel yarns to a row of latch needles.
- Loop each yarn with its neighbour in a set pattern.
- Take up the mesh in a flat sheet and cut the pieces.
