# Ding white ware

> A white body and a clear ivory glaze are enough: the beauty is the thin wall, the carved line and the quiet tone.

Thin ivory-white porcelain of the Northern Song court. The potters of Ding carve or mould fine plant designs under a clear glaze and fire the bowls rim down, so they bind the bare rim with metal.

**Period:** 960–1279 · **Era:** Medieval glaze · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Ding yao, Northern Song white porcelain, Ding ware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Ding white ware, you MUST:

1. Throw the bowl with a wall 2 to 3 mm thick and turn it on the outside to a fine profile.
2. Cut the design in the leather-hard body with a comb or a knife, in lines 1 to 2 mm wide.
3. Press a moulded design of peony, lotus or fish into the inside of a dish for a sharp relief.
4. Cover the piece in a clear glaze with a trace of iron, so that it fires to an ivory white.
5. Stack the bowl rim down on a ring support to fire many pieces in one kiln.
6. Leave the rim bare after firing and bind it with a band of silver or copper.
7. Allow a few tear-like runs of glaze on the outside; they are a mark of the kiln.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Conical and flaring bowls, small dishes with a lobed rim, flat plates and small cups on a low foot.
- **Profile:** A straight, open cone, or a shallow dish with a wide mouth; the line from foot to rim has no break.
- **Foot and rim:** A narrow, neat foot ring with a bare unglazed rim, which a band of metal often covers; the lobed rim has 6 or 8 points.
- **Proportion:** Bowl diameter 12 to 25 cm, height 5 to 9 cm, and a wall 2 to 3 mm thick at the rim.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep one clean curve from the foot to the mouth.
  - Carve or mould the decoration only on the inside, in one band.
  - Leave the rim bare, because the bowl fires upside down.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** porcelain
- **Secondary:** silver
- **Avoid:** earthenware · stoneware · terracotta
- **Rule:** Use a fine white kaolin body that fires dense and nearly translucent. Do not use a coloured stoneware: the glaze needs a white ground.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing thin walls on the wheel · turning the outside when leather-hard · combing and carving the inside · press moulding in a fired clay mould · metal banding of the bare rim
- **Joins:** Handles are rare; a metal band covers the bare rim after the firing.
- **Details:** Fine comb lines under the glaze, a ring of bare rim, and small tear-like runs of glaze on the outside.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** clear-feldspathic · bare-clay
- **Decorations:** incised · relief-moulding · undecorated
- **Pattern:** Peony and lotus scrolls, ducks, fish, and wild geese on a water ground, cut or moulded in one band inside the bowl.
- **Gloss:** Soft gloss, about 50 to 70 GU, with a warm ivory tone.
- **Wear:** The glaze stays smooth; the metal band on the rim wears or leaves a green stain.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** One ivory white, a warm cream where the glaze is thick, and a brown trace at the tear runs.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A kiln that burns wood and, from the 11th century, coal; the bowls stand rim down on ring supports in saggars.
- **Atmosphere:** mixed
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at a low heat · dipping in clear glaze · stacking rim down on ring supports · glaze firing at about 1300 °C · binding the bare rim with metal
- **Scale:** The kilns of Quyang supplied the court and the market in the north of China.
- **Signs:** A bare ring at the rim, tear-like runs on the outside, and fine comb lines that show through the glaze.

### Inverted firing (process)

A bowl is fired upside down on its rim in a ring of fireclay, so that the wall stays thin and true. The rim has no glaze and takes a metal band. Origin: ceramics, Hebei, China, c. 1000.

- Throw a thin bowl and coat it with glaze.
- Wipe the glaze from the rim.
- Set the bowl rim-down on a stepped ring of fireclay.
- Fire the stack, then bind the bare rim with copper or silver.
