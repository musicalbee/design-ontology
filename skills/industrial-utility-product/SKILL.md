# Industrial utility product

> A tool-like object earns its form from the job it does and the strain it takes; any surface that does no work is left off.

Fixtures made for work, not for show: enamel shades, cage lamps, cast iron, steel lockers, conduit and brass ship hardware. The form follows the duty, and the finish is hard and repairable.

**Period:** 1910–present · **Era:** Reform and craft · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Factory and workshop fixtures, Industrial vernacular, Utility hardware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Industrial utility product, you MUST:

1. Make each part for one duty: a shade throws light down, a cage guards the bulb, a hook holds a load.
2. Choose cast iron, mild steel, aluminium and glass for strength; use 2 to 4 mm of steel for a shade or a locker.
3. Coat steel in vitreous enamel or powder paint that is hard, washable and 0.1 to 0.3 mm thick.
4. Show every bolt, rivet and weld; a worker must be able to repair each part with a spanner.
5. Use a simple dome, a cylinder or a box with a radius of 3 to 10 mm on the edges.
6. Use stock sizes: standard tube, pipe, sheet and bar, in a few colours such as white, black and grey.
7. Add a guard, a cage or a rib only where the object needs it, and keep the guard open.

## Form

- **Geometry:** The dome, the cylinder, the box and the tube, joined by visible bolts, brackets and welds.
- **Silhouette:** A heavy, simple outline: a wide dome shade on a short stem, a box with a frame, or a tube bent at a right angle.
- **Edges:** Rolled or flanged edges with a radius of 3 to 10 mm; a cast edge has a parting line and a fillet of 2 to 5 mm.
- **Proportion:** A dome shade is 30 to 40 cm wide and about half as deep; a locker is 180 cm high and 30 to 40 cm wide.
- **Ornament:** None. The pattern is the repeat of rivets, ribs, vents and perforations that do a job; they cover 0 to 5% of the surface.
- **Rules:**
  - Each object has 1 to 3 main bodies and a visible fastener for each part.
  - Steel sheet is 1.5 to 4 mm thick; cast iron is 5 to 10 mm thick.
  - No part is there only for looks; each rib, vent and guard has a duty.
  - Fasteners are bolts, rivets or welds, and they are in view.
  - Colour is white, black, grey, olive or a single signal colour, and the metal tone shows at each chip.

## Techniques

### Vitreous enamelling (material)

A thin coat of glass that is fused to iron or steel at about 800 °C. It makes a hard, bright, washable surface that resists heat and rust. Origin: industrial design, Central Europe, c. 1850.

- Clean the metal and coat it with a ground layer of glass powder and water.
- Fire the part at 760 to 840 °C until the glass melts into one skin.
- Apply a top coat of 0.1 to 0.3 mm in white or a single colour and fire it again.
- Leave the edges rolled: the enamel chips at a sharp edge and shows the dark metal.
