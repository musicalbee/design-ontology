# Wedge Design Car

> A fast car cuts the air as a blade does. It has one straight line from a low nose to a high tail, with flat panels and sharp creases.

The car as a low wedge. A pointed nose, a flat bonnet that rises to a high tail and a long straight crease along the flank. It has pop-up lamps and sharp, flat panels with matte black trim.

**Period:** 1968–1989 · **Era:** Youthquake · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Wedge supercar, Folded-paper car, Italian wedge

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Wedge Design Car, you MUST:

1. Draw one straight line from a low nose to a high tail, at a rise of 8 to 12 degrees.
2. Keep the body very low, with a height of 1.05 to 1.15 m.
3. Make the panels flat and join them in sharp creases, with a strong line at the waist.
4. Hide the headlamps in the nose, and open them in pop-up pods.
5. Slope the windscreen at a very low angle, and run the glass in a long wedge to the tail.
6. Use matte black trim, black louvres and black wheel arches to cut the body into planes.
7. Trim the cabin in cord, velour or plaid, with a flat dashboard of black plastic and rows of small switches.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A very low wedge: a sharp nose, a straight flat bonnet and a roof line that rises in one slope to a high, short tail.
- **Line:** A single straight crease from the nose to the tail, and a flat shoulder over a narrow glass area.
- **Fit:** Two seats, laid back, close to the floor; the cabin is tight and the roof is low.
- **Proportion:** Height 1.05 to 1.15 m; width 1.8 to 2 m; length 4.2 to 4.5 m; nose height about 60 percent of the tail height.
- **Rules:**
  - Set the windscreen at a very low angle, merged with the bonnet.
  - Cut the flank with one sharp crease and a deep air intake behind the door.
  - Hide each headlamp in a pop-up pod in the flat nose.
  - Fit big, flat rear glass or a louvred panel, and a wide, flat tail with a row of lamps.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Automotive clay modelling (process)

A designer shapes a full-size car in warm industrial clay over a frame. The clay is cut, smoothed and checked in light before any metal is made. Origin: fashion, Detroit, United States, c. 1930.

- Build a wood and foam frame in the size of the car.
- Warm the clay and press it onto the frame, then cut it with rakes and scrapers.
- Check the highlights in light and measure the form for the dies.

### Glass-fibre moulding (process)

Glass-fibre mat is laid in a mould and wet with resin. The resin sets and leaves a light, rust-free shell. Origin: fashion, Newark, United States, c. 1936.

- Coat the mould with wax and gel coat.
- Lay mat in the mould and wet it with resin.
- Let the resin set, then lift the shell and trim it.

### Injection moulding (process)

Hot plastic is pushed into a steel mould under high pressure. The part cools, and the mould opens to release it. Origin: fashion, Newark, United States, 1872.

- Melt the plastic pellets in a heated barrel.
- Press the melt into the closed mould.
- Cool the part, open the mould and push the part out.
