# Underwear as Outerwear

> What was hidden is now the dress: a corset, a bra or a slip shown in the open gives the body a built, public form.

Designers of the 1980s and 1990s took the corset, the cone bra and the slip out of the wardrobe and put them on the street. The bodices were boned satin and wool, worn as tops, jackets and dresses.

**Period:** 1984–1999 · **Era:** Power and subculture · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Corsetry as dress, Lingerie dressing, Bustier and corset top

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Underwear as Outerwear, you MUST:

1. Cut the corset from historical patterns, with 10 to 20 boned panels and a busk or a laced back.
2. Boil down the idea to one garment: a boned bodice that stands as a top, a jacket or a dress.
3. Use outer cloth for the underwear shape: satin, wool tweed, leather or cotton drill.
4. Cone the cup in satin and set it at the bust, with a metal-cone bra for an extreme version.
5. Pair the corset with a plain skirt, a tailored jacket or trousers, so that the contrast shows.
6. Show the garment fully: a visible busk, a visible lace and a visible strap.
7. Add a slip dress in silk satin, 2 to 3 mm of seam and a bias cut.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A built hourglass worn as an outer garment: a hard bodice, a bare shoulder and a skirt or trousers below.
- **Line:** A strong curve from the bust to the hip, with the garment edge in plain view.
- **Fit:** Close and firm at the torso, with the bone and the lace in the open; the lower half is loose or tailored.
- **Proportion:** Waist 55 to 65 cm; corset length 30 to 40 cm; cone cup 12 to 15 cm out from the body.
- **Rules:**
  - Cut the corset to be seen: finish the bone channels and the lace as decoration.
  - Pair the boned top with a long skirt, wide trousers or a man's jacket.
  - Set the neckline straight or at the bust line, with no cover.
  - Use a cone cup only as a large, bold shape.
  - Add a long coat or a jacket over the corset for the street.

### Boning (process)

A way to stiffen a bodice with thin strips of whalebone, cane or steel set in narrow channels, so that the cloth holds the shape of the torso. Origin: fashion, Europe, c. 1550.

- Sew two or three layers of strong cloth together.
- Stitch narrow channels, 6 to 10 mm wide, from the waist to the chest.
- Push one strip of bone or steel into each channel and close the end.
- Bind each edge of the garment in leather or tape.
