# Jasperware (Neoclassical)

> A matt coloured stoneware with white Greek and Roman reliefs lets an English factory make the antique world for the library table.

A fine, dense, unglazed stoneware that Josiah Wedgwood made from 1775. It is stained in pale blue, green or lilac and carries white relief figures in the classical manner.

**Period:** 1775–1830 · **Era:** Industrial pottery · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Wedgwood jasper, Jasper ware, Etruria jasper

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Jasperware (Neoclassical), you MUST:

1. Stain the whole body, not only the surface, with cobalt, so that the blue shows in each chip.
2. Leave the surface unglazed and fine-polished, so that it has a velvet matt look.
3. Press each white relief in a pitcher mould, 1 to 3 mm thick, and fix it to the damp body with water.
4. Take the figures from Greek vases and Roman gems: dancing women, sacrifices, Cupid and muses.
5. Use the solid jasper for fine pieces and the jasper dip, a thin coloured skin on a white body, for cheaper pieces.
6. Divide the surface into bands: a laurel or key-pattern border, a main frieze and a plain foot.
7. Fire the body once to full density at about 1200 to 1250 °C.
8. Mark the base with the factory name impressed in the clay.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Urns, vases with a lid, ewers, pedestal vases and small bowls, after antique marble, bronze and Greek pottery.
- **Profile:** A clear classical outline: a round or egg-shaped body, a high foot with a square plinth, and a lid with an acorn or a knob.
- **Foot and rim:** A square or round plinth foot that is turned sharp; the rim is a plain band or a row of egg-and-dart.
- **Proportion:** Height 10 to 35 cm; the wall is 4 to 7 mm thick, and the plinth is one fifth of the height.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the outline simple, so that the reliefs are the ornament.
  - Turn the foot and the edges sharp on a lathe.
  - Place the relief frieze at the widest part of the body.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** jasperware
- **Secondary:** black-basalt
- **Avoid:** porcelain · earthenware · bone-china
- **Rule:** Use a dense white stoneware of barium sulphate, clay, flint and flux, stained through with metal oxides. Black basalt is its dark companion body.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing and turning on a lathe · press moulding of vases · sprig moulding of the white reliefs · slip casting of small forms · engine turning of the foot
- **Joins:** Each relief is cut from the mould, wetted and pressed onto the body; fine undercut edges are trimmed with a knife.
- **Details:** A sharp, undercut edge to each relief, a faint seam of the mould that the finisher removes, and an impressed mark on the base.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** bare-clay
- **Decorations:** sprigging · relief-moulding · undecorated
- **Pattern:** A frieze of white figures from antique art on a pale ground, with a laurel, a key pattern or a flower swag as border.
- **Gloss:** Matt to satin, about 2 to 10 GU; the surface looks like fine-grained stone.
- **Wear:** The surface takes grease and marks; a chipped relief shows the white clay, and a sharp edge softens with use.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A matt single ground colour in a pale tone, with the white relief as the only contrast.

- **Kiln:** A coal-fired bottle kiln, with the pieces in saggars.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** mixing of the stained body in the slip house · moulding and pressing of the white reliefs · fixing of the sprigs to the damp body · a single firing to full density · fine polishing of the foot on the lathe

- **Signs:** A matt, even ground; undercut relief edges; and a slight warp where the base rested on the saggar.

### Sprig moulding (process)

A small relief is pressed in a mould of fired clay, lifted out and fixed to the damp body with water. Potters use it to add figures and leaves. Origin: ceramics, Staffordshire, England, c. 1740.

- Carve the relief in reverse in a small mould of fired clay.
- Press a thin piece of soft clay into the mould and trim it flat.
- Lift the sprig out with the tip of a knife.
- Wet the body and press the sprig on it, then fettle the edge.
