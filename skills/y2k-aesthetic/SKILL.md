# Y2K Aesthetic

> Technology is friendly and the future is shiny, so an interface should look like a new gadget that is already finished.

Chrome and translucent plastic, bubble shapes, silver and electric blue, and small pixel and tech type on cold gradients. It came from the dot-com boom and the first iMac in 1998, and it pictured the year 2000 as a clean, shiny future.

**Period:** 1997–2004 · **Era:** Postmodern & Early Digital · **Domain:** Graphic & UI Design · **Also called:** Millennium Futurism, Cyber Y2K, Techno-Optimism 2000, Web 1.5 Chrome

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "y2k", "2000s", "millennium", "chrome", "cyber", "dot-com", "futuristic", "iMac G3", "bubble", "frutiger", "techno", "glossy".

Good for: music and fashion sites, tech product launches, nostalgia campaigns, game and app landing pages, event promotions.

Do not use it when:
- The brand needs a serious, quiet or handmade tone.
- The interface is dense data work where gloss and glow slow the eye.
- The product must follow a current flat platform design system.

## System directives

When generating UI or graphic layouts in Y2K Aesthetic, you MUST:

1. Fill large areas with vertical or radial gradients that run from a pale tint to a saturated hue, over a 40% to 60% lightness range.
2. Make buttons and badges as glossy pills with a radius of 999px and a white highlight that covers the upper 45% of the shape.
3. Use translucent plastic panels at 55% to 80% opacity with a 1px white inner highlight and a coloured tint.
4. Place chrome, globe, orbit and star motifs behind the content, at 15% to 40% of the viewport width.
5. Pair a wide rounded display face with a small 10px to 12px tech sans or pixel face for labels and metadata.
6. Build the palette from silver, ice blue and one electric accent, and add one acid hue for warnings and tags.
7. Add small functional detail: serial numbers, version tags, tiny arrows and 1px dividers in a light grey.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Matte flat fills with no gradient.
- **NEVER:** Torn paper, grain and photocopy texture.
- **NEVER:** Earth-tone palettes.
- **NEVER:** Square corners on buttons and badges.
- **NEVER:** Serif body text with a literary tone.
- **NEVER:** Heavy black outlines and hard offset shadows.
- **NEVER:** Hand-drawn scribbles and rough ink edges.

## Spatial & grid logic

- **Grid mechanics:** A 12-column grid at 960px or 1024px maximum width with 16px gutters; modules sit in table-like rows, and a floating hero object breaks the grid by up to 80px.
- **Whitespace ratio:** 20% to 35% of the surface stays empty; the spacing scale is 4, 8, 16, 32, 64px, with tight gaps of 4px inside component groups.
- **Alignment rules:** Centre the hero and the logo, flush-left the navigation and body text, and stack components in a symmetrical column with the main object on the central axis.
- **Container geometry:** Pills and capsules with 999px radius for controls, panels with a 16px to 24px radius, and orbs or circles at 48px to 240px for hero shapes.

## Typography

- **Classifications:** rounded geometric sans · wide techno sans · pixel and bitmap sans · humanist sans
- **Tracking:** Display sizes take +0.04em to +0.12em in upper case; body stays at 0; 10px labels take +0.1em.
- **Leading:** Display 1.1; body 1.5; labels 1.2.
- **Hierarchy rules:**
  - Use weights 400 and 700, with display in upper case at 32px to 64px.
  - Set labels and metadata in 10px to 12px upper case with +0.1em tracking.
  - Give the display text a vertical gradient fill from white to ice blue or a 1px dark edge, and never a flat grey.
  - Keep body copy at 14px to 16px in dark navy on a light panel.

## Colour & lighting

- **Palette structure:** Silver and ice blue cover 60% of the surface; one electric blue or cyan covers 25%; one acid accent in lime or hot pink covers under 5%.
- **Contrast rules:** Body text in navy holds 12:1 or better on ice and silver panels; white text on electric blue holds 4.5:1 and appears at 14px bold or larger only.
- **Surface treatment:** Glossy and translucent: vertical gradients, a white specular band over the top 45% and tinted plastic panels at 55% to 80% opacity.
- **Light and shadow:** Light comes from the top, with a white highlight at the top edge and a soft coloured shadow below; add a cyan outer glow of 12px to 24px on active elements.

## Materiality & texture

- **Surface simulation:** Translucent coloured plastic and brushed chrome, as on the 1998 iMac G3 and brushed metal gadgets.
- **Border rules:** 1px borders in a lighter tint of the fill, with a 1px white inner highlight; metal edges use a 2px light-to-dark gradient.
- **Blur and elevation:** Soft shadows with 8px to 24px blur and a 4px to 8px vertical offset at 25% opacity; background blur is rare and stays under 8px.

## Ornamentation & iconography

- **Iconography:** Glossy 3D-like icons at 32px to 48px with a 1px outline, a top highlight and a reflection; small pixel arrows at 12px.
- **Illustration:** Chrome blobs, globes, orbit rings, stars and lens flares drawn as smooth vector or 3D renders; figures are simple, shiny and faceless.
- **Decorative elements:** chrome blobs · orbit rings · four-point stars · lens flares · tiny serial numbers · holographic gradients · globe wireframes

## Motion & interactivity

- **Born on screen:** yes
- **Transition physics:** Smooth, springy and slightly floaty: objects glide in, overshoot by 4% to 8% and settle; glows pulse slowly and Flash-style tweens loop.
- **Easing:** cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)
- **Duration (ms):** 320
- **Hover and active:** Hover raises the gloss band to 55% opacity and adds a 16px cyan glow; active presses the button by 1px and darkens the gradient by 10%.

- **Icons:** duotone, stroke 1.5 on a 24px field, round caps, round joins, container: circle. Use: `<svg class="icon"><use href="pack/icons.svg#home"/></svg>`. An icon takes `currentColor`.
- **Icon family:** the sprite is cut from Pixelarticons (Regular) by Gerrit Halfmann, licence MIT. Reason: Pixel-grid forms: the screen technology of the period shows in the icon. It is an open-source stand-in for a demo. For a product, use the icon library of the brand and choose the family by the rules below.
- **Display type:** weight 800, tracking 0.04em, leading 1.05, case: uppercase.
- **Weights:** display 800, subhead 700, body 400, bold in text 700, labels and buttons 700. The style uses 3 weights (400, 700, 800) and no other. The display is 400 heavier than the body. The accent is case and tracking: an upper-case label is the accent, so the weights stay few.
- **Body type:** 16px, leading 1.55.
- **Page:** hero stacked, center aligned, regular density, accent tilt 0°.
- **Text colour:** `--color-text` is for the main reading field only. On each field, use its token: `--color-on-bg`, `--color-on-surface`, `--color-on-primary`, `--color-on-accent`.

### How to choose an icon pack

An icon pack is a typeface for pictures. Choose it with the same care as the text face. These rules hold for an open-source set and for a licensed library.

1. **The stroke matches the stem.** Set an icon next to the body text at text size. The line of the icon and the stem of a letter have the same weight.
2. **Terminals and corners follow the radius token.** Butt caps and mitre joins go with a radius of zero. Round caps and round joins go with a soft radius.
3. **The fill mode follows the technology of the era.** Print eras cut solid forms. Screen eras with depth fill and shade the form. Current product interfaces draw an outline.
4. **The metaphors are not younger than the style.** Each picture is an object that the era knew. An icon with a later metaphor breaks the period.
5. **One family, one grid.** All icons come from one family and one variant, drawn on one field. Do not mix families on one surface.
6. **The family covers the product.** List each concept that the product needs before you commit. A family that has no icon for one of them is the wrong family.
7. **The colour is a token.** An icon takes currentColor. A second tone comes from a token, not from the file.
8. **The licence permits the use.** Read the licence of the set. Give credit where the licence asks for it. A product with a brand uses its licensed library and applies the same rules to choose the family.

## Lineage fallbacks

When the user asks for a variation, do not improvise. Blend toward the neighbour named here, and load its skill.

- If the user asks for **more natural**: move toward [`frutiger-aero`](../frutiger-aero/SKILL.md) (Frutiger Aero). Add sky, grass and water imagery, soften the chrome to a 20px glass radius and move the palette to green and aqua; keep the gloss and the rounded type.
- If the user asks for **more minimal**: move toward [`flat-design`](../flat-design/SKILL.md) (Flat Design). Remove the gradients, highlights and glows, keep two hues and the pill shapes at a 12px radius.
- If the user asks for **more translucent**: move toward [`glassmorphism`](../glassmorphism/SKILL.md) (Glassmorphism). Raise background blur to 16px to 24px, lower panel opacity to 20% to 40% and drop the chrome; keep the cool palette.
- If the user asks for **less glossy**: move toward [`swiss-international`](../swiss-international/SKILL.md) (Swiss International Style). Use a 12-column grid, one sans serif and one blue accent; keep the small upper-case labels.
