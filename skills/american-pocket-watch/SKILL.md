# American pocket watch

> A watch is a public tool. Each part is made to a gauge, the face is clear and the case lasts for decades.

The plain, tough pocket watch that American factories made from 1850 with machine-cut interchangeable parts. It has a white enamel dial with black Arabic numerals and a gold-filled or silver case. The rail grade movement kept time for the railroads.

**Period:** 1850–1930 · **Era:** Victorian dress · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Railroad watch, American factory watch, Waltham style

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in American pocket watch, you MUST:

1. Cut each plate and wheel to a gauge, so that a spare part fits any watch of the same model.
2. Print black Arabic numerals 1 to 12 on a white enamel dial, with a full minute track to 60.
3. Make the hands as blued spades or plain batons, so that the time shows at a glance.
4. Adjust a rail grade movement to keep time within 30 seconds a week in five positions.
5. Fit 21 jewels in a rail grade movement, and set them in gold settings.
6. Set the hands with a lever beside the crown, so that a knock cannot move them.
7. Make the case 10 to 14 mm thick, with a screw bezel and a snap back that keep out dust.
8. Mark the movement with the maker, the grade and a serial number.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A thick round pocket watch of 16 or 18 size, 43 to 46 mm wide. It has a bow on a pendant and an open-face or hunter case.
- **Line:** A heavy circle with a deep band, a ring bow and a large crown.
- **Fit:** The watch rides in a waistcoat pocket on a chain with a fob, and its weight sits low against the body.
- **Proportion:** Case thickness to width is about 1 to 3.5. The dial fills about 80 per cent of the width. The crown is as wide as the bow.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the numerals large: each numeral is at least 3 mm tall.
  - Run the minute track to the edge of the dial so that the minute hand can be read exactly.
  - Put the small seconds dial at 6 o'clock and leave the 12 free.
  - Make the bow a plain ring that does not turn on the pendant by accident.

### Interchangeable parts watchmaking (process)

A factory method in which machines cut every part to a gauge, so that a part from one watch fits any other watch of the same model. Origin: fashion, Roxbury, Massachusetts, 1850.

- Cut each part on a machine to a gauge of 0.01 mm.
- Sort the parts into bins by model, not by watch.
- Assemble the watch from parts that the worker does not file or fit.
