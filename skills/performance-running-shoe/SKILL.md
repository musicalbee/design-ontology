# Performance running shoe

> A running shoe is a tool. Every part, from the weight to the heel height, is set by the way the foot lands and the number of kilometres it runs.

The shoe made for distance running. It has a light nylon mesh and suede upper, a foam midsole, a waffle or lugged rubber outsole, and a raised, cushioned heel.

**Period:** 1972–present · **Era:** Youthquake · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Jogging shoe, Waffle trainer, Cushioned trainer

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Performance running shoe, you MUST:

1. Keep the shoe under 300 g in a men's size 9, and cut weight before decoration.
2. Build the upper from nylon mesh with suede reinforcement at the toe and the heel.
3. Mould the midsole from EVA foam, 2 to 3 cm thick at the heel and thinner at the forefoot.
4. Set the heel 8 to 12 mm higher than the forefoot, and flare it for a heel strike.
5. Mould a waffle or lug pattern into the rubber outsole for grip.
6. Add a padded collar and tongue for comfort at long distance.
7. Add reflective trim and a visible stripe for the road.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A low, wedge-shaped shoe: a flared heel, a rounded toe box and a bold side stripe.
- **Line:** A line that slopes up from toe to heel, with a swoosh or a stripe along the side.
- **Fit:** Snug at the heel and the midfoot, roomy in the toe box, with a padded collar.
- **Proportion:** Heel height 25 to 35 mm; forefoot height 15 to 25 mm; mass under 300 g.
- **Rules:** Make the heel wider than the arch. · Place a stripe or a mark on the side. · Keep the upper to a few panels. · Use contrast colour on the heel counter.

## Fabrics & materials

- **Primary:** nylon-mesh · suede · eva-foam · vulcanised-rubber
- **Secondary:** ripstop-nylon · microfibre-suede · neoprene · calfskin
- **Avoid:** patent-leather · shell-cordovan · vegetable-tanned-leather · satin · brocade
- **Rule:** Use nylon mesh and thin suede for a light, breathable upper, EVA foam for the cushion and a rubber outsole for grip. Use leather for a heavy training shoe only.

## Construction

- **Methods:** cut-and-sew nylon mesh upper · suede overlays stitched at the toe and the heel · EVA foam midsole moulded in a press · waffle outsole moulded in rubber · padded collar and tongue · stitched or bonded heel counter
- **Closures:** Flat or round laces through 7 to 8 eyelets, with a padded tongue.
- **Details:** A side stripe, a flared heel, a waffle sole, a padded collar, a reflective trim and a heel tab.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** matte · screen-printed · synthetic-dyed
- **Pattern:** Colour blocks and a side stripe in a contrast colour; fine mesh in a single bright colour.
- **Gloss:** Mesh and suede at 2–10 GU; foam and rubber at 10–30 GU.
- **Wear:** The shoe loses its cushion after 600 to 800 km; worn midsole foam and a flat tread show the end of its life.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Air cushioning (technology)

A sealed bag of gas in the midsole of a shoe, which gives a spring under the heel without a heavy foam. Origin: fashion, Beaverton, Oregon, c. 1977.

- Seal a bag of gas in a film of polyurethane.
- Set the bag in the heel of the foam midsole.
- Show the bag in a window of the midsole.

### Waffle sole (process)

A rubber sole moulded in a grid of small studs, which grip the ground and weigh little. Origin: fashion, Eugene, Oregon, c. 1971.

- Mould the rubber in a grid of studs, 3 to 5 mm high.
- Bond the sole to a foam midsole.
- Place the studs in a square grid.
