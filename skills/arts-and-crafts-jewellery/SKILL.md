# Arts and Crafts Jewellery

> A jewel is a piece of craft. The maker's hand, a modest metal and a soft stone count for more than the weight of the gold.

Hand-made jewels of silver, enamel and soft stones, from the guilds and studios of Britain after 1890. They show visible hammer marks, wire work and cabochons in place of the bright, cut stones of the shops.

**Period:** 1890–1920 · **Era:** Victorian dress · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Guild jewellery, Hand-made studio jewels of the Arts and Crafts movement

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Arts and Crafts Jewellery, you MUST:

1. Make each piece by hand from a drawing of your own, and leave the hammer marks and the file marks on the metal.
2. Choose silver, copper and low-carat gold in place of platinum and large diamonds.
3. Set cabochon stones such as moonstone, opal, turquoise and amethyst in a plain bezel.
4. Use enamel in small areas of blue, green and white, fired on silver or gold.
5. Twist silver wire and small beads into tendrils, leaves and birds, and join them with open, visible solder.
6. Draw the form from a natural plant or a medieval jewel: a hawthorn, a peacock, a pomegranate.
7. Sign the piece with the maker's mark, not a factory mark.
8. Keep each piece to one idea and to two or three materials.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A medium pendant, buckle or necklace of open work: a central drop hung in a chain of links, with small leaves and beads.
- **Line:** Soft, hand-drawn and slightly uneven; curves taken from a plant, with a small bead at each end of a wire.
- **Fit:** Worn on a loose dress, a smock or a high-waist gown, on a long chain at the chest.
- **Proportion:** A pendant is 4 to 8 cm tall on a 40 to 50 cm chain; a belt buckle is 8 to 12 cm wide.
- **Rules:**
  - Hang a pendant on a chain of hand-made links of unequal size.
  - Set a single moonstone or opal in a bezel with a wire border.
  - Apply enamel in a small panel and fire it on silver.
  - Mark each piece with the maker's initials on the back.

### Vitreous enamelling (material)

A thin coat of glass that is fused to iron or steel at about 800 °C. It makes a hard, bright, washable surface that resists heat and rust. Origin: industrial design, Central Europe, c. 1850.

- Clean the metal and coat it with a ground layer of glass powder and water.
- Fire the part at 760 to 840 °C until the glass melts into one skin.
- Apply a top coat of 0.1 to 0.3 mm in white or a single colour and fire it again.
- Leave the edges rolled: the enamel chips at a sharp edge and shows the dark metal.
