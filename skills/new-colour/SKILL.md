# New Colour

> Colour is the subject and not a decoration, and the everyday world is rich enough to need no drama.

Colour photography of the plain American scene: a car park, a kitchen, a tricycle. Saturated dye-transfer and slide colour carries the picture, and the composition looks casual and exact at the same time.

**Period:** 1970s–early 1980s · **Era:** Colour and the topographic turn · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** New Color, Colour as subject, Dye-transfer vernacular

## When to load this skill

Good for: lifestyle and fashion backdrops, editorial colour stories, retro campaign imagery, image prompts for 1970s scenes.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs monochrome or a cinematic, graded look.
- The scene is a controlled studio set.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in New Colour, you MUST:

1. Choose the subject for its colour relationships, and keep to three or four dominant hues in the frame.
2. Shoot on slide or dye-transfer stock at ISO 25 to 64 for deep, saturated colour with a fine grain.
3. Use a 35mm to 50mm lens at f/5.6 to f/11 and a low or oblique viewpoint at 1 to 3m.
4. Accept the light as found: midday sun, shop windows and fluorescent tubes, and let the mixed colour casts show.
5. Keep the composition casual but exact: put the subject off centre, with a strong colour mass in balance.
6. Show the ordinary: cars, diners, kitchens and car parks, with no hero and no punchline.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Black and white conversion or desaturated grading.
- **NEVER:** Golden hour glow and a colour cast added in the lab.
- **NEVER:** Centred hero subjects with a clear message.
- **NEVER:** Picture-postcard scenery.
- **NEVER:** Shallow depth of field that blurs the colour of the background.
- **NEVER:** A uniform filter look over the whole frame.

## Composition

- **Framing:** Square or 3:2 frame, with the subject cropped slightly by the edge and a large colour field in the frame.
- **Viewpoint:** Low, tilted or at eye height, 1 to 3m from the subject; the camera looks at the thing as an equal.
- **Geometry:** Colour blocks and horizon lines; a single diagonal of a car or a cable.
- **Subject placement:** Off centre, with a colour mass of 30–40% of the frame as a counterweight.

## Light

- **Quality:** Found light, as it is: hard sun or the flat light of a shop and a kitchen.
- **Direction:** Any; often overhead sun for saturated colour with short shadows.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 3:1 (1.6 stops)
- **Colour temperature:** 5500K (daylight); 3200–4300K for tungsten and fluorescent mix
- **Time of day:** Midday or afternoon

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** 35mm slide or colour negative; 8×10 in for dye-transfer prints
- **Focal length:** 35mm to 50mm (35mm format)
- **Aperture:** f/5.6 to f/11
- **Shutter:** 1/125s to 1/250s
- **Film speed:** ISO 25–64 (Kodachrome), ISO 100 (Kodacolor)
- **Focus:** Sharp on the subject with the background held to a depth of 3m to infinity.
- **Support:** Handheld
- **Equipment:** 35mm rangefinder or SLR · standard prime · slide film

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Deep, dense dye colours with clean whites: reds and cyans are the strongest, and shadows keep a rich colour.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Set the white point at 94% and the black point at 3%, with 18% grey at the midpoint.
  - Raise saturation by +25% and vibrance by +10%.
  - Push red hue by +4° and cyan by −3° to match dye-transfer.
  - Add a gentle S-curve of +15% contrast; keep shadows coloured and not black.
  - Keep the colour casts of mixed light; do not set a neutral white balance.
  - Add film grain, amount 12%, size 25%.
- **Grain:** Kodachrome 64: very fine, visible only at 40 × 50 cm

## Subject & staging

- **Subjects:** car park · diner · kitchen interior · tricycle · gas station · shop sign
- **Staging:** None; the photographer uses the world as it is and the first frame is often the best.
- **Direction:** People are casual and look away, or are absent.

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:**
  - Process the slide film in E-6 or K-14 at standard time.
  - Make an 8×10 dye-transfer print from the slide or negative, with a deep, glossy surface.
  - Keep the print true to the film; make no local colour corrections.
  - Mount unframed, in small sets.
- **Output:** A glossy dye-transfer or chromogenic print at 30 × 40 cm with a dense, saturated surface.
