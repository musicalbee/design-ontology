# Mumblecore

> A cheap digital camera and a few friends are enough to film the small, awkward moments of a relationship, so the film trusts the talk and not the polish.

A no-budget film about young people who talk, fail to say what they mean and drift. It uses a small digital camera, friends as actors, real rooms and half-improvised speech. The picture is plain and the mood is awkward.

**Period:** 2002–2012 · **Era:** Lightweight digital and the manifesto · **Domain:** Film & Video · **Also called:** Low-budget talk cinema, Slackavetes

- The brief needs a glossy, high-end look.
- The story needs a plot of action or spectacle.

## System directives

When planning, shooting, prompting, editing or grading moving images in Mumblecore, you MUST:

1. Shoot with a small digital camera and a crew of 2 to 5 people, with a budget under 100,000 USD.
2. Cast friends and non-professional actors, and write an outline with 50% or more of the lines improvised.
3. Film in real flats, cars and cafés with the available light, and add no lamp.
4. Frame in 16:9 or 4:3 with a 28mm to 50mm lens, and keep the camera at eye level.
5. Hold a take for 30 to 120 s, and let the pauses and the stammer stay in the cut.
6. Zoom or re-frame in the take, and let the camera hunt for the person who speaks.
7. Use a few quiet songs by friends and no score, and keep the sound of the room.

## Composition

- **Framing:** A plain 16:9 frame at eye level, with a loose, shifting medium shot; the second person often sits half out of the frame.
- **Staging in depth:** 2 planes: a speaker in the foreground and a room or a street behind, as found.
- **Subject placement:** Wherever the person sits; the frame corrects 0.3 to 1 s late.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule | Value |

## Camera & movement

- **Movement:** A handheld follow, a small zoom to find the speaker, and a long static two-shot.
- **Support:** Handheld small digital camera; a tabletop or a car seat
- **Camera height:** 1.2 to 1.7 m, eye level or seated level
- **Move speed:** Walking pace 1.0 to 1.5 m/s; a zoom over 1 to 3 s

## Lens & format

- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 or 4:3
- **Frame rate:** 24 fps or 30 fps
- **Shutter angle:** 180° (1/48 s)
- **Focal length:** a zoom lens of 28mm to 70mm equivalent
- **Aperture:** f/2.8 to f/5.6, auto exposure
- **Resolution:** standard definition DV or 720p to 1080p
- **Focus:** Autofocus, and a slow drift between two faces
- **Equipment:** a small DV or HD camcorder · an on-camera microphone · a shotgun microphone · a tripod for some scenes

## Light

- **Quality:** Whatever the room has: a window, a ceiling lamp and a screen glow; a flat look with a clipped window.
- **Direction:** From a window or a lamp in the frame.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 2:1 or lower, left as found
- **Colour temperature:** Mixed, left as found: 3200K lamps and 5600K windows
- **Source:** Windows, ceiling lamps and the screens of a laptop or a phone

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** A plain colour image straight from the camera, with a little lift in the shadows and no stylised grade.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Set the white balance to the room and do not match mixed lamps.
  - Lift the blacks to 5% and keep the contrast at 0 to +5%.
  - Keep the saturation at 90% to 100%.
  - Leave the video noise in the shadows.
  - Do not add a LUT, grain or a vignette.
- **Texture:** Standard-definition video noise and a flat, clipped window

### Handheld camera (tool)

A camera that the operator carries on the body, with no tripod or dolly; the shake of the body shows in the frame. Origin: film & video, Paris, c. 1960.

- Use a light camera and a wide lens of 18mm to 35mm so that the shake stays small in the frame.
- Follow the action at the pace of a walking person and let the frame move 2% to 5% off the subject.
- Do not stabilise in post; accept a late refocus and a missed frame as part of the take.
- Shoot long takes of 30 seconds or more, so that the camera seems to live in the room.
