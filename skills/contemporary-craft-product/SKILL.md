# Contemporary craft product

> A good object is made by a person from one real material, and it gains value with use: its marks, its tone and its slow wear are the ornament.

The handmade object of the quiet home: stoneware with a speckled glaze, solid oak with an oiled finish, washed linen and unlacquered brass, made in small batches and left with the marks of the hand.

**Period:** 2000–present · **Era:** Contemporary · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Quiet luxury objects, Japandi objects, Slow craft objects

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Contemporary craft product, you MUST:

1. Use a solid material, not a veneer: oak 25 to 50 mm thick, stoneware with a wall of 5 to 8 mm.
2. Finish with oil, soap, wax or a matte glaze; keep the gloss below 15 GU.
3. Leave one mark of the maker on each object: a throwing ring, a plane mark or a hammer facet.
4. Make each form from a simple shape with slightly uneven edges and a soft radius of 3 to 10 mm.
5. Keep to a palette of 4 or 5 warm neutrals: oat, sand, clay, bone and a soft black.
6. Use brass without lacquer, so that it darkens with the hand and the years.
7. Make in batches of 1 to 100 pieces and sign the base or the back.

## Form

- **Geometry:** The cylinder, the squat jar, the slab and the plain box, with slightly uneven edges and soft volumes.
- **Silhouette:** A calm, low and broad outline with a heavy base and a small, quiet detail on top.
- **Edges:** Soft radius of 3 to 10 mm on wood; a pulled lip and an uneven rim on a vessel; a chamfer of 2 mm on a slab.
- **Proportion:** A table top is 30 to 45 mm thick; a vase is 20 to 35 cm high with a wall of 5 to 8 mm; a low sofa seat is 38 cm high.
- **Ornament:** None applied. The grain, the glaze, the throwing rings and the weave are the only surface pattern.
- **Rules:**
  - Each object uses 1 to 3 natural materials in view.
  - Each object shows at least 1 mark of the hand or the tool.
  - A vessel has a wall of 5 mm or more and a visible foot.
  - A surface has no gloss above 15 GU, except a brass part.
  - A colour other than the colour of the material covers less than 10% of the object.

## Materials

- **Primary:** stoneware · oak · linen · solid-brass · wool · limestone
- **Secondary:** ash · walnut · cotton · jute · travertine · plaster · terracotta · washi-paper · rattan · cane · leather · bronze · boucle · bamboo · clear-glass · microcement
- **Avoid:** chrome-plated-steel · abs-plastic · plastic-laminate · pvc · acrylic · fibreglass
- **Rule:** Wood, clay, stone and cloth make about 85% of the visible surface; brass is the one metal.

## Finish & texture

- **Finishes:** oiled · soaped · waxed · matte-glaze · unglazed · unlacquered · patinated · washed
- **Textures:** open-grain · tool-marked · speckled-clay · throwing-rings · slub-weave · hammered · knurled · fine-grain
- **Gloss:** 2–12 GU on wood and glaze; 40–60 GU on aged brass
- **Patina:** The style wants a surface that wears in well: brass darkens, oak takes a soft tone, linen softens, and a worn edge is a good sign.

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** brass
- **Section:** Rods and handles 12–22 mm; a tap body 30–45 mm
- **Joints:** Dovetails, pegs and through tenons in view on solid wood; plain screws on the back only.
- **Handles:** A turned oak knob, a leather loop or a brass bar of round section.
- **Switches and taps:** A small oak or brass toggle on a wood block; a knurled brass tap on a wall-mounted spout.

## Palette

## Manufacture

- **Processes:** wheel throwing and hand glazing of stoneware · hand joinery of solid oak · plane and scraper work · hand weaving and garment washing of linen · sand casting of brass
- **Scale:** A workshop of 1 to 10 makers, with batches of 1 to 100 pieces, or a small studio with named craft partners.
- **Signs:** Throwing rings and a glaze that pools in the base, a plane mark on oak, a hand-set peg, and a maker mark on the foot.

### Exposed joinery (process)

A joint that stays in view, so that the way of making is part of the look. Origin: interior, Giza, c. 2500 BCE.

- Cut the joint in the main wood: a through-tenon of 6 to 12mm shows beyond the face.
- Peg the joint with a wooden pin of 8 to 12mm; do not hide it with filler or paint.
- Use one joint type in a room or a building; repeat it at each corner.
