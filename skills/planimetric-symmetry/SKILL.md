# Planimetric symmetry

> A film is a constructed object, so the frame is composed as a flat, symmetrical tableau and the camera moves only at right angles to it.

A frame built like a diagram: one central axis, a flat back wall, pastel colour blocks and a camera that tracks or pans at right angles. Every object is placed. The look is a miniature world with a dry joke.

**Period:** 1996–today · **Era:** Lightweight digital and the manifesto · **Domain:** Film & Video · **Also called:** Dollhouse frame, Centred tableau, Flat staging

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "wes anderson", "symmetrical shot", "planimetric", "centred composition", "dollhouse look", "pastel film", "grand budapest", "one-point symmetry".

Good for: brand films and fashion campaigns, title sequences, video prompts for a boutique or a hotel, music video with a built set.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a documentary or an observed, unplanned moment.
- The shot needs a depth of field and a hard, dramatic light.

## System directives

When planning, shooting, prompting, editing or grading moving images in Planimetric symmetry, you MUST:

1. Put the key subject on the vertical axis of the frame and mirror the set about it, so that 90% or more of the visual weight is balanced left to right.
2. Shoot head-on to the back wall, with the lens axis at 90° to it, and keep the horizon and all verticals true.
3. Move the camera only on the three right-angle axes: a track along the wall at 0.5 to 1.5 m/s, a pan of 90° or 180° at a steady rate, or a straight push-in over 3 to 6 s.
4. Limit the palette to 4 to 6 flat colours, one dominant at 40% to 50% of the frame, with the rest in blocks of 5% to 15%.
5. Stage the actors in a line or a pyramid on one or two planes, facing the lens, and give a deadpan line with no overlap.
6. Cut at an average shot length of 3 to 5 s and use a whip pan or a title card of 1 to 3 s as a chapter break.
7. Fix the frame size in advance for each part: 1.37:1, 1.85:1 or 2.39:1, and change it only at a chapter.

## Composition

- **Framing:** A centred, flat frame: the back wall fills the picture at 90° to the lens, and the set repeats left and right of the axis.
- **Staging in depth:** 2 to 3 planes parallel to the lens, 1 to 3 m apart: a front prop, the actors on one line and a flat set or wall behind.
- **Subject placement:** On the centre axis, or in a pair about it; the eyes sit on the upper third line and look into or across the lens.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule | Value |
|---|---|---|
| symmetry-axis | Place the subject on the vertical axis and mirror the set about it, with doors, windows and props in pairs. | bilateral, 90% to 100% mirrored mass, 1 axis |
| framing | Square the lens to the back wall and keep every vertical plumb, so that the frame reads as a flat plan. | 1 frame plane, lens axis at 90° to the wall, 0° tilt |
| colour-distribution | Fill the frame with one dominant flat colour and 3 to 5 accents in small blocks. | 40% to 50% dominant, accents 5% to 15% each |
| layering-depth | Stage the actors on one or two planes parallel to the lens, with a flat wall behind. | 2 to 3 planes, 1 to 3 m apart |
| leading-lines | Let floor lines, rails and shelves run at right angles to the lens axis, and meet at the central axis. | lines at 0° or 90° only |

## Camera & movement

- **Movement:** Right-angle moves only: a lateral track along the wall, a 90° or 180° pan, a straight push-in or a pull-out. No handheld follow, no diagonal.
- **Support:** Dolly on rails, tripod with a fluid head, cable cam for a cross-section shot
- **Camera height:** 1.2 to 1.5 m, at the actors' chest and eye level
- **Move speed:** Track 0.5 to 1.5 m/s; pan 20° to 40° per second; push-in over 3 to 6 s

## Lens & format

- **Aspect ratio:** 1.37:1, 1.85:1 or 2.39:1, fixed for each chapter
- **Frame rate:** 24 fps; 12 fps or 24 fps stop-motion for the miniature set
- **Shutter angle:** 180° (1/48 s)
- **Focal length:** 25mm to 40mm (35mm format); a wide lens at a distance for flat planes
- **Aperture:** f/4 to f/8 for deep, even focus across the planes
- **Resolution:** 35mm or 4K digital; a clean, sharp image
- **Focus:** Deep focus on all planes; the front prop and the back wall are both sharp.
- **Equipment:** rail dolly · wide prime set · cable cam · miniature and scale models

## Light

- **Quality:** Soft, even and frontal, with a low contrast and a bright fill, as in a storybook illustration.
- **Direction:** From the front and above, close to the lens axis; no deep shadows on the back wall.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 2:1 (1 stop)
- **Colour temperature:** Daylight 5600K or warm tungsten 3200K, with a gel to match the set
- **Source:** Large soft boxes and bounce at the camera

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** A pastel, flat palette in blocks. Skin stays warm, blacks are lifted, and one dominant hue colours each chapter.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Lift the black point to 6% to 8% so that the shadows read as flat colour.
  - Set the highlights at 92% to 95% and keep a low contrast of −10% with a soft S-curve.
  - Push saturation of the dominant hue by +20% and mute the other hues by −10%.
  - Warm the whites to 5200K and add a 3% to 5% pink in the midtones.
  - Keep grain at 5% to 8%; do not add a vignette or a flare.
- **Texture:** Fine grain at 5% to 8%, clean flat colour with no digital noise

## Editing & rhythm

- **Average shot length:** 3 to 5 s (estimate; check Cinemetrics for each title)
- **Shot length range:** 1 s for an insert to 12 s for a long lateral track
- **Cutting logic:** Cut between flat tableaux on the beat of the line. Hold the frame until the joke lands, then whip or cut to the next.
- **Transitions:** Hard cut, whip pan of 0.2 to 0.4 s, a title card as a chapter break of 1 to 3 s, an iris in or out.
- **Runtime:** 90 to 110 min

## Sound

- **Approach:** Clean, close dialogue with exact foley for each prop. Rooms are quiet, and the sound of an object is a gag.
- **Dialogue:** Deadpan, a line at a time, with no overlap and a pause of 0.5 to 1 s before the reply.
- **Music:** A pop or classical song picked for each chapter, and a harpsichord or marimba theme; each cue starts on a cut.
- **Loudness:** −23 LUFS integrated, with dialogue peaks at −6 dBFS

## Titles & graphics

- **Treatment:** Chapter cards and a narrator's text in a centred block on a flat colour, with an inset or a drawn map as a graphic.
- **Type:** A geometric or slab serif in upper case, centred, one colour from the palette; each card stays for 1 to 3 s.

### Perspective grid (process)

Lines that run to one vanishing point on a horizon, so that a flat surface reads as a floor in deep space. Origin: painting, Florence, c. 1415.

- Put the horizon at 50% to 65% of the height and draw 12 to 24 lines to one vanishing point.
- Space the cross lines so that each gap is about 0.8 of the gap in front of it.
