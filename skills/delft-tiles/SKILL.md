# Delft tiles

> A cheap square tile and a quick brush make a clean, washable wall that still holds a small picture in each unit.

Small square tin-glazed tiles that Dutch potteries made by the million for walls, kitchens and fireplaces. Each tile holds one small picture in blue or purple and a corner motif, and a set of tiles makes a wall.

**Period:** 1620–1800 · **Era:** The porcelain race · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Dutch wall tiles, Delftse tegels, Dutch blue and white tiles

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Delft tiles, you MUST:

1. Keep the tile square, 13 cm by 13 cm, and 5 to 8 mm thick, so that all tiles fit one wall.
2. Paint one small subject in the centre: a ship, a child at play, a flower, an animal or a soldier.
3. Add a corner motif of 4 parts, the ox head, the spider or the fleur-de-lis, to join four tiles in a pattern.
4. Paint in cobalt blue on the raw tin glaze; use manganese purple for a second colour in a few sets.
5. Draw the subject with a pricked paper pattern, then fill the shapes with a loose wash.
6. Set the tiles in a grid with joints under 2 mm, and add a plinth tile of 13 by 6.5 cm at the floor.
7. Use biblical scenes on fireplace walls, and bright flowers on the walls of the kitchen and the dairy.
8. Fire each tile in a stack with stilts; accept the small marks of the stilts on the edge.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Square wall tiles, plinth tiles, fireplace tiles, tile tableaux of flower vases and tulips, and small plaques.
- **Profile:** A flat thin face with a thick glaze that swells slightly at the edge; the tile edge is bevelled and soft.
- **Foot and rim:** The back is rough with a mortar key; the glaze runs over the edge and leaves a thin lip on the face.
- **Proportion:** A tile is 13 cm square and 5 to 8 mm thick; a fireplace wall uses 100 or more tiles.
- **Rules:**
  - Make every tile the same size, so that they exchange.
  - Put the subject in a field of about 8 cm, and leave a white margin.
  - Place the corner motif in the same colour as the subject.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · bone-china
- **Rule:** Use a buff earthenware from mixed local and imported clay. It fires soft at a low heat and takes a thick tin glaze. Keep porcelain out.

## Forming

- **Methods:** rolling a slab of clay · cutting the tile with a wooden template · drying the tile in a rack · pricking a paper pattern and pouncing it · painting on the raw glaze
- **Joins:** Tiles are set in lime mortar on the wall, with a plinth tile at the floor line.
- **Details:** Pounce dots along the drawn line, three stilt marks on the face, a thin edge lip of glaze and a small mark of the pottery on the back.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** tin-glaze · lead-glaze
- **Decorations:** underglaze-cobalt · underglaze-polychrome
- **Pattern:** One small subject in the centre of each tile in cobalt blue, with an ox head or spider corner motif; some sets use manganese purple.
- **Gloss:** Soft gloss, about 50 to 75 GU.
- **Wear:** The thick glaze chips at the corners and shows the buff body; the blue stays fresh and the white turns a little grey.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Cobalt blue on a milk-white ground; manganese purple, and later a little yellow and green, as the only extra colours.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A wood-fired kiln, later a coal-fired kiln, with the tiles in stacks.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 980 °C · dipping in tin glaze · painting on the raw glaze · glaze firing in stacks
- **Scale:** Some 30 potteries in the Delft area made millions of tiles in a year, and painters worked at piece rates.
- **Signs:** Stilt marks, a thin edge lip of glaze and a slight blur of the blue.

### Pounce transfer (process)

A drawing on paper is pricked along its lines and dabbed with a bag of charcoal dust, so that dots of the design go onto the surface below. Origin: painting, Florence, Italy, c. 1400.

- Draw the design at full size on paper.
- Prick holes along each line, 2 to 3 mm apart.
- Lay the sheet on the surface and dab it with a bag of charcoal or graphite dust.
- Paint over the dots; the dust burns away in the kiln or wipes off.
