# Victorian boot

> A Victorian woman covers the ankle, so the boot is the shoe of every day and the cloth upper shows the cut of the last.

The ankle boot of Victorian dress. It has a kid or cloth upper, a row of buttons or a lace front, an elastic side gusset, and a small stacked heel.

**Period:** 1851–1914 · **Era:** Victorian dress · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Button boot, Elastic-sided boot, Balmoral boot

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Victorian boot, you MUST:

1. Cover the ankle: raise the shaft 12 to 20 cm above the heel.
2. Close the shaft with 8 to 14 small buttons, or with a lace front of 8 to 10 eyelets.
3. Set a 2 to 4 cm stacked heel under the waist of the sole.
4. Cut a narrow, slightly pointed toe on a last made for a left and a right foot.
5. Add a toe cap of patent leather or kid in a second colour for day wear.
6. Insert an elastic gusset of vulcanised rubber webbing in each side for a slip-on boot.
7. Stitch the sole to the upper by welt or by pegs; do not glue it.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A close boot that wraps the foot and the ankle, with a narrow toe and a small heel under a long skirt.
- **Line:** A straight shaft from the ankle bone to the calf, a gentle arch, and a toe that points slightly.
- **Fit:** Snug at the ankle, held by the buttons or the elastic gusset; the vamp grips the instep.
- **Proportion:** Shaft height about 1.5 times the length of the heel to toe line of the vamp; heel 2 to 4 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the boot narrow, so that the skirt hem hides all but the toe.
  - Place the buttons on the outside of the shaft, in a close row.
  - Make the toe cap and the heel in the same colour or in one contrast colour.
  - Wear the boot with a long skirt that clears the ground by 2 to 5 cm.

## Fabrics & materials

- **Primary:** kidskin · serge · calfskin
- **Secondary:** patent-leather · vegetable-tanned-leather · vulcanised-rubber · suede
- **Avoid:** eva-foam · pvc-leathercloth · abs-plastic · ripstop-nylon
- **Rule:** Use fine kid for the upper of a dress boot and wool serge for a walking boot with a leather toe cap. Use a leather sole. Rubber appears only in the elastic gusset.

## Construction

- **Methods:** lasting over a wooden last for each foot · pegged or welted sole · stitched toe cap · bound buttonholes on the shaft · elastic gusset set into the side seam · stacked leather heel built in layers
- **Closures:** A row of covered or metal buttons closed with a button hook; or a lace front; or an elastic side gusset with a pull tab.
- **Details:** A patent toe cap, a stitched vamp seam, a cloth top in a contrast colour, and a lining of linen or kid.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** burnished · patent-lacquered · matte · embroidered · quilted
- **Pattern:** Plain leather in one colour; a perforated toe cap or a stitched pattern on the vamp for display.
- **Gloss:** Kid and serge at 5–20 GU; patent toe caps at 70–90 GU.
- **Wear:** A boot gains creases at the ankle and a scuffed toe with age. Clean polish keeps it neat, and a worn boot looks respectable but old.

### Elastic gusset (material)

A side panel of rubber webbing set into the shaft of a boot, so that the boot slips on without laces or buttons. Origin: fashion, London, England, c. 1837.

- Set a panel of rubber webbing, 4 to 6 cm wide, into each side of the shaft.
- Cover the seam with a binding in matching cloth or leather.
- Add a pull tab at the top of the back seam.
