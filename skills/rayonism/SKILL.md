# Rayonism

> What the eye sees is the sum of the rays of light that objects throw back, so the painter draws the crossing of the rays and not the objects.

Pictures built from lines that stand for rays of light thrown back by objects. The rays cross in sharp, coloured beams, and the object breaks into a spray of lines in a Cubist and Futurist space.

**Period:** 1910–1914 · **Era:** Avant-garde · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Rayism, Luchism

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "rayonism", "rayonist", "rayism", "larionov", "goncharova rayonist", "rays of light as lines", "russian avant-garde 1913".

Good for: poster and cover art with a sense of speed, music and event branding, editorial illustration of light and energy, pattern and textile with a sharp line.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a calm, soft or warm mood.
- The picture must show a clear likeness or a tidy diagram.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Rayonism, you MUST:

1. Draw the rays as straight, narrow lines of 3–15mm, in a fan or a bundle that crosses the picture.
2. Cross the rays at 10–45°, and let the form appear where two or more rays meet.
3. Set the ray lines in contrasting colours, one warm against one cool, over a ground of broken colour.
4. Break the object into 8–20 shards with a sharp edge, and keep the shards in motion along the rays.
5. Keep the space flat, with 2–3 layers of overlapping beams, and avoid a vanishing point.
6. Show a little of the subject, such as a forest, a street or a fish, so that the eye finds it in the beams.
7. Paint with a dry, quick stroke so that the beams have a feathered edge.

## Composition

- **Layout:** A fan of straight ray lines that cross at the centre, at angles of 10–45° from the vertical, with small shards of the object caught in the beams.
- **Focal point:** The crossing of the strongest rays, within the central 40% of the canvas.
- **Format:** A canvas at 4:5 to 3:2; the rays run off the edge, so that the picture seems to go on.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| leading-lines | Run 15–40 ray lines across the picture, all in 2–3 main directions, to lead the eye to the crossing. |
| rhythm | Repeat a ray at an interval of 10–30mm, with the angle shifting by 3–8° from one bundle to the next. |
| contrast | Set a warm ray against a cool one at each crossing, with a value gap of 30% or more. |
| layering-depth | Reduce the depth to 2–3 layers of overlapping beams, with no horizon. |
| focal-point | Put the densest crossing at one third from an edge, in the sharpest contrast. |
| balance | Balance the main fan by a lighter bundle on the other diagonal, at about one third of its mass. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Bright contrasting beams of warm and cool colour over a ground of broken earth and green, with dark accents to hold the crossings.
- **Distribution:** A dominant hue family 40–50%, its contrast 25–35%, earth and neutral 15–20%, dark accent under 5%.
- **Ground:** A broken ground of earth, grey and green that shows between the beams.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint, matt, with a lean medium so that the stroke stays dry.
- **Support:** Canvas, often a medium size.
- **Brushwork:** Long, narrow, straight strokes of 3–15mm for each ray; dry brush for the feathered edge; short dabs in the ground.
- **Paint handling:** 2–3 layers of opaque colour; the rays go over the ground, and they show as a ridge of 1–2mm.
- **Edges:** Sharp at the line of the ray, soft and feathered where two beams cross.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** Light is the subject: each ray is a line of light thrown back by an object.
- **Modelling:** By the crossing of the rays, and not by shading; the form is the place where the rays meet.
- **Shadow:** No cast shadow; a dark ray or a deep blue beam stands for the shadow.
- **Temperature:** Warm and cool rays set at each crossing, so that the crossing flashes.

## Subject

- **Typical:** A forest, a city street, a glass, a fish, a portrait, a beam of light, caught in a net of rays.
- **Figure scale:** The main motif fills 40–80% of the picture, but is hard to read at first.
- **Treatment:** The scene is seen as energy; the mood is fast, hard and bright, and the object is hidden in the rays.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Matt, with no varnish on the dry paint.
- **Texture:** Dry and streaked, with ridged ray lines over a flat ground.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Diagonal composition (motif)

The main forms lie on a tilted axis, so that the picture seems to move and to cut across the field. Origin: painting, Petrograd, 1915.

- Set the main axis at 30° to 60° to the frame edge; 45° is the strongest.
- Let a large form and a small form lie on the same diagonal at a ratio of 4:1 or more.
- Use one saturated colour (red) against a white or black field.
- Leave a clear field around the diagonal: at least 50% of the area stays empty.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Donkey's Tail** (crew, Moscow, 1911–1914), exhibited the style. A Moscow group of the most radical painters who broke with the Jack of Diamonds. Larionov led it. It showed folk art and child-like pictures, and it ended with its last show in 1914. Larionov announced Rayonism at the Target show in 1913.
