# Orphism

> Colour alone can build the form and the movement of a picture, so the painter makes new wholes from pure colour and no longer shows the object.

Cubist structure with the bright colour of the Fauves, pushed toward pure abstraction. Discs and arcs of pure colour set side by side make rhythm and light, and the subject melts away.

**Period:** 1912–1914 · **Era:** Avant-garde · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Orphic Cubism, Simultanism

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "orphism", "orphic cubism", "simultanism", "delaunay", "kupka", "simultaneous contrast", "discs of colour", "rings of colour".

Good for: poster and cover art with a bright rhythm, textile, fashion and pattern design, music and dance branding, lyrical abstract illustration.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a calm, muted or tonal mood.
- The picture must show a clear object or a likeness.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Orphism, you MUST:

1. Build the picture from arcs and discs of pure colour, each 50–300mm across, in overlapping rings.
2. Set complementary colours side by side in each ring, so that the contrast makes the colour vibrate.
3. Break the object into planes as in Cubism, then drop the object when the colour is able to carry the form.
4. Repeat a circle or an arc 5–12 times, with the colour shifting by one step each time, to give the eye a beat.
5. Keep the space shallow, with 2–3 layers, and let the discs overlap so that they appear to move.
6. Use a bright, clear colour in the full spectrum, and keep black to a minimum.
7. Take the subject from the modern world, such as a tower, a window, a city or an aeroplane, and then dissolve it.

## Composition

- **Layout:** A central burst of overlapping discs and arcs, with a few straight planes of a broken object behind them, on a hidden grid at a slant of 10–30°.
- **Focal point:** The brightest ring, a clash of complementary colours, within the central 40% of the canvas.
- **Format:** A square or near-square canvas of 1:1 to 4:5, or a tall panel; the rings may run to the edge.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| rhythm | Repeat a disc or an arc 5–12 times, with each ring 10–25% smaller or one step brighter than the last. |
| contrast | Set complementary pairs side by side in 60% or more of the rings, at full chroma and a value gap of 30%. |
| layering-depth | Reduce the depth to 2–3 overlapping layers, with no horizon and no vanishing point. |
| focal-point | Place the strongest ring within the central 40% and make it 25–40% of the picture width. |
| colour-distribution | Spread 4–5 hues across the picture, each 15–25%, and keep the neutrals under 10%. |
| balance | Balance the burst by a smaller group of arcs on the opposite diagonal, at about one third of its mass. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** The full spectrum at high chroma, in complementary pairs, with white and a little black or deep blue to hold the rings.
- **Distribution:** Four or five hues each 15–25%, white or pale ground 10–15%, dark under 5%.
- **Ground:** A white or pale ground that shows between the rings.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint, thin and clear, with little medium.
- **Support:** Primed canvas, often large, in a square or near-square format.
- **Brushwork:** Even, short strokes of 5–15mm that fill each ring; the stroke follows the arc and does not show as gesture.
- **Paint handling:** 1–2 coats of opaque or semi-opaque colour, 0.5–1mm thick; the rings are painted next to each other and are not blended.
- **Edges:** Clean edges between colours, with no dark outline; the contrast of the colours makes the edge.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** Light comes from the colour: a bright ring stands for a flash, a sun or an electric lamp.
- **Modelling:** By a step of colour from warm to cool across a ring, in 3–5 steps, and not by grey.
- **Shadow:** No cast shadow; a ring is darker only through a cool or a complementary colour of the same value.
- **Temperature:** Warm and cool rings set next to each other so that the picture seems to pulse.

## Subject

- **Typical:** A tower, a window on a city, a disc of light, a dancer, an aeroplane, an abstract rhythm.
- **Figure scale:** The main motif fills 50–90% of the picture and is hard to name.
- **Treatment:** The motif begins in the modern world and then dissolves in colour; the mood is lyrical and bright.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Matt to satin.
- **Texture:** Smooth, even coats with a faint weave.

### Simultaneous contrast (process, invented here)

Pure colours set side by side so that each one raises the force of the other, with the picture built from the contrast alone. Origin: painting, Paris, France, 1912.

- Set a complementary pair side by side in each ring or plane.
- Keep each colour pure and unmixed, at full chroma.
- Repeat the pair in steps of colour, so that the picture beats like a rhythm.
