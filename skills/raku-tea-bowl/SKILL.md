# Raku tea bowl

> A tea bowl need not be thrown, round or bright: hand-built, a little uneven and fired fast, it can calm the mind and ask nothing of the eye.

Hand-built, low-fired tea bowls that Chōjirō made in Kyoto for the tea master Sen no Rikyū. The potter pulls each bowl from the hot kiln, and the glaze is black or red.

**Period:** 1580–1700 · **Era:** Blue and white · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Raku ware, Black raku, Red raku, Chōjirō tea bowl

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Raku tea bowl, you MUST:

1. Build the bowl by hand from a coil or a pinched ball of clay; do not use the wheel.
2. Cut the shape with a bamboo knife until the wall is 8 to 12 mm thick and fits the palm.
3. Make the bowl sit low and heavy, with a flat or slightly concave base.
4. Cover the body with one lead glaze, black or red, in one colour.
5. Fire each bowl alone in a small kiln at 800 to 1000 °C and pull it out while hot.
6. Leave a trace of the hand: a dent, a spatula cut or a slight lean.
7. Make a black bowl for the tea master and a red bowl for the guest.
8. Keep the bowl plain, so that the tea and the room are the show.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** A short cylinder or a rounded bowl with a wide mouth, made to hold in two hands. The wall is thick, and the mouth may be slightly oval.
- **Profile:** A low, straight or gently curved wall rises from a flat base; the lip turns in a little, and one side may be higher.
- **Foot and rim:** A low, wide foot cut by hand and left unglazed; the rim is uneven and rounded, and the glaze may stop above the foot.
- **Proportion:** Diameter 10 to 12 cm and height 7 to 9 cm; the wall is 8 to 12 mm thick and the bowl is heavy for its size.
- **Rules:**
  - Build the shape by hand and keep it a little asymmetric.
  - Keep the wall thick, so that the hand does not burn on hot tea.
  - Glaze in one colour, black or red; add no painted pattern.
  - Cut the foot by hand and leave a small area of bare clay.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** raku-clay
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · bone-china
- **Rule:** Use an open, grogged clay that takes the shock of the hot kiln. Keep a dense stoneware or a porcelain body out: it cracks when pulled hot.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pinching a ball of clay into a bowl · building up the wall with coils · cutting and shaping with a bamboo knife · carving the foot ring by hand when leather-hard · drying slowly in the shade
- **Joins:** The bowl is one piece; a coil join is smoothed into the wall and cannot be seen.
- **Details:** Knife marks on the wall, a dent made by the thumb, an uneven rim and a hand-cut foot with tongs marks left by the kiln.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** raku-glaze · bare-clay
- **Decorations:** undecorated · incised
- **Pattern:** One black or red lead glaze, matt to satin; a few bowls carry a spatula cut or an incised line, and a rare piece has a white glaze.
- **Gloss:** Matt to satin, about 20 to 70 GU; black raku is a little brighter, red raku is softer.
- **Wear:** The glaze wears at the rim and shows the clay body. Tea stains the glaze and the crackles.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** One tone for each bowl: deep black, brick red, or a buff white. The clay shows at the foot.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A small kiln for one or two bowls, fed with charcoal; the potter opens it and pulls the glowing bowl out with iron tongs.
- **Atmosphere:** raku
- **Processes:** bisque firing at about 800 °C · dipping in lead glaze · short glaze firing of one bowl at a time · pulling the bowl out of the hot kiln with tongs · cooling in the open air
- **Scale:** One workshop in Kyoto, from 1580; one potter made a few bowls in each firing.
- **Signs:** A mark where the tongs gripped the glaze, fine crackles and soft colour variation from the short firing.

### Raku firing (process)

A short, hot firing of one small piece. The potter opens the kiln and pulls the glowing piece out with tongs. Origin: ceramics, Kyoto, Japan, c. 1580.

- Bisque fire the hand-built piece to about 800 °C.
- Dip it in a lead glaze.
- Fire it alone in a small kiln until the glaze melts, at 800 to 1000 °C.
- Pull it out with tongs and let it cool in the air.
