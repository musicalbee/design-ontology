# Nordic Modernism

> Modern architecture should serve the whole person: use natural materials, scarce daylight and a human scale to make function warm.

Modern architecture softened for a cold, dark climate: white plaster and brick, warm timber, daylight shaped with care and plans that bend to the site. Alvar Aalto, Arne Jacobsen and Sigurd Lewerentz made function humane.

**Period:** 1930s–1970s · **Era:** Modern Movement · **Domain:** Architecture · **Also called:** Scandinavian modernism, Nordic Classicism to Functionalism, Aalto modernism

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "nordic modernism", "scandinavian architecture", "alvar aalto", "arne jacobsen", "paimio", "villa mairea", "bent plywood", "functionalism", "finnish architecture".

Good for: houses and cabins, libraries, schools and hospitals, cultural centres, interior-led residential renders.

Do not use it when:
- The brief asks for a monumental, sculptural or high-rise object.
- The brief asks for strict machine-like repetition and no natural material.

## System directives

When designing, describing or rendering buildings and interiors in Nordic Modernism, you MUST:

1. Bend the plan to the site and the sun: use wings, fans and courtyards at angles of 15 to 45° and not a rigid box.
2. Use natural materials in honest form: brick, timber and copper beside white plaster, with a tactile detail at every touch point.
3. Treat daylight as scarce: tall, deep-set windows, roof lights and light-coloured surfaces that spread low sun into the room.
4. Keep the scale human: ceiling heights of 2.4 to 3m in the rooms, and low massing of 2 to 4 storeys.
5. Design the fittings with the building: door handles, lamps, chairs and stairs in bent plywood and brass.
6. Join inside and outside with a terrace, a garden room or a courtyard, and let the landscape come into the plan.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Cold, rigid, glass-and-steel boxes without a human scale.
- **NEVER:** Heavy applied ornament or historical pastiche.
- **NEVER:** Large, undivided open-plan halls with no warm material.
- **NEVER:** Large unshaded west-facing glazing in a low-sun climate.
- **NEVER:** Synthetic, glossy finishes that hide the material.
- **NEVER:** Plans that ignore site, slope, trees and orientation.

## Massing & form

- **Form:** Low, articulated wings or a fan in plan, with a main block and lower service bars, set into the contour of the site.
- **Proportion:** Long and low: 2 to 4 storeys, with wings 3 to 6 times longer than they are tall.
- **Roof:** A flat or very low-pitched roof with deep eaves, or a gentle curved roof in copper or turf.
- **Relation to ground:** The building steps with the slope; terraces, stone walls and planting join it to the ground.

## Structure

- **System:** Reinforced concrete column frames with brick or plastered infill; timber for roofs and canopies.
- **Span and module:** Column grids of 5 to 7.5m; ceilings of 2.4 to 3m; stair and window modules of 600mm.
- **Expression:** The frame is quiet; the material of the wall and the detail of the joint carry the expression.

## Materials

- **Primary:** white plaster · brick · timber (pine, birch) · copper
- **Secondary:** bent plywood · brass · ceramic tile · granite
- **Finish:** Matt and tactile: whitewashed or tinted plaster, oiled wood, hand-laid brick, patinated metal.

## Facade

- **Composition:** Plain plastered or brick surfaces, with window bands set to the room use and one accent of timber or tile.
- **Openings:** Deep-set ribbon and corner windows sized for the sun path; the window-to-wall ratio is 25 to 40%.
- **Solid to void:** Solid leads: 60–75% opaque, with warm material in the reveals.

## Light & space

- **Daylight:** Low sun is brought deep into the plan by light wells, roof lights and pale surfaces; lamps give a warm local light.
- **Spatial sequence:** A compact, welcoming entry, then a stair or hall that turns and opens to the garden or courtyard.
- **Interior character:** White walls, wood ceilings, a brick or tile feature, curved plywood furniture and warm pendant lamps.

## Ornament

- **Attitude:** Ornament comes from material and craft: no applied pattern, but the grain, the joint and the form of the handle.
- **Details:** bent plywood · brass handles · wood slat ceilings · coloured tile · rope-wrapped columns · pendant lamps

### Roof light (process)

An opening in the roof that lets daylight fall into a room from above. Origin: architecture, Rome, Italy, c. 125.

- Make each opening 1% to 5% of the floor area under it.
- Splay the sides of a deep opening, so that the light spreads before it reaches the floor.
- Put the roof light over the place of use: a reading table, a stair or the centre of a hall.
