# Early mountaineering

> Warmth comes from many thin layers, and wind is the enemy. The climber wears wool next to the skin and one tight cotton shell on top.

The dress of the climber before nylon: layers of wool, silk and flannel under a windproof gabardine smock, with tweed breeches, puttees and nailed boots.

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Early mountaineering, you MUST:

1. Wear 4 to 6 thin layers, so that the climber can add or shed one at each rest.
2. Put wool or silk next to the skin, to hold heat when wet.
3. Close the outer smock with a deep hood and a drawcord at the hem.
4. Weave the outer cloth tight enough to stop a 40 km/h wind.
5. Cut the breeches full at the thigh and tight at the knee, over a wool puttee.
6. Cut the sleeve long, with a wrist cuff that closes over the glove.
7. Stitch the shoulder and the elbow with a double seam.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A long, loose smock with a hood over many layers, tweed breeches that gather at the knee, and a puttee and a heavy boot.
- **Line:** A hooded neck, a loose body to mid-thigh, and a tight cuff at the wrist and the knee.
- **Fit:** Loose in the body to take the layers; close at the wrist, the knee and the ankle to keep the wind out.
- **Proportion:** Smock to mid-thigh; breeches end at the knee; roughly 1 to 1.3 chest to hip with the layers on.
- **Rules:**
  - Use a long hooded smock over the layers.
  - Seal the wrist with a close cuff and the waist with a drawcord.
  - Gather the breeches at the knee over a wool puttee.
  - Wear a wool cap or balaclava under the hood.
  - Carry spare wool mitts inside the smock.

### Layering system (skill, invented here)

A set of thin layers, each with one task. A base next to the skin, a warm mid layer and a shell against wind and rain. The wearer adds or sheds a layer to fit the work. Origin: fashion, Himalaya, c. 1924.

- Put a base layer next to the skin that moves sweat away.
- Add a mid layer of wool or fleece to hold warm air.
- Close the outfit with a shell that stops wind and rain, and open it when the work warms the body.

### Yarn proofing (process)

A rain cloth made from yarn that is proofed before it is woven, so that the cloth sheds water and the body can still breathe. Origin: fashion, Basingstoke, England, 1879.

- Proof the cotton or wool yarn with a water-repellent finish before the weave.
- Weave the yarn in a steep, tight twill.
- Dye the cloth in khaki or stone, and cut it into a coat.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Alpine Club** (collective, London, 1857–now), gathered the style. The first mountaineering club of the world. Its members and its expedition committees planned the Everest parties of the 1920s.
- **Burberry outfitters** (company, Basingstoke, England, 1856–now), made the style. The outfitters of Thomas Burberry. They made gabardine in 1879 and sold the officer's trench coat and the windproof smock of the Everest parties.
