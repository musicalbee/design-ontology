# Fireclay butler sink

> A kitchen sink must be deep, heavy and glazed so hard that a pot, a brush and boiling water do not mark it.

A deep, square, white sink cast in fireclay. Thick walls, a rolled rim and a weir overflow make a sink that takes heat and weight, and it stays in use in kitchens today.

**Period:** 1880 to now · **Era:** Art pottery and reform · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Belfast sink, London sink, Fireclay kitchen sink

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Fireclay butler sink, you MUST:

1. Cast the sink in one piece of fireclay, with a wall 20 to 30 mm thick.
2. Make the basin 450 to 600 mm deep in front, so that a large pan fits flat.
3. Cut a weir overflow, a slot 40 mm wide, at the back of the Belfast type.
4. Keep the inner corners rounded with a radius of 25 to 40 mm, so that dirt does not stay.
5. Glaze every face in white, and glaze the inside twice for a hard surface.
6. Roll the front rim into a bead 30 mm wide, which makes a clean edge.
7. Set the sink on two brick piers or cantilever brackets, with a gap of about 20 mm to the wall.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** A deep rectangular sink in two types: the London sink with a flat rim and the Belfast sink with a weir overflow.
- **Profile:** Straight, vertical walls and a flat floor with a slight fall to the waste outlet; the front is plain and square.
- **Foot and rim:** A rolled front rim and a flat back with a tap ledge, and a waste hole of 40 to 50 mm in a recess.
- **Proportion:** Typical size 600 by 460 mm, or 760 by 455 mm, and 250 mm deep; weight 40 to 60 kg.
- **Rules:**
  - Make the front thick and plain, with no ornament.
  - Slope the floor 1 in 30 to the waste, so that water does not stand.
  - Leave a flat shelf at the back for 2 taps or a swan neck tap.
  - Keep the colour white or off-white.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** fireclay
- **Secondary:** stoneware · brass · wood
- **Avoid:** vitreous-china · bone-china · stainless-steel · plastic
- **Rule:** Use a coarse buff fireclay with grog, which holds a thick cast without warping. The glaze hides the body on the face.

## Forming

- **Methods:** slip casting in plaster moulds · hand fettling of the weir and the waste · press moulding of thick parts · dry and sponge the glaze face · hand spraying of glaze
- **Joins:** The sink is one casting; the taps and the waste are fitted on site with brass parts and a putty seal.
- **Details:** A glaze-free foot, a coarse buff back, a maker's stamp on the back, and a small thick drip line under the rim.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** sanitary-glaze · salt-glaze · clear-feldspathic
- **Decorations:** undecorated · relief-moulding
- **Pattern:** No pattern. A moulded maker name or size on the back face is the only mark.
- **Gloss:** High gloss, about 75 to 90 GU, on the white faces.
- **Wear:** The glaze crazes at the weir and the corners, and a grey mark of pots appears on the floor of the sink in use.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** White glaze on the face, buff body on the back and base, and dark brass on the tap.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A coal-fired down-draught kiln, later a gas tunnel kiln, with long and slow cooling.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** cast and dry for days · spray or dip glaze · fire once at 1200 to 1300 °C · cool for 2 days · grind the base flat · pack for transport
- **Scale:** A factory makes some hundreds of sinks in a week, and a large kiln takes 20 to 30 at a time.
- **Signs:** A flat ground base, small stilt marks on the edge, and a thick glaze at the inner corners.

### Slip casting (process)

Liquid clay is poured into a plaster mould. The plaster takes water from the clay and a wall forms on the inside of the mould. Origin: ceramics, Staffordshire, England, c. 1745.

- Make a plaster mould in two or more parts.
- Pour slip into the mould and wait 10 to 20 minutes for a wall of 3 to 5 mm.
- Pour out the rest of the slip.
- Open the mould when the clay is leather-hard and fettle the seam.
