# Quiet luxury fashion

> Real luxury is quiet. It shows in the touch of the cloth and the fit of the cut, and only a person who knows will see it.

The logo-free, high-cost dress of the 2000s and after. It uses cashmere, loden and fine wool in neutral colours, with perfect cut and hand finish. The price is in the cloth and not in a mark.

**Period:** 2006–present · **Era:** Contemporary · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Stealth wealth, Old money dress, Logo-free luxury, Understated luxury

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Quiet luxury fashion, you MUST:

1. Use no logo and no monogram on the outside of the garment.
2. Choose cloth of the highest grade: cashmere of 14 to 16 micron, fine wool, loden and silk.
3. Keep to neutral colours: camel, oatmeal, navy, grey, ivory and chocolate.
4. Cut a relaxed, long and straight line, with a soft shoulder and room in the body.
5. Finish by hand: linked knit seams, hand-set buttons and a fine hem.
6. Make each garment last 10 years or more, in a classic form.
7. Build the outfit from a few pieces that mix in one palette.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A relaxed, long and soft rectangle: a roomy coat, wide trousers, a fine knit and a loose shirt, in one tone.
- **Line:** A soft, natural shoulder, a straight body, and a hem at the calf or the ankle.
- **Fit:** Easy and generous; the cloth drapes on the body with no strain.
- **Proportion:** Coat to mid-calf; trouser leg 22 to 26 cm at the hem; knit hem at the hip; sleeve length to the wrist bone.
- **Rules:**
  - Cut a soft shoulder with no pad.
  - Keep the coat long and the trouser wide and straight.
  - Wear a fine knit under a coat, in a tone close to it.
  - Match the palette of the whole outfit within one family.
  - Choose a plain leather shoe or a loafer and a bag with no mark.

### Fully fashioned knitting (process)

A way to knit each part of a garment to shape, and then to join the parts by linking stitch to stitch, so that the seam is flat. Origin: fashion, Loughborough, England, c. 1864.

- Knit each panel to its final shape, with the shaping marked by decreases on the edge.
- Join the panels by linking stitch to stitch on a linking machine or by hand.
- Wash and block the piece so that the seams lie flat.
