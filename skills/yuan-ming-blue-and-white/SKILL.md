# Yuan and Ming blue-and-white porcelain

> Cobalt blue painted straight on white porcelain and sealed under a clear glaze gives a lasting picture that cannot rub or fade.

White porcelain painted in cobalt blue under a clear glaze. Jingdezhen potters made it in the Yuan dynasty and refined it for the Ming court into the best-known ceramic of the world.

**Period:** 1330–1644 · **Era:** Medieval glaze · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Jingdezhen blue and white, Qinghua porcelain, Chinese blue-and-white

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Yuan and Ming blue-and-white porcelain, you MUST:

1. Paint cobalt oxide on the dry, unfired body with a soft brush, then dip in a clear glaze.
2. Fire once at 1280 to 1330 °C in a strong flame, so that the porcelain turns white and dense.
3. Divide the surface into bands: a main field, a shoulder band, and a foot border of lappets or waves.
4. In the Yuan style, fill the field with dense bands; in the Ming style, leave more white ground.
5. Draw dragons, lotus scrolls, phoenixes, flowers and landscapes in outline and wash.
6. Use a deep blue with dark spots in the early work, and a clean soft blue in the later Ming work.
7. Write the reign mark in six characters in a ring or a square under the base.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Large dishes, jars, meiping vases, pear-shaped bottles, moon flasks, bowls and stem cups.
- **Profile:** A strong, round body with a high shoulder; the profile is full and has a clean, firm curve.
- **Foot and rim:** A neat foot ring that is bare of glaze or has a thin glaze; the rim is thin and often with a brown or blue line.
- **Proportion:** Dish diameter 35 to 50 cm in the Yuan; jar height 30 to 45 cm; wall 4 to 10 mm, thin for the Ming bowl.
- **Rules:**
  - Throw large jars in two parts and join at the waist.
  - Keep the base of the foot flat and unglazed or lightly glazed.
  - Run the painting in bands that follow the line of the form.
  - Place the main scene on the belly and the borders at the shoulder and the foot.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** porcelain
- **Avoid:** earthenware · bone-china · creamware
- **Rule:** Use a white, hard porcelain of kaolin and china stone. It must be dense, white and slightly translucent in thin walls.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the wheel · turning the foot and the wall · press moulding of flasks · hand painting under the glaze · dipping in glaze
- **Joins:** Large vases are thrown in two or three parts and joined at the neck; handles and spouts are luted with slip.
- **Details:** A clear glaze with a faint blue-green tint, dark spots in early cobalt, and a base mark of the reign in six characters in later pieces.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** clear-feldspathic
- **Decorations:** underglaze-cobalt
- **Pattern:** Dragons and waves, lotus scrolls, peonies, phoenixes and landscape scenes in bands; the Ming style leaves more white between motifs.
- **Gloss:** Glassy, about 80 to 95 GU, with a clear, slightly blue-green cast.
- **Wear:** The painting stays stable under the glaze; the glaze may show fine crackle and a small brown rim of iron.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Cobalt blue on a white ground: a dense dark blue in the Yuan and a soft clear blue in the Ming.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** An egg-shaped kiln at Jingdezhen, fired with pine wood, which holds a lot of saggared pieces.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:** dry the formed body · paint cobalt on the unfired body · dip in clear glaze · single firing in saggars · slow cooling

- **Signs:** A clear glaze with a blue-green tint, cobalt with dark spots, and a thin white foot with a reign mark.

### Cobalt underglaze painting (process)

Cobalt oxide is painted on the raw or bisque body. A clear glaze covers it, and one firing turns the paint a deep blue. Origin: ceramics, Jingdezhen, Jiangxi, China, c. 1330.

- Grind cobalt ore and mix it with water.
- Paint the design on the dry body with a brush.
- Dip the piece in a clear glaze.
- Fire it once at 1250 to 1300 °C.
