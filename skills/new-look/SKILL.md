# New Look

> After rationing, a woman's dress shows plenty: a small waist, a full skirt and the hand work of the couture house.

The couture line that Christian Dior showed in February 1947. It has round shoulders, a small built waist and a full calf-length skirt in metres of wool and silk. It answered the rationed, square dress of the war.

**Period:** 1947–1957 · **Era:** War and the New Look · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Corolle line, Dior New Look, Post-war couture silhouette

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in New Look, you MUST:

1. Cut the waist 5 to 8 cm smaller than the natural waist, and hold it with a boned inner belt.
2. Set the hem 30 to 40 cm above the floor, at mid-calf.
3. Give a day skirt a hem of 10 to 25 m, and an evening skirt up to 80 m.
4. Round the shoulder with no pad; let the line run down from the neck in one curve.
5. Pad the hip with a basque so that the waist looks smaller.
6. Build the shape inside the dress with canvas, boning and a tulle petticoat.
7. Finish each seam and each hem by hand.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** An hourglass: a fitted bodice, a small waist and a skirt that stands out in a wide bell or falls in a narrow pencil.
- **Line:** A soft round shoulder, a waist at the natural line, and a hem at mid-calf.
- **Fit:** Close and built on the bodice; the full skirt stands free of the leg on its petticoat.
- **Proportion:** Waist to hip about 1 to 1.6; hem 30 to 40 cm above the floor; jacket length to the top of the hip.
- **Rules:**
  - Pinch the jacket at the waist and flare it over the hip into a padded basque.
  - Pleat or gather the skirt at the waist so that it opens in a bell.
  - Keep the neckline and the collar small, so that the waist leads.
  - Pair a full skirt with a narrow bodice, and a pencil skirt with a short, wide jacket.

## Fabrics & materials

- **Primary:** worsted-suiting · taffeta · satin · tulle
- **Secondary:** silk-twill · velvet · cambric · crepe-de-chine · kidskin
- **Avoid:** jersey · spandex-jersey · denim · polar-fleece
- **Rule:** Choose a cloth with body that holds a pleat and a bell. Use fine wool for day, silk taffeta and satin for evening, with tulle and canvas inside.

## Construction

- **Methods:** toile fitted on the client · boned inner corselet · horsehair canvas in the basque · knife and box pleats at the waist · hand-sewn hems and linings · tulle petticoat in three or more layers
- **Closures:** Covered buttons down the front of the jacket; hooks and a side zip on the skirt, hidden under a placket.
- **Details:** A round collar or a shawl collar, a padded basque on the jacket, and a self belt or none. Gloves and a small hat finish the look.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** matte · glazed · embroidered · beaded
- **Pattern:** Plain cloth for day in one colour; for evening, embroidery and beads on the bodice or a scatter on the skirt.
- **Gloss:** Wool at 2–5 GU for day; satin and taffeta at 30–60 GU for evening.
- **Wear:** The style wants a new, pressed piece; the couture house repairs and re-presses it, and a worn piece loses its line.

### Toile fitting (skill)

A trial garment in plain calico, fitted and pinned on the client or on a stand before the real cloth is cut. Origin: fashion, Paris, France, c. 1858.

- Cut the toile from calico of the same weight as the final cloth.
- Pin and mark the changes on the body, then take the toile apart and use it as the pattern.
- Fit again in the real cloth two or three times before the hand finish.
