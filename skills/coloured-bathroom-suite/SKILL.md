# Coloured bathroom suite

> Sanitary ware can be a colour of the room, and one even glaze on every piece makes a bathroom that matches.

A bath, a basin and a WC glazed in the same strong colour: pink, green, blue, yellow or later avocado. The even gloss glaze and the matching set make the bathroom a room of colour.

**Period:** 1928–1975 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Pastel bathroom suite, Pink and green sanitary ware, Matching coloured fixtures

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Coloured bathroom suite, you MUST:

1. Glaze the basin, the pan, the cistern and the bath in one colour from the same batch.
2. Choose one pastel or earth colour: pink, sky blue, mint, buttercup yellow or avocado green.
3. Shape each piece with soft curves and a radius of 30 to 60 mm on every edge.
4. Keep the glaze even, with a gloss of 85 to 95 GU and no pattern.
5. Match the colour of the tiles, the towels and the walls to the suite.
6. Add chrome taps with a small white or clear cross handle.
7. Mould a shell, a ridge or a streamline band as the only relief.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** A white suite with a coloured bath only.
- **NEVER:** Two different shades of one colour in a set.
- **NEVER:** A printed flower pattern on the glaze.
- **NEVER:** A dark matte glaze that stains.
- **NEVER:** A thin shape with a sharp edge.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** A set of a pedestal basin, a close-coupled WC, a bidet and a bath with a panel, all with round, soft shapes.
- **Profile:** Smooth, full curves with a streamlined swell at the front of the basin and a low, wide cistern on the pan.
- **Foot and rim:** A rolled, thick rim with a soft radius, a pedestal that follows the line of the bowl, and a flat base.
- **Proportion:** Basin 560 to 650 mm wide, 3 to 4 tap holes with a chrome mixer; wall 8 to 14 mm; bath 1700 mm long.
- **Rules:** Use one colour for all pieces of a set. · Round every edge and corner, so that there is no hard line. · Keep the form of each piece in line with the next piece. · Let the glaze be the only decoration.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** vitreous-china · fireclay
- **Secondary:** chrome · brass
- **Avoid:** earthenware · bone-china · stainless-steel · plastic
- **Rule:** Use a dense white vitreous body, so that a thin coloured glaze looks clean and does not stain. The colour sits in the glaze, not in the body.

## Forming

- **Methods:** slip casting in plaster moulds · pressure casting of large parts · fettling and sponging of seams · spray application of glaze · hand finishing of the rim
- **Joins:** The pedestal and the basin are separate casts; the cistern and the pan are bolted with rubber seals and chrome fittings.
- **Details:** A moulded maker name on the back, a clean glaze line at the foot, and a slight tone change at a thick corner.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** coloured-gloss · sanitary-glaze
- **Decorations:** undecorated · relief-moulding
- **Pattern:** No pattern. A plain colour covers each piece, and the only relief is a shell, a rib or a streamline band.
- **Gloss:** High gloss, about 85 to 95 GU, even over the whole surface.
- **Wear:** The colour fades in the sun, a grey mark appears at the plug and the tap, and a thin glaze crazes with age.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** One strong or soft colour for the whole suite, from pink and mint to avocado, with chrome as a bright accent.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A gas or oil tunnel kiln, with one long glost firing.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** cast in a plaster mould · dry and fettle · spray the coloured glaze · fire at 1200 to 1250 °C · check the colour against a tile · pack the set together

- **Signs:** A thin tone change at thick corners, a stilt mark under the foot and a number of the batch on the back.

### Slip casting (process)

Liquid clay is poured into a plaster mould. The plaster takes water from the clay and a wall forms on the inside of the mould. Origin: ceramics, Staffordshire, England, c. 1745.

- Make a plaster mould in two or more parts.
- Pour slip into the mould and wait 10 to 20 minutes for a wall of 3 to 5 mm.
- Pour out the rest of the slip.
- Open the mould when the clay is leather-hard and fettle the seam.
