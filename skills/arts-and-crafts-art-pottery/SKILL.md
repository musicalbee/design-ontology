# Arts and Crafts art pottery

> A pot is a work of art when one person makes it from clay and glaze, and the hand is allowed to show.

Hand-made British art pottery after 1872. The potters of Lambeth, Chelsea and Fulham used salt glaze and ruby lustre, and made each piece one by one with a visible hand.

**Period:** 1872–1920 · **Era:** Industrial pottery · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** British art pottery, Lambeth art pottery, De Morgan lustreware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Arts and Crafts art pottery, you MUST:

1. Make each piece by hand, with the maker's mark or initials incised in the base.
2. Throw or build the form with small irregularities of 1 to 3 mm; do not hide the throwing rings.
3. Fire a salt glaze at about 1200 °C and let it run to an orange-peel surface.
4. Fire a ruby or gold lustre in reduction at about 600 to 800 °C, so that the copper and silver show as a film.
5. Paint or incise plants, birds, fish and fantastic creatures, in a free line after Persian and Iznik models.
6. Use a limited palette of 3 or 4 colours: blue, turquoise, brown and ruby.
7. Keep the form plain and the surface rich.
8. Place the pot at home as a decorative object, not as a service.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Vases, bottle vases, lidded jars, bowls and grotesque bird jars, in a full, simple shape.
- **Profile:** A round belly with a short or tall neck; some pieces have a lobed or fluted wall, and the grotesque jars have a head as a lid.
- **Foot and rim:** A plain, slightly irregular foot, often unglazed; the rim is rolled or flared, with a thick lip.
- **Proportion:** Height 15 to 40 cm; the wall is 4 to 10 mm, thick and heavy in the hand.
- **Rules:**
  - Throw the main form on the wheel and add incised detail while the clay is soft.
  - Keep the foot and the base unglazed or lightly glazed.
  - Sign the piece with a cipher, a date and the name of the shop.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stoneware · earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · jasperware
- **Rule:** Use a buff or grey stoneware for salt glaze and an earthenware for tile and lustre. Make the body coarse enough to show the hand.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the wheel · hand modelling of animal forms · incising and carving in leather-hard clay · press moulding of tiles · painting with brush and slip
- **Joins:** Handles, feet and heads are added while the clay is soft and blended by hand; the join stays visible.
- **Details:** Visible throwing rings, a thumb mark under the handle, an incised monogram, and a glaze that gathers thick in the lower curves.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** salt-glaze · lead-glaze · tin-glaze
- **Decorations:** lustre · incised · underglaze-polychrome · sgraffito
- **Pattern:** Fish, birds, flowers and Persian scrolls in ruby lustre or in blue and turquoise; incised bands of birds and animals on salt-glazed stoneware.
- **Gloss:** Satin to high gloss, 30 to 80 GU, with a variable surface; lustre gives a metallic sheen.
- **Wear:** Salt glaze keeps its orange-peel texture for a long time; lustre dulls where it is touched.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Deep, earthy blues and greens with a ruby and gold lustre, on a pale or buff ground.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A small coal or wood kiln; a special muffle or reduction kiln for lustre.
- **Atmosphere:** salt-or-soda
- **Processes:** biscuit firing of the body · salt glaze firing at about 1200 °C · painting of the lustre pigment on a glazed tile or pot · reduction lustre firing at 600 to 800 °C with smoke · hand finishing of the foot
- **Scale:** One to a few thousand pieces a year from a small shop; each pot is different.
- **Signs:** A visible hand, uneven glaze, orange-peel salt glaze, and a lustre that shifts colour in the light.

### Lustre firing (process)

A thin film of copper or silver on a glazed pot gives a metallic shine. The potter paints a pigment on the glaze and fires it in smoke at a low heat. Origin: ceramics, Iraq, c. 800.

- Mix a copper or silver compound with clay and vinegar to make a paste.
- Paint the paste on the fired glaze.
- Fire the pot at 600 to 800 °C and fill the kiln with smoke in the second half.
- Wash off the clay after the firing to show the metallic film.
