# French New Wave

> A film is a personal essay shot on location with light gear, so the director's eye and the street replace the studio and the script department.

Light 35mm cameras, fast black-and-white stock and real streets. A handheld camera, a jump cut and a director who shows the hand. The film talks about film.

**Period:** 1959–1967 · **Era:** Portable cameras and the new waves · **Domain:** Film & Video · **Also called:** Nouvelle Vague

Load it when the user asks for: "french new wave", "nouvelle vague", "godard", "truffaut", "jump cut", "breathless look", "handheld black and white", "90s arthouse street".

Good for: fashion and lifestyle films, music video on location, independent short film, video prompts for a city walk.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs polish, a hero product shot or controlled colour.
- The piece needs a clear, closed story arc of 3 acts.

## System directives

When planning, shooting, prompting, editing or grading moving images in French New Wave, you MUST:

1. Shoot on location with a handheld or wheelchair-dolly 35mm camera, at 24 fps and a 180° shutter (1/48 s).
2. Use available light with a bounce from a ceiling or a wall, at a key-to-fill ratio of about 2:1, on stock pushed 1 to 2 stops (ISO 400 to 1600 equivalent).
3. Frame at 1.37:1 or 1.66:1 with a 28mm to 50mm lens, and keep the camera at eye level, 1.4 to 1.7 m.
4. Cut at an average shot length of 5 to 8 s (estimate; check Cinemetrics for each title) and use a jump cut that removes 1 to 2 s inside one shot.
5. Let one tracking shot run 20 to 60 s along a street or a corridor, and let the actor look into the lens for 1 to 2 s.
6. Record sound on location or post-dub it, and let the music stop or switch in mid-bar, with no fade.
7. Cite a film or a book in a shot or a line, and break the fourth wall once in each sequence.

## Composition

- **Framing:** A loose 1.37:1 or 1.66:1 frame; the street, a room and a face are all in view, and the frame can cut a head at the edge.
- **Staging in depth:** Two planes: a face or a back in front of 3 to 6 m of the street; passers-by cross the frame and are not cleared.
- **Subject placement:** Off centre, walking or turning; a two-shot of 2 faces at 0.5 to 1 m apart.

## Camera & movement

- **Movement:** Handheld follow and long tracking shots from a wheelchair, a cart or a car. Pans are quick, with a whip at 90° per second.
- **Support:** Handheld, wheelchair or mail-cart dolly, car mount
- **Camera height:** 1.4 to 1.7 m, eye level
- **Move speed:** 1.0 to 1.5 m/s, walking pace; whip pan 90° per second

## Lens & format

- **Aspect ratio:** 1.37:1 or 1.66:1; 2.35:1 for some colour titles
- **Frame rate:** 24 fps
- **Shutter angle:** 180° (1/48 s)
- **Focal length:** 28mm to 50mm (35mm format)
- **Aperture:** f/2.8 to f/5.6 with the stock pushed
- **Resolution:** 35mm negative; scan at 2K or 4K
- **Focus:** Zone focus at 2 to 4 m; the background stays readable.
- **Equipment:** lightweight 35mm camera · fast black-and-white stock · wheelchair dolly · portable tape recorder

## Light

- **Quality:** Available light, with a bounce for the face; mixed street and window light.
- **Direction:** From the window or the street; a photoflood bounced off the ceiling gives a flat top light.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 2:1 (1 stop)
- **Colour temperature:** Daylight 5600K or street tungsten 3200K
- **Source:** Window, street lamp and a bounced photoflood

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Natural black and white with a full grey scale and visible grain. Colour titles keep primaries but stay un-graded.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Convert to black and white and set the black point at 5% and the white point at 95%.
  - Keep a gentle S-curve of +15% contrast and a visible midtone of 18% grey.
  - Let the highlights burn: roll off only the top 3% and allow window light to clip.
  - Add grain at 18% to 25% to match stock pushed 2 stops.
  - Do not add a vignette, a tint or a LUT.
- **Texture:** Visible silver grain, amount 18% to 25%

## Editing & rhythm

- **Average shot length:** 5 to 8 s (estimate; check Cinemetrics for each title)
- **Shot length range:** 0.5 s for a jump cut to 60 s for a long track
- **Cutting logic:** Cut for the beat of the scene, not for continuity. Drop 1 to 2 s from the middle of a take and let the jump show.
- **Transitions:** Hard cut, jump cut, iris, a freeze frame of 1 to 3 s, a title card as a cut.
- **Runtime:** 80 to 100 min

## Sound

- **Approach:** Location sound or post-dubbed sound, with room noise and traffic. The track can drop out for 1 to 3 s.
- **Dialogue:** Quick, overlapping, often off camera; subtitles are not a part of the style.
- **Music:** Jazz or a piano theme in short bursts; the music cuts in and out with no fade.
- **Loudness:** −23 LUFS integrated, with dialogue peaks at −6 dBFS

## Titles & graphics

- **Treatment:** Plain white capitals on black or on a street sign; sparse credits, a title card that cuts in on a beat.
- **Type:** Sans-serif upper case, 1 to 2 s on screen, no animation.

## Techniques

### Jump cut (montage)

A cut inside one continuous shot that removes time and leaves the jump in view. Origin: film & video, Paris, 1960.

- Remove 0.3 to 2 seconds inside one shot and keep the framing within 10% of the frame width, so that the jump shows.
- In a talk to camera, cut on the word and not on the action: one cut every 2 to 4 seconds.
