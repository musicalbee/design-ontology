# Prairie School

> A house should belong to its land: low horizontal lines, a free plan around the hearth and honest materials make an American architecture of the prairie.

Long low houses with broad overhanging roofs, ribbon windows and a central hearth. Frank Lloyd Wright and the Chicago architects built for the flat Midwest: the horizontal line rules, and rooms flow into each other.

**Period:** 1900–1917 · **Era:** Fin de Siècle · **Domain:** Architecture · **Also called:** Prairie style, Wrightian, Chicago Prairie

- The brief asks for a symmetric formal front.

## System directives

When designing, describing or rendering buildings and interiors in Prairie School, you MUST:

1. Stress the horizontal: low hipped roofs of 1:6 to 1:4 pitch, eaves that project 1 to 2.5m and long unbroken lines of sill and band.
2. Plan around a central masonry hearth, and let the living rooms flow into each other in a cross-shaped or L-shaped plan.
3. Group the windows in bands of casements with art-glass geometry, 0.8 to 1.2m above the floor, so that the rooms feel sheltered.
4. Make the entrance low and indirect, 2.0 to 2.2m high, to compress the visitor before the open living room of 3 to 3.5m.
5. Use long thin Roman brick with deep raked horizontal joints and flush vertical joints, to stress the horizontal line.
6. Design the whole house as one work: built-in furniture, lamps, glass and planters follow one geometric motif.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Tall vertical proportions and steep pitched roofs.
- **NEVER:** Closed boxlike rooms separated by corridors.
- **NEVER:** Applied historical ornament or classical columns.
- **NEVER:** A grand central front entrance on the street axis.
- **NEVER:** Painted smooth surfaces that hide the brick and timber.
- **NEVER:** Window openings set high and narrow in vertical strips.

## Massing & form

- **Form:** Low interlocking horizontal volumes in a cross or an L, with terraces, balconies and a tall chimney as the vertical anchor.
- **Proportion:** Very long and low: the house is 4 to 8 times longer than its eave height of 2.8 to 3.4m.
- **Roof:** A low hipped or gabled roof with a deep overhang of 1 to 2.5m, and a pitch of 1:6 to 1:4.
- **Relation to ground:** A raised base of masonry, 0.6 to 1.2m, with planters and terrace walls that tie the house to the garden.

## Structure

- **System:** Load-bearing brick with timber floors and roofs; later steel beams for the cantilevered roof and balconies.
- **Span and module:** A planning module of 1.2 to 1.5m; cantilevered eaves of up to 6m on steel; room spans of 4 to 7m.
- **Expression:** The horizontal courses, the cantilevered slabs and the wood bands state the structure, and the chimney anchors it.

## Materials

- **Primary:** Roman brick · oak and cypress timber · limestone coping · stucco
- **Secondary:** art glass in lead cames · copper roofing · terracotta tile · cast concrete
- **Finish:** Natural and matt: brick, stained wood and plaster in earth colours, left to age.

## Facade

- **Composition:** Layered horizontal bands: a masonry base, a band of windows, a wide frieze and a deep soffit under the roof.
- **Openings:** Continuous bands of casement windows with geometric art glass; the window-to-wall ratio is 25 to 40%.
- **Solid to void:** Balanced on the upper floor: 60–75% solid.

## Light & space

- **Daylight:** Light enters low and from many sides under the deep eaves, and art glass turns it into a pattern on the floor.
- **Spatial sequence:** A low, compressed entry opens to a high, open living room around the hearth; the rooms flow into each other.
- **Interior character:** A ceiling of 2.4 to 2.7m with wood bands, a central brick hearth, built-in seats and shelves and warm lamp light.

## Ornament

- **Attitude:** Ornament is geometric and integral: it comes from the pattern of the structure and the abstraction of the prairie plant.
- **Details:** art-glass windows · stained wood banding · built-in furniture · stone planters · raked horizontal brick joints · geometric lamps
