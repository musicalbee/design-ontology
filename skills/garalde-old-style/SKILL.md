# Garalde old-style serif

> A text letter keeps the rhythm of the hand that wrote it, so the eye reads a page without effort.

The roman of the Renaissance book: moderate contrast, a diagonal stress from the broad-nib pen, bracketed serifs and a small x-height. It is the default text face of the long tradition and reads well for hours.

**Period:** 1495–1750 · **Era:** Hand press · **Domain:** Typeface · **Also called:** Old-style serif, Garalde, Aldine, Renaissance antiqua

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "old-style serif", "garamond", "caslon", "garalde", "book typography", "classic serif", "literary type", "renaissance type".

Good for: books and long-form reading, literary magazines, academic and museum publishing, wine, food and heritage brands.

Do not use it when:
- The text sits at 12 px or less on a low-resolution screen.
- The brief asks for a cold, technical or neutral voice.

## System directives

When choosing, pairing, setting or briefing a typeface: a text face for a book or a product, a display face for a title, or a type brief for a designer in Garalde old-style serif, you MUST:

1. Keep the stroke contrast between 2:1 and 3.5:1, measured on the lower-case o.
2. Tilt the stress of the o, e and c 15–30° to the left of the vertical, as a broad-nib pen does.
3. Give the serifs a curved bracket and a slight cup; the head serifs of b, d, h, k and l slope down to the left.
4. Hold the x-height at 0.58–0.70 of the cap height, so the ascenders rise clearly above the x-height.
5. Set the capitals at the classical proportions: a wide O, C, D and N, and a narrow E, F, L and S.
6. Use old-style figures (with ascenders and descenders) in running text.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** A vertical stress with hairlines under 0.3 of the stem.
- **NEVER:** Unbracketed, square slab serifs.
- **NEVER:** A large x-height that crowds the ascenders.
- **NEVER:** Tight, negative tracking in text sizes.
- **NEVER:** Lining figures in the middle of a paragraph.
- **NEVER:** Fake bold or fake italic made by the software.

## Classification

- **Vox atypi:** Garalde (old style)
- **Din 16518:** Gruppe II: Französische Renaissance-Antiqua
- **Note:** A Humanist (Venetian) face has a sloped bar on the e and heavier serifs; a Garalde has a level bar and more contrast. Transitional faces start when the stress turns vertical.

## Anatomy

- **Contrast:** Moderate: the thin parts are 0.3–0.5 of the stem, so the strokes swell and taper as with a pen.
- **Stress:** Diagonal, 15–30° to the left of the vertical; the thinnest parts of the o are at about 11 and 5 o'clock.
- **Serifs:** Bracketed and slightly cupped; the head serifs of the lower case are wedges that slope down to the left.
- **Terminals:** Pear-shaped or teardrop terminals on a, c, f and r; the ear of the g is short.
- **Aperture:** Open: the c, e and s have wide mouths, and the bowl of the a is small.
- **Key letters:** A small-eyed e with a level bar, a two-storey g with a large lower loop, and an M with splayed sides and serifs on top.

## Metrics

- **X height min:** 0.58
- **X height max:** 0.7
- **Contrast min:** 2
- **Contrast max:** 3.5
- **X height:** The x-height is 0.58–0.70 of the cap height and about 0.40–0.45 of the em; ascenders rise above the capitals.
- **Contrast:** The side of the o is 2 to 3.5 times as thick as its top.
- **Width:** The lower case is of medium width: the mean advance of a to z is 0.43–0.50 em.
- **Figures:** Old-style proportional figures in text; lining tabular figures only in tables.

## Setting

- **Text sizes:** Set text at 10–12 pt in print and 17–21 px on screen; the small x-height needs 1–2 px more than a sans.
- **Display sizes:** Use the text cut up to 36 pt; above that, use a display cut with finer hairlines if the family has one.
- **Leading:** Set the leading at 1.3–1.5 times the size for text, and 1.05–1.15 for titles.
- **Tracking:** Set 0 em tracking in text; letter-space capitals and small caps by 0.05–0.10 em.
- **Measure:** Set 60–72 characters a line for a book page, 45–60 in two columns.
- **Case and features:** Use true small caps for acronyms, the f-ligatures (fi, fl, ff), and the italic of the family for emphasis.

## Pairing

- **Rule:** Pair it with a face of a different class and a similar x-height; let the serif carry the text and the sans carry labels.

| With | Role | Why |

### Roman capital proportions (motif)

Capital letters with the proportions of Roman stone inscriptions: some letters wide and round, some narrow, and each one built for the eye, not for one measure. Origin: architecture, Rome, 113.

- Draw O as a near circle, and M about as wide as the cap height.
- Draw E, F, L and S at about half the width of O.
- Set the stem at about one tenth of the cap height for a classical weight; for signs, increase it by up to half.
- Cut serifs, or leave them out, but keep the width rules.
