# Minimalist fashion

> After the show of the 1980s, dress is plain: a perfect cut in fine cloth needs no ornament.

The plain, quiet dress of the 1990s. It has clean lines, fine cloth in grey, black and white, no logo and no trim, and a cut that does all the work.

**Period:** 1990–2000 · **Era:** Power and subculture · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** 1990s minimalism, Clean line fashion, Pared-back dress

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Minimalist fashion, you MUST:

1. Cut each piece with as few seams as the body allows.
2. Remove each trim, logo, pocket flap and extra button that has no use.
3. Choose fine, dense cloth in wool, silk crepe, poplin and cashmere.
4. Keep the palette to black, white, grey, navy and camel.
5. Cut a straight, long line: a slim trouser, a long jacket and a column dress.
6. Hide the seam and the facing, and finish the inside as clean as the outside.
7. Keep the shoulder natural, with no pad.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A narrow, straight column: a slim trouser, a long, lean jacket and a simple shift or slip dress.
- **Line:** A natural shoulder, a straight body with no waist, and a long vertical line.
- **Fit:** Close but easy; the cloth follows the body without a cling and has no extra fabric.
- **Proportion:** Jacket to mid-thigh; trouser leg 18 to 20 cm at the hem; lapel 6 to 7 cm; dress hem at the knee or the ankle.
- **Rules:** Cut a narrow lapel or none. · Keep the trouser slim and the hem clean. · Remove the pocket flap and sew the pocket inside the seam. · Use one cloth and one colour for the whole outfit. · Finish with a plain flat shoe or a slim sandal.

## Fabrics & materials

- **Primary:** worsted-suiting · poplin · crepe-de-chine · jersey
- **Secondary:** gabardine · linen-cloth · flannel · cashmere-knit
- **Avoid:** brocade · jacquard · velvet · tartan · machine-lace
- **Rule:** Choose a fine, plain cloth with a clean surface: wool crepe, poplin, silk crepe and a fine knit, with no pattern.

## Construction

- **Methods:** close-fitting seamless cut · concealed seams and facings · bound and turned edges · invisible zip · single-colour cloth · fine hand finishing inside
- **Closures:** A hidden zip or hook, or one flat button; a coat with a hidden placket.
- **Details:** No visible pocket flap, a narrow collar or none, a clean hem and a plain, flat button.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** matte · mercerised · sanforised
- **Pattern:** Plain colour only; a very fine weave or a rib is the only texture.
- **Gloss:** Wool and crepe at 2–6 GU; poplin at 10–25 GU; silk at 20–40 GU.
- **Wear:** The style wants a clean, new surface; a crease or a mark shows at once.

### Toile fitting (skill)

A trial garment in plain calico, fitted and pinned on the client or on a stand before the real cloth is cut. Origin: fashion, Paris, France, c. 1858.

- Cut the toile from calico of the same weight as the final cloth.
- Pin and mark the changes on the body, then take the toile apart and use it as the pattern.
- Fit again in the real cloth two or three times before the hand finish.
