# Suprematism

> Art should free itself from the object: pure shapes in a limitless white field give the feeling of form and of space and nothing else.

Flat geometric shapes, such as a square, a bar, a circle and a cross, that float on a white field. The picture shows no object: it shows pure feeling and the sense of space.

**Period:** 1915–1935 · **Era:** Avant-garde · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Malevich's Suprematism, Pure feeling

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "suprematism", "suprematist", "malevich", "black square", "lissitzky", "floating geometry", "geometric abstraction", "white on white".

Good for: poster and cover with bold geometry, brand marks and icons from flat shapes, gallery and book design, layout studies with floating blocks.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a figure, a story or a literal scene.
- The picture needs warm texture or a hand-made look.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Suprematism, you MUST:

1. Build the picture from 1–12 flat geometric shapes: a square, a rectangle, a circle, a cross, a bar.
2. Float the shapes on a white or pale field, and leave 50–70% of the picture empty.
3. Tilt the shapes at 10–45° from the vertical, so that they rise, fall or drift.
4. Limit the colour to 2–4 flat hues and black, with each shape in one flat colour.
5. Paint the shapes with a hard, clean edge and no brush texture in the colour.
6. Hold the weight in balance by size and position, not by symmetry.
7. Show no object, no horizon, no light source and no shadow.

## Composition

- **Layout:** A few flat shapes, tilted and floating on an open white field, linked by diagonals; a large shape set off-centre and small shapes along its axis.
- **Focal point:** The largest shape, set at one third from an edge, with the strongest value contrast against the field.
- **Format:** A canvas at 1:1 to 4:5; a size of 0.5 to 1m; the field is white and takes 50–70% of the area.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| negative-space | Keep 50–70% of the picture as a white or pale field, with no shape closer than 8% of the width to the edge. |
| balance | Balance the large shape against 2–4 small shapes by area and distance, and hold the centre of mass within 10% of the picture centre. |
| scale | Set a ratio of 4:1 or more between the largest and the smallest shape. |
| leading-lines | Align the shapes along one diagonal axis at 20–45° from the horizontal. |
| contrast | Make the focal shape black or red on white, with a value gap of 70% or more. |
| hierarchy | Use 3 levels: one large shape, 2–3 medium shapes and 2–5 small shapes. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A white field with black and a few flat primary hues: red, blue, yellow, and sometimes a grey or a green.
- **Distribution:** White field 55–70%, black 10–15%, one main hue 10–15%, other hues under 10% each.
- **Ground:** A white or off-white ground, flat and unmodulated.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint, thin and matt, brushed flat.
- **Support:** A fine canvas with a white ground.
- **Brushwork:** Flat, even fills with a stroke that does not show; the edge is drawn with a small brush of 3–8mm.
- **Paint handling:** 2–3 thin, opaque coats; no impasto and no glaze; a slight crackle with age is accepted.
- **Edges:** Hard and clean, with a small wobble of under 1mm from the hand.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** None: the field is even and the shapes carry no light.
- **Modelling:** None; each shape is one flat colour.
- **Shadow:** None; shapes cast no shadow on the field.
- **Temperature:** Neutral white field; warm and cool shapes in contrast.

## Subject

- **Typical:** A black square, a red square, a cross, a floating bar, a circle, a group of tilted rectangles and a white-on-white form.
- **Figure scale:** No figure; the largest shape fills 20–40% of the picture.
- **Treatment:** Pure and silent; the shapes are a feeling of form and not a thing.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Matt, even and flat.
- **Texture:** Smooth, with a fine craquelure in old works.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Diagonal composition (motif)

The main forms lie on a tilted axis, so that the picture seems to move and to cut across the field. Origin: painting, Petrograd, 1915.

- Set the main axis at 30° to 60° to the frame edge; 45° is the strongest.
- Let a large form and a small form lie on the same diagonal at a ratio of 4:1 or more.
- Use one saturated colour (red) against a white or black field.
- Leave a clear field around the diagonal: at least 50% of the area stays empty.

### Primary shapes (motif, invented here)

The circle, the square and the triangle in flat colour, used as the whole picture or as a sign. Origin: painting, Petrograd, Russia, 1915.

- Use 1 to 3 shapes at large size; let one shape fill 30% to 60% of the frame.
- Fill each shape with one flat colour; no gradient and no outline.
- Put the shapes on a plain ground with 30% or more empty space.
