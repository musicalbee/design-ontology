# Tailfin-Era Car

> After the war a car shows plenty: each year it is longer, lower and brighter, with a fin and chrome that copy the jet aircraft.

The big American car of the jet age. A long, low body with tail fins, a wide chrome grille, a wrap-around windscreen and two-tone paint. The cabin has bright vinyl and cloth.

**Period:** 1948–1963 · **Era:** War and the New Look · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Jet-age car, Fifties American car, Detroit chrome

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Tailfin-Era Car, you MUST:

1. Raise a tail fin at the rear wing, from 5 cm in 1948 to 30 cm in 1959.
2. Widen the grille to the full width of the car, in a bar of chrome with bright teeth.
3. Curve the windscreen round the corners to give a wrap-around view.
4. Paint the body in two tones, split by a chrome spear along the flank.
5. Fit bumpers with bullet-shaped guards, and set the tail lamps in the fin.
6. Make the interior bright, with vinyl in a woven pattern, nylon cloth and an aluminium dashboard trim.
7. Change the body each year, so that a new model looks longer and lower than the last.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A long, low, wide car with a flat bonnet, a wrap-around windscreen, a thin roof and a tail with a fin at each rear wing.
- **Line:** A horizontal body line that rises in the rear wing into a fin, and a chrome spear along the flank.
- **Fit:** A bench seat for six, with the occupants low in a wide, soft cabin; the doors are long and the roof is thin.
- **Proportion:** Length 5.2 to 5.8 m; height 1.3 to 1.4 m; fin height up to 30 cm. The bonnet and the boot are both long and flat.
- **Rules:**
  - Set the fin at the rear wing, with a tail lamp in or under it.
  - Fit a grille of the full width, with chrome bars and bullet guards.
  - Split the body into two or three paint tones along a chrome line.
  - Put a dart or a spear of chrome on the flank, from the front wheel to the fin.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Automotive clay modelling (process)

A designer shapes a full-size car in warm industrial clay over a frame. The clay is cut, smoothed and checked in light before any metal is made. Origin: fashion, Detroit, United States, c. 1930.

- Build a wood and foam frame in the size of the car.
- Warm the clay and press it onto the frame, then cut it with rakes and scrapers.
- Check the highlights in light and measure the form for the dies.

### Chrome plating (process)

A thin layer of chromium is put on steel by electrolysis. The layer gives a hard, bright surface that does not tarnish. Origin: fashion, New York, United States, c. 1924.

- Clean the steel part and plate it with copper and nickel.
- Put the part in a chromic acid bath and pass a current through it.
- Buff the part to a mirror finish.

### Sheet-metal pressing (process)

A press forms a flat steel sheet into a body panel in one stroke. The panels are then welded into a body. Origin: fashion, Philadelphia, United States, c. 1914.

- Cut the steel sheet to size and load it into the die.
- Close the press so that the sheet takes the shape of the die.
- Weld the panels into one shell and grind the seams smooth.
