# Longquan celadon

> A vessel reaches beauty when the glaze alone makes it: a thick, quiet green like jade that hides all other ornament.

Green-glazed stoneware and porcelain from the kilns of Longquan. Potters built up thick layers of glaze that fire to a soft jade-green, with a plain form that gives the glaze all the beauty.

**Period:** 1100–1550 · **Era:** Medieval glaze · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Longquan ware, Southern Song celadon, Kinuta celadon

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Longquan celadon, you MUST:

1. Apply the glaze in 3 to 8 thin layers, with a firing or a drying between layers.
2. Reach a glaze 1 to 2 mm thick that is opaque, soft and rich in tone.
3. Fire in strong reduction at 1200 to 1280 °C, so that the iron turns the glaze green.
4. Use a plain, strong shape with a clean profile; let the glaze be the decoration.
5. Add only low relief, incised lines or applied moulded figures, under the glaze.
6. Wipe the glaze off the foot ring, or leave a small part bare so that it turns orange in the fire.
7. Choose pale blue-green (fenqing) or deeper plum-green (meizi qing) glaze, never a mixed palette.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Plum vases, bottle vases, dishes, large chargers, incense burners and kinuta (mallet-shaped) vases.
- **Profile:** A full, smooth belly and a short neck; the silhouette stays calm and unbroken.
- **Foot and rim:** A thin foot ring that is bare of glaze and fires red-brown; a thin lip and a clean edge.
- **Proportion:** Vase height 20 to 40 cm and a wall 3 to 8 mm thick; a charger diameter up to 60 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the profile simple, so that the glaze flows evenly over it.
  - Use a thick foot ring on a large piece to carry the weight.
  - Place relief decoration low, under the thick glaze, so the glaze softens it.
  - Leave the foot ring unglazed and bright orange.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stoneware · porcelain
- **Avoid:** earthenware · bone-china · creamware
- **Rule:** Use a pale, dense body of porcelain stone and clay that fires grey-white. It must carry a thick glaze without slumping.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the wheel · turning the foot · press moulding of relief panels · applied relief figures · incising with a tool
- **Joins:** Parts of a large vase are thrown and joined at the shoulder; handles and applied figures are luted with slip.
- **Details:** An orange-red unglazed ring at the foot, a thin brown line at the rim where the glaze thins, and a smooth, thick glaze edge at the base.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** celadon · crackle-glaze
- **Decorations:** undecorated · relief-moulding · incised
- **Pattern:** A plain glaze field; relief lotus petals, double fish or a dragon, only as a low shape under the glaze.
- **Gloss:** Soft satin to gloss, about 40 to 65 GU, with the depth of jade.
- **Wear:** Light crackle may appear in a deliberate net; the green stays stable and the foot ring darkens.

## Palette

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A long dragon kiln that climbs a hillside, fired with wood; one kiln holds thousands of pieces.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 900 °C · multiple glaze layers · reduction firing · slow cooling
- **Scale:** Hundreds of kilns in Longquan county made ware for the court and for export by sea.
- **Signs:** A thick glaze edge near the foot, bubbles in the glaze, spur marks, and an orange unglazed ring.

### Layered glazing (process)

A glaze is put on in several thin coats with a firing or a drying step between them. The layers make a thick, deep and soft surface. Origin: ceramics, Longquan, Zhejiang, China, c. 1150.

- Bisque fire the pot.
- Dip or brush a thin coat of glaze and let it dry.
- Repeat the coat up to ten times.
- Fire the pot in reduction, so that the glaze turns green and soft.
