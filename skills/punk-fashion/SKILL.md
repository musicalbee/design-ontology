# Punk fashion

> Dress is a protest that the wearer makes by hand: the garment is torn, pinned and written on, and it shocks on purpose.

The dress of the London punk scene from 1976. It has ripped and pinned T-shirts, tartan bondage trousers, studded leather jackets, rubber and PVC, and safety pins as ornament.

**Period:** 1976–1984 · **Era:** Youthquake · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Punk dress, Bondage look, Seditionaries look, UK punk

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Punk fashion, you MUST:

1. Tear, slash or cut open the cloth, and pin or lace the opening shut with a safety pin or a cord.
2. Print a slogan or an image on a plain cotton T-shirt by screen print, in one or two flat colours.
3. Cut the trouser narrow, to 16 to 18 cm at the hem, and join the legs with a bondage strap.
4. Use black leather, tartan, PVC and rubber as the main cloths.
5. Fix 20 or more metal studs or spikes along the collar and the shoulder of a leather jacket.
6. Hold the look with chains, zips, safety pins and dog collars.
7. Make or modify the piece yourself, and keep each stitch and tear visible.

### Screen printing (process)

Ink pushed through a fine mesh with a squeegee; a stencil on the mesh blocks the parts that do not print. Origin: print, Manchester, 1907.

- Stretch a silk or polyester mesh of 40–120 threads a centimetre on a frame.
- Make the stencil by hand cut film or by a photo emulsion exposed through a film positive.
- Pull thick, opaque ink across the screen with a rubber squeegee in one even stroke.
- Print each colour from its own screen and let each layer dry before the next.
