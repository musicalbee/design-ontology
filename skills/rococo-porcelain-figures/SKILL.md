# Rococo porcelain figures

> A porcelain figure is a toy for the table of a prince: white, glossy and light, it turns a scene of court play into a small, bright piece of theatre.

Small hand-painted porcelain figures of lovers, shepherds, harlequins and monkeys, made for the dessert table of the court. They are white, glossy and light, in S-curves with lace, flowers and gilt.

**Period:** 1731–1770 · **Era:** The porcelain race · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Meissen figures, Kändler figures, Commedia dell'arte porcelain, Dresden figures

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Rococo porcelain figures, you MUST:

1. Model the figure in a twisting S-curve, with the head turned against the hip.
2. Keep the figure 10 to 30 cm high, with a base that is a small mound or a scrolled plinth.
3. Cast the figure from many small piece moulds, and join the parts with slip.
4. Dip real lace in slip and drape it on the dress, so that it fires to a stiff white lace.
5. Add flowers, small birds and leaves by hand to the base and the hair.
6. Paint the figure with overglaze enamels in clear, light colours, and fix them at 700 to 800 °C.
7. Add gilt scrolls on the base and the hem, and burnish them after the third firing.
8. Choose a scene from the stage or the court: Harlequin, Columbine, a gallant, a shepherdess, a monkey in a wig.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Single figures and small groups: gallants and ladies, shepherds, harlequins, craftsmen, cries of a town and animals, on a low scroll base.
- **Profile:** A twisting S-curve from the foot to the head, with a gesture that is half a step and half a bow; the dress swings out in wide skirts.
- **Foot and rim:** A small rocaille base with C-scrolls, moulded and gilt, or a grass mound with applied flowers; the underside is open with a vent hole.
- **Proportion:** Height 10 to 30 cm; the body is 3 to 4 mm thick, and the head is about one sixth of the height.
- **Rules:** Make the pose a turn and a gesture, never a plain stand. · Apply lace, flowers and small parts by hand. · Keep the glaze thin and clear over the white body. · Paint light, clear colours and keep the flesh tone pale. · Gild the base and the hem only in small scrolls.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** european-hard-paste
- **Secondary:** soft-paste-porcelain
- **Avoid:** earthenware · creamware · stoneware · terracotta · parian
- **Rule:** Use a white, hard, translucent paste that takes a clear glaze. Soft-paste works for English and French makers, but the figure stays white and thin-walled.

## Forming

- **Methods:** modelling of a master figure in clay or wax by the modeller · moulding in many small plaster or clay piece moulds · casting or pressing of the parts in the moulds · joining of parts with slip · dipping of lace in slip · hand painting in enamels
- **Joins:** Arms, heads, hats and props are cast apart and fixed to the body with slip. The joins are cleaned and hidden by hand work.
- **Details:** A small vent hole in the base, a fine hair line in the face, a crossed swords or crown mark under the base, and a slightly uneven edge of the lace.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** clear-feldspathic · biscuit
- **Decorations:** overglaze-enamel · gilding · sprigging · relief-moulding
- **Pattern:** Painted flowers, striped and spotted dress, stripes on a harlequin suit and gilt scrolls on the base. Sprigged flowers and leaves are added by hand.
- **Gloss:** High gloss, about 85 to 100 GU on the glaze; gilding is burnished and bright.
- **Wear:** The enamel colours rub at the edges and the gilt wears at the hem. Chips on lace, fingers and flowers are common, and old repairs are often visible.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A white, glossy ground with clear, light enamel colours in small areas: pink, yellow, turquoise and purple, set with gold.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A wood-fired round kiln with saggars for the hard paste, and a small muffle kiln for the enamels and the gilt.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 900 °C · dipping in glaze · glaze firing at about 1400 °C · enamel firing at 700 to 800 °C · gilding firing at about 650 °C
- **Scale:** A manufactory with modellers, repairers and painters; some 100 or more models a year at the peak, and large dessert services of figures.
- **Signs:** A thin, even glaze, a fine vent hole, crossed swords under the base, and a mix of mould lines and hand-cut lace.

### Modular figure assembly (process)

A figure is made in many standard parts, such as heads, torsos, arms and props, and the parts are joined and finished by hand. One set of moulds gives many different figures. Origin: ceramics, Lintong, Shaanxi, China, c. 221 BCE.

- Model the figure and cut it into parts.
- Press or cast each part in its own mould.
- Join the parts with slip while the clay is soft.
- Finish the joins and the face by hand, so that each figure differs.
