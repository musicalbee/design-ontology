# Empire line dress

> After the Revolution, a dress drops the hoop and the stays and shows a light, straight line from a waist under the bust.

The high-waisted dress after the French Revolution: a light, straight muslin gown that falls from a seam under the bust, like Greek dress.

**Period:** 1795–1820 · **Era:** Revolution and Regency · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Empire waist, Regency dress, Directoire dress, Neoclassical dress

- the brief needs rich, heavy silk

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Empire line dress, you MUST:

1. Set the waist seam directly under the bust, about 10 cm above the natural waist.
2. Cut the skirt straight or slightly gored, and let it fall to the ankle in soft folds.
3. Make the day dress in white or pale muslin of 30 to 90 g per square metre.
4. Keep the neckline low and square or round, and the sleeve short and puffed.
5. Draw the bodice in with a cord or a ribbon under the bust.
6. Border the hem with a band of white embroidery, 15 to 30 cm deep.
7. Add a spencer jacket or a shawl for warmth; do not add a layer of petticoats.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A tall, narrow column: a short bodice, a waist just under the bust and a straight skirt to the ankle.
- **Line:** A low neckline, a short puffed sleeve and an unbroken vertical fall of cloth from the bust to the hem.
- **Fit:** Close at the bust where the seam sits, then free; the skirt hangs from the seam and shows the leg.
- **Proportion:** Bodice about 15 cm long from shoulder seam to waist seam; skirt about 4 to 5 times the bodice; hem at the ankle.
- **Rules:**
  - Place the waist seam under the bust, and draw it in with a cord.
  - Cut the skirt in straight panels so that it falls in vertical folds.
  - Keep the colour pale and the cloth fine; one layer is enough in a warm room.
  - Wear a short spencer jacket or a long shawl over the dress, not a cage or a hoop.
  - Border the hem and the neckline with a band of white or gold embroidery.

## Fabrics & materials

- **Primary:** muslin · cambric · satin · silk-twill
- **Secondary:** tulle · bobbin-lace · velvet · linen-cloth · taffeta
- **Avoid:** brocade · damask · jersey · denim · polar-fleece
- **Rule:** Choose a fine, light cloth that drapes: white muslin and cambric for day, satin and net for evening, velvet or twill outside.

## Construction

- **Methods:** waist seam under the bust · drawstring at the neck and the waist · straight panels gathered at the back · short stays or a front-laced bodice · hem band worked in white embroidery
- **Closures:** Ties and drawstrings at the back; later buttons or hooks at the back of the bodice.
- **Details:** Puff sleeves, a low neckline, a sash under the bust, a spencer jacket or a cashmere shawl, and a small turban or bonnet.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** matte · embroidered · block-printed
- **Pattern:** White on white: sprigs and borders in tambour work on the muslin; evening dress in plain satin with a silver or gold trim.
- **Gloss:** Muslin at 2–5 GU; satin evening dress at 40–60 GU.
- **Wear:** Fine muslin tears and yellows; a dress is washed and re-starched, and fine pieces show mends.

### Tambour embroidery (skill)

A fast chain stitch made with a fine hook on cloth that is stretched tight in a frame; later the same hook sews beads on silk. Origin: fashion, France and Britain, c. 1760.

- Stretch the cloth tight in a round frame.
- Pull a loop of thread up through the cloth with the hook, and chain the loops along the pattern.
- Thread beads on the yarn first, and chain them into the cloth to make a beaded surface.
