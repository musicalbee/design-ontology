# Americana workwear

> Work dress earns its form by use. Each seam, pocket and rivet answers a failure that the worker met, and the garment looks better when it is old.

The hard-wearing dress of miners, railmen and farmers. An indigo denim overall or a duck canvas coat, with copper rivets at the stress points, built for price and long wear.

**Period:** 1873–1955 · **Era:** Victorian dress · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** American work clothes, Frontier workwear, Denim and duck work dress

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Americana workwear, you MUST:

1. Rivet each stress point: the pocket corners and the base of the fly.
2. Weave the cloth heavy: 400 to 600 g per square metre in denim, 500 g or more in duck.
3. Double-stitch the felled seams so that no seam opens under load.
4. Set the pocket where the hand falls: two on the chest, two at the hip, one for the rule.
5. Cut the bib or jacket with room to bend and reach, and size it by use, not by fashion.
6. Dye the warp in indigo and leave the weft white, so that the cloth fades along the wear.
7. Use brass or copper hardware that does not rust.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A straight, roomy garment. Bib overalls with a front pocket, a boxy chore coat with large patch pockets, and a straight leg that clears a boot.
- **Line:** A straight shoulder, a wide sleeve, a short boxy body and a straight, uncut hem.
- **Fit:** Loose in the seat, the thigh and the arm so that the worker can climb, kneel and lift; the garment shrinks on the first wash.
- **Proportion:** Jacket to the top of the hip; trouser inseam 80 to 84 cm; roughly 1 to 1 chest to hip.
- **Rules:**
  - Place a patch pocket with a bar tack on each side of the chest.
  - Cut a straight leg with a rise that lets the wearer bend.
  - Fix a metal button or a rivet at each stress point.
  - Add a hammer loop and a rule pocket on the trouser.
  - Keep the front closed with a row of metal buttons or a bib.

## Fabrics & materials

- **Primary:** denim · canvas
- **Secondary:** corduroy · flannel · chino-twill · khaki-drill · vegetable-tanned-leather · copper · brass
- **Avoid:** satin · taffeta · tulle · ripstop-nylon · polar-fleece · patent-leather
- **Rule:** Choose a heavy cotton twill or plain weave: indigo denim for the trouser and the overall, duck or corduroy for the coat. The cloth must take hard wear and age well.

## Construction

- **Methods:** flat-felled double-needle seams · copper rivet at each pocket corner · bar tack at each stress point · indigo-dyed warp, white weft · heavy cotton duck for the coat · chain-stitched hems on a loop-stitch machine
- **Closures:** Brass or iron tack buttons, a button fly, and a metal buckle at the back of the waist.
- **Details:** Pocket rivets of copper, an arcuate stitch on the pocket, a hammer loop, a rule pocket and a leather or paper patch at the waist.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** indigo-dyed · sanforised · distressed · matte · stonewashed
- **Pattern:** Plain indigo, a plain tan duck, and a hickory-style stripe; no print.
- **Gloss:** Denim and duck at 1–6 GU, dry and flat; wear brings a slight polish at the knee and the seat.
- **Wear:** Raw denim fades along the creases and the seams, and the shape of the wearer's pocket content shows through; the owner repairs with a patch.

### Copper rivet reinforcement (process)

A small copper rivet set at a point of strain, such as a pocket corner or the base of a fly. The seam does not tear. Origin: fashion, Reno, Nevada, 1873.

- Find the seam that tears first under load, such as the corner of a pocket.
- Punch a hole through both layers of cloth at the end of the seam.
- Set a copper rivet through the hole and close it with a die.
