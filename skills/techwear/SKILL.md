# Techwear

> The city is a hard environment. Dress for it as for a mountain, with a shell, a system of pockets and straps, and a black, sealed surface.

City dress made of technical outdoor cloth. A black membrane shell with taped seams, many pockets, straps and quick buckles, with a cut for motion in the street.

**Period:** 2002–now · **Era:** Contemporary · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Tech apparel, Urban technical wear, Function-first street wear

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Techwear, you MUST:

1. Cut the shell from a 3-layer membrane laminate and tape each seam.
2. Build 10 to 20 pockets into the garment so that no bag is needed.
3. Use a metal or a polymer quick-release buckle, rated for the load.
4. Cut articulated knees and elbows that follow a 3-D pattern.
5. Dye the whole garment black or in a dark neutral for a single surface.
6. Add a modular system: a hood, a detachable collar and a snap-in layer.
7. Fit a water-resistant zip with a storm flap along each seam line.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A sculpted, articulated black shell and a tapered trouser with many pockets, straps and a quick buckle.
- **Line:** A sharp, structured shoulder, a long, articulated sleeve and a tapered ankle.
- **Fit:** Roomy but cut in 3-D; a tapered leg and a close hem keep the line clean.
- **Proportion:** Jacket to the hip or the thigh; roughly 1 to 1.1 chest to hip; tapered ankle of 16 to 20 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Make the garment in black or a single dark neutral.
  - Tape the seams and seal the zips on each shell.
  - Add a removable hood and a high collar that closes over the chin.
  - Place the pockets so that the arm reaches them with no strain.
  - Use a quick buckle at the waist, the ankle and the cuff.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Garment dyeing (process)

A garment is dyed after it is sewn, in a drum. Cloth, thread and tape take one colour and each piece differs a little. Origin: fashion, Italy, c. 1980.

- Sew the garment from undyed cloth and thread that takes dye.
- Tumble the garment in a drum of dye and water.
- Rinse, dry and finish the garment, and check the colour.

### Membrane lamination (material)

A thin membrane with pores that block water drops. Pass water vapour, bonded between a face cloth and a liner to make a shell cloth. Origin: fashion, Newark, Delaware, 1976.

- Stretch the membrane of PTFE until it holds billions of pores in each square centimetre.
- Bond the membrane between a nylon face cloth and a liner.
- Tape each seam of the finished garment, so that no water enters at a stitch.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Instagram** (platform, web, 2010–now), published the style. A photo and video app. Home and food images spread on it, and it carried trends such as dopamine decor and the film grain look.
- **Stone Island** (company, Italy, 1982–now), made the style. The label of Massimo Osti, launched in 1982. It tested new cloths and garment dyes on sport and military shapes, and it set the base of techwear.
- **W. L. Gore and Associates** (company, Newark, Delaware, 1958–now), made the style. The firm of Wilbert and Robert Gore. It made the Gore-Tex membrane and, from 1976, laminated it to cloth for shells.
