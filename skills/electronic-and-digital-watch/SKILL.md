# Electronic and digital watch

> A watch is an electronic instrument, so it shows the time as digits. A crystal with no moving parts keeps the time.

The watch with no hands and no spring. A quartz crystal keeps the time, and a red light or a grey liquid crystal shows the digits. The Seiko Astron of 1969 and the Pulsar of 1972 set it.

**Period:** 1969–1989 · **Era:** Youthquake · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Quartz watch, LED watch, LCD watch, Digital watch

1. Keep the time with a quartz crystal that vibrates 32,768 times each second.
2. Show the time as digits in red LED or in black on a grey LCD, with a seven segment shape for each digit.
3. Make the case in steel, gold or plastic, as a flat, bold shape with a square or a cushion form.
4. Hide the display until the user presses a button on the case side, for the early LED models.
5. Add functions in rows of buttons: date, alarm, stopwatch and a second time zone.
6. Make the case 30 to 45 mm wide and 8 to 12 mm thick, with a flat top and a clean face.
7. Use a black or a dark red display glass with no hands and no markers.
8. Price the watch by its function, so that a watch with more functions costs more.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A flat case in a square, cushion or tonneau form. It has a blank black face and buttons on the side.
- **Line:** Straight edges with rounded corners, a single plane on the top and a slim profile.
- **Fit:** The watch lies flat on the wrist on a bracelet or a thin strap.
- **Proportion:** Case thickness to width is about 1 to 4. The display fills about 50 per cent of the face. The digits are 6 to 8 mm tall.
- **Rules:**
  - Use one display, one font and no dial print apart from small labels.
  - Place the buttons on the side, in a line, with the same size.
  - Make the case in one tone, so that the display provides the only contrast.
  - Keep the face free of marks except for brand and function labels.

### Quartz movement (technology)

A timekeeper in which a quartz crystal vibrates at a fixed rate, and a circuit counts the vibrations to move the hands or to show digits. Origin: industrial design, New York, United States, 1927.

- Cut a quartz crystal to vibrate at 32,768 Hz.
- Divide that rate in a circuit down to one pulse each second.
- Drive a stepping motor or a display with each pulse.
