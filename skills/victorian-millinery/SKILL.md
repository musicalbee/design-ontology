# Victorian millinery

> A hat completes a lady's dress, and the trim shows the skill of the milliner.

The women's hats and bonnets of Queen Victoria's reign. A wire and buckram frame carried silk, velvet, felt or straw, trimmed by hand with ribbon, flowers and feathers. The shape followed the dress, from deep poke bonnet to small tilted hat.

**Period:** 1840–1900 · **Era:** Victorian dress · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Victorian hats and bonnets, Poke bonnet and trimmed hat

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Victorian millinery, you MUST:

1. Build the shape on a wire and buckram frame, then cover it with cloth cut on the bias.
2. Cut the brim of a poke bonnet 8 to 12 cm deep in the 1840s, so that the face looks out of a tunnel.
3. Shrink the bonnet to a small hat at the back of the head by the 1860s, as the hair is dressed higher.
4. Tie the strings under the chin in a silk bow 4 to 6 cm wide.
5. Mass the trim at one side or at the front: flowers, ribbon loops, a plume or a bird.
6. Match the cloth of the trim to the dress of the wearer.
7. Use felt for winter, silk for the evening and plaited straw for summer.
8. Sew the lining and the head band by hand.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A small frame that stands high or far forward on smooth hair, with the trim massed at one side.
- **Line:** A curved brim line that frames the face, and a crown that slopes back to the nape.
- **Fit:** The hat perches on the head and is held by ribbon strings under the chin or by a long hat pin.
- **Proportion:** Poke brim 8–12 cm deep in the 1840s; bonnet width 20–24 cm; crown 8–10 cm high; trim up to half the height of the hat.
- **Rules:**
  - Frame the face with the brim, and keep the hair smooth under it.
  - Shorten the brim and raise the crown as the decades pass, until the bonnet is a small hat in the 1870s.
  - Place the main trim at the front or at one side, never at the centre back.
  - Fill the space between the brim and the cheek with a ruche of tulle or lace.

## Construction

- **Methods:** wire and buckram frame · felt steamed and blocked on a wooden block · bias-cut silk covering · hand-made silk flowers · ribbon bows sewn by hand · hand-sewn lining and head band
- **Closures:** Ribbon strings tied under the chin, or a long hat pin through the crown.
- **Details:** A ruche of tulle at the brim edge, a curtain at the nape and a bow at the side. Flowers, a plume or a bird trim the crown.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** matte · glazed · embroidered · beaded · synthetic-dyed · fulled

- **Wear:** A new, crisp trim is the aim; sun fades silk, and a limp bow or a crushed brim ends the life of the hat.

### Hat blocking (process)

A milliner steams a felt or straw hat body and pulls it over a carved wooden block, so that it dries in the shape of the crown and the brim. Origin: fashion, England, c. 1800.

- Soften the hat body with steam or hot water.
- Pull the body down over the block and tie it at the crown line with a cord.
- Dry it on the block, then trim the brim edge and sew in the lining.
