# Power dressing

> Dress is a uniform of authority. A woman at work wears the wide shoulder and the sharp line of the man's suit, and so claims the same standing.

The working woman's suit of the 1980s. It has a tailored jacket with wide padded shoulders, a skirt or trousers in wool and a silk blouse with a bow. The colour is strong and plain.

**Period:** 1980–1990 · **Era:** Power and subculture · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Power suit, Dress for success, Shoulder-pad suit, 1980s office wear

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Power dressing, you MUST:

1. Pad the shoulder so that it measures 2 to 4 cm wider than the natural shoulder line.
2. Cut the jacket to the hip, with a nipped waist or a straight box line.
3. Set the skirt hem at the knee, and cut the trouser straight with a pleat.
4. Choose a fine worsted wool, gabardine or crepe in a plain colour.
5. Pair the jacket with a silk blouse, a bow or a shirt, and keep the neck covered.
6. Match the jacket and the skirt or trouser in one cloth.
7. Finish with a gold button, a belt, a plain pump and a structured bag.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** An inverted triangle: a wide, padded shoulder, a narrow waist or straight body and a skirt or trouser that is slim to the knee.
- **Line:** A strong horizontal shoulder, a hip-length jacket, and a hem at the knee.
- **Fit:** Close at the waist and the hip; the shoulder stands out from the body on its pad.
- **Proportion:** Shoulder pad 1 to 2 cm thick; jacket to the top of the thigh; skirt hem at mid-knee; lapel 8 to 10 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Square the shoulder with a pad in the jacket and in the blouse.
  - Nip the jacket at the waist, or cut it straight in a long box shape.
  - Keep the skirt slim and at the knee.
  - Wear a bow or a plain shirt at the neck.
  - Match the colour of the jacket and the skirt.

### Shoulder padding (process)

A pad of wadding or foam that is set in the sleeve head of a jacket or a blouse, so that the shoulder line is wider and square. Origin: fashion, Paris, France, and Hollywood, United States, c. 1930.

- Cut a pad of wadding or foam, 1 to 2 cm thick, to the shape of the shoulder.
- Sew the pad into the sleeve head between the lining and the cloth.
- Press the shoulder so that the line is square and level.
