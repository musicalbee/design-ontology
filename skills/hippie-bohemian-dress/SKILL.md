# Hippie bohemian dress

> Dress is free of the factory and of the rules of the shop: handmade, worldly, loose on the body and rich in colour.

The loose, layered dress of the counterculture from 1966 to 1975. It has long cotton and velvet, Indian prints, embroidery, tie-dye, flared jeans, suede waistcoats and the Afghan coat.

**Period:** 1966–1975 · **Era:** Youthquake · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Hippie fashion, Flower power dress, Boho dress, Summer of Love dress

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Hippie bohemian dress, you MUST:

1. Cut the dress loose from the shoulder, with no darts, and let it fall to the ankle or the mid-calf.
2. Cut the flare of the trouser from the knee, so that it measures 25 to 30 cm at the hem.
3. Choose soft natural cloth, such as Indian cotton muslin, velvet or suede, and not a stiff synthetic.
4. Layer three or more pieces, such as a waistcoat, a blouse and a long skirt or a dress.
5. Dye by hand with a tie-dye or an indigo bath, so that no two pieces match.
6. Decorate with embroidery, beads, patches and block-printed motifs from India and the Middle East.
7. Wear the piece worn and mended; add bells, a headband and bare feet or sandals.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A loose, long column or a gathered peasant shape over flared trousers, with layers that hide the waist.
- **Line:** A soft shoulder, a high or no waist, wide sleeves, and a hem at the ankle.
- **Fit:** Free and flowing; the cloth hangs from the shoulder or the yoke and moves with the walker.
- **Proportion:** Hem at the ankle or 15 to 25 cm below the knee. Flare 25 to 30 cm at the hem. Sleeve cuff 12 to 20 cm wide.
- **Rules:**
  - Gather the skirt at a yoke so that it falls in soft folds.
  - Cut the sleeve wide and long, in a bell or a bishop shape.
  - Wear a short suede or embroidered waistcoat over a loose blouse.
  - Flare the jean from the knee and wear it low on the hip.
  - Add a long scarf, beads and a band for the hair.

## Construction

- **Methods:** gathered yoke with no darts · hand embroidery on the yoke and cuff · tie-dye and indigo vat dyeing · patchwork of printed cloth · crochet and macramé trim · mending and patching by hand
- **Closures:** Ties, drawstrings, wooden toggles and a few buttons; often none at all.
- **Details:** Bell sleeves, a lace-up front, bells and beads, fringe on the suede, and a patch on the knee of the jean.

## Surface & finish

- **Pattern:** Paisley and floral block prints, tie-dye bursts, embroidery in bright wool, and patchwork.
- **Gloss:** Cotton and velvet at 2–10 GU; suede at 1–3 GU; the style has no high gloss.
- **Wear:** The style welcomes wear: faded dye, mended cuffs and a soft, washed cloth.

### Tie-dye (process)

A way to colour cloth in which parts are tied, folded or bound before the dye bath, so that the bound parts resist the dye. Origin: fashion, Nara, Japan, c. 750.

- Fold, twist or gather the wet cloth, and bind it with string or rubber bands.
- Soak the cloth in a dye bath, or apply dye to each section.
- Rinse, untie and dry the cloth; each piece shows a different pattern.
