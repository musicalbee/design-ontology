# Vintage Coachbuilt Car

> A fine car is two works. The maker builds the chassis. The coachbuilder makes the body and the cabin to the taste of one buyer.

The pre-war luxury car. A long bonnet on a bare chassis, with a body that a coachbuilder made to order in aluminium over wood. The cabin is leather, walnut and cloth.

**Period:** 1919–1939 · **Era:** Interwar modern · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Coachbuilt classic, Vintage luxury car, Pre-war grand car

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Vintage Coachbuilt Car, you MUST:

1. Make the bonnet 40 to 50 percent of the car length, with the radiator upright at its front.
2. Build the body on an ash frame and clad it in hand-beaten aluminium panels.
3. Keep the mudguards separate from the body, in a long sweep from each wheel.
4. Fit the cabin with burr walnut veneer on the dashboard and the door caps.
5. Trim the seats in hide, and the rear compartment in wool cloth or silk damask.
6. Chrome or nickel plate the lamps, the radiator and the bumpers, and keep the plate bright.
7. Paint in two tones, one for the body and one for the mudguards or the roof, with a coach line between.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A long, narrow car with an upright radiator, a very long bonnet, separate, long curved mudguards and a compact cabin set well back.
- **Line:** A strong horizontal line from the radiator to the cabin, a curved running board and a rear that tapers or ends in a spare wheel.
- **Fit:** The cabin sits behind the rear axle line of the engine; the occupants sit low, in an upright formal posture.
- **Proportion:** Bonnet length 40 to 50 percent of the car; wheelbase 3.3 to 4.3 m; body height about 1.7 m.
- **Rules:**
  - Set the driver far back, behind a long bonnet and a flat scuttle.
  - Run a coach line along the waist and the bonnet in a second colour.
  - Mount big separate headlamps on the front mudguards or on a bar.
  - Hold the rear compartment for the passengers behind a glass partition in a limousine.

## Construction

- **Methods:** ash frame cut to a full-size drawing · aluminium panels beaten over a wood buck · burr walnut veneer matched in pairs · hand-sewn hide and pleated cloth · nickel and chrome plating of the brightwork · many coats of hand-rubbed cellulose paint
- **Closures:** Doors hinged at the rear edge; plated handles; a folding hood with a plated frame on a tourer.
- **Details:** A radiator mascot, a pair of big lamps and a luggage trunk at the rear. A silk blind covers the back window. The dashboard panel is engine-turned.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** polished · guilloche · gloss-paint · burnished · engraved · gilded

## Techniques

### Chrome plating (process)

A thin layer of chromium is put on steel by electrolysis. The layer gives a hard, bright surface that does not tarnish. Origin: fashion, New York, United States, c. 1924.

- Clean the steel part and plate it with copper and nickel.
- Put the part in a chromic acid bath and pass a current through it.
- Buff the part to a mirror finish.

### Coachbuilding (skill)

The making of a car body by hand on a wood frame, and the fit of that body to a chassis that the client bought. Origin: fashion, Europe, c. 1600.

- Build the body frame from ash timber and join it with metal brackets.
- Beat the aluminium or steel panels over a wood form and fit them to the frame.
- Fit the trim, the upholstery and the brightwork, then paint the body in many coats.
