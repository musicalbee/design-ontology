# Kyo-yaki overglaze

> A Kyoto potter can make tea ware as rich as a screen painting: a thin body, a pale crackle glaze, enamel and gold.

Fine pottery from Kyoto that Nonomura Ninsei made at the kiln of Omuro. A thin crackle glaze takes enamel colours and gold in painted designs of flowers, hills and seasons.

**Period:** 1650–1750 · **Era:** The porcelain race · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Kyō ware, Kyoto ware, Ninsei ware, Kyoto enamelled ware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Kyo-yaki overglaze, you MUST:

1. Throw a thin, smooth form on the wheel, with a wall of 3 to 5 mm.
2. Cover the form with a pale crackle glaze that is fired first at about 1100 °C.
3. Paint the design in enamel colours and gold on the fired glaze, and fire again at 800 °C.
4. Place the design to fit the form: wisteria down a tall jar, peony round a water jar.
5. Keep part of the glaze plain, so that the painted area has room.
6. Use the Kyoto court palette: green, blue, red, purple, black and gold.
7. Sign the base with the maker's seal, as the Omuro kiln did.
8. Make each piece one of a small set, for a tea master or a temple.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Tall tea jars, water jars, incense burners and tea bowls with a smooth round body. Each form gives a large field for the painter.
- **Profile:** A tall jar rises in a smooth shoulder and a short neck; a water jar is a round drum with two lugs; a bowl has a soft curve and a thin rim.
- **Foot and rim:** A small, neat foot ring with the seal of the kiln; the rim is thin and round, and a lid in matching ware or in lacquer fits the mouth.
- **Proportion:** A tea jar is 15 to 20 cm high; the wall is 3 to 5 mm thick, so the form is light and the glaze is thin.
- **Rules:** Throw a thin, round, clean form. · Paint a flowing design that wraps round the form. · Leave one third of the glaze plain. · Add gold to flowers and leaf edges.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stoneware
- **Secondary:** earthenware
- **Avoid:** bone-china · terracotta · raku-clay
- **Rule:** Use a fine, pale Kyoto clay that fires hard and takes a thin glaze. Keep a coarse rough clay out: the thin glaze needs a smooth ground.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the kick wheel · turning the wall thin when leather-hard · fitting the lid on the wheel · painting the design with a fine brush on the fired glaze · applying gold leaf or gold paint on the enamel
- **Joins:** Lugs and knobs are made separately and luted with slip; the lid is thrown with the jar and ground to fit.
- **Details:** A fine crackle in the glaze, a painted seal on the base, and gold highlights on flowers and leaves.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** crackle-glaze · clear-feldspathic · lead-glaze · bare-clay
- **Decorations:** overglaze-enamel · gilding · iron-painting · undecorated
- **Pattern:** Wisteria, peony, pine and plum, hills and seasons in enamel green, blue, red and purple with gold lines on a cream crackle glaze.
- **Gloss:** Satin to soft gloss, about 40 to 80 GU; the enamel stands a little above the glaze.
- **Wear:** The enamel and the gold wear first at the rim and at the high parts of the painted flowers; the crackle takes a stain from tea.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Cream ground, a rich enamel palette of green, blue, red, purple and black, and gold lines. The painting uses 4 to 6 colours.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A small climbing kiln at the Omuro kiln near Ninna-ji temple, and a small muffle kiln for the enamel firing.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** bisque firing of the thrown and turned form · dipping in a clear crackle glaze · glaze firing at about 1100 °C · painting in enamel and gold · enamel firing at about 800 °C
- **Scale:** A few master potters, with a small workshop, made each piece for tea masters, temples and the court of Kyoto.
- **Signs:** A fine crackle, a raised line of enamel, a gold edge and a seal on the base.

### Overglaze enamelling (process)

Coloured glass enamels are painted on a glazed and fired piece. A third firing at a low heat fixes them on the glaze. Origin: ceramics, Jingdezhen, Jiangxi, China, c. 1465.

- Fire the piece with a clear glaze.
- Paint the design with enamels of red, green, yellow and blue.
- Fire it again in a small muffle kiln at 750 to 850 °C.
- Add gold in a last low firing, when the design needs it.
