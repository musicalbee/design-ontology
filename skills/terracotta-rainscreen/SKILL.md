# Terracotta rainscreen

> A wall of fired clay can be a light, ventilated skin: the panel keeps the rain from the insulation, and the air behind it dries the wall.

A modern wall skin of extruded, hollow terracotta panels that hang on rails with an air gap behind them. The clay face is matt, flat and in earth colours, with a thin glaze or an engobe.

**Period:** 1990 to the present · **Era:** Plural and contemporary · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Ventilated terracotta facade, Extruded terracotta cladding, Baguette terracotta facade

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Terracotta rainscreen, you MUST:

1. Extrude the panel hollow in a die; the wall of the panel is 15 to 30 mm thick.
2. Hang each panel on a rail of stainless steel or aluminium, with an air gap of 20 to 50 mm.
3. Keep the joint open, 6 to 10 mm, so that the wall can drain and dry.
4. Use a matt face in earth colours: red, brown, buff, grey and white.
5. Make the panel in a width of 300 to 600 mm and a length up to 1.5 m, to match the grid of the building.
6. Fire the panel once; the engobe or the glaze fires together with the body.
7. Use baguettes or fins for a facade with depth and shadow.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Hollow, extruded panels, flat or with a profile; baguettes, fins, louvres and tile planks that hang in a regular grid.
- **Profile:** A flat or lightly ribbed face with a sharp edge; the panel section is a hollow rectangle with webs and a groove for the clip.
- **Foot and rim:** The top and bottom edges have grooves for a rail clip; the sides are square and open at the joint, with a small drip edge.
- **Proportion:** A panel is about 300 to 600 mm wide, 600 to 1500 mm long and 30 to 40 mm deep; the wall is 15 to 30 mm thick.
- **Rules:** Make the panel hollow and extruded. · Hang it on a rail with an air gap. · Keep the joint open and straight. · Use a matt face and one earth colour for the wall.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** terracotta
- **Secondary:** stoneware · stainless-steel · steel-frame
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · jasperware · parian · fired-brick
- **Rule:** Use a dense, pure terracotta clay with fine grain, so that the extruded panel fires true and stays flat. Keep the colour even in each batch.

## Forming

- **Methods:** extrusion through a die · wire cutting to length · controlled drying · single firing with the glaze or engobe
- **Joins:** Each panel hangs on a clip and rail of stainless steel; no mortar binds the panels, and the joint stays open for drainage.
- **Details:** A fine ribbed back, a groove for the clip, a very sharp edge and a slight colour change from one firing to the next.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** bare-clay · matte-glaze · satin-glaze
- **Decorations:** undecorated · digital-inkjet · relief-moulding
- **Pattern:** Plain fields of one colour in a regular grid; some panels carry a fine groove, a baguette profile or a printed image in a ceramic ink.
- **Gloss:** Matt to satin, about 5 to 35 GU; the face reflects little light and shows the grain of the clay.
- **Wear:** The panel keeps its colour and shape for decades; a dirty film washes off in rain, and a damaged panel can be swapped.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Earth colours in clean fields: red, brown, buff, grey and white, with a matt face; the facade gets variety from the grid, the depth and the shadow.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A roller or tunnel kiln with a controlled curve; the panels run through in one firing, and the heat is the same for each load.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** extrusion and drying of the panel · application of an engobe or a glaze · a single firing at about 1100 °C · cutting and drilling to size
- **Scale:** Factories in Germany and elsewhere make panels by the thousand square metres; a single building can use more than 5,000 m².
- **Signs:** A ribbed back, a rail groove at the edge and an open joint with a shadow.

### Ceramic rainscreen (process)

A wall skin of hollow clay panels on rails, with an air gap behind. The panels keep rain off the wall, and the air dries it. Origin: ceramics, Germany, c. 1990.

- Extrude the panel hollow in a die, with a wall of 15 to 30 mm.
- Fire the panel once, with an engobe or a glaze if the design needs it.
- Hang each panel on a clip and a rail of stainless steel.
- Keep an air gap of 20 to 50 mm and an open joint of 6 to 10 mm.
