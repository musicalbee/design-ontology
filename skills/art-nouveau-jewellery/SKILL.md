# Art Nouveau Jewellery

> A jewel is a work of art: the design, the enamel and the line count for more than the value of the diamonds.

Jewels of the whiplash line. They show women, insects, orchids and dragonflies in enamel, glass, horn and opal. The design counts for more than the price of the stones.

**Period:** 1895–1914 · **Era:** Victorian dress · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Jugendstil jewellery, Lalique-style jewels, Plique-a-jour jewellery

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Art Nouveau Jewellery, you MUST:

1. Draw the piece in one flowing, asymmetrical line that comes from a stem, a wave or a lock of hair.
2. Choose a subject from nature or from a woman's body: a dragonfly, an orchid, a poppy, a swan, a head with loose hair.
3. Use enamel in many thin layers, and use plique-a-jour with no back plate, so that light passes through the colour.
4. Mix precious and cheap materials by their look: horn, glass and opal beside gold and diamonds.
5. Keep diamonds small and use them as a point of light, not as the main form.
6. Set a large opal, a moonstone or a piece of carved glass at the centre of the piece.
7. Make each piece a unique work and sign it on the front or the back.
8. Hang a pendant on a fine chain so that the piece moves with the body.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A large, flowing piece: a dragonfly corsage ornament, a pendant with a woman's head, or a hair comb with a spray of leaves.
- **Line:** A whiplash curve, asymmetrical and organic, with long stems and wings and no straight edge.
- **Fit:** Pinned at the corsage, hung on a long chain, or set in the hair as a comb.
- **Proportion:** A corsage ornament is 8 to 15 cm wide; a pendant is 5 to 9 cm tall; a comb is 10 to 15 cm tall.
- **Rules:**
  - Let one curved line run through the whole piece.
  - Fit a plique-a-jour panel in each wing or leaf, inside a thin gold wire frame.
  - Carve a horn comb with a leaf or an insect in relief.
  - Set one large opal or a glass cabochon at the centre.

## Construction

- **Methods:** plique-a-jour enamel in a gold wire cell · layers of translucent enamel on gold · carved horn with a gold mount · small diamonds set as points of light · hand engraving and chasing of the gold · a single carved glass or opal as the centre stone
- **Closures:** A pin with a safety catch on a brooch; a wire loop and a chain on a pendant; teeth on a comb.
- **Details:** A woman's head, an insect with open wings, a spray of orchid or poppy, a long drop at the base and a signed back.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** plique-a-jour · enamelled · carved · engraved · cabochon · faceted
- **Pattern:** Flowing stems, wings and hair; no repeat and no straight border.
- **Gloss:** High on enamel and gold, 70 to 95 GU; horn and opal give a soft, deep glow.
- **Wear:** Plique-a-jour enamel is thin and breaks if knocked; horn dries and cracks if kept in heat.

### Vitreous enamelling (material)

A thin coat of glass that is fused to iron or steel at about 800 °C. It makes a hard, bright, washable surface that resists heat and rust. Origin: industrial design, Central Europe, c. 1850.

- Clean the metal and coat it with a ground layer of glass powder and water.
- Fire the part at 760 to 840 °C until the glass melts into one skin.
- Apply a top coat of 0.1 to 0.3 mm in white or a single colour and fire it again.
- Leave the edges rolled: the enamel chips at a sharp edge and shows the dark metal.
