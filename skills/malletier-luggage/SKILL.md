# Malletier luggage

> A trunk is a small room that a traveller trusts, and each part of it serves a use.

The travel trunks of the French malletier. A light wooden frame carries coated canvas or leather, bound with brass and fitted to its use. The flat lid stacks in a ship's hold or on a train.

**Period:** 1854–1939 · **Era:** Victorian dress · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Trunk-maker's luggage, Flat-topped travel trunk

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Malletier luggage, you MUST:

1. Make the lid flat, so that trunks stack 3 high in a hold or a luggage van.
2. Build the frame of poplar wood, 8 to 12 mm thick, light and strong.
3. Cover the frame in canvas coated with grey lacquer, so that it sheds rain.
4. Bind the edges and corners with brass and secure them with 4 to 6 mm nails.
5. Fit a tumbler lock and two or four latches in brass.
6. Fit the inside to the use: a wardrobe trunk with rails, a bed trunk, a hat trunk, a tray for linen.
7. Line the inside with striped linen cloth, and add initials by hand.
8. Repeat a stripe, a check or a monogram on the canvas, so that a porter knows the trunk at a glance.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A rigid box with a flat lid, square corners and a small handle at each end.
- **Line:** A straight edge bound in brass, with the lid line at about one third of the height.
- **Fit:** The trunk is not carried far: porters lift it by its side handles, and it travels flat on a cart or a ship's floor.
- **Proportion:** Steamer trunk about 90 cm long, 50 cm wide and 35 cm high; wardrobe trunk up to 110 cm high.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the top flat and the corners square, so that trunks stack.
  - Cover each corner and each edge in brass or in leather binding.
  - Divide the inside into trays, so that the owner packs without a heap.
  - Pad the lid and the base with canvas and a wooden slat frame to protect the content.

### Coated canvas (material, invented here)

Cotton or linen canvas covered with lacquer, paint or resin, so that it sheds rain, wipes clean and takes a printed pattern. Origin: fashion, Paris, France, 1854.

- Stretch the canvas flat and apply the coat in thin layers, 3 or more.
- Dry each layer, then stove or press the surface to a smooth finish.
- Print or stripe the pattern before the last coat, so that the coat protects it.
