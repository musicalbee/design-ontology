# Scandinavian modern product

> A good object is a plain tool for a person: it is made of local wood and wool, shaped to the hand and the body, and built to last for decades.

Everyday objects of bent birch, teak, oak and wool from the Nordic workshops: low, soft in outline and made for use. Craft joints are kept, and makers sell to ordinary homes.

**Period:** 1930–1975 · **Era:** Machine modern · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Danish modern, Nordic functionalism, Swedish Grace to Scandinavian modern

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Scandinavian modern product, you MUST:

1. Use solid wood or bent plywood in its own colour: birch, beech, oak, ash or teak, with an oiled or soaped surface.
2. Round each edge that a hand or an arm touches to a radius of 4 to 15 mm.
3. Cut each joint by hand or machine to a fine fit, and show it where it carries load.
4. Shape each seat and arm to the body, with a curve in at least one plane.
5. Keep ornament to one detail: a grain pattern, a woven seat or a brass part.
6. Use wool, linen or leather in undyed or muted colours for the soft parts.
7. Make glass lamp shades in 2 or 3 layers so that no bulb is in direct view.
8. Draw each object for series production in a small workshop, and offer it at a price that a family can pay.

## Form

- **Geometry:** Soft rectangles, long curves and tapered legs; the plane of a seat or a top is clear, and each leg is thinner at the foot.
- **Silhouette:** A low, light outline: a thin top on slim, splayed or tapered legs, with air under the body.
- **Edges:** Rounded edges with a radius of 4 to 15 mm on all parts that touch the body; a tapered leg ends in a small chamfer.
- **Proportion:** A dining chair seat is 45 cm high; a sideboard is 70 to 80 cm high and 150 to 200 cm long, on legs of 15 to 25 cm.
- **Ornament:** Almost none. Detail comes from the grain, the joint, a woven cord seat or a brass sleeve on a leg.
- **Rules:**
  - Each leg or arm tapers by 20% to 40% from top to foot.
  - Each wood part is solid or laminated wood in one species; the object has at most 2 species.
  - A visible joint is a finger joint, a through tenon or a dowel.
  - A lamp shade has 2 or 3 layers that hide the light source.
  - A coloured part covers less than 15% of the object.

## Finish & texture

- **Finishes:** oiled · soaped · lacquered · natural-fibre · brushed · clear-gloss · matte-glaze · acid-etched

- **Gloss:** 3–15 GU on wood; 60–90 GU on glass and metal parts

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** brass
- **Section:** Stems and legs of metal 10–16 mm; wooden legs 30–45 mm at the top
- **Joints:** Finger joints, through tenons and dowels, cut to a fine fit and left in view.
- **Handles:** A cut-out grip in the door, a turned wood knob of 25 to 35 mm, or a slim brass or leather pull.
- **Switches and taps:** A white or wooden rocker switch; a single-lever tap with a straight steel spout.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Laminated timber (material)

Thin boards or veneers glued in layers, so that wood forms long spans, bent shapes and large panels. Origin: architecture, Weimar, 1906.

- Glue boards of 20 to 45mm for glulam; set the grain of all layers in one direction.
- Cross the layers at 90° for a panel (CLT); use 3, 5 or 7 layers.
- Bend veneers of 1 to 3mm in a mould for furniture; glue 5 to 9 layers.
- Leave the layered edge in view as a line pattern.

## Media

The salons, schools, magazines, publishers, galleries and platforms that carried this style.

- **Artek** (workshop, Helsinki, 1935–now), made the style. A furniture company that Alvar Aalto and three partners started to sell his bent birch furniture.
- **Stockholm Exhibition** (exhibition, Stockholm, 1930–1930), launched the style. An exhibition of design and housing. Its light, glass pavilions brought functionalism to the Nordic countries.
