# Colour Field Painting

> Colour alone can carry feeling and space: a large, simple field of hue, with no gesture and no drawing, surrounds the viewer and acts at once.

Very large canvases of broad, even fields of colour with soft edges. The colour is the subject; it is stained into the cloth or floated in layers, and it fills the sight of the viewer.

**Period:** 1948–1975 · **Era:** Avant-garde · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Colour field, Post-painterly abstraction, Stain painting

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "colour field", "color field", "rothko", "frankenthaler", "morris louis", "stain painting", "soft edged rectangles", "luminous colour block".

Good for: backdrops and gradients for calm interfaces, wellness, meditation and music cover art, wall art and hotel and lobby identity, title cards with one strong hue.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a clear figure, a product or a message in the image.
- The picture must show drawing, texture or a gesture.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Colour Field Painting, you MUST:

1. Make the canvas large, 2m or more on one side, so that the field fills the view of a person at 1–2m.
2. Divide the picture into 1–3 broad fields of colour, with no drawn line.
3. Soak thin paint into raw or primed cloth, so that the colour is the cloth and no stroke shows.
4. Soften the edge of each field, with a feathered border of 20–60mm, so that the colour seems to glow.
5. Choose hues of close value and strong chroma, so that the fields pulse and hover.
6. Hold the layout simple and frontal, with fields stacked or spread on a plain rectangle.
7. Leave out the figure, the object, the horizon and the gesture.

## Composition

- **Layout:** One to three broad rectangles or stains of colour, stacked or spread across a large frontal canvas, with soft edges and a thin margin of ground.
- **Focal point:** The centre of the largest field, or the edge between two fields, at the middle third of the canvas.
- **Format:** A large canvas of 2m or more, in a vertical or square format at 4:5 to 1:1; the colour reaches or passes the edge.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| scale | Make the canvas 2m or more on one side, so that the field fills 90% or more of the view at a distance of 1.5m. |
| colour-distribution | Divide the area into 2–3 fields with a ratio of 3:2 or 2:1, and let one hue take 50–60%. |
| contrast | Set 2 hues of near value, within 15%, and a hue gap of 60° or more, so that the edge vibrates. |
| balance | Stack the fields in a loose vertical order, with the heaviest field in the lower half or the middle. |
| negative-space | Leave a thin margin of 5–10% of the width where the ground or a quiet hue frames the fields. |
| hierarchy | Make one field the main one at 40–60% of the area, and the others smaller and quieter. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Broad, saturated, luminous fields of one or two hues with a close value, and a quiet ground; the colour is the subject.
- **Distribution:** Main field 40–60%, second field 25–35%, third field or ground 10–20%.
- **Ground:** Raw, unprimed cloth or a thin white ground that the stain enters.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Thin oil or diluted acrylic, poured, rolled or sponged so that it soaks in.
- **Support:** Raw or lightly primed cotton duck, stretched, 2m or more on a side.
- **Brushwork:** No visible stroke; the paint is poured, stained or rolled; a soft brush or sponge blends the edge over 20–60mm.
- **Paint handling:** 1–10 very thin layers of 0.1mm or less, with an even, matt, stained look; no impasto.
- **Edges:** Soft, feathered or stained, with a slight bleed of 20–60mm at the field edge.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** The colour is the light: the fields glow from inside and have no outer source.
- **Modelling:** None; the field is flat, with a slight change of tone over 3–5 layers.
- **Shadow:** None; depth comes from the push and pull of near hues.
- **Temperature:** Warm fields advance and cool fields recede; the pair vibrates at the edge.

## Subject

- **Typical:** Stacked fields of colour, a stain on raw cloth, a hovering rectangle, a pour of colour.
- **Figure scale:** No figure; the field is larger than the viewer.
- **Treatment:** Quiet, total and open; the viewer stands before the colour and takes it in as a place.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Matt, with a soft stained look.
- **Texture:** The weave of the cloth shows; the paint has no ridge.

## Techniques

### Soak-stain (process)

Thin paint is poured on raw, unprimed canvas that lies on the floor; the cloth drinks the colour and the colour and the weave become one. Origin: painting, New York, 1952.

- Thin oil with turpentine, or acrylic with water, until it flows like ink.
- Lay the canvas flat and unprimed on the floor; pour in pools and guide the paint with a sponge or a tilt of the cloth.
- Leave 30% to 60% of the cloth bare so that the raw colour is part of the picture.
- Work at a size of 2 metres or more so that the field is wider than the viewer's sight.
