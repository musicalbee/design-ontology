# Greek black-figure pottery

> A pot is a surface for story: black silhouettes on red clay, made sharp with incised lines, tell a myth in frieze after frieze.

Greek vases in which the painter draws figures in a black gloss slip on the red clay and cuts the details with a sharp point. A three-stage firing makes the black and the red.

**Period:** c. 700–480 BC · **Era:** Ancient clay · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Attic black-figure, Corinthian black-figure

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "black-figure", "greek vase", "attic pottery", "exekias", "amphora painting", "corinthian vase", "ancient greek pottery".

Good for: a museum or educational plate on Greek pottery, a myth scene on a vessel, an amphora or krater in a classical interior prompt.

Do not use it when:
- the brief needs figures that look modelled and three-dimensional
- the brief needs a glazed colour surface

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Greek black-figure pottery, you MUST:

1. Paint each figure as a solid black silhouette in gloss slip on the red clay.
2. Incise the inner lines of the figures with a sharp point to show the red clay below.
3. Add purple-red and white slip for flesh of women, hair, and clothing accents.
4. Divide the pot into panels and friezes that run round the body in horizontal bands.
5. Fire in three stages: oxidation, reduction and re-oxidation, at 800 to 950 °C.
6. Keep the main scene on the shoulder or the belly, and a lotus and palmette band below the handles.
7. Write the name of the painter or the potter in a short line on the vase, when it is signed.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Amphorae, kraters, hydriai and kylikes with a clear division into foot, belly, shoulder, neck and lip.
- **Profile:** The profile is a taut S-curve; an amphora has an ovoid body and a narrow neck, and a kylix has a shallow bowl on a stem.
- **Foot and rim:** A flared or stepped foot, a flat or spreading lip, and two vertical handles that join the neck and the shoulder.
- **Proportion:** Height 25 to 60 cm, wall 3 to 6 mm, and the foot diameter about one third of the maximum diameter.
- **Rules:**
  - Throw large vases in two or three parts and join them at the neck and the foot.
  - Keep the wall thin and even; the form is as precise as the painting.
  - Place the main picture in a rectangular field between the handles.
  - Leave the underside of the foot as the reserved red clay.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware · terracotta
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · bone-china
- **Rule:** Use a fine, iron-rich Attic clay that fires to a warm orange-red. Refine the clay by settling until the wall is smooth and thin.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the wheel in parts · turning the foot when leather-hard · joining parts with slip · hand-pulled handles
- **Joins:** Parts are joined with a thin clay slip; handles are made separately and luted on at the neck and the shoulder.
- **Details:** A reserved line at the foot, a glossy black band at the lip, and a light stilt or finger mark where the pot stood in the kiln.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** attic-gloss
- **Decorations:** black-figure · incised · undecorated
- **Pattern:** Mythic and daily scenes in a frieze, with figures in profile; palmettes and lotus bands fill the zones above and below.
- **Gloss:** The black slip is bright and glassy, about 60 to 80 GU; the red clay is satin.
- **Wear:** The black can flake at the edges and show a dull red-brown; the incised lines stay sharp.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Two colours that the firing makes, black and red, with small touches of purple-red and white for accent.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A wood-fired updraught kiln with a fire chamber below the floor of the pots and a vent at the top.
- **Atmosphere:** mixed
- **Processes:** paint the slip on the leather-hard pot · oxidation phase at 800 °C · reduction phase with green wood to 950 °C · re-oxidation while the kiln cools
- **Scale:** Workshops in the Kerameikos of Athens had a master potter, several painters and many slaves and apprentices.
- **Signs:** A sharp edge between black and red, a small red stain where the black fired in oxidation, and a stilt mark under the foot.

### Three-stage firing (process)

One firing in three stages turns a slip black and leaves the clay red. Air is let in, shut out, and let in again. Origin: ceramics, Corinth, Greece, c. 700 BCE.

- Fire the pot in an open kiln with air, so that the whole pot turns red.
- Close the vents and add green wood, so that the whole pot turns black.
- Open the vents and let the air in again at a lower heat.
- Take out the pot: the fine slip stays black and the bare clay becomes red.
