# Shaker object

> An object that serves its use well is beautiful; the maker adds no ornament, shows each joint and makes each piece with honest care.

The plain household goods of the Shaker communities: ladder-back chairs, peg rails, oval boxes and chests of pine, maple and cherry, built for use with no applied ornament.

**Period:** 1790–1900 · **Era:** Proto-industrial · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Shaker craft, Shaker utility design

## When to load this skill

Good for: a plain, handmade kitchen or hall, a reissue of a peg rail, a box or a chair, a still life of useful objects, a hardware schedule for a farmhouse.

Do not use it when:
- The brief asks for gloss, glamour or ornament.
- The room needs soft, deep upholstery.

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Shaker object, you MUST:

1. Make each object for its use; remove each part that has no job.
2. Use thin, light parts: a chair post of 25 to 32 mm and a rail of 12 to 20 mm.
3. Show the joint: a dovetail, a mortise or a tenon stays in view.
4. Use wood in a plain finish: oil, wax or a thin matte paint in red, yellow, blue or green.
5. Hang each chair on a peg rail of 7 to 9 cm pegs, so that the floor is free for the broom.
6. Make a box or a drawer in 3 to 7 sizes that nest, with swallowtail fingers of thin maple.
7. Keep the line straight and the turning simple: a ball finial and a taper are the only decoration.

## Form

- **Geometry:** Straight lines, rectangles and ovals; the round peg, the tapered post and the oval band are the only curves.
- **Silhouette:** A light, upright outline with open space: a tall narrow chair with 3 to 4 slats, and a plain box case on a thin frame.
- **Edges:** A small, hand-softened edge of 1 to 3 mm; no moulding and no bead.
- **Proportion:** A ladder-back chair is about 100 cm high with a seat at 43 cm; a post is about 1/4 as thick as it is wide in the seat.
- **Ornament:** None. A ball finial on a chair post, and a tapered foot, are the only added forms.
- **Rules:**
  - Each object has 0 applied ornament.
  - Each joint is a plain mortise, tenon or dovetail, and stays in view.
  - A seat is woven of cotton tape, cane or rush, in 1 or 2 colours.
  - Each part is as thin as its load allows; a post is 25 to 32 mm.
  - A painted surface is a matte, flat colour; a bare surface has an oil or wax finish.

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** black
- **Section:** Pegs 25–30 mm; stems and rods 6–12 mm; a hinge 3 mm thick
- **Joints:** Plain dovetails, mortises and tenons in view; wooden pegs and small cut nails.
- **Handles:** A small turned wooden knob of 25 to 30 mm, or a plain wooden peg.
- **Switches and taps:** Before 1880 there is no electric switch; the period-faithful reissue is a cotton-braided cable with a plain wooden turn switch. A tap is a plain iron or brass cock over a stone basin.

## Techniques

### Exposed joinery (process)

A joint that stays in view, so that the way of making is part of the look. Origin: interior, Giza, c. 2500 BCE.

- Cut the joint in the main wood: a through-tenon of 6 to 12mm shows beyond the face.
- Peg the joint with a wooden pin of 8 to 12mm; do not hide it with filler or paint.
- Use one joint type in a room or a building; repeat it at each corner.
