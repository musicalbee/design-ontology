# Japanese modern craft product

> A useful object made with skill from a local material is beautiful without ornament; the maker's hand and the material leave the only decoration.

Japanese household objects of bamboo, paper, clay, iron and plain wood: the mingei idea of beauty in ordinary things, joined to post-war modern design and light industrial series.

**Period:** 1950–present · **Era:** Post-war modern · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Mingei-influenced design, Japanese modern product, Kogei-modern

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Japanese modern craft product, you MUST:

1. Use a local material of plain character: bamboo, washi paper, clay, cast iron, cotton or ash.
2. Leave the mark of the hand in view: throwing rings, a brush stroke, a weave or a tool cut.
3. Join parts without hidden fixings: a joint, a lashing or a single brass rod stays in view.
4. Draw a form from use, in a pure curve or a straight line, with no applied pattern.
5. Keep colours to the natural tones of the material, with indigo, iron brown and unbleached white.
6. Let the object leave space round it: a small body in an open, empty room.
7. Make each object so that it improves with use, and mend it with the same craft.
8. Join the craft method to a light series, so that a household can own the object.

## Form

- **Geometry:** Pure curves and straight lines: the circle, the arc, the square and the plane, in thin sheet, rod and woven strip.
- **Silhouette:** A small, calm outline with open space round it; a stool of two bent shells, a round lantern or a plain bowl.
- **Edges:** Soft radius of 3 to 15 mm on wood and clay; a thin edge on paper and bamboo.
- **Proportion:** A low table is 30 to 40 cm high; a stool is 35 to 40 cm high; a paper lantern is 30 to 60 cm across.
- **Ornament:** None applied. The only pattern is the weave of cane, the rings of the potter's wheel and the grain of the wood.
- **Rules:**
  - Each object has 1 to 3 parts and no applied pattern.
  - Each joint is a lashing, a peg, a tenon or a single metal rod in view.
  - Clay carries the marks of the wheel and a matte, earth-coloured glaze.
  - Paper and bamboo parts keep the natural fibre in view.
  - A colour other than the material tone is indigo, iron black or white.

## Finish & texture

- **Finishes:** oiled · soaped · natural-fibre · piece-dyed · matte-glaze · unglazed · vitreous-glaze · blackened
- **Textures:** open-grain · fine-grain · plain-weave · slub-weave · throwing-rings · speckled-clay · plaited · satin-smooth
- **Gloss:** 0–10 GU on all main surfaces; 20–40 GU on glazed clay
- **Patina:** The style values age: oiled wood darkens, paper yellows, iron takes a dark skin, and a repair is a mark of care.

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** black
- **Section:** Wire and rod 3–8 mm; handles and bails of cast iron 10–15 mm
- **Joints:** Peg and wedge, lashing with cord, or a single brass rod through two bent shells; always in view.
- **Handles:** A cut notch, a bamboo or cane loop, or a plain wooden peg of 20 mm.
- **Switches and taps:** A small square plate of white porcelain or plain wood; a simple cylindrical tap in dark metal.

## Manufacture

- **Processes:** hand throwing and wood-fired glazing of clay · bending and lashing of bamboo · making of washi paper from bark fibre · wood joinery without nails · bending of plywood in a mould · casting of iron in sand
- **Scale:** Workshops and village potteries of a few craftspeople, and short industrial series of a few thousand pieces for a national market.
- **Signs:** Throwing rings in clay, a bamboo lashing, a visible brass rod, a slight irregular glaze and the unbleached colour of paper.

### Exposed joinery (process)

A joint that stays in view, so that the way of making is part of the look. Origin: interior, Giza, c. 2500 BCE.

- Cut the joint in the main wood: a through-tenon of 6 to 12mm shows beyond the face.
- Peg the joint with a wooden pin of 8 to 12mm; do not hide it with filler or paint.
- Use one joint type in a room or a building; repeat it at each corner.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Japan Folk Crafts Museum** (gallery, Tokyo, 1936–now), exhibited the style. A museum of everyday craft that Yanagi Soetsu founded to show and teach the beauty of plain, hand-made objects. It made the mingei idea known to makers and designers.
