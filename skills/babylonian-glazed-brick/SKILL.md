# Babylonian glazed brick

> A mud-brick city wall becomes a royal image when each brick is moulded, glazed blue and fired, so that the animals of the gods stand in a row along the road.

Moulded bricks of fired clay that Neo-Babylonian builders glazed in blue, yellow and white. They set the bricks into walls of lions, bulls and dragons on the gates and the Processional Way of Babylon.

**Period:** 604–539 BC · **Era:** Ancient clay · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Ishtar Gate brick, Neo-Babylonian glazed brick relief

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Babylonian glazed brick, you MUST:

1. Mould each brick as one part of a relief, and number the bricks on the side for the builders.
2. Glaze the visible face in a vivid blue, and keep the figures in white, yellow and black.
3. Set the animals in a rhythm: a lion, a bull or a dragon every 1 to 2 m along the wall.
4. Frame a field of figures with bands of rosettes and a plain blue ground.
5. Lay the bricks in courses, so that the joints of the wall run along the lines of the relief.
6. Fire a moulded brick once more after the glaze; a thin bitumen mortar binds the courses.
7. Face the walls of gates and processional streets only: the ordinary city stays in plain brick.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Rectangular glazed bricks that make up walls, gate towers and wall friezes. Each brick carries part of a moulded animal or a rosette band.
- **Profile:** A flat wall face with low relief; the figures stand 2 to 4 cm proud of the ground and the gate towers step back in rectangular recesses.
- **Foot and rim:** A brick has no foot. The edges are square and sharp so that the courses fit; the top of the wall ends in a stepped crown of glazed brick.
- **Proportion:** A brick is about 30 to 35 cm long and 10 to 12 cm thick; the Ishtar Gate rose to about 14 m and the animals measure about 1 m long.
- **Rules:** Mould the relief in sections that match the brick size. · Keep the ground a flat, deep blue. · Repeat one animal along the wall in strict rows. · Put the border of rosettes above and below the animals.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** fired-brick
- **Secondary:** terracotta
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · stoneware · parian · concrete · steel-frame
- **Rule:** Press a coarse, sandy brick clay into wooden moulds and dry it; the glaze needs a porous, rough body. Keep fine white bodies out of the wall.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pressing clay into wooden relief moulds · cutting the brick to the grid · drying in the sun · numbering the bricks on the side
- **Joins:** The bricks are laid in courses with bitumen as the mortar, so that each brick sits in the place that the number gives.
- **Details:** A number or a mark on the side of a brick, a slightly uneven glaze edge, and a thin dark line of glaze between the figures.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** alkaline-glaze
- **Decorations:** relief-moulding · undecorated
- **Pattern:** Blue ground with white and yellow lions, golden bulls and snake-dragons in rows, and bands of white and yellow rosettes at the top and bottom.
- **Gloss:** Glassy and bright, about 70 to 90 GU; the blue glaze is thick and the colour deepens where it pools in the relief.
- **Wear:** The glaze flakes from the edges of the brick and shows the sandy body; the blue stays strong and the white turns cream.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A deep lapis-blue ground with warm white and yellow figures; the colour in the glaze comes from copper, cobalt and antimony.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A large updraught brick kiln with wood and reed fuel; the glazed bricks sit in rows with space round each piece.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** moulding and drying of the bricks · a first firing of the brick body · painting of the glaze in colour fields · a second firing of the glaze at about 950 °C
- **Scale:** About 15 m of wall for each gate tower; the Ishtar Gate and the Processional Way used many thousands of bricks.
- **Signs:** A numbered brick side, a wall of repeated animals, and a blue glaze that ran a little over the edge of the relief.

### Glazed brick relief (process)

Bricks are pressed in a relief mould, glazed in colour and fired, then laid in numbered courses so that the figures form on the wall. Origin: ceramics, Babylon, Mesopotamia, c. 604 BCE.

- Press the brick clay into a wooden mould that carries a part of the relief.
- Dry the brick and fire it once, then paint the glaze in colour fields.
- Fire the brick a second time at about 950 °C so that the glaze melts.
- Number the side of each brick and lay the courses in bitumen or lime mortar.
