# Blue willow transferware

> A copper plate puts a Chinese-style blue picture on cheap white earthenware, so that every household owns the pattern that once only a rich house could buy.

White earthenware that carries a blue pattern in the Chinese manner, printed from an engraved copper plate. The willow pattern, with a bridge, a tree, a boat and two birds, is the best known of the many designs.

**Period:** 1790–1900 · **Era:** Industrial pottery · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Willow pattern, Blue and white transfer-printed earthenware, English blue printed ware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Blue willow transferware, you MUST:

1. Engrave the pattern on a copper plate in fine lines, and print it with cobalt ink on thin tissue.
2. Rub the wet tissue on the biscuit ware, so that the blue lines pass to the surface.
3. Fix the print in a low firing, then dip the piece in clear glaze, and fire it again.
4. Arrange the picture in a ring: a border, a main scene, and a small repeat on the rim.
5. Draw the willow scene with a bridge, a tree, a pagoda, a boat, a fence and two birds in the sky.
6. Use one tone of blue that is deep and even, and a clear glaze that stays white.
7. Put the pattern name and the maker mark in the base, so that a customer can order again.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Plates, soup plates, meat dishes, tureens, sauceboats and bowls, in a plain classical outline.
- **Profile:** A round plate with a flat rim and a shallow well; the printed pattern covers the whole face.
- **Foot and rim:** A low plain foot with a glazed base, and a rim with a moulded edge or a plain edge; the pattern name is in the base.
- **Proportion:** Plate diameter 20 to 26 cm and wall 4 to 5 mm thick; the border is about one quarter of the width.
- **Rules:**
  - Divide the face into a border on the rim and a scene in the well.
  - Cover the face with print, so that no white shows for more than 2 cm.
  - Repeat the same pattern on every piece in the service.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** pearlware
- **Secondary:** creamware · bone-china
- **Avoid:** european-hard-paste · stoneware · terracotta
- **Rule:** Use a white earthenware with a clear glaze. Pearlware has a trace of cobalt, so that it looks white against the blue.

## Forming

- **Methods:** jiggering of plates · press moulding of tureens and dishes · engraving a copper plate · transfer printing on tissue · dipping in clear glaze
- **Joins:** Handles and knops are moulded and luted with slip; the plate rim carries a moulded edge.
- **Details:** A small seam in the print where two sheets of tissue meet, a trace of blue in the glaze pool at the foot, and the pattern name in the base.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** lead-glaze · clear-feldspathic
- **Decorations:** transfer-print · underglaze-cobalt · relief-moulding
- **Pattern:** The willow scene: a bridge with three figures, a willow tree, a pagoda, a boat, an island and two birds, with a border of fence, trellis and flowers.
- **Gloss:** Bright gloss, about 80 to 90 GU, on a white to blue-white ground.
- **Wear:** The blue stays under the glaze; the glaze crazes in a fine net, and the cutlery marks the well.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** One deep cobalt blue on a bright white ground; the blue is the only colour.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A coal-fired bottle kiln; the ware goes in saggars for the biscuit, the print and the glaze.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 1150 °C · printing from a copper plate on tissue · firing the print at a low heat · dipping in clear glaze · glaze firing at about 1050 °C
- **Scale:** Dozens of Staffordshire factories printed the pattern, in millions of pieces a year, for the home and for export.
- **Signs:** A seam in the print, a pattern name and a maker mark in the base, and a blue tone that is even from piece to piece.

### Underglaze transfer printing (process)

A pattern is printed from an engraved copper plate on thin paper. The paper is rubbed on to the biscuit ware and the glaze covers the print. Origin: ceramics, Caughley, Shropshire, England, c. 1781.

- Engrave the design on a copper plate.
- Fill the lines with cobalt colour and print on tissue paper.
- Rub the tissue on to the biscuit piece and wash off the paper.
- Dip in glaze and fire.
