# Super Normal product

> A good object is one that nobody notices: it does its job, it fits the hand and the room, and it needs no explanation or signature.

The anonymous everyday object: a chair, a clock, a kettle or a shelf in matte white plastic, pale wood and steel, drawn so that it looks right and leaves no trace of the designer.

**Period:** 2006–present · **Era:** Contemporary · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Supernormal design, Anonymous everyday objects, Quiet product design

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "super normal", "supernormal design", "anonymous object", "everyday product", "matte white plastic", "pale wood chair", "muji-like", "morrison chair".

Good for: a calm flat or office moodboard, product briefs for household goods, a clean still life for a catalogue, hardware schedules for a rental unit.

Do not use it when:
- The brief asks for a bold statement or a sculptural object.
- The room needs ornament, colour or craft marks.

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Super Normal product, you MUST:

1. Draw the most usual form of the object; change a proportion by 5 to 10% and no more.
2. Use one matte colour on 90% of the surface: white, pale grey or the colour of the wood.
3. Round each edge with a radius of 2 to 6 mm so that the hand finds no sharp line.
4. Join the parts with a narrow gap of 1 to 2 mm; show no screw head on a main face.
5. Use one material for each part: pale wood for the frame, plastic for the shell, steel for the hardware.
6. Make the object for a long life: it uses standard parts and it ages without damage.
7. Print no logo and no ornament on the face of the object.

## Form

- **Geometry:** The plain cylinder, the rounded rectangle and the shallow dome, with small softened edges.
- **Silhouette:** A familiar outline that fits a type: a chair has four legs and a seat, a kettle has a body and a handle.
- **Edges:** Soft edges with a radius of 2 to 6 mm; a thin shadow gap of 1 to 2 mm at each joint.
- **Proportion:** A chair seat is 45 cm high; a clock face is 20 to 30 cm wide; a kettle body is about 12 cm in diameter and 20 cm high.
- **Ornament:** None. The only mark is a small, quiet detail such as a hanging hole or a small dot of colour.
- **Rules:**
  - Each object keeps the standard outline of its type, with a change of 10% or less.
  - Each object uses 1 or 2 materials in view.
  - Each edge has a radius of 2 mm or more.
  - Each joint has a gap of 1 to 2 mm or none.
  - A colour other than white, grey or the wood covers less than 5% of the object.

## Materials

- **Primary:** abs-plastic · polypropylene · ash · beech · birch-plywood · cotton
- **Secondary:** stainless-steel · aluminium · porcelain · vitreous-china · clear-glass · frosted-glass · opal-glass · borosilicate-glass · oak · linen · wool · mild-steel · solid-surface · stoneware · canvas · plastic-laminate
- **Avoid:** mahogany · rosewood · velvet · silk · macassar-ebony · bronze
- **Rule:** Matte plastic and pale wood make about 70% of the visible surface; steel, glass and plain cloth make the rest.

## Finish & texture

- **Finishes:** moulded-matte · oiled · soaped · brushed · painted-matte · vitreous-glaze · acid-etched · piece-dyed
- **Textures:** satin-smooth · fine-grain · brush-line · plain-weave
- **Gloss:** 3–12 GU on plastic and wood; 20–40 GU on brushed steel
- **Patina:** The style wants a surface that ages without marks: wood takes a soft tone, and plastic and steel stay as they are.

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** stainless
- **Section:** Rods and handles 8–14 mm; a tap body 25–35 mm
- **Joints:** Narrow gaps, hidden fixings on main faces, and a few screws on the back or the base.
- **Handles:** A thin bar, a small round hole or a flat tab cut into the body.
- **Switches and taps:** A flat rocker in matte white; a slim steel lever tap on a plain cylinder.

- **Processes:** injection moulding of plastic · plywood pressing · CNC machining of solid wood · steel stamping and brushing · glaze casting of porcelain

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Shadow gap (process)

A narrow recessed joint between two surfaces that casts a shadow, so that each surface seems to float apart from the next. Origin: architecture, Verona, c. 1958.

- Cut the gap 10 to 25mm wide and 10 to 20mm deep.
- Hold the gap at a constant width along its full length.
- Use it where new meets old: between a wall and a ceiling, a floor and a skirting, or a new insert and a historic wall.
