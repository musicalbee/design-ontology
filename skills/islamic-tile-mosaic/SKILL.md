# Islamic tile mosaic

> A building surface becomes a woven pattern of light and colour when many small tiles in turquoise, cobalt and black are cut to fit one geometric plan.

Wall and dome cladding that the Seljuk and Timurid potters made from glazed tiles in one colour. They cut each tile into small shapes and set the pieces together in a geometric or calligraphic pattern.

**Period:** 1200–1500 · **Era:** Medieval glaze · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Seljuk tile mosaic, Cut-tile mosaic, Persian tile mosaic

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Islamic tile mosaic, you MUST:

1. Glaze each plain tile in one colour: turquoise from copper, blue from cobalt, black from manganese and iron.
2. Cut the fired tile with a chisel into the shapes of one pattern: stars, kites, lozenges and calligraphic strokes.
3. Draw the full pattern first, then set the cut pieces face down on it and pour plaster over the back.
4. Build the pattern on a grid of 6, 8 or 12 points so that the shapes repeat without a gap.
5. Use the Kufic script as a band 30 to 60 cm wide, in white or blue on a dark field.
6. Keep the tesserae 1 to 2 cm thick, with joints under 2 mm, so that the surface reads as one skin.
7. Place the mosaic on the parts that people look at: the mihrab, the portal and the dome.
8. Add a few lustre or relief tiles to a flat mosaic for a change of light.

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Bright modern colours such as pink, lime green or orange.
- **NEVER:** A single repeating tile that makes a wallpaper pattern with no cut shapes.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Cut-tile panels for domes, portals, mihrab niches and walls, tile friezes with Kufic script, and small star-and-cross wall tiles with lustre.
- **Profile:** A flat face with a very small relief: the cut pieces lie in one plane, and relief bands and lustre tiles add a slight change.
- **Foot and rim:** Panels have no foot. The edge of a panel meets a plaster or brick frame, and a border band of two or three lines closes the pattern.
- **Proportion:** Tesserae are 1 to 6 cm across and 1 to 2 cm thick; star-and-cross lustre tiles are about 20 cm square.
- **Rules:**
  - Cut every piece to the pattern; do not paint the shapes on a large tile.
  - Keep the colours to five: turquoise, cobalt, black, white and a little lustre.
  - Close each pattern with a border band that keeps the grid of the field.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stonepaste
- **Secondary:** earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · creamware
- **Rule:** Use a white stonepaste of quartz, glass frit and a little clay for the tile. Use a red earthenware for the plain backing. Keep porcelain and stoneware out: they do not suit this glaze.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pressing the tile in a flat mould · drying the tile in the shade · chiselling the fired tile into small shapes · setting the pieces face down on a drawn pattern · pouring a plaster backing
- **Joins:** The pieces are bedded in plaster over a brick or rubble wall, and the joints are filled with a thin mortar.
- **Details:** Chisel marks on the back of each piece, a sharp cut edge on the glazed face, and a slight change of level between neighbours.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** alkaline-glaze · lead-glaze
- **Decorations:** tile-mosaic · lustre · cuerda-seca · relief-moulding · incised
- **Pattern:** Interlocking stars, kites and girih shapes in turquoise, cobalt and black, with white Kufic inscriptions and a little lustre on small wall tiles.
- **Gloss:** Bright and glassy, about 70 to 90 GU, with a slight iridescence where the glaze is old.
- **Wear:** Alkaline glaze gives a silver bloom and fine crazing after centuries; some pieces fall out and leave a pale plaster ground.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A turquoise and cobalt field with black lines and white script; lustre and gold give a warm accent.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A small updraught kiln fired with wood or brushwood; the tiles stand on stilts in saggars.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing of the pressed tile · glaze firing of each colour in its own batch · a third, reduction firing at about 600 °C for lustre tiles · cold cutting of the fired tile
- **Scale:** One dome needed tens of thousands of cut pieces; workshops in Konya, Kashan and Samarkand worked for one patron at a time.
- **Signs:** Small kiln stilt marks on the back of the tile, a copper-green edge on some turquoise, and irregular colour from batch to batch.

### Cut tile mosaic (process)

Glazed tiles of one colour are cut into small shapes, and the shapes are set face down in a plaster bed to form a pattern. The bed holds the pieces after it dries. Origin: ceramics, Anatolia and Iran, c. 1200.

- Glaze and fire plain tiles in 5 or 6 colours.
- Cut each tile into pieces with a chisel and a hammer.
- Lay the pieces face down on a drawn pattern, with joints under 1 mm.
- Pour a bed of plaster over the back and let it set.
