# Bias-cut glamour

> Silk cut across the grain drapes by itself, so that the body shows a line with no boning and no pads.

The long, close evening gown of the 1930s: silk satin cut on the cross grain, so that it drapes and follows the body.

**Period:** 1927–1939 · **Era:** Interwar modern · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Bias-cut gown, Vionnet cut, 1930s Hollywood gown

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Bias-cut glamour, you MUST:

1. Cut the cloth at 45 degrees to the grain, so that it stretches up to 20 per cent.
2. Drape the pattern on a small wooden doll, 80 cm high, before the full-size cut.
3. Keep seams to a minimum: a gown needs two to four main pieces.
4. Let the waist return to the natural line, and let the gown run in a smooth curve over the hip.
5. Hang the skirt for 24 hours before the hem is sewn, so that the bias settles.
6. Choose a heavy crêpe or a satin of 70 to 100 g per square metre.
7. Finish with a cowl neck, a low back or a halter, and no other trim.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A long, narrow column that follows the body from the shoulder to the floor, with a flare at the hem.
- **Line:** A smooth curve over the bust and hip, a bare or low back, and a long hem that floats, with a small train.
- **Fit:** Close on the body without darts; the stretch of the bias does the fitting.
- **Proportion:** The waist is at the natural line. The hem reaches the floor at the back and the ankle at the front. The flare at the hem is 1.5 to 2.5 m.
- **Rules:**
  - Cut each main piece on the true bias, and join two pieces with a single seam at the side.
  - Let the cloth hang from the shoulder or the neck, with a halter or a cowl.
  - Leave out all hard shapes: no pads, no boning, no stiff lining.
  - Wear the gown over a slip that is cut on the bias in the same silk.
  - Add one jewel or one flower at the shoulder; keep the cloth plain.

### Bias cutting (skill)

The cutting of woven cloth at 45 degrees to the threads, so that it stretches, falls in soft folds and follows the body. Origin: fashion, Paris, France, c. 1922.

- Fold the cloth so that the cut line runs at 45 degrees to the warp.
- Drape the shape on a small doll, and mark the seams on the true bias.
- Hang the cut pieces for a day before the hem is sewn, so that the bias settles.
