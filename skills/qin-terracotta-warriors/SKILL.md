# Qin terracotta warriors

> An emperor takes an army of clay into the grave, and a factory system of standard parts and hand finish makes each soldier a different man.

Life-size grey terracotta soldiers, horses and officials that the workshops of the First Emperor made for his tomb. Each body is built from standard parts, and the head is finished by hand.

**Period:** 221–206 BC · **Era:** Ancient clay · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Terracotta Army, Qin tomb figures, Bingmayong

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Qin terracotta warriors, you MUST:

1. Make each figure life-size: 175 to 190 cm for a soldier and up to 200 cm for a general.
2. Build the legs as solid columns and the torso as a hollow coil-built tube, then join the parts with slip.
3. Press the head in a two-piece mould and add ears, nose, hair and beard by hand to the leather-hard clay.
4. Give each face its own mouth, brow and moustache, so that no two heads match.
5. Put the rank in the dress: a general wears armour with a tied bow and a high cap, and an archer kneels.
6. Fire the grey body in reduction at 950 to 1050 °C, and leave it unglazed.
7. Paint the fired figure with lacquer and mineral pigments, in red, green, purple and blue.
8. Stamp or incise the name of the workshop and the master on the body, as a record of who made it.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Standing and kneeling soldiers, archers, charioteers, generals and officials, at life size, with horses of 150 cm at the shoulder.
- **Profile:** An upright figure with a straight spine and a slightly turned head; the torso is a long tube and the legs are solid columns.
- **Foot and rim:** A rectangular plinth 3 to 5 cm thick that carries the feet; hands are cast apart and fitted at the wrist, and a hand holds a real bronze weapon.
- **Proportion:** Soldier height 175 to 190 cm and weight about 110 to 135 kg; the wall of the torso is 2 to 4 cm thick, and the legs are solid.
- **Rules:** Keep the full life-size scale for every human figure. · Join the head, arms, torso and legs from separate parts. · Show armour as rows of plates with rivets and ties. · Give the face a calm, direct look with a strong jaw. · Mount each figure on a flat plinth and fit it in a rank.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** terracotta
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · stoneware · european-hard-paste
- **Rule:** Use a local loess clay mixed with fine sand, which fires to a dense grey body. Keep the surface matt and free of any glaze.

## Forming

- **Methods:** coil building of the hollow torso · moulding of heads in two-piece clay moulds · hand modelling of hair, ears and beard · hand carving of armour detail on the leather-hard clay · fitting of cast hands and arms with slip
- **Joins:** The parts are joined with wet clay slip at the wrist, neck, waist and ankle. The figure dries slowly for weeks before it goes into the kiln.
- **Details:** A stamped workshop name on the body, thumb marks inside the hollow torso, and a small hole in the torso that lets steam out in the kiln.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** bare-clay
- **Decorations:** cold-painted-pigment · incised · relief-moulding
- **Pattern:** A lacquer base coat and mineral pigments on the dress and the skin. Red, green, purple, blue and white mark the rank and the unit.
- **Gloss:** Matt, below 5 GU; the lacquer layer is dull and the grey clay shows where the paint is lost.
- **Wear:** The paint flakes off in the air within weeks of the dig. The grey clay and the tool marks show, and the pieces are often found broken and mended.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A grey clay ground with flat mineral colour on the dress: red for coats, green and purple for trousers, flesh pink for faces.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A brick updraught kiln that holds a few figures at a time, and fires them in a smoky, reducing flame.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:** drying of the figure for several weeks · firing in reduction to a grey body · lacquer base coat on the fired figure · mineral pigment painting · fitting of bronze weapons
- **Scale:** About 8000 soldiers, 130 chariots and 670 horses in four pits, made by a state workshop system in some 40 years.
- **Signs:** Grey colour from reduction, stamped workshop marks, mould seams hidden by hand work, and joins at the wrist and waist.

### Modular figure assembly (process)

A figure is made in many standard parts, such as heads, torsos, arms and props, and the parts are joined and finished by hand. One set of moulds gives many different figures. Origin: ceramics, Lintong, Shaanxi, China, c. 221 BCE.

- Model the figure and cut it into parts.
- Press or cast each part in its own mould.
- Join the parts with slip while the clay is soft.
- Finish the joins and the face by hand, so that each figure differs.
