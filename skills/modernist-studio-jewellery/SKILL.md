# Modernist Studio Jewellery

> A jewel is a small sculpture. An artist makes it by hand from an idea about form, and the metal and the stones serve the idea.

Small sculptures to wear, made by one artist at the bench. The materials are silver, brass wire and cut crystal, in abstract forms from modern art, with no regard for the value of the metal.

**Period:** 1940–1969 · **Era:** War and the New Look · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Studio jewellery, Modern handmade jewellery, Sculptural jewellery of the 1940s and 1950s

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Modernist Studio Jewellery, you MUST:

1. Begin with an abstract form from modern art: a mobile, a line, a plane or a biomorphic shape.
2. Make each piece by hand at the bench, and make no more than a few of each.
3. Use a modest metal such as sterling silver, brass or copper, and choose it for its form, not its value.
4. Cut a stone to a new design, for example a rock crystal with an optical line cut under the surface.
5. Bend and join wire into an open line, and let the empty space count as part of the design.
6. Keep the piece to one gesture and to two or three materials.
7. Leave the hand marks, and sign each piece with a mark.
8. Design the piece for the body: a pin that sits on the lapel, a cuff that follows the arm.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A small abstract sculpture: an open brooch of wire, a cuff of bent silver, a pendant of rock crystal in a metal frame.
- **Line:** Open and linear, with curves and straight lines used as in a drawing; asymmetrical.
- **Fit:** Pinned on a plain lapel, hung on a short chain over a knit, worn on the wrist as a cuff.
- **Proportion:** A brooch is 4 to 9 cm wide; a pendant is 3 to 8 cm tall; a cuff is 2 to 5 cm wide.
- **Rules:**
  - Make the design as a drawing in line, with open space around it.
  - Mount a stone cut with an optical line in a plain bezel of silver.
  - Bend brass wire into a free line, and join the ends with a visible solder.
  - Place the piece off centre on the dress.

### Lost-wax casting (process)

A method that melts a wax model out of a mould and fills the empty space with molten metal. Origin: industrial design, Near East and South Asia, c. 4000 BCE.

- Carve or form the model in wax.
- Cover the model with clay and bake it, so that the wax melts out.
- Pour molten metal into the empty mould and break the mould.
