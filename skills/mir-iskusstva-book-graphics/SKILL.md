# Mir iskusstva book graphics

> A single firm line and flat tints, set inside an ornamental frame, make a fairy-tale page that reads as a unit with its border and letters.

A firm, even black pen outline, in the manner of a woodcut, is filled with flat watercolour tints. The key line and the tints print in separate runs. Folk ornament frames each page.

**Period:** 1899–1930 · **Era:** Industrial press · **Domain:** Print · **Also called:** Bilibin style, World of Art book graphics, Графика Билибина, Мир искусства

## System directives

When specifying, prompting or art-directing a printed piece or its look on screen: a poster, a plate, a book page, a zine, a label or a print-look illustration in Mir iskusstva book graphics, you MUST:

1. Draw the outline with a pen in one even weight of about 0.4–0.6 mm at print size; do not swell or taper it.
2. Fill each area with one flat tint, with no shading; use 3–6 tints from a set of vermilion, Prussian blue, ochre and green.
3. Treat wood, fur, cloth and water as repeated patterns of short strokes inside the contour.
4. Frame each picture with a border of folk ornament, and draw the title letters by hand to match.
5. Draw the picture, the border and the text block as one page.
6. Print the black line as the key and the tints as separate runs; keep the register within 0.5 mm.
7. Use a smooth cream paper that shows the flat colour without a texture.

## Process

- **Family:** planographic
- **Matrix:** A zinc plate or a stone for the black key line, and a stone for each tint.
- **Press:** A flatbed lithographic press at a state printing works, with one run for each colour.
- **Image making:** The artist draws the outline in ink and paints a colour guide in watercolour. A craftsman separates the tints and draws each one on its own stone.
- **Edition:** Thousands of copies of a tale book; the copies match.

### Lithography (process)

A print from a flat stone or plate on which a greasy drawing takes ink and water repels it; the artist draws as on paper. Origin: print, Munich, c. 1796.

- Draw on the stone with a greasy crayon or a tusche wash; the drawing takes ink and the rest stays wet.
- Pull the print in a press at a run of 20 to 200 copies for an artist print, or more for a poster.
- Use one stone for each colour; 3 to 5 stones give a poster.
- Keep the soft crayon grain and the dark, velvet black.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Expedition for the Preparation of State Papers** (state programme, St Petersburg, 1818–1919), made the style. The Russian state printing works for banknotes, bonds and stamps. It also printed colour books, among them Bilibin's fairy-tale series. Goznak took over its work in 1919.
- **Mir iskusstva** (collective, St Petersburg, 1898–1924), gathered the style. A circle of Russian artists and writers and its journal. The journal ran from 1899 to 1904, and the group met again from 1910 to 1924. It made the book page into an art form.
