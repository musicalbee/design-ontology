# Surrealism

> A picture can show the mind below reason, and so it joins plain, exact things in a way that no waking logic allows.

Dream pictures in a clear, exact style: real objects in impossible places, meetings and changes of scale. The detail is precise and the logic is wrong, so the image feels both real and strange.

**Period:** 1924–1960s · **Era:** Avant-garde · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Veristic surrealism, Dream painting

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "surrealism", "surrealist", "dali", "magritte", "max ernst", "dreamlike", "impossible scene", "melting", "uncanny", "hyperreal dream".

Good for: album, book and film cover art, advertising with a visual paradox, editorial illustration of a concept or a mind, fashion and perfume campaign.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a plain, literal or documentary picture.
- The picture must be quick, loose or decorative.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Surrealism, you MUST:

1. Paint each object with exact, plain detail, as in a photograph, and make the meeting of objects impossible.
2. Place the scene in a wide, empty, deep space: a low horizon at 25–35% of the height and a long, clear perspective.
3. Change the scale on purpose: an object 3 to 10 times too large or too small against its neighbour.
4. Melt, split or combine forms: a soft clock, a body of drawers, a bird that is a leaf.
5. Use a cool, calm, even light with long, hard shadows, as at dusk or in a dream.
6. Keep the surface smooth and the touch hidden, so that the strange image looks real.
7. Give the picture an enigmatic title that does not explain it, and leave one object unresolved.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** A logical, explained scene that has a clear story.
- **NEVER:** Loose, gestural paint that draws attention to the hand.
- **NEVER:** Flat decorative colour with no depth.
- **NEVER:** Cute, cartoon-like or horror-film images.
- **NEVER:** A crowded picture with no open space.
- **NEVER:** Realistic scale and order between the objects.

## Composition

- **Layout:** A wide, empty plain or room in deep perspective, with a low horizon at 25–35% of the height and 2–5 objects set apart in the space, each with a long shadow.
- **Focal point:** The main impossible object, set at one third from an edge, in the sharpest detail and the brightest value of the picture.
- **Format:** A canvas at 4:3 to 3:2, small to medium (0.3 to 1m); the sky or the far plain takes 40–60% of the height.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| scale | Set one object at 3 to 10 times its true size against its neighbour, so that the scale does not agree with the space. |
| negative-space | Keep 50–70% of the picture as empty plain, sky or wall around the 2–5 objects. |
| layering-depth | Build 3 layers: a near object, a middle object and a far horizon, with sharp detail in all three and a gradual cool haze at the back. |
| focal-point | Place the one impossible object at the third line, and give it the strongest contrast, at 40% or more above the ground value. |
| lighting-layers | Use a low, even, cool light with a long hard shadow of 2 to 5 times the object height, and a glow only at the horizon. |
| grouping | Group 2–3 unrelated objects in one tight cluster, and place the next group at least 30% of the picture width away. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A cool, calm, natural palette of dusk blue, sand and grey-green, with one warm or acid accent on the impossible object.
- **Distribution:** Sky and far plain 40–50%, ground and wall 30–40%, objects 10–20%, accent under 5%.
- **Ground:** A smooth, white or pale grey ground, with thin, hidden layers over it.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint, thin and smooth, with glazes.
- **Support:** A fine canvas or panel with a smooth, white ground.
- **Brushwork:** Small, fine strokes of 1–2mm, blended away; no visible hand and no impasto.
- **Paint handling:** 4–8 thin glazed layers; the surface is polished, and the transitions are seamless.
- **Edges:** Hard and exact at the objects; soft only in the far sky and the haze.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** A low, calm, even light, like dusk or the hour before a storm, from one side and slightly behind.
- **Modelling:** Smooth and exact, with 6–8 steps; the form looks solid and the surface looks touched.
- **Shadow:** Long, hard and dark, at 2 to 5 times the height of the object, with a clear edge, on a pale ground.
- **Temperature:** A cool overall key, with a small warm glow at the horizon or on one object.

## Subject

- **Typical:** A dream plain with melting or floating objects, a body that is a cabinet, an eye, a bowler hat, a bird, a door, and a landscape of a sleeper.
- **Figure scale:** Objects of 10–30% of the picture height, set apart in a wide space; a figure may be tiny or huge.
- **Treatment:** Calm, exact and cold; the strange thing is shown without a sign of surprise.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Smooth and glossy, with a hidden touch.
- **Texture:** None visible; an even, polished surface like a photograph.

### Long shadow (motif)

A shadow far longer than its object, cast by a low sun, so that a still scene feels strange and charged. Origin: painting, Paris, 1914.

- Cast the shadow at a low angle: the shadow is 2 to 5 times the height of the object.
- Keep one light direction in the whole scene.
- Leave the shadow flat and dark, at 40% to 70% opacity.
- Show the shadow of something outside the frame, to hint at an unseen presence.
