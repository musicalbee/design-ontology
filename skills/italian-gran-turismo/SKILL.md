# Italian Gran Turismo

> A fine car is a sculpture that has a purpose. A taut, light body with no extra line, made by a carrozzeria around a fast engine.

The lean coupe for long, fast journeys. A low body of hand-beaten aluminium, thin pillars, a long bonnet and wire wheels. The cabin has leather, a wood-rim wheel and round gauges.

**Period:** 1947–1972 · **Era:** War and the New Look · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Italian GT, Berlinetta, Carrozzeria GT

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Italian Gran Turismo, you MUST:

1. Keep the body low, with a roof height of 1.25 to 1.35 m.
2. Make the overhangs short, and hold a long bonnet over a front engine.
3. Beat the body from aluminium sheet by hand over a wood buck, and fit it to a light tube frame.
4. Use thin roof pillars and a large glass area, so that the cabin looks like a bubble.
5. Limit chrome to a thin line: a grille, a bumper blade and the door handles.
6. Fit wire wheels with a knock-off hub, and leave the arches clean.
7. Trim the cabin in leather, with a wood-rim steering wheel and round, deep-set gauges.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A low, taut two-door coupe, with a long bonnet, a compact cabin set back and a short, tapered tail.
- **Line:** A clean waist line that runs from the headlamp to the tail, and a roof that curves down into a fastback or a short notch.
- **Fit:** Two seats close to the floor, with the driver tight to the wheel and the pedals; there is little space for bags.
- **Proportion:** Roof height 1.25 to 1.35 m; wheelbase 2.4 to 2.6 m; the bonnet is about a third of the car length.
- **Rules:**
  - Draw one simple surface that flows without a break from front to rear.
  - Set a small oval grille low in the nose, with a fine bar or a chrome blade.
  - Keep the pillars thin and the glass large.
  - Add air outlets, a louvre or a small badge as the only side detail.

- **Secondary:** vegetable-tanned-leather · flannel · felt · pressed-steel · suede · bakelite
- **Avoid:** pvc-leathercloth · abs-plastic · fibreglass · carbon-fibre · jacquard
- **Rule:** Use light, honest metal and hide. Use aluminium for the body, thin plated metal for the line, leather for the seats and wood for the wheel rim.

## Construction

- **Methods:** tube frame covered by an aluminium body · panels beaten by hand over a wood buck · full-size plaster or wood models · leather cut and sewn by hand in a pleated pattern · wire wheels with a centre lock · engine-turned metal on the fascia
- **Closures:** Doors with a thin chrome handle or a push button, quarter windows that open, and a small boot lid with a lock.
- **Details:** Wire wheels, a Nardi-style wood-rim wheel, a small oval grille, a rear window set low, and a badge on the wing.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** polished · brushed · gloss-paint · burnished · matte · guilloche
- **Pattern:** Plain body colour with no stripe; a simple stitched leather pattern in the cabin.
- **Gloss:** Cellulose paint at 70–85 GU; brushed aluminium at 20–40 GU; leather at 5–15 GU.
- **Wear:** Paint is repainted by hand; aluminium dents are beaten out; leather and wood take a patina that owners prize.

- **Processes:** a design drawn by the carrozzeria master · aluminium panels beaten by hand · leather cut and sewn · wood wheel rim turned and finished · hand painting in cellulose

### Coachbuilding (skill)

The making of a car body by hand on a wood frame, and the fit of that body to a chassis that the client bought. Origin: fashion, Europe, c. 1600.

- Build the body frame from ash timber and join it with metal brackets.
- Beat the aluminium or steel panels over a wood form and fit them to the frame.
- Fit the trim, the upholstery and the brightwork, then paint the body in many coats.
