# Modern Men's Briefs

> A man's underwear gives support and freedom of movement. A short knitted brief with a sewn-in waist is better than a loose drawer or a long suit.

The short, knitted, elastic-waist brief with a Y-shaped overlapping fly that Coopers, Inc. of Kenosha launched in 1935, and that replaced the loose drawers and the union suit.

**Period:** 1935–1969 · **Era:** Interwar modern · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Y-front briefs, Jockey brief, Knitted briefs

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Modern Men's Briefs, you MUST:

1. Knit the body of combed cotton in a close, soft jersey, with a stretch in both directions.
2. Cut the leg short and high, with an edge at the upper thigh.
3. Sew an elastic waistband into the top edge, 3 to 4 cm wide.
4. Sew a Y-shaped seam at the front, so that two layers overlap at the fly.
5. Make the front pouch of a double layer of cloth for support.
6. Finish each seam flat, with no ridge against the skin.
7. Keep the colour to white, grey or a plain tone.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A short, close brief with a high-cut leg and a flat elastic waist.
- **Line:** A clean, level waist line and a curved leg line at the upper thigh.
- **Fit:** Close and supportive; the knit stretches with the body and the waistband holds the brief in place.
- **Proportion:** Rise 22 to 28 cm; leg opening at the upper thigh; waistband 3 to 4 cm wide.
- **Rules:**
  - Cut the front in two layers, with an overlap that forms the Y seam.
  - Sew a flat elastic waistband to the top edge.
  - Bind the leg openings with a narrow knitted band.
  - Keep the back in one piece, with no seam at the centre.
  - Print or weave the maker's name on the waistband only.

### Y-front fly (skill)

A cut for a brief in which two panels overlap in a Y shape at the front, so that the garment opens without a button and holds the body. Origin: fashion, Kenosha, Wisconsin, 1934.

- Cut the front in two pieces with a curved seam in the shape of a Y.
- Lap one piece over the other and stitch the two together at the base.
- Set an elastic band into the waist.
