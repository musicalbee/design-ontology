# Düsseldorf School

> Seen without drama and in great detail, an ordinary place becomes a map of the way we live.

Large, cool, frontal colour pictures of buildings, rooms, crowds and landscapes in fine detail. The method comes from the Bechers' typologies, and the scale comes from the museum wall.

**Period:** 1976–today · **Era:** Colour and the topographic turn · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** Becher school, Typological photography

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "düsseldorf school", "becher", "gursky", "struth", "typology", "large format colour", "museum-scale photograph".

Good for: architectural and industrial documentation, annual-report imagery with a cool authority, fine-art series, image prompts for flat, frontal, grid-based scenes.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a hero moment, warm light or a person with a story.
- The final size is small, where the detail is lost.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in Düsseldorf School, you MUST:

1. Use a 4×5 in or digital medium format camera, with a lens of 90mm to 150mm.
2. Photograph in flat grey light with a shadow ratio of 1.5:1 or less, and with no people posed.
3. Keep the camera level and square to the subject, so that verticals stay vertical.
4. Use a viewpoint raised by 3 to 20m, or a straight-on view at 1.5m, and hold it for the whole series.
5. Print large: 1.5m to 3m on the long edge, with sharpness from edge to edge.
6. Work in series of 20 or more, so that the type is the subject.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Dramatic skies and golden-hour light.
- **NEVER:** Tilted horizons and wide-angle distortion.
- **NEVER:** Saturation boosted past the scene.
- **NEVER:** Picturesque framing that invites a story.
- **NEVER:** One-off hero images with no series.
- **NEVER:** Heavy vignette.

## Composition

- **Framing:** Frontal and complete; the subject sits whole in the frame with a margin of sky or ground.
- **Viewpoint:** Level, often raised by 3 to 20m; the camera sits square to the plane of the subject.
- **Geometry:** A grid of windows, shelves, boxes or people; repeats make the picture.
- **Subject placement:** Centred or on a strict grid line, with a margin of 10–25% on each side.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| symmetry-axis | Set the main axis at 0.5 of the width, within 0.02. |
| rhythm | Repeat a window, shelf or figure at one constant pitch, with 12 to 60 repeats in the frame. |
| proportion | Keep the 4:5 or 5:4 ratio of the plate, with no crop. |
| negative-space | Leave 10–25% of the frame as sky or ground. |
| unity-variety | Hold one distance, one height and one light across 20 to 100 prints. |

## Light

- **Quality:** Flat, soft and overcast: no cast shadow edges.
- **Direction:** Overhead and omnidirectional under cloud
- **Key to fill ratio:** 1.5:1 or less (under 1 stop)
- **Colour temperature:** 6500K (overcast daylight)
- **Time of day:** Midday under an even grey sky

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** 4×5 in or 8×10 in sheet film; digital medium format at 100 megapixels
- **Focal length:** 90mm to 150mm (large format); 45mm to 80mm (medium format)
- **Aperture:** f/22 to f/32
- **Shutter:** 1/4s to 4s
- **Film speed:** ISO 100 (Portra 160)
- **Focus:** Focus at one third into the scene; the depth of field covers the full subject.
- **Support:** Heavy tripod
- **Equipment:** view camera or medium format camera · tripod · cherry-picker or scaffold for height · light meter

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Neutral colour, slightly cool, with no creative grade; the surfaces keep their local tint.
- **Grade recipe:** Set the white balance at 6500K, tint 0. · Set the black point at 3% and the white point at 96%. · Apply +5% midtone contrast, and no more. · Reduce saturation by 5% overall and keep local hues exact. · Correct lens vertical and horizontal lines to 0°. · Add no grain, no vignette and no sharpening halo.
- **Grain:** None: fine detail, sharp across the whole plate

## Subject & staging

- **Subjects:** industrial towers · apartment blocks · libraries and stock exchanges · crowds in a hall · portraits against grey · landscapes from a raised view
- **Staging:** Minimal. The maker picks the place, the hour and the grey light, and then removes the weather and the anecdote.
- **Direction:** For a portrait: a neutral face, a straight stance and a grey ground; no smile.

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:**
  - Scan the sheet at 4000 dpi or capture at the sensor's base ISO.
  - Correct geometry to 0° and colour to neutral grey.
  - Print as a C-type or pigment print on a face-mounted sheet, 1.5 to 3m wide.
  - Hang as a grid of one type with 6 to 20 prints.
- **Output:** A very large, sharp colour print or a grid of typology prints.
