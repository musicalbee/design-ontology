# Scandinavian modern tableware

> A few plain shapes that stack, a warm satin glaze and a quiet hand mark make tableware that is made in a factory but feels human.

Plain, stackable tableware from the factories of Finland and Sweden. Artists on the staff of the factory design simple forms in a few shapes, glaze them in satin or matt colours, and add a quiet hand mark.

**Period:** 1950–1975 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Nordic modern ceramics, Finnish and Swedish studio-industry tableware, Stackable Nordic dinnerware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Scandinavian modern tableware, you MUST:

1. Design a service of a few shapes that are fit for every meal, and let them stack in a column.
2. Make each piece a plain solid, and leave the surface to the glaze.
3. Glaze in a satin or matt colour: white, sand, grey, olive or a deep blue.
4. Add a small relief of ribs or a thin incised line, and no more.
5. Let an artist, not a stylist, design the service, and put the name in the base.
6. Make the factory a studio: the artist works on the line and on one-off pieces.
7. Choose a wall 4 to 6 mm thick, which is strong enough for daily use and stacking.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Flat plates, stacking bowls, shallow serving dishes, covered tureens, and cups and jugs that stack.
- **Profile:** A plain solid with a soft edge; the bowl has a straight wall and a flat base, and the plate has a narrow rim.
- **Foot and rim:** A flat base with a small foot ring, glazed or bare; the rim is round and plain.
- **Proportion:** Plate diameter 17 to 26 cm and wall 4 to 6 mm thick; the pieces stack in one column.
- **Rules:** Make every piece fit the next in a stack. · Keep the edge round and plain, and the surface quiet. · Give each piece one single use, and name it by that use.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stoneware · earthenware
- **Secondary:** porcelain · wood
- **Avoid:** bone-china · european-hard-paste · creamware
- **Rule:** Use a strong stoneware or earthenware that stacks and stands the daily wash. Wood is for trays and lids. Do not use a thin porcelain with gold: the style is warm and plain.

## Forming

- **Methods:** jiggering of plates and bowls · slip casting of jugs · press moulding of dishes · incising and wax-resist decoration by hand · glaze dipping and brushing
- **Joins:** Handles are cast with the body or luted with slip; a wooden lid or tray is made to fit.
- **Details:** A small hand mark: a brush stroke, an incised line, or a signature of the artist in the base.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** satin-glaze · matte-glaze · clear-feldspathic
- **Decorations:** undecorated · relief-moulding · incised
- **Pattern:** Plain; a ring of fine ribs, a thin incised band, or a small leaf or a stroke in a second tone.
- **Gloss:** Satin to matt, about 20 to 55 GU.
- **Wear:** The glaze keeps a quiet sheen and shows a mark of the stack at the rim; the tone stays even.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Warm, quiet colours in a satin glaze: white, sand, grey, olive and a deep blue, with a dark brown for the hand mark.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A gas or electric tunnel kiln; a biscuit firing and a glaze firing in clean air.

- **Signs:** The artist name in the base, a quiet sheen, and a light hand mark under the glaze.

### Serial repetition (process)

One motif or one frame, repeated with small changes in a set of works, so that the viewer reads the change and not the object. Origin: painting, Paris, 1891.

- Fix the motif, the format and the viewpoint; change one variable only, such as the hour, the colour or the stripe width.
- Make at least 5 works in the set and show them in one row at one height.
- Keep the spacing between works equal to a fixed unit, such as 10 cm or one panel width.
- Remove the hand: use a mechanical edge, a stencil or a plain lens.
