# Early wristwatch

> Time must be on the wrist, where a pilot, a soldier or a driver can read it with one glance and no free hand.

The small watch on a leather strap that replaced the pocket watch between 1904 and the 1920s. It has a 28 to 35 mm case with wire lugs, a white enamel dial and luminous numerals. The trench version has a wire guard over the glass.

**Period:** 1904–1930 · **Era:** Belle Époque and the Great War · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Wristlet, Trench watch, Early Santos wristwatch

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Early wristwatch, you MUST:

1. Make the case 28 to 35 mm wide, so that it fits under a cuff and on a thin wrist.
2. Fix the strap to the case with wire or fixed lugs, so that the strap does not slip.
3. Print large Arabic numerals on a white enamel dial for a quick read on a moving wrist.
4. Paint the numerals and hands with luminous paint, so that the dial shows in the dark.
5. Cover the glass of a field model with a wire guard that turns a blow.
6. Seal the case with a screw back and a screw bezel, so that sweat and dust stay out.
7. Fit a small movement, 25 to 29 mm wide and not more than 8 mm thick.
8. Cut a long, narrow strap of calf leather with a plain pin buckle.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A small round or square case, 28 to 35 mm wide, with wire or fixed lugs on a leather strap.
- **Line:** A flat, compact case with short lugs; the glass stands a little above the bezel.
- **Fit:** The strap wraps the wrist and the watch lies flat under a cuff.
- **Proportion:** Case thickness to width about 1 to 4; strap width 14 to 18 mm; strap length 180 to 210 mm.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the lugs short so that the case sits on the top of the wrist.
  - Make the numerals at least 3 mm tall.
  - Fit the wire guard to the lugs, not to the glass.
  - Cut the strap in one piece with a pin buckle and two keepers.

### Luminous dial (technology)

A dial with hands and marks that carry paint that glows in the dark, so that the wearer reads the time without a lamp. Origin: fashion, United States, 1914.

- Paint the numerals and the hands with a luminous compound.
- Apply a layer of at least 0.2 mm, so that the glow lasts the night.
- Choose tritium or strontium aluminate paint, because radium paint is no longer safe.
