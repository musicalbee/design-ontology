# Nordic national romanticism

> A picture can give a young nation a face by painting its own land, light, legends and home with the best methods of Europe.

Painting that builds a nation's image from its own land, myths and home life: pale northern light, lake and forest, folk legend and the farm room. Painters trained in Paris bring back plein-air method and apply it to Nordic subjects.

**Period:** 1880s–1900s · **Era:** Nineteenth century · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** National Romantic painting, Golden Age of Finnish Art, Skagen and Gothenburg school

Load it when the user asks for: "national romanticism", "nordic painting", "carl larsson", "gallen-kallela", "kalevala", "scandinavian folk", "midsummer", "northern landscape", "swedish interior".

Good for: Nordic heritage and tourism art direction, book illustration for myth and legend, calm interior and home brands, landscape image prompts.

Do not use it when:
- The brief asks for urban energy, glamour or a hot, saturated palette.
- The subject comes from a culture other than the Nordic one, where the folk symbols would be borrowed.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Nordic national romanticism, you MUST:

1. Paint the northern landscape in a cool, pale key: a long horizon, a low sun and a sky that covers 40–60% of the height.
2. Use a clear, decorative outline on figures and legends: a contour of 2–4mm and flat or lightly modelled fields of colour.
3. Simplify the scene into 3 to 5 large shapes (lake, forest wall, sky, a figure) and tilt the space slightly flat.
4. Draw on national material: epic poem, folk costume, farm interiors, birch, spruce and granite.
5. Paint the interior as a bright, tidy, white-and-red room with natural window light and a pattern of folk textiles.
6. Keep the colour restrained and cool, with one warm accent in red ochre, a wooden building or a costume.
7. Combine plein-air observation with a symbolist or decorative arrangement: the scene is true, and the layout is designed.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Tropical or Mediterranean colour and a warm golden sun.
- **NEVER:** Classical gods, Roman ruins and Italianate scenery.
- **NEVER:** Heavy dark chiaroscuro studio lighting.
- **NEVER:** Busy ornate frames of detail without a clear silhouette.
- **NEVER:** Urban, industrial or cosmopolitan modern-life subjects.
- **NEVER:** Sentimental detail that hides the simple structure of the land.

## Composition

- **Layout:** Large, simple bands or masses: horizon at 40–60% of the height, a forest edge or lake shore as a decorative line, and the figures set as a frieze across the lower third.
- **Focal point:** A figure or a red building at 1/3 from the side, against the pale area of sky or water.
- **Format:** Panoramic landscape at 3:2 to 2:1; a tall 1:2 format for legend and decorative panels.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| negative-space | Keep 30–50% of the picture as calm sky, lake or snow, with almost no detail. |
| rhythm | Repeat trunk, wave or pattern shapes 6–15 times in a regular beat, with a small change of height and spacing. |
| colour-distribution | Set cool blue, green and grey at about 75% of the area, and a warm red or ochre accent at 5–10%. |
| layering-depth | Use 3 planes: a dark foreground edge, a middle band of trees or figures, and a pale distance. |
| unity-variety | Hold the picture to one dominant colour key and vary the shapes inside it. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A cool, light northern key of blue-grey, forest green and white, with one warm accent in red ochre and a touch of gold.
- **Distribution:** Cool blues, greys and greens 60–70%, white and snow 15–20%, warm earth 10–15%, the red accent under 5%.
- **Ground:** A pale grey or cream ground that keeps the picture light.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint, with tempera and watercolour for decorative and folk subjects.
- **Support:** Canvas, often large for public commission; paper for the watercolour sketch.
- **Brushwork:** Broad, flat strokes of 10–30mm in sky and water, and a firm, drawn contour of 2–4mm on figures and trunks.
- **Paint handling:** Thin, matt layers with little impasto; the decorative pictures use flat, tempera-like fields.
- **Edges:** Clear silhouette edges on figures and trunks against a soft sky; lost edges only in mist and snow.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** Low, long northern light: a midsummer night glow or a diffuse winter day, with a long cast shadow.
- **Modelling:** Light modelling in 3–4 steps; the form reads as a clear shape against the sky more than as a volume.
- **Shadow:** Cool blue and violet shadows, with snow as a light-blue plane.
- **Temperature:** A cool overall key with a single warm window, timber wall or costume as the focal accent.

## Subject

- **Typical:** Lake and forest, birch and pine, mid-winter and midsummer, folk legend and the epic Kalevala, farm interiors, peasant costume.
- **Figure scale:** Figures take 15–40% of the height in landscape and 60–90% in the decorative panel.
- **Treatment:** A calm, solemn or dreamy mood; the nation and its home are shown as lasting and dignified.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Matt, with a clear and dry surface.
- **Texture:** Light canvas weave and visible broad strokes in the sky; flat and even in the decorative fields.
