# Jian tenmoku ware

> A bowl for whisked white tea must be dark, so that the foam shows; the kiln, not the brush, makes the pattern in an iron-rich glaze.

Dark stoneware tea bowls of the Song dynasty. A thick iron-rich glaze runs down the cone-shaped bowl in streaks and spots and pools in a bead at the foot.

**Period:** 1000–1279 · **Era:** Medieval glaze · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Jian ware, Jianzhan, Hare's fur tea bowl, Temmoku tea bowl

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Jian tenmoku ware, you MUST:

1. Make the bowl dark, because the white foam of whisked tea must show against it.
2. Throw a cone with a thick wall, 6 to 9 mm, to keep the tea hot.
3. Pull in the rim below the lip, so that the tea does not spill and the foam stays in the bowl.
4. Dip the bowl in an iron glaze that holds 5 to 9 per cent iron oxide.
5. Let the glaze run: stop it about 1 cm above the foot, where it ends in a thick bead.
6. Fire in reduction to about 1300 °C so that the iron forms streaks, drops and spots.
7. Leave the foot ring and the lower wall bare, in a dark grey or purple-brown clay.
8. Add a silver or copper band on the rim for a bowl of high rank.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** A deep conical tea bowl with a wide mouth, made for whisked powdered tea. A small number of ewers and stands accompany it in the tea service.
- **Profile:** Straight sides rise at about 45 degrees from a small foot and turn in just below the lip, so that the rim shows a clear step.
- **Foot and rim:** A short, narrow, bare foot with a straight-cut ring; the rim is thin where the glaze breaks to brown and the lip may carry a silver band.
- **Proportion:** Diameter 11 to 13 cm and height 6 to 7 cm; the wall is 6 to 9 mm thick and the bowl feels heavy for its size.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the cone shape and the step below the lip.
  - Keep the foot small and bare, and the lower wall unglazed.
  - Choose a body that fires dark grey, brown or purple-black, rich in iron.
  - Make the bowl fit one hand and hold hot tea without a handle.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stoneware
- **Secondary:** silver
- **Avoid:** porcelain · earthenware · bone-china
- **Rule:** Use a local stoneware clay that is rich in iron and fires to dark grey or brown. Keep a white body out: the dark clay adds colour to the glaze.

## Forming

- **Methods:**
  - throwing on the wheel in one cone
  - turning the foot ring when leather-hard
  - dipping the bowl in glaze by hand, with the foot held in the fingers
  - stacking the bowls in saggars, one in each
- **Joins:** The bowl has no joins; a metal rim band of silver or copper is fixed on the lip after the firing.
- **Details:** A bead of glaze at the foot, thin brown breaks at the rim, finger marks where the potter held the bowl, and sand marks under the foot from the saggar.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** tenmoku · bare-clay
- **Decorations:** undecorated
- **Pattern:** Fine streaks like a hare's fur, round silver spots like oil on water, or feather marks like a partridge; the kiln makes each one.
- **Gloss:** Glossy to high gloss, about 70 to 95 GU on the glazed wall; the bare foot is dull.
- **Wear:** The rim wears to a brown line where the glaze is thin. The silver band tarnishes, and the glaze keeps its depth.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Black to brown glaze with silver, russet or blue-grey streaks and spots. The only light colour is the bare grey clay at the foot.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A long climbing dragon kiln on a hill slope, fired with wood; the bowls stand in saggars in rows.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:**
  - single high firing of the bowl with the glaze on the raw or lightly fired clay
  - reduction with wood fuel
  - slow cooling, so that iron crystals grow in the glaze
  - hold at the peak, so that the glaze runs and forms the bead
- **Scale:** Many kilns in the hills of Fujian made thousands of bowls for the market, and the court chose the best for tribute.
- **Signs:** A bead of glaze at the foot, stilt or sand marks under the foot, and spots of different size where gas bubbles broke in the glaze.

### Tenmoku iron glaze (material)

A thick glaze with a high share of iron oxide. In a reduction firing the iron forms streaks, spots and a dark, glassy body. Origin: ceramics, Jianyang, Fujian, China, c. 1000.

- Mix a glaze with 5 to 9 per cent of iron oxide.
- Dip the bowl and wipe the glaze from the foot.
- Fire in reduction to 1250 to 1320 °C and hold the peak.
- Cool slowly, so that iron crystals grow in the glaze.
