# Abbasid lustreware

> A cheap clay pot can shine like metal when a white tin glaze and a thin film of reduced silver and copper turn into a mirror of gold.

The first pottery with a metallic sheen. Potters in Abbasid Iraq painted copper and silver oxides over an opaque white tin glaze and fired them in smoke to a gold-brown shine.

**Period:** 800–1050 · **Era:** Medieval glaze · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Early Islamic lustre, Basra lustre, Lustre-painted Abbasid ware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Abbasid lustreware, you MUST:

1. Cover the earthenware body with an opaque white tin glaze 0.5 to 1 mm thick.
2. Fire the glaze first, then paint metal oxides in vinegar or ochre over it.
3. Fire a second time at 600 to 700 °C in a smoky, reducing kiln, so that the metal film forms.
4. Polish the cooled surface to remove the red clay residue and show the gold-brown shine.
5. Fill the surface with geometric bands, birds, vegetal scrolls and Kufic-like script.
6. Leave dark ground with light motifs, or the reverse, in two clear tones.
7. Keep the shape simple: a flared bowl on a low foot ring, so the sheen catches the light.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Wide shallow bowls, small dishes and tile fields; later also jugs and albarelli with a narrow neck.
- **Profile:** A flaring bowl with straight or slightly curved walls and a flat or domed inside for painting.
- **Foot and rim:** A low foot ring or a flat base; the rim is plain, with a thin line of paint at the edge.
- **Proportion:** Bowl diameter 18 to 25 cm, height 6 to 9 cm, and a wall 5 to 8 mm thick.
- **Rules:**
  - Throw the bowl in one piece and turn a low foot ring when leather-hard.
  - Keep the inside open and wide, so that one pattern fills the field.
  - Divide the field into a centre medallion and a ring of border motifs.
  - Leave a small area of white glaze plain to frame the lustre.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · bone-china
- **Rule:** Use a pale buff earthenware that fires soft and takes an opaque white glaze. The body must not be translucent or hard.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the wheel · turning the foot · press moulding of tiles · slip dipping
- **Joins:** Handles and spouts are pulled and luted with slip; most pieces are thrown in one piece.
- **Details:** Three stilt marks inside the foot, a thin pale border, and a brown or green sheen that changes with the angle of the light.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** tin-glaze · alkaline-glaze
- **Decorations:** lustre · underglaze-cobalt
- **Pattern:** A central medallion with a bird or a flower, bands of dots and stripes, vegetal scrolls and Kufic-like script round the rim.
- **Gloss:** The tin glaze is glossy, about 60 to 75 GU; the lustre adds a metallic mirror reflection.
- **Wear:** The lustre film may flake or turn dull; the glaze crazes and the white shows brown through the cracks.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A white ground with metal tones from the lustre: gold, copper, olive and brown, and a rare cobalt blue.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A wood- or reed-fired kiln, with a second lower firing in a muffle or in a smoky chamber for the lustre.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:** biscuit firing · tin glaze dipping · glaze firing at about 1000 °C · painting with lustre pigment · second firing in smoke at 600 to 700 °C · polishing
- **Scale:** Workshops in Basra and Samarra made a large amount of ware; a lustre piece costs two firings and a skilled painter.
- **Signs:** A metallic sheen that changes colour with the angle, a brown line where the metal film is thin, and a polished surface without clay marks.

### Lustre painting (process)

A paint with copper and silver salts is brushed on a glazed pot. A second firing in a smoky kiln leaves a thin layer of metal with a gold or copper shine. Origin: ceramics, Basra, Iraq, c. 800.

- Fire the pot with a tin glaze and let it cool.
- Paint the pattern with a mix of copper or silver salts, ochre and vinegar.
- Fire the pot again at about 600 °C in a smoky kiln.
- Wash off the ochre to show the metallic film.
