# Mass Timber

> A building can be made of engineered timber panels and beams, and so store carbon, build fast off site and show the warm grain of its own structure.

Buildings of engineered wood. Cross-laminated and glued timber panels and beams make floors, walls and frames, and tall buildings of 6 to 25 storeys with warm interiors and a low carbon footprint.

**Period:** 1995–present · **Era:** Postmodern and After · **Domain:** Architecture · **Also called:** New wood architecture, Engineered timber architecture, CLT architecture

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "mass timber", "cross-laminated timber", "clt", "glulam", "new wood architecture", "timber tower", "engineered wood building", "brock commons", "low carbon building", "exposed timber structure".

Good for: mid-rise offices and housing, schools and libraries, timber tower concepts, low-carbon building briefs.

Do not use it when:
- The brief asks for a wide open span of more than 20m or a deep wet basement.
- The brief asks for a thin glass curtain wall.

## System directives

When designing, describing or rendering buildings and interiors in Mass Timber, you MUST:

1. Use cross-laminated timber panels of 0.1 to 0.3m for floors and walls, and glulam beams and columns on a grid of 6 to 9m.
2. Show the timber: leave the soffit, the beam and the column visible, with fire-char allowances of 40 to 60mm.
3. Prefabricate in the factory, and lift the panels to the site in a plan with a tolerance of 2 to 5mm.
4. Keep the grid regular and repeating, because the panel and the truck size set the module.
5. Protect the timber from water: use a deep eave, a rainscreen and a ventilated cavity of 20 to 40mm.
6. Combine with a concrete core or hybrid floor for stiffness and fire when the building passes 8 storeys.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Timber hidden behind plasterboard on every surface.
- **NEVER:** A grid so irregular that each panel is unique.
- **NEVER:** Open timber end grain exposed to the rain.
- **NEVER:** Fake wood-pattern cladding on a steel frame.
- **NEVER:** Long untreated timber spans without fire design.
- **NEVER:** Dark opaque finishes that hide the grain.

## Massing & form

- **Form:** A regular rectangular block of 6 to 25 storeys, with stacked floors and a timber grid on the facade.
- **Proportion:** A bay of 6 to 9m by 6 to 9m; storey height of 3.0 to 3.6m; a plan of 15 to 25m deep.
- **Roof:** A flat roof with a timber panel deck and a deep overhang, or a green roof.
- **Relation to ground:** A concrete or masonry base keeps the timber 0.3 to 0.5m above the ground and out of the splash zone.

## Structure

- **System:** Cross-laminated timber floors and walls, glulam beams and columns, often with a concrete core.
- **Span and module:** A grid of 6 to 9m; slab span of 5 to 8m; CLT thickness of 0.1 to 0.3m; glulam sections 0.2 by 0.4m.
- **Expression:** The glulam column, the beam and the panel edge are the structure and the interior, and they are left visible.

## Materials

- **Primary:** cross-laminated timber · glulam · laminated veneer lumber
- **Secondary:** concrete core · steel connectors · glass · charred timber cladding
- **Finish:** Natural and warm: sealed or oiled timber with visible grain, and char or clear sealer outside.

### Laminated timber (material)

Thin boards or veneers glued in layers, so that wood forms long spans, bent shapes and large panels. Origin: architecture, Weimar, 1906.

- Glue boards of 20 to 45mm for glulam; set the grain of all layers in one direction.
- Cross the layers at 90° for a panel (CLT); use 3, 5 or 7 layers.
- Bend veneers of 1 to 3mm in a mould for furniture; glue 5 to 9 layers.
- Leave the layered edge in view as a line pattern.
