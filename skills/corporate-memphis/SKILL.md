# Corporate Memphis

> Friendly, abstract people with unreal bodies make a corporate product feel human and inclusive without the risk of a specific face.

Flat vector illustrations of people with oversized limbs and tiny heads, drawn in a bright palette of 4 to 6 hues with no outlines. Facebook's Alegria system set it in 2017, and many software firms copied it.

**Period:** 2017–2022 · **Era:** Touchscreen · **Domain:** Graphic & UI Design · **Also called:** Alegria, Big Tech Art, Flat Vector People, Globohomo

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "corporate memphis", "alegria", "flat people", "big tech illustration", "tiny heads long arms", "saas illustration", "startup illustration", "flat vector people", "friendly illustration".

Good for: SaaS landing pages, onboarding screens, empty states, explainer sections, help centres.

Do not use it when:
- The brand needs a distinct voice, because the style is the most copied look of the late 2010s.
- The topic is serious, medical or financial and a playful tone would reduce trust.
- The audience is design-literate and reads the style as generic or dismissive.

## System directives

When generating UI or graphic layouts in Corporate Memphis, you MUST:

1. Draw people with limbs 1.5 to 3 times longer than natural, hands and heads reduced to 10 to 15% of body height, and torsos of simple curved shapes.
2. Fill shapes with flat colour and use no outline, no gradient and no shading; add one 10% darker tone for a fold only when needed.
3. Colour skin in non-realistic tones such as purple, blue, green and orange, and use 4 to 6 hues across the whole scene.
4. Place figures with small plants, phone screens, charts and floating geometric shapes, scaled to be larger than the people.
5. Use curved, rounded outlines of shapes with a minimum 8px radius, and give limbs a stretched, noodle-like form.
6. Put the illustration at 40 to 60% of the width of a hero section and pair it with a left-aligned sans-serif headline.
7. Keep faces blank or reduced to a dot or a simple mark, with no detailed eyes or mouths.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Realistic human proportions with a head of 12.5% of body height.
- **NEVER:** Outlines, gradients and drop shadows on figures.
- **NEVER:** Photographic faces in the illustrated hero.
- **NEVER:** A single skin colour that is the same across all figures.
- **NEVER:** Detailed facial expressions and fine eyelashes.
- **NEVER:** More than 6 hues in one scene.
- **NEVER:** Illustrations with hand-drawn texture or visible brush marks.

## Spatial & grid logic

- **Grid mechanics:** A 12-column web grid with 24px gutters and a 1200px maximum width; hero sections split into a 5-column text block and a 7-column illustration block.
- **Whitespace ratio:** 40–55% of the surface stays empty; the spacing scale is 8, 16, 24, 48, 80, 120px, and illustrations sit in large clear areas.
- **Alignment rules:** Headlines and text sit flush left; illustrations are placed at the right or the centre, and figures overlap their container edges by 10 to 20% to break the box.
- **Container geometry:** Rounded rectangles with a 12 to 24px radius for cards and 999px pill buttons; background blobs of organic curved shapes sit behind the figures.

## Typography

- **Classifications:** geometric sans serif · humanist sans serif
- **Tracking:** Display sizes take -0.02em to 0em; body stays at 0; labels take +0.02em.
- **Leading:** Display 1.1 to 1.2; body 1.5 to 1.6.
- **Hierarchy rules:**
  - Use bold (600 to 700) for headlines and regular (400) for body text.
  - Set headlines at 40 to 72px and body at 16 to 20px, with a ratio of at least 2.5 between them.
  - Set headlines in sentence case, in near-black or deep navy, never in the illustration palette.
  - Limit a headline to 8 to 12 words and a supporting line to 20 to 25 words.

## Colour & lighting

- **Palette structure:** A neutral white or very light base, a deep navy for text, and 4 to 6 saturated flat hues in illustration, with no hue on more than 25% of the scene.
- **Contrast rules:** Text holds at least 4.5:1; Deep Navy on white reaches 14:1; light hues such as Marigold and Mint stay on illustration and never carry text.
- **Surface treatment:** Opaque flat colour fields with no gradient and no outline; background blobs use one pale tint at 100% opacity.
- **Light and shadow:** None. There is no light source; at most one tone 10% darker marks an overlap or a fold.

## Materiality & texture

- **Surface simulation:** None; figures and objects are flat cut-out shapes that imitate vector paper cut-outs only in their silhouette.
- **Border rules:** No outlines on illustration shapes; UI cards take a 1px border in #E5E7F0 or a soft 0 8px 24px shadow at 8% navy.
- **Blur and elevation:** No blur on illustration; UI cards may use one shadow of 0 8px 24px at 8% opacity and no more than 2 elevation levels.

## Ornamentation & iconography

- **Iconography:** Solid duotone icons on a 24px grid with a 2px stroke and rounded terminals in navy and one accent hue.
- **Illustration:** Flat vector scenes of 1 to 4 figures with long arms and legs, tiny heads and non-realistic skin tones, shown with plants, devices and floating shapes.
- **Decorative elements:** background blobs · floating circles and squiggles · oversized plants · giant phone and chart props · overlapping geometric shapes

## Motion & interactivity

- **Born on screen:** yes
- **Transition physics:** Soft, rounded movement: shapes float with a 2 to 4s sine loop of 8 to 12px, and sections fade and rise by 24px on scroll.
- **Easing:** cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)
- **Duration (ms):** 400
- **Hover and active:** Hover lifts buttons by 2px and darkens the fill by 8%; active returns them to 0px; illustration elements shift by 4 to 8px with a gentle overshoot.

- **Icon family:** the sprite is cut from Phosphor (Duotone) by Phosphor Icons, licence MIT. Reason: A friendly round line with one flat tint: the same two-tone logic as the illustrations. It is an open-source stand-in for a demo. For a product, use the icon library of the brand and choose the family by the rules below.
- **Display type:** weight 700, tracking -0.02em, leading 1.1, case: none.
- **Weights:** display 700, subhead 600, body 400, bold in text 600, labels and buttons 600. The style uses 3 weights (400, 600, 700) and no other. The display is 300 heavier than the body. The accent is weight: a heavier word is the accent, so the steps between the weights are clear.
- **Body type:** 17px, leading 1.6.
- **Page:** hero split, left aligned, airy density, accent tilt 0°.
- **Text colour:** `--color-text` is for the main reading field only. On each field, use its token: `--color-on-bg`, `--color-on-surface`, `--color-on-primary`, `--color-on-accent`.

### How to choose an icon pack

An icon pack is a typeface for pictures. Choose it with the same care as the text face. These rules hold for an open-source set and for a licensed library.

1. **The stroke matches the stem.** Set an icon next to the body text at text size. The line of the icon and the stem of a letter have the same weight.
2. **Terminals and corners follow the radius token.** Butt caps and mitre joins go with a radius of zero. Round caps and round joins go with a soft radius.
3. **The fill mode follows the technology of the era.** Print eras cut solid forms. Screen eras with depth fill and shade the form. Current product interfaces draw an outline.
4. **The metaphors are not younger than the style.** Each picture is an object that the era knew. An icon with a later metaphor breaks the period.
5. **One family, one grid.** All icons come from one family and one variant, drawn on one field. Do not mix families on one surface.
6. **The family covers the product.** List each concept that the product needs before you commit. A family that has no icon for one of them is the wrong family.
7. **The colour is a token.** An icon takes currentColor. A second tone comes from a token, not from the file.
8. **The licence permits the use.** Read the licence of the set. Give credit where the licence asks for it. A product with a brand uses its licensed library and applies the same rules to choose the family.

### Isometric projection (process)

A drawing with three axes at equal angles and one scale, with no vanishing point, so that sizes can be measured. Origin: architecture, Cambridge, 1822.

- Draw the three axes at 120° to each other; on a pixel grid, use a 2:1 slope (26.57°).
- Keep one scale on all three axes and draw no vanishing point.

## Lineage fallbacks

- If the user asks for **more serious**: move toward [`swiss-international`](../swiss-international/SKILL.md) (Swiss International Style). Replace illustrated people with objective photography, reduce the palette to black, white and one accent, and set text flush left on a grid.
- If the user asks for **more wild**: move toward [`memphis-design`](../memphis-design/SKILL.md) (Memphis Design). Add terrazzo speckle, zigzags and squiggles in saturated primaries with black outlines of 3px; keep the flat fills.
- If the user asks for **less cartoon**: move toward [`flat-design`](../flat-design/SKILL.md) (Flat Design). Replace figures with geometric icons and colour blocks of solid fills; keep the palette and the layout.
