# Art Nouveau tiles

> A factory tile can carry the line of a plant, if the mould and the glaze do the drawing.

Moulded earthenware tiles with a plant line in low relief and a deep, soft glaze. The tile joins a flowing stem, a lily or a woman's hair to a sea-green or plum ground. Factories made them for shop fronts, stairs, hearths and tube stations.

**Period:** 1895–1915 · **Era:** Art pottery and reform · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Jugendstil tiles, Secessionist tiles, Barbotine tiles, Moulded Art Nouveau tiles

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Art Nouveau tiles, you MUST:

1. Draw the design as one curved line, thicker at the base and thin at the tip, that flows across 2 to 4 tiles.
2. Press the line in low relief of 1 to 3 mm, so that the glaze pools in the hollows.
3. Use a matt or satin glaze with a soft break in colour at each edge of the relief.
4. Choose muted colours: sea green, plum, ochre, grey blue and cream, with 2 or 3 in one panel.
5. Make the tile 6 inches square or a tall panel, and plan a border and a frieze for the same line.
6. Use a motif from nature: lily, iris, poppy, peacock feather, or a woman with long hair.
7. Apply the colour in thick pools, or draw each line with a trail of slip, so that each colour keeps to its field.
8. Set the tiles in hearths, porches, stair risers and shop fronts, where the line leads the eye.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Square wall tiles, tall panels, hearth and fireplace tiles, friezes, and stair risers.
- **Profile:** A flat tile with a low modelled relief of 1 to 3 mm; the relief edges are soft and rounded.
- **Foot and rim:** The back is ribbed for the mortar; the edge is square and the glaze stops at the arris.
- **Proportion:** A tile is 6 inches square, or 6 by 12 inches for a frieze; a panel joins 2 to 6 tiles.
- **Rules:**
  - Let the main line cross the joint between tiles.
  - Keep the relief low so that the tile stacks and the glaze does not run off.
  - Fix 3 or 4 glaze colours for a design, and keep each to its area.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · stoneware
- **Rule:** Use a white or buff earthenware that presses well in a plaster mould and takes a soft glaze. Keep porcelain and stoneware out.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pressing the tile from dust in a steel die · making a plaster master from a drawn design · pouring a thin trail of slip along the line · dipping the tile in a coloured glaze · wiping the high relief by hand
- **Joins:** Tiles are set in cement on a wall or a hearth, and the line is planned across the joint.
- **Details:** A soft pool of glaze in each hollow, a thin break on each edge of the relief, and a pottery mark on the back.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** matte-glaze · lead-glaze · coloured-gloss
- **Decorations:** relief-moulding · underglaze-polychrome · slip-trailing
- **Pattern:** A curved plant line in low relief; one or two soft glaze colours pool in the hollows and break on the high points.
- **Gloss:** Matt to satin, 10 to 60 GU.
- **Wear:** The relief edge is the first to wear and shows the pale body; the pools of glaze stay deep for decades.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Muted sea and plant tones with one warm accent; the break of the glaze gives the second tone of each colour.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A coal-fired or gas muffle kiln, with the tiles in saggars.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** pressing in a steel die · biscuit firing at about 1100 °C · dipping or trailing in glaze · glaze firing at 1000 to 1050 °C
- **Scale:** Factories pressed tiles by the thousand in a day, and the same line could go into a thousand hearths.
- **Signs:** Soft pools of glaze, a thin break at the edge of the relief and clean, square edges.

### Whiplash line (motif)

A long S-shaped line whose curve changes along its length, drawn from a plant stem; it joins structure and ornament in one stroke. Origin: architecture, Brussels, c. 1893.

- Draw each line as an S-curve with a radius that changes along its length.
- Swell the stroke from 1 to 4 units along the curve, as a stem thickens at the base.
- Join letters, frame and plant forms with the same line; leave no straight edge to break the flow.
- Use few colours, 2 to 4, in muted tones.
