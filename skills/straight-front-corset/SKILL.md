# Straight-Front Corset

> A corset that is cut straight in front and low on the bust is healthier and gives the new S-shaped line of 1900.

The long, straight-busk corset of the Edwardian years. It presses the stomach flat, throws the hip back and the bust forward, and gives the S-curve of the 1900 silhouette.

**Period:** 1900–1908 · **Era:** Belle Époque and the Great War · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** S-bend corset, Health corset, Swan-bill corset, Edwardian corset

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Straight-Front Corset, you MUST:

1. Cut the front busk long and straight, 35 to 45 cm, so that the stomach lies flat.
2. Cut the top edge low at the bust, so that the bust is free and sits forward.
3. Cut the back to curve in at the waist and out at the hip, to push the hip back.
4. Reduce the pressure on the stomach, and put the hold on the hip and the back.
5. Make the body of coutil or strong cotton, with spring steel in the busk and the bones.
6. Add four suspenders to the lower edge, for the stockings.
7. Trim the top with machine lace and a narrow ribbon.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** An S-curve: a full bust forward, a small waist, and a hip pushed back, with a flat, long front.
- **Line:** A long line from the bust to the thigh; the front is straight and the back curves in.
- **Fit:** Firm on the waist and the hip, low and loose on the bust; the busk guides the stomach flat.
- **Proportion:** Busk 35 to 45 cm; waist 55 to 60 cm; the corset reaches 10 to 15 cm below the waist at the front.
- **Rules:** Run the front busk straight from the bust to the groin. · Cut the top edge at or below the bust line. · Set a steel strip at each side of the back lacing. · Hang four suspenders from the lower edge. · Cover the top with a corset cover of cotton and lace.

## Fabrics & materials

- **Primary:** coutil · satin · spring-steel · elastic-webbing
- **Secondary:** machine-lace · cambric · taffeta · frame-knit-hosiery · whalebone · silk-twill · brass
- **Avoid:** spandex-jersey · powernet · nylon-tricot · jersey
- **Rule:** Use coutil for the body and spring steel for the busk; add satin or silk for show, with elastic webbing in the suspenders.

## Construction

- **Methods:** long panels cut to a graded pattern · straight busk of two spring-steel plates · spring-steel bones at each seam · metal eyelets in a laced back · elastic suspenders at the hem · lace and ribbon at the top edge
- **Closures:** A long busk of studs and loops at the front, with a cord laced through metal eyelets at the back.
- **Details:** Suspenders of elastic with a metal clip, a ribbon at the top, a small lace flounce, and a stay-bone at each hip.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** matte · glazed · embroidered · block-printed
- **Pattern:** Plain white or pale colour; fine broderie, small flowers or a stripe on a corset for show.
- **Gloss:** Coutil at 3–8 GU; satin and taffeta at 30–60 GU.
- **Wear:** The lower edge creases from sitting, and the busk bends after a year of daily use.

### Metal eyelet (technology)

A ring of steel that is pressed into the lacing hole of a corset, so that the cord can pull hard and the hole does not tear. Origin: fashion, Europe, 1827.

- Punch a small hole in the cloth.
- Press a steel ring through the hole and fold the rim over the cloth.
- Lace a cord through the rings in a cross pattern.
