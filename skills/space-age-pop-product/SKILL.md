# Space Age pop product

> The new plastics free the form: one mould makes a whole object in one piece, and its colour and curve show a hopeful, fast, technical future.

The objects of the plastic age: chairs, lamps and tubs moulded in one piece from fibreglass, acrylic and ABS, with chrome and bright colour, in rounded forms that look like capsules, eggs and pods.

**Period:** 1960–1975 · **Era:** Post-war modern · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Space Age design, Pop plastic design, Sixties plastic furniture

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Space Age pop product, you MUST:

1. Mould each major body in one piece, so that the object has no visible joint on its main surface.
2. Draw each body as a sphere, an egg, a pod or a tube with soft corners of 25 mm radius or more.
3. Give each object one saturated colour: orange, red, yellow, white or black, with a gloss finish.
4. Use chrome-plated steel for the stem, the base or the frame; keep it bright and thin.
5. Cut round holes or slots in a shell where the form needs light, air or a seat.
6. Show the object as a sculpture: it stands alone and looks good from every side.
7. Make each volume read as a capsule: round windows, round hatches and round buttons.

## Form

- **Geometry:** The sphere, the egg, the capsule, the tube and the pod, with a round hole as the main detail.
- **Silhouette:** A single smooth mass on a slim chrome stem or a low plinth, with no seam on the outline.
- **Edges:** Soft edges with a radius of 25 to 40 mm; no sharp corner on a body; the lip of a shell has a radius of 5 to 10 mm.
- **Proportion:** A pod chair is about 100 cm high and wide; a pendant globe is 30 to 50 cm in diameter; a stem is 3 to 5 cm thick.
- **Ornament:** None applied. The colour, the gloss and the round cut-outs give the surface its interest.
- **Rules:**
  - Each body is one moulded piece with no more than one visible seam.
  - Each exterior corner has a radius of 25 mm or more.
  - Each object uses one bright colour on 80% or more of its surface.
  - Chrome covers less than 25% of the object and it carries the load.
  - A round or oval cut-out is the only surface detail.

## Materials

- **Primary:** fibreglass · acrylic · abs-plastic · polypropylene · plastic-laminate · chrome-plated-steel
- **Secondary:** aluminium · chrome-plated-brass · stainless-steel · opal-glass · coloured-glass · wool · vitreous-china · mirror-glass · leather · enamelled-steel
- **Avoid:** mahogany · wrought-iron · terracotta · washi-paper · jute
- **Rule:** Plastic makes about 70% of the visible surface; chrome and glass make most of the rest, and fabric appears only as a thin pad.

## Finish & texture

- **Finishes:** moulded-gloss · polished · clear-gloss · lacquered · gloss-enamel
- **Textures:** mirror-smooth · satin-smooth
- **Gloss:** 85–98 GU on moulded plastic and chrome; 10–40 GU only on glass and cloth
- **Patina:** The style wants a new, bright and unmarked surface; a scratch or a yellow tint is a fault.

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** chrome
- **Section:** Stems and tubes 30–50 mm; a rail or a rod 10–16 mm
- **Joints:** One-piece mouldings; a chrome plate or a swivel bolt joins the shell to the base.
- **Handles:** A round hole in the body, a flush disc or a short chrome loop.
- **Switches and taps:** A large round push button or a flat disc rocker in coloured plastic; a chrome lever tap on a smooth cylinder.

## Techniques

### Cantilever (process)

A beam or slab fixed at one end that carries load beyond its support, so that it seems to hang in the air. Origin: architecture, Northern Europe, c. 1300.

- Fix the beam at one end and anchor it with a back span of at least 1 part to every 2 parts of the overhang.
- Keep the free end thin; a depth of 1/8 to 1/10 of the span suits steel and concrete.
- Repeat the horizontal line in a run of 2 or 3 projecting planes to give a strong shadow.
