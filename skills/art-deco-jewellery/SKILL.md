# Art Deco Jewellery

> A jewel is a machine-age design: a hard, flat pattern of line, angle and contrast in white metal and stones.

Hard, bright geometry in platinum. The stones are diamonds in baguette and square cuts, black onyx, rock crystal and carved coloured stones, set in strips, fans and sunbursts. It was shown at Paris in 1925.

**Period:** 1925–1939 · **Era:** Interwar modern · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Jazz Age jewellery, Moderne jewellery, Tutti Frutti jewellery

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Art Deco Jewellery, you MUST:

1. Build the design from straight lines, circles and angles, in a symmetrical pattern.
2. Contrast white and black: diamonds and platinum beside black onyx or black enamel.
3. Cut stones in sharp shapes: baguette, square, triangle and calibre, and set them flat in rows.
4. Use platinum or white gold for the frame, with the claws hidden and the lines as thin as a thread.
5. Add one strong colour in a carved stone: emerald, ruby or sapphire carved as a leaf, a fruit or a flower (the Tutti Frutti style).
6. Take forms from the machine, from Egypt, from India and from Cubism: a fan, a sunburst, a stepped skyline.
7. Make the piece as a strip, a clip or a bracelet that moves, with a flat front and a hinge.
8. Set stones with no visible metal, in the mystery setting, so that the stones look like one surface.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A flat, geometric jewel: a strap bracelet, a double clip brooch, a long drop earring or a ring with a large central stone.
- **Line:** Straight and symmetrical, in steps, fans and circles, with sharp corners.
- **Fit:** A strap bracelet lies flat on the wrist, a clip sits on the dress at the hip, a long earring hangs below the bobbed hair.
- **Proportion:** A strap bracelet is 2 to 4 cm wide. A double clip is 6 to 8 cm wide. A long drop earring is 5 to 8 cm long.
- **Rules:**
  - Set baguette diamonds in rows of three, five or seven, with a black onyx band between them.
  - Fit a strap bracelet from many flat links that lie close to the wrist.
  - Wear a long earring with a geometric drop over a bobbed haircut.
  - Pin a double clip at the hip or the lapel, and join the two halves as a brooch.

### Mystery setting (skill)

A method that sets cut stones on a hidden rail so that no metal shows between them and the stones form one surface. Origin: fashion, Paris, France, 1933.

- Cut each stone with a groove on its sides.
- Slide the stones on thin metal rails inside a frame.
- Close the frame so that no claw or prong shows.

### Platinum mounting (process)

A frame of platinum, a metal strong enough to hold stones with thin lines, so that the metal nearly disappears. Origin: fashion, Paris, France, c. 1900.

- Draw the frame with thin, open lines.
- Saw and file the platinum and join the parts with a torch.
- Set each stone in fine claws and add a milgrained edge.
