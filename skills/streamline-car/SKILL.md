# Streamline Car

> A fast car looks like a drop of water: one smooth shell with no break in the line, from the grille to the tail.

The car as a teardrop. A smooth, rounded body with the mudguards merged into the sides, a sloped tail, a raked grille and bands of chrome that show speed.

**Period:** 1933–1950 · **Era:** Interwar modern · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Streamliner, Streamline Moderne car, Aerodynamic car

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Streamline Car, you MUST:

1. Round the nose, and set the grille at a rake of 10 to 20 degrees.
2. Merge the mudguards into the body, so that the flank has one unbroken curve.
3. Slope the tail in a long taper, or end it in a short fastback of about 30 degrees.
4. Hide the headlamps in the wings or behind the grille, and cover the rear wheel with a skirt.
5. Run two or three horizontal chrome bands along the body, to show speed.
6. Divide the windscreen in two and slope it back by 25 to 30 degrees.
7. Set the cabin with a dashboard of Bakelite and painted metal, and mohair cloth on the seats.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A teardrop: a rounded nose, a body that swells at the middle and a long tail that narrows to a point. The wheels sit inside the body line.
- **Line:** A smooth curve from the grille over the roof to the tail, with chrome speed lines along the flank.
- **Fit:** The cabin is closed and rounded; the doors follow the curve of the body, and the windows are narrow and curved.
- **Proportion:** Length to height about 3 to 1; the roof is 1.5 to 1.6 m; the tail tapers over 25 to 30 percent of the car.
- **Rules:**
  - Cover the rear wheels with a fender skirt.
  - Slope the windscreen and the rear window to match the line of the roof.
  - Use a waterfall grille: tall, narrow, with fine vertical or horizontal bars.
  - Pair a body of one colour with a chrome band, or two soft tones split at the waist.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Chrome plating (process)

A thin layer of chromium is put on steel by electrolysis. The layer gives a hard, bright surface that does not tarnish. Origin: fashion, New York, United States, c. 1924.

- Clean the steel part and plate it with copper and nickel.
- Put the part in a chromic acid bath and pass a current through it.
- Buff the part to a mirror finish.

### Sheet-metal pressing (process)

A press forms a flat steel sheet into a body panel in one stroke. The panels are then welded into a body. Origin: fashion, Philadelphia, United States, c. 1914.

- Cut the steel sheet to size and load it into the die.
- Close the press so that the sheet takes the shape of the die.
- Weld the panels into one shell and grind the seams smooth.

### Wind-tunnel testing (tool)

A model or a car stands in a tunnel with a strong air flow. The test shows how the body pushes the air and how much drag it makes. Origin: fashion, London, United Kingdom, 1871.

- Fix the model to the balance in the tunnel.
- Run the fan at a set speed and measure the drag and the lift.
- Change the body form and test again.
