# Abstract Expressionism

> A painting is the record of an act, and its scale, its marks and its colour put the viewer inside the feeling and not in front of an image.

Large abstract paintings in which the act of painting is the subject: gesture, drip and slash in one camp, large soft fields of colour in the other. The canvas is an arena for an event, and it is big enough to surround the viewer.

**Period:** 1940s–1960s · **Era:** Avant-garde · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Action painting, New York School, Colour Field (early)

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Abstract Expressionism, you MUST:

1. Paint at a scale of 2 to 5m on the long side, so that the canvas fills the field of view at a distance of 1–2m.
2. Show every mark: stroke, drip, splash and scrape at 20–200mm, laid by the whole arm and not by the wrist.
3. Cover the canvas edge to edge in an all-over composition, with no single focal point and a similar density at the edges and the centre.
4. Drop the object entirely; the picture refers to nothing outside its own marks.
5. Hold a deliberate tension between a few opposed forces: black against white, thick against thin, order against chance.
6. In the colour-field branch, replace the mark with 2–4 large, soft-edged fields of thin colour stacked vertically.
7. Keep the first mark visible: show corrections, drips and the traces of the process.

## Composition

- **Layout:** An all-over field of marks with no central focus, or in the colour-field branch 2–4 stacked horizontal fields that float on a ground.
- **Focal point:** None: the eye travels across the whole canvas; any accent sits within a field of equal density.
- **Format:** A very large canvas, 2 to 5m wide, at ratios from 1:1 to 2:1; the work reaches past the edge on all four sides.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| scale | Make the work 2–5m on the long side so that a viewer at 1–2m sees it as an environment. |
| rhythm | Lay a gestural mark 50–200 times at changing speed and length, with no regular beat. |
| balance | Reach an informal balance across the field by equal density at the four edges within 15%. |
| contrast | Use a single strong tension: black against white, or one saturated field against a deep neighbour. |
| negative-space | In the colour-field branch, leave 20–40% of the canvas as an open ground between the fields. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A restricted palette of 3–5 colours, used at full strength: black and white for gesture; deep red, ochre, blue and black for colour field.
- **Distribution:** Gesture: a dominant ground 50–60%, a contrasting mark colour 30–40%, accent 5–10%. Colour field: 2–4 fields each 20–40%.
- **Ground:** Raw or white-primed canvas; sometimes a coloured stain on raw duck canvas.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint and house enamel, thinned for the drip and used thick for the slash.
- **Support:** Very large stretched or unstretched canvas, often on the floor or on a wall.
- **Brushwork:** Broad, fast strokes of 50–200mm with a house brush, a stick or a trowel; drips of 2–10mm in a free line.
- **Paint handling:** Wet-in-wet, with layers of 3–10 passes; thick impasto of 5–20mm against thin stain; drips are left, never wiped.
- **Edges:** Ragged, torn or soft-bled edges between marks; in the colour-field branch, the edge is a soft halo of 20–50mm.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** No depicted light; the colour field glows by chroma and by the thin paint over a light ground.
- **Modelling:** None. Value change comes from the density and overlap of marks, not from a lit form.
- **Shadow:** None. Darkness is a black mark or a deep field with its own weight.
- **Temperature:** Gesture: a cool black and white with one warm accent. Colour field: warm and cool fields next to each other, with a vibration at the edge.

## Subject

- **Typical:** No subject; the title may allude to myth, nature or a date, as in an 'Untitled' or a 'Number 19'.
- **Figure scale:** No figure; the human body is the measure of the scale of the canvas.
- **Treatment:** Direct, serious and personal: the mark is a trace of the artist's body and decision.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Matt to semi-gloss, with no varnish; the raw state of the paint is kept.
- **Texture:** Rough and thick: drips, ridges, canvas weave and stains remain in view.

### Soak-stain (process, invented here)

Thin paint is poured on raw, unprimed canvas that lies on the floor; the cloth drinks the colour and the colour and the weave become one. Origin: painting, New York, 1952.

- Thin oil with turpentine, or acrylic with water, until it flows like ink.
- Lay the canvas flat and unprimed on the floor; pour in pools and guide the paint with a sponge or a tilt of the cloth.
- Leave 30% to 60% of the cloth bare so that the raw colour is part of the picture.
- Work at a size of 2 metres or more so that the field is wider than the viewer's sight.
