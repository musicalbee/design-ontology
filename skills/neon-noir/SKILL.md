# Neon noir

> Coloured signs and a synth score turn the noir trap into a glowing night city, so the viewer reads loneliness in a clean, still, stylised frame.

A night crime film in colour. A lone driver or a replicant moves through a wet city of signs. Magenta and cyan light hits glass, rain and chrome. The pace is slow, the score is synthesised and the symmetry is cold.

**Period:** 1981–today · **Era:** Tape, cable and the blockbuster · **Domain:** Film & Video · **Also called:** Neo-noir, Synth noir

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "neon noir", "neo-noir", "drive 2011", "blade runner", "magenta and cyan night", "synth noir".

Good for: night city and car video prompts, music videos and title sequences, perfume, fashion and car films, game and trailer art direction.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a warm, daylight or family tone.
- The scene needs fast action with a lot of cuts.

## System directives

When planning, shooting, prompting, editing or grading moving images in Neon noir, you MUST:

1. Light the night with neon in magenta and cyan, and let one sign be the key and a second sign the rim.
2. Compose in 2.39:1 with the figure small and central, in a symmetric street or a corridor.
3. Wet the ground so it mirrors the signs, and add haze to show the beam.
4. Move the camera on a slow dolly or a car mount at 0.2 to 0.6 m/s, or hold it still.
5. Hold a take for 6 to 15 s, with a pause before and after each line and a long close shot on the face.
6. Use a synthesiser score with a steady pulse, and keep the dialogue short.
7. Keep the hero silent and the violence short, sudden and clear.

## Composition

- **Framing:** A symmetric 2.39:1 frame: a lone figure in a lit corridor, a street or a car, with a sign in the top corner.
- **Staging in depth:** 3 planes: a wet foreground, the figure and a far sign or tower in haze.
- **Subject placement:** The figure centred or on a third line, at 15% to 30% of the frame height.

## Light

- **Quality:** Coloured and hard from neon tubes, with a haze that shows the beam and a deep black.
- **Direction:** From the side or behind, with the sign in the frame as a key.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 6:1 or higher
- **Colour temperature:** Mixed: neon at 2700K to 10000K, or gels in magenta and cyan
- **Source:** Neon signs, LED panels and car lamps

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** A graded night with a magenta key and a cyan fill, deep teal blacks and clean, glossy highlights.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Shift the shadows toward teal by 10% and the highlights toward magenta by 10%.
  - Raise the saturation of magenta and cyan by 20% to 30%.
  - Set the black point at 3% and keep the blacks deep.
  - Add a bloom of 10% to 15% on the neon.
  - Add light grain at 5% to 10%.
- **Texture:** Glow around the neon, wet gloss and light grain

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Chiaroscuro (process)

A hard contrast of light and dark that models form: one strong source, deep shadow and a small area of light. Origin: painting, Italy, c. 1500.

- Light the subject from one hard source at 30° to 60° above and to the side; let the shadow side fall to near black.
- Keep the light on 20% to 30% of the frame and let the rest sink into shadow.
- Set the key light to fill light at a ratio of 8:1 or more for a dramatic mood.
- Cut hard shapes into the light with blinds, grilles or foliage so that the shadow becomes a pattern.

### Coloured gel lighting (process)

A white lamp covered with a sheet of coloured film, so that a set or a face is lit in red, blue or green with no natural cause. Origin: film & video, Rome, c. 1964.

- Put a saturated gel (red, blue or green) over a hard source; use one colour for each zone of the set.
- Light the face from the side in one colour and the wall behind it in a second colour from the opposite side.
- Keep the key light at one stop or more above the fill so that the colour reads as shadow and as light.
- Do not justify the colour with a lamp in the frame; the colour is the mood.

### Neon glow (material)

Light as a line of coloured gas, with a soft halo that spills onto the dark ground around it. Origin: graphic & ui design, Paris, 1912.

- Draw the tube as a line of 2 to 6mm in one saturated colour; use no more than 2 colours in one sign.
- Add a halo of 10 to 40px at 30% to 60% opacity in the same hue.
- Set the sign on a ground darker than 10% lightness, so that the halo shows.
- Keep a bright, near-white core inside the line.
