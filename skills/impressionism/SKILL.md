# Impressionism

> A picture records the impression of light on a surface at one moment, not the object as it is known to be.

Painting of the first look: quick, broken strokes of pure colour that record changing light on a modern scene. The painters work outdoors, drop the outline and the black shadow, and let the viewer's eye mix the colour.

**Period:** 1872–1886 · **Era:** Nineteenth century · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Plein-air painting, Impressionist painting

- The brief asks for a hard graphic outline or a dark dramatic mood.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Impressionism, you MUST:

1. Paint the scene in one session of 2 hours or less, so that the light is the same across the canvas.
2. Apply colour in separate, visible touches of 5–20mm and let them mix in the eye at a distance.
3. Paint shadow in colour: use blue, violet or green at the same value as the lit ground, and use no black.
4. Set the value range narrow: most of the picture sits within 3 or 4 steps of a 10-step scale.
5. Put complementary pairs side by side (orange and blue, red and green) to make the vibration of light.
6. Crop as a snapshot: let the edge cut figures and objects, and place the horizon high or low.
7. Choose modern leisure as the subject: boulevards, rivers, gardens, stations and cafés.

## Composition

- **Layout:** An open, snapshot-like frame with an uneven weight; diagonal paths, rivers and rows of trees lead the eye, and there is no central pyramid.
- **Focal point:** A small area of the strongest contrast or the warmest colour, on a third line, at 15–25% of the picture area.
- **Format:** Landscape canvas at 4:3 or 3:2; figures and objects are cropped by the edges; the horizon sits at 1/3 or 2/3.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| rule-of-thirds | Place the horizon at 1/3 or 2/3 of the height, and the main accent near a third intersection. |
| negative-space | Let sky, water or a wall of bright light fill 30–50% of the canvas. |
| leading-lines | Use a road, a river bank or a row of trees as a diagonal that enters from a corner. |
| colour-distribution | Set 60% of the area in a dominant key (all warm or all cool), 30% in its neighbours, and 10% in the complement. |
| framing | Use an edge crop or a foreground branch to frame the view, as if the painter had no time to arrange it. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A bright, high-key palette of pure pigments, with shadows in complementary colour and a few mixed greys; the colour is optical, and mixing stays on the canvas.
- **Distribution:** Light and mid values 70–80%, saturated touches 15–20%, dark accents under 10%.
- **Ground:** A pale, light-grey or cream commercial ground, which shows through gaps between touches.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint from pre-made metal tubes, applied wet-in-wet.
- **Support:** A portable canvas, 50 by 65cm or smaller, with a pale commercial ground.
- **Brushwork:** Short, broken strokes of 5–20mm, laid in different directions for each element: horizontal for water, vertical for trees, a dab for flowers.
- **Paint handling:** Alla prima in one session; thick paint (impasto) in the lights and thin, dragged paint in the shadows; colours stay unmixed on the palette.
- **Edges:** Lost or vibrating edges: a form dissolves into its neighbour; no outline anywhere.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** Natural daylight outdoors, which changes with the hour; the picture shows one hour and one weather.
- **Modelling:** Form is built by a change of colour temperature and not by dark and light: a warm lit side against a cool shadow side at the same value.
- **Shadow:** Coloured shadow in blue or violet with a value only 1–2 steps below the light; reflected colour from nearby surfaces.
- **Temperature:** A dominant warm light against cool shadow, or the reverse at dusk; the contrast is the picture.

## Subject

- **Typical:** Gardens, rivers and bridges, haystacks, boulevards in rain, cafés and railway stations, promenading figures.
- **Figure scale:** Figures are small and sketched (10–40% of the height), or they are cropped by the edge.
- **Treatment:** Faces are suggested by 2–3 touches; modern life as it is, with no moral or myth.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Matt to semi-matt, with no varnish on the first exhibitions.
- **Texture:** Strong impasto ridges and a visible weave; the surface looks like fast work.
