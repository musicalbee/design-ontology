# New Topographics

> The ordinary, man-made landscape deserves the formal care that the sublime wilderness once received, and the photographer's voice should stay out of it.

Flat, neutral, frontal views of suburbs, car parks and industrial sites, with no drama and no opinion. It shows the built landscape as a catalogue of facts, in a cool, exact, almost clerical style.

**Period:** 1975–1985 · **Era:** Colour and the topographic turn · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** Deadpan landscape, Man-altered landscape

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "new topographics", "deadpan", "typology", "suburban landscape", "flat light", "bernd and hilla becher", "lewis baltz", "robert adams".

Good for: architecture and real estate documentation, urban research, catalogue and archive imagery, brutalist and modernist building studies.

Do not use it when:
- The brief wants drama, glamour or emotional warmth.
- The subject is a person and not a place.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in New Topographics, you MUST:

1. Shoot frontally from a fixed height of 1.5m with the camera level, so that verticals stay parallel.
2. Wait for flat overcast or hazy light at a ratio of 2:1 or less, so that no shadow leads the eye.
3. Stand 10 to 50m from the subject, and fill 60% of the frame with ordinary built structure.
4. Use a view camera at f/22 to f/45 so that everything from the kerb to the horizon is sharp.
5. Keep the tonal scale narrow and neutral: black point at 5%, white point at 95%, with no toning.
6. Treat the subject as a type: photograph the same kind of object in a series, from the same distance and angle.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Dramatic skies, golden hour and sunset colour.
- **NEVER:** Wide-angle distortion and tilted horizons.
- **NEVER:** Picturesque framing that prettifies a car park or a tract house.
- **NEVER:** Human emotion or an ironic gesture inside the frame.
- **NEVER:** Heavy burning and dodging that adds mood.
- **NEVER:** Saturated colour and decorative texture.

## Composition

- **Framing:** A calm, horizontal 4×5 or 8×10 frame; the subject sits in the middle band and the sky takes under 30%.
- **Viewpoint:** Frontal, at 1.5m height, 10 to 50m from the subject, with level verticals.
- **Geometry:** Orthogonal grids of houses, signs, lots and wires; the horizon runs at the same height in each picture of a series.
- **Subject placement:** Centred or evenly balanced; no subject is the hero, so the eye scans the whole frame.

## Light

- **Quality:** Flat and shadowless: overcast, haze or open shade.
- **Direction:** From everywhere; if the sun is out, it comes from behind the camera and does not draw a shadow edge.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 2:1 or lower (1 stop or less)
- **Colour temperature:** 6500K (overcast)
- **Time of day:** Overcast day, or late morning haze

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** 4×5 in or 8×10 in view camera; 6×7 medium format as a modern equivalent
- **Focal length:** 150–210mm on 4×5 (about 40–55mm in 35mm format)
- **Aperture:** f/22 to f/45
- **Shutter:** 1/15s to 1s on a tripod
- **Film speed:** ISO 100–125
- **Focus:** Set the plane of sharp focus with a little front tilt to cover the kerb to the horizon.
- **Support:** Tripod with a spirit level
- **Equipment:** view camera with rising front · dark cloth · spirit level

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Neutral black and white with a narrow, even scale, or restrained, desaturated colour; no tone.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Convert to monochrome with a neutral mix (R 33, G 33, B 33).
  - Set the black point at 5% and the white point at 95%.
  - Keep midtone contrast flat: a straight curve, with −10% contrast.
  - Match the exposure of each picture in a series to within 0.3 stop.
  - Apply no dodging, burning, vignette or tone.
  - Sharpen at radius 0.5px, amount 60%.
- **Grain:** ISO 100 fine grain; not visible at 20 × 25 cm

## Subject & staging

- **Subjects:** tract houses · car parks · mobile homes · industrial parks · water towers · road edges
- **Staging:** None; the photographer chooses a spot and a time, and records the site as it is.
- **Direction:** No people, or people small and incidental; no pose.

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:** Develop normally and print on a neutral paper, grade 2. · Print each series to the same density and the same size. · Hang as a set in even rows of 20 to 40 pictures. · Add a plain caption of place and date.
- **Output:** A small, quiet, grey print, about 20 × 25 cm, in a series with a plain caption.
