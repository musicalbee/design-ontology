# Photorealism

> A painting can match the camera: the artist works from a photograph, keeps its sharp focus and its flat modern light, and shows the city as the lens sees it.

Paintings that copy a photograph in every detail: glass, chrome, signs and street reflections, in sharp, even focus. The hand is hidden and the picture is as cool as a camera.

**Period:** 1966–1980 · **Era:** Avant-garde · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Hyperrealism, Super-realism

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "photorealism", "photorealist", "hyperrealism", "richard estes", "bechtle", "painted like a photograph", "street reflections", "chrome and glass", "super-realism".

Good for: automotive, retail and city campaign art, editorial art on urban life, product scenes with exact glass and metal, book covers with a flat, modern look.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs expression, a hand-made touch or a loose mood.
- The picture must be abstract, symbolic or romantic.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Photorealism, you MUST:

1. Work from a photograph, and transfer it by a grid or a projector, with an error of under 2%.
2. Keep the whole picture in sharp focus, with detail down to 1–2mm in the background.
3. Choose urban, banal subjects: a shop front, a car, a diner, a street, a sign, a window.
4. Paint reflections in glass and chrome, with 2–4 reflected scenes layered over the main view.
5. Hold an even, flat light with no drama, in a clear, high-key daylight.
6. Hide the brush: smooth, blended paint, with an airbrush or a fine brush for the gradients.
7. Show no comment on the subject, and keep the mood neutral and cool.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Visible gestural brush strokes.
- **NEVER:** A soft, blurred or selective focus.
- **NEVER:** A dramatic, moody or romantic light.
- **NEVER:** A heroic, historical or allegorical subject.
- **NEVER:** A flattened, abstracted or simplified form.
- **NEVER:** A painted comment on the subject, such as satire.

## Composition

- **Layout:** A frontal or three-quarter street view with a flat, even light, strong perspective lines to a vanishing point, and layers of reflection in glass.
- **Focal point:** A shop window, a car or a sign, at one third from an edge; there is no single dramatic centre, and the detail is equal across the picture.
- **Format:** A large canvas at 3:2 to 2:1, 1.5m or more wide, so that the detail reads as a photograph.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| leading-lines | Run the kerb, the roof line and the shop front to one vanishing point, with all lines within 2° of the true perspective. |
| layering-depth | Build 3–4 layers: the reflected street, the glass, the shop interior and the far background, each in sharp focus. |
| rhythm | Repeat a window, a sign or a car 4–12 times in perspective, with the step shrinking at the correct rate. |
| unity-variety | Hold one flat daylight and one level of focus across the picture, with detail at 1–2mm everywhere. |
| framing | Frame the view by the edge of a building or a window, and cut the scene as a camera crop does. |
| grouping | Group the signs, the cars and the lights in clusters of 3–5 items, as they sit in the photograph. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** The colour of a colour photograph of the period: bright signs and chrome set in a mid-tone street, with blue-grey reflections and a pale sky.
- **Distribution:** Street and building mid-tones 40–50%, sky and glass reflections 25–30%, bright signs and cars 15–20%, dark shadows 5–10%.
- **Ground:** A white, smooth acrylic ground, sanded between coats.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Acrylic or thin oil, applied with an airbrush, a fine brush and a razor for highlights.
- **Support:** Primed canvas, smooth, 1.5m or more wide.
- **Brushwork:** None in view: smooth, thin, blended layers; fine sable strokes of 1–2mm for sign and chrome detail.
- **Paint handling:** 8–20 thin layers; the surface is smooth and the paint shows no ridge.
- **Edges:** Sharp where the photograph is sharp, with exact highlights of 1–3mm on glass and chrome.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** Flat, bright, even daylight, as the camera caught it; no drama and no clear time of day.
- **Modelling:** Smooth and exact, in 8–10 steps; the gradients on chrome and glass are airbrushed.
- **Shadow:** Short, mid-value and neutral; 15–25% of the area, with a sharp edge.
- **Temperature:** Neutral to slightly cool; warm signs and cars against cool glass and sky.

## Subject

- **Typical:** A shop front, a car park, a diner, a street with signs, a window with reflections, a suburban house with a car.
- **Figure scale:** People are few and small, at 5–10% of the picture height, or absent.
- **Treatment:** Cool and deadpan; the view is plain, urban and exact, and offers no comment.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Satin to gloss and smooth, like a print.
- **Texture:** Flat; no ridge and no stroke in view.
