# Ivy style

> A man's dress is plain, a little worn and made to last; it shows that he belongs and does not show that he tries.

The dress of the American college man: an unpadded, natural-shouldered sack jacket, an oxford button-down shirt, chinos and flannels, worn with ease.

**Period:** 1950–1969 · **Era:** War and the New Look · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Ivy League look, American trad, Natural-shoulder style, Eastern establishment dress

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Ivy style, you MUST:

1. Cut the jacket on a straight sack line with a natural shoulder and no pad.
2. Use a 3-roll-2 front with a soft lapel, and a single vent at the back.
3. Wear an oxford cloth shirt with a button-down collar that rolls in a soft curve.
4. Pair the jacket with flannel grey trousers or cotton chinos with a plain front and a cuff of 4 cm.
5. Choose tweed, flannel or seersucker by the season, and cotton oxford in pale stripes.
6. Add a knit tie, a Shetland jumper or a repp-striped tie in two or three colours.
7. Keep shoes in brown leather with a thick sole, and let them age.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A straight, easy line from the shoulder to the hem, with no waist suppression.
- **Line:** A soft, natural shoulder, a 3-roll-2 lapel, a straight trouser and a button-down collar.
- **Fit:** Easy on the chest and the waist; the jacket hangs straight from the shoulder.
- **Proportion:** Jacket length to the base of the thumb; lapel about 7 cm; trouser with a straight leg, a plain front and a 4 cm cuff.
- **Rules:**
  - Leave the shoulder unpadded so that it follows the line of the body.
  - Roll the top button of the front so that the lapel curves in one line.
  - Mix a tweed jacket with grey flannel trousers, and a blue shirt with a plain tie.
  - Wear brown leather shoes, and let them show wear.
  - Keep colour in the tie, the sock and the shirt stripe.

## Construction

- **Methods:** unlined or half-lined sack jacket · natural shoulder with no pad · button-down collar on the shirt · back loop and a box pleat on the shirt · plain-front trousers with a cuff · cinch strap at the back of the trousers
- **Closures:** Three buttons with the top one rolled; a button-down collar; a plain fly with a button.
- **Details:** A hook vent, a flap pocket, a patch pocket, a keeper loop on the shirt, and leather elbow patches on the jumper.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** matte · napped · fulled · sanforised · mercerised
- **Pattern:** Plain, or a stripe, check, madras plaid or a repp tie; tweed in muted herringbones.
- **Gloss:** All cloth at 1–5 GU; the surface is dry and a little soft.
- **Wear:** The style wants a worn look: a collar that frays, a jumper that is darned, a shoe that is scuffed.

### Button-down collar (tool)

A shirt collar whose points button to the body of the shirt, so that the collar stays down and rolls in a soft curve. Origin: fashion, New York, United States, 1896.

- Sew a small buttonhole in each collar point.
- Sew a button on the body of the shirt at the matching place.
- Cut the collar from soft oxford cloth so that it rolls when it is buttoned.
