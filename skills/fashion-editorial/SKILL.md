# Fashion editorial photography

> Fashion sells a feeling before it sells a thing, so the photograph builds the whole world around the clothes.

Planned pictures of clothes, bodies and attitude for a magazine page. Light, set, pose and edit are all designed, so that the image sells a world and not only a garment.

**Period:** 1930s–today · **Era:** Constructed images · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** Magazine fashion, High-fashion editorial

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "fashion editorial", "vogue", "avedon", "penn", "helmut newton", "campaign photograph", "magazine fashion", "beauty shot".

Good for: fashion campaigns and lookbooks, magazine spreads, beauty and accessories, image prompts for styled studio scenes.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a candid or documentary feel.
- The garment must be shown for fit and cut at true proportion only.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in Fashion editorial photography, you MUST:

1. Use an 85mm to 135mm lens at f/5.6 to f/11, so that the body keeps its proportion and the clothes stay sharp.
2. Light with one large source: a 1.2m to 2m soft box or a beauty dish, with a key-to-fill ratio of 3:1 to 8:1.
3. Pose from the hip and the neck: make long lines, and give the hands a task.
4. Choose a set, a location or a colour sweep that carries the story, and place the model in it with 40–60% negative space.
5. Retouch skin to a natural texture, and grade for a clear mood: pastel, hard flash or film noir.
6. Shoot 300 to 1000 frames for 6 to 12 pages.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Flat catalogue light with a stiff front pose.
- **NEVER:** Heavy beauty-filter skin and plastic textures.
- **NEVER:** Shots with no set, no story and no direction.
- **NEVER:** Stock poses that ignore the garment.
- **NEVER:** Wide-angle distortion of the body.
- **NEVER:** Competing props that hide the clothes.

## Composition

- **Framing:** Vertical and clean; the figure is a strong line, often cropped at the knee or the hand.
- **Viewpoint:** Waist to eye height, sometimes low for power; the camera is square to the body.
- **Geometry:** Long diagonals from a leg, an arm or a train; a set line cuts the figure.
- **Subject placement:** Off centre, with 40–60% negative space or a colour field for type.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| focal-point | Let 1 figure take 40–60% of the height; the eye goes to the face and then to the garment. |
| negative-space | Leave 40–60% of the page empty for a title or a pull quote. |
| balance | Use asymmetric balance: the figure on one third of the width, an object or a shadow on the opposite third, at a weight ratio of 0.6–0.8. |
| leading-lines | Use 1 to 3 diagonals, a leg, an arm or a train, that run to the face. |
| colour-distribution | Set 60% in one field, 30% in the garment hue and 10% in an accent such as lip or shoe. |

## Light

- **Quality:** A large soft source for beauty, or a hard flash for edge; the light is shaped on purpose.
- **Direction:** 45° front-side and 30° above, with a fill card at the opposite side.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 3:1 to 8:1 (1.5–3 stops)
- **Colour temperature:** 5600K strobe; 3200K for tungsten mood
- **Time of day:** Studio, any hour; on location, the hour before sunset

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** Medium format or digital full frame; tethered to a screen
- **Focal length:** 85mm to 135mm
- **Aperture:** f/5.6 to f/11
- **Shutter:** 1/125s to 1/200s (flash sync)
- **Film speed:** ISO 100
- **Focus:** Focus on the near eye; keep the garment inside the depth of field at f/8.
- **Support:** Tripod or handheld with a monolight
- **Equipment:** medium format or full frame body · 85mm and 135mm lenses · soft box 1.2m to 2m · reflector or fill card · wind machine

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Clean colour with a chosen cast: pastel and bright, or deep and moody, with natural skin.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Set the white balance at 5600K and tint +3.
  - Set the black point at 2% and the white point at 98%.
  - Apply +20% midtone contrast with a soft S-curve.
  - Lift saturation by +15% but protect skin with a skin-tone mask at −10%.
  - Retouch blemishes only; keep pores at 70% of the original texture.
  - Add a clean grain, amount 8%, to unify the retouch.
- **Grain:** Light and even: a film-like layer that hides retouch seams

## Subject & staging

- **Subjects:** models in couture or ready-to-wear · accessories and shoes · beauty close-ups · set pieces and architecture as a stage · motion: a coat, a hair, a train
- **Staging:** Full. A team of 6 to 20 people: stylist, hair, make-up, set designer, art director and the maker.
- **Direction:** Give the model a character, a motion and a gaze; reshoot the pose in steps of 3 to 5 frames.

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:**
  - Tether to a screen and review each frame with the art director.
  - Select 3 to 5 frames per look from 300 to 1000 shots.
  - Retouch skin and fabric, and match colour across the spread.
  - Lay out with type, 40–60% negative space and a clean margin.
- **Output:** A magazine spread or campaign image of 8×10 in at 300 dpi, with room for type.

## Techniques

### Three-point lighting (process)

A key light, a fill light and a back light on one subject, so that the face is modelled and stands out from the ground. Origin: film & video, Hollywood, United States, c. 1915.

- Set the key light 30 to 45 degrees off the camera axis and above eye level.
- Set the fill on the other side at 1/4 to 1/2 of the key.
- Put the back light behind the subject, so that a thin rim of light separates hair and shoulders from the ground.
