# Creamware, neoclassical

> A light cream body and a clear glaze, in a plain classical form, outsell both porcelain and delftware, because the whole service is cheap, even and easy to match.

Cream-coloured lead-glazed earthenware that is light, cheap and strong. Potters in Staffordshire and Leeds make plain, classical forms with a feather edge or pierced work, and sell it to every table in Europe.

**Period:** 1762–1830 · **Era:** Industrial pottery · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Queen's ware, Wedgwood creamware, Leeds creamware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Creamware, neoclassical, you MUST:

1. Fire a body of flint and white clay to a cream earthenware at 1100 °C.
2. Dip the piece in a clear lead glaze that fires to a pale yellow-cream gloss.
3. Keep the form plain and balanced: a circle, a straight rim, a loop handle.
4. Press or mould the edge with a feather or a shell, in a regular pattern of fine lines.
5. Pierce the wall with a punch for openwork baskets, in rows of small holes 3 to 4 mm wide.
6. Print or paint a pattern in a narrow band, and leave most of the surface plain.
7. Make the service in 50 or more shapes, so that a buyer can order any piece again.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Plain plates with a feather edge, covered tureens, oval platters, pierced baskets and sauceboats, in a strict classical outline.
- **Profile:** A clean circle with a flat rim and a shallow well; the rim carries a moulded shell edge, a bead or a plain band.
- **Foot and rim:** A low, plain foot with a glazed base; the rim is straight, with a moulded feather edge.
- **Proportion:** Plate diameter 22 to 25 cm and wall 3 to 4 mm thick; a service of 50 or more pieces.
- **Rules:** Keep the outline a plain circle or a strict oval. · Use one moulded edge on every plate in the service. · Keep the handle a simple loop or a plain knop.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** creamware
- **Secondary:** pearlware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · terracotta
- **Rule:** Use a light cream earthenware with a clear lead glaze, which is cheap and hard. Do not use porcelain: the style is the cheap, fine alternative.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing and turning on the lathe · press moulding in plaster moulds · jiggering of plates · piercing with a punch · engine turning on a lathe · dipping in glaze
- **Joins:** Handles are pressed in a mould and luted with slip; the knop is a plain ball or a small shell.
- **Details:** A small tripod mark of the kiln stilts, a pale green pool of glaze in the foot, and the maker name stamped in the base.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** lead-glaze
- **Decorations:** pierced · transfer-print · overglaze-enamel · relief-moulding · hand-banding · undecorated
- **Pattern:** Mostly plain; a feather-edge rim, a narrow band of enamel or a small printed scene or garland.
- **Gloss:** Soft gloss, about 60 to 80 GU, on a cream ground.
- **Wear:** The glaze crazes in a fine net over time and takes a brown tone in the cracks.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A single cream tone, a pale yellow-green where the glaze pools, and a black or brown print for the narrow band.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A coal-fired bottle kiln, with a biscuit firing at a high heat and a glaze firing at a lower heat.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 1100 °C · dipping in lead glaze · glaze firing at about 1050 °C · enamel or print firing at a low heat
- **Scale:** Large factories at Burslem, Etruria and Leeds made it by the thousand, and sold it all over Europe and America.
- **Signs:** Stilt marks, a faint cream tone, pale green pools of glaze, and the maker name stamped in the base.

### Engine turning (skill)

A lathe with a cutting tool cuts a regular pattern of fine lines in a flat metal face. The pattern catches the light and shows no tool marks. Origin: fashion, Europe, c. 1600.

- Fix the metal blank on the rose engine, and set the cam that gives the pattern.
- Cut each line at a depth of 0.05 to 0.1 mm, and keep the pitch the same.
- Polish the cut lines, or fill them with clear enamel.
