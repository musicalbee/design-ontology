# Scandinavian interior photography

> A room looks livable when the light is honest and the objects are few, so the picture shows the light first.

Calm, bright pictures of rooms: pale wood, white walls, a window and a few objects. The light is soft, the lines are straight and the space is allowed to breathe.

**Period:** 2005–today · **Era:** Constructed images · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** Nordic home editorial, Soft daylight interior

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "scandinavian interior photo", "nordic home photograph", "soft daylight interior", "airy room", "kinfolk look", "tilt-shift interior", "real estate editorial".

Good for: furniture and homeware catalogues, interior magazines, property marketing with a calm look, image prompts for bright rooms.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs drama, deep colour or night mood.
- The room is cluttered and must stay as it is.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in Scandinavian interior photography, you MUST:

1. Shoot with a tilt-shift or a 24mm to 35mm lens at f/8 to f/11, with the camera level at 1.0m to 1.2m high.
2. Use window light only: turn off lamps, and add a white 1.2m reflector on the shadow side.
3. Keep a key-to-fill ratio of 2:1 to 3:1, so that shadows stay soft and open.
4. Correct all verticals to 0° and hold the horizon at 0°.
5. Set 3 to 5 objects in the room; leave 40–60% of the frame as wall, floor or air.
6. Grade to cool daylight, 5500–6000K, and a low saturation of −10 to −20% in a palette of white, pale wood and one green.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Overhead lamps, flash or mixed colour casts.
- **NEVER:** Converging verticals from a tilted camera.
- **NEVER:** Cluttered shelves and busy patterns.
- **NEVER:** Heavy HDR with glowing windows.
- **NEVER:** Saturated, warm yellow grades.
- **NEVER:** Wide-angle stretch below 20mm.

## Composition

- **Framing:** Square-on or a soft corner view; the room reads as a calm box.
- **Viewpoint:** Level at 1.0m to 1.2m high, 3 to 6m from the far wall.
- **Geometry:** Horizontal planes and vertical edges; a window and a table form a quiet grid.
- **Subject placement:** Centred on a wall or off-centre on a third, with 40–60% as open space.

**Principles**

## Light

- **Quality:** Soft and even window daylight with a white bounce.
- **Direction:** From the side window at 90° to the lens; a reflector fills the opposite side.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 2:1 to 3:1 (1–1.5 stops)
- **Colour temperature:** 5500–6000K (cool daylight)
- **Time of day:** Overcast morning, or midday with a diffuser at the window

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** Digital full frame or medium format; tilt-shift lens for vertical control
- **Focal length:** 24mm to 35mm (tilt-shift 24mm preferred)
- **Aperture:** f/8 to f/11
- **Shutter:** 1/15s to 1s
- **Film speed:** ISO 100
- **Focus:** Focus one third into the room; the depth of field covers 1m to infinity.
- **Support:** Tripod, with a level on the hot shoe
- **Equipment:** tilt-shift 24mm lens · sturdy tripod · 1.2m white reflector · diffuser for the window · spirit level

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Soft, cool daylight colour with lowered saturation and open shadows.
- **Grade recipe:** Set the white balance at 5800K, tint 0. · Raise shadows by +25 and lower highlights by −20. · Reduce saturation by −12% and warm tones by −5%. · Add +10% clarity on wood grain only. · Correct verticals to 0° and horizon to 0°. · Add no grain and no vignette.
- **Grain:** None: clean, with a low noise floor at ISO 100

## Subject & staging

- **Subjects:** living rooms · dining nooks · kitchens with wood and white · bedrooms with linen · a window with a plant · chairs and a table
- **Staging:** Styled: remove clutter, set 3 to 5 objects, add a plant, a throw and a ceramic; steam the linen.
- **Direction:** Check every line in live view; adjust objects in steps of 2cm.

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:** Shoot a 3-frame bracket at ±1 stop for window detail. · Blend by hand for windows; no automatic HDR. · Correct verticals and colour casts. · Export at 4:5 or 3:2 sRGB, 2400px on the long edge.
- **Output:** A bright, quiet room image for a magazine page, a brand site or a catalogue.
