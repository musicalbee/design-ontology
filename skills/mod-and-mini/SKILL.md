# Mod and mini

> Fashion belongs to the young and the street: a short hem, a clean cut and a bold print show speed and fun.

The young London look of the 1960s: the miniskirt, the short shift, a slim jacket and a bold print, sold by small boutiques.

**Period:** 1960s · **Era:** Youthquake · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Swinging London dress, Miniskirt and shift, Mod fashion, Carnaby Street look

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Mod and mini, you MUST:

1. Raise the hem to 10 to 20 cm above the knee for a miniskirt.
2. Cut the dress as a short, straight shift or an A-line, with the waist seam low or gone.
3. Keep the line clean: no darts that show, no ruffle, no trim.
4. Use bold shapes and op-art prints in black and white, or flat bright colours.
5. Use tights in opaque wool or nylon, and a flat or low-heeled shoe.
6. Cut the mod suit slim, with a short jacket, narrow lapels and a narrow trouser.
7. Make the fashion in small runs, and sell it in a boutique that opens late and plays music.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A short, straight or A-line shift, a miniskirt above the knee and a slim jacket.
- **Line:** A high hem, a low or absent waist, a round neckline, a small collar and a bare arm.
- **Fit:** Close and light on the body; the dress hangs from the shoulder and does not hold the waist.
- **Proportion:** Hem 10 to 20 cm above the knee on the miniskirt; jacket ends at the hip bone; trouser leg about 40 cm at the hem.
- **Rules:**
  - Raise the hem above the knee and keep the cut straight.
  - Use a clean seam, a round collar and a zip in place of buttons or bows.
  - Print the cloth in a large, flat, high-contrast pattern.
  - Add white tights or opaque tights, and a flat or low-heeled shoe.
  - Cut a man's jacket short, with two buttons and a narrow lapel.

### PVC-coated cloth (material)

A woven or knit cloth that is coated with plasticised PVC, so that it is shiny, waterproof and easy to wipe clean. Origin: fashion, Akron, Ohio, United States, 1926.

- Spread a paste of PVC and plasticiser on a knit or woven back.
- Heat the coated cloth so that the layer fuses and sets with a smooth, shiny face.
- Cut and weld or tape the seams so that they keep out the water.
