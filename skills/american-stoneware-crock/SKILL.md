# American stoneware crock

> A crock must hold butter, pickles or pork for a winter, so the body is dense stoneware and the only ornament is one quick blue brush stroke.

Straight-sided stoneware for the farm and the dairy: crocks, churns, jugs and jars. A salt or slip glaze makes the wall tight, and a swift cobalt motif marks the size.

**Period:** 1800–1910 · **Era:** Industrial pottery · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Salt-glazed crock, Cobalt-decorated stoneware, Butter churn and jug ware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in American stoneware crock, you MUST:

1. Throw a straight-sided crock with a wall 10 to 14 mm thick; the weight resists frost and knocks.
2. Add two ear handles or a ring of lugs, 4 cm below the rim, to lift a 10-litre pot.
3. Mark the volume in gallons on the front: stamp or brush the number, 1 to 6.
4. Brush one cobalt motif, a bird, a flower or a leaf, in a few strokes of slip; the stroke blurs a little in the fire.
5. Glaze the pot with salt, or dip it in a brown Albany slip.
6. Fire the pots at 1200 to 1250 °C in a groundhog or beehive kiln, and throw salt at the peak.
7. Keep the rim plain and flat, so that a cloth and a stone fit on top.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Straight-sided crocks, ovoid jugs, churns, preserving jars and bean pots in sizes of 1 to 6 gallons.
- **Profile:** A tall cylinder that swells a little at the shoulder; a jug has a round shoulder and a narrow neck with a loop handle.
- **Foot and rim:** A flat base with a sharp edge; a plain flat rim, thick enough for a lid or a cloth cover.
- **Proportion:** Height 20 to 45 cm; width about two thirds of the height; wall 10 to 14 mm.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the wall straight and the rim flat, so that a plate sits on top.
  - Fix ear handles near the rim.
  - Put the volume mark and one motif on the front, in the upper half.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stoneware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · creamware
- **Rule:** Use a grey or buff stoneware of local clay that vitrifies at 1200 °C and does not leak. Keep fine white bodies out: the pot is heavy and plain by design.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the wheel in large lumps of 5 to 20 kg · pulling and fixing ear handles · brushing cobalt slip · stamping the maker and size · wire-cutting the base from the wheel
- **Joins:** Ear handles and lugs are made as a coil and luted with slip; a jug handle is pulled in one stroke and bent in a loop.
- **Details:** A visible throwing spiral on the wall, a thin blue smear at the edge of the brush, and a flat spot where the pot rested in the kiln.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** salt-glaze · albany-slip · bare-clay
- **Decorations:** underglaze-cobalt · incised · slip-trailing · undecorated
- **Pattern:** One cobalt bird, flower, leaf or swag on the front, a large number for the size, and sometimes a line of incised waves round the shoulder.
- **Gloss:** Salt glaze is a satin skin of 20 to 40 GU; brown Albany slip is a glossy dark coat of 50 to 70 GU.
- **Wear:** The glaze is hard and does not wear; the body shows a grey or tan chip at the rim.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A plain ground of grey, tan or brown with one strong blue; the cobalt is the only bright colour.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A beehive or groundhog kiln fired with wood or coal; salt is thrown at the top of the firing.
- **Atmosphere:** salt-or-soda
- **Processes:** air-dry the pots for days · stack them in the kiln · raise the temperature to about 1250 °C · throw salt through the ports · cool for two days
- **Scale:** Hundreds of potteries along the Hudson, in New Jersey and in Ohio; the ware was cheap and sold by the gallon.
- **Signs:** A stamped gallon number, a blue brush stroke with a soft blur, and an orange-peel skin on the salt glaze.

### Salt glazing (process)

Rock salt is thrown into a kiln at stoneware heat. The salt vapour reacts with the clay and forms a thin hard glaze with an orange-peel texture. Origin: ceramics, Rhineland, Germany, c. 1400.

- Fire the stoneware to 1200 to 1300 °C.
- Throw 1 to 2 kg of rock salt for each cubic metre through ports in the kiln wall.
- Seal the ports and let the vapour settle for 20 to 30 minutes.
- Keep the wall 4 mm or more thick, so that the pot does not crack in the heat shock.
