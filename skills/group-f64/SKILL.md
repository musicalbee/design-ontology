# Group f/64

> Pure photography needs no borrowed look from painting; the large camera, the small stop and the full tonal scale are enough.

Sharp, rich black-and-white contact prints of rocks, plants, shells and faces, made on large cameras at the smallest stop. The group named itself after that stop.

**Period:** 1932–1935 · **Era:** Photography as art · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** f/64 school, West Coast straight photography

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "group f/64", "f/64", "zone system", "ansel adams", "edward weston", "large format landscape", "contact print".

Good for: landscape and nature photography briefs, black-and-white print grading, botanical and rock studies, image prompts for monumental sharp scenes.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs soft mood, motion or a candid moment.
- The camera is small and handheld; the f/64 numbers do not apply.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in Group f/64, you MUST:

1. Use an 8×10 in view camera and a lens of 210mm to 300mm, stopped down to f/45 to f/64.
2. Expose for the shadows and develop for the highlights: place the darkest detail on Zone III and the brightest on Zone VII or VIII.
3. Make a contact print on glossy paper (grade 2) with no enlargement, so that the print is as sharp as the negative.
4. Show the whole scale: a pure black in the deepest shadow and a clean white in the highest light, with 9 or 10 zones between.
5. Take the subject as it is: a rock, a pepper, a dune or a face, framed by the shape of the plate.
6. Mount the print on white board with no frame decoration.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Cropping a print to repair the framing.

- **NEVER:** Enlarging a small negative.

## Composition

- **Framing:** Full plate, exact; the edge of the 8×10 negative is the edge of the print.
- **Viewpoint:** Low tripod, 0.5m to 5m from the subject; the camera is level with the form.
- **Geometry:** Curves, ripples and ridges; one strong form against a quiet ground.
- **Subject placement:** Centred or on a third; the main form fills 60–90% of the plate.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| focal-point | Let 1 primary form fill 60–90% of the plate; a second form takes under 15%. |
| layering-depth | Hold 3 planes sharp at f/64: near 0.5m, middle 5m and far to infinity. |
| contrast | Print 9 to 10 zones, from pure black at Zone 0 to paper white at Zone X. |
| proportion | Keep the 4:5 ratio of the 8×10 plate, with no crop. |
| rhythm | Repeat a ripple, a scale or a leaf at an interval of 1/8 to 1/12 of the plate width. |

## Light

- **Quality:** Clear, hard-edged light with deep, detailed shadows.
- **Direction:** Side light at 45° to 90° to bring out texture; backlight for leaves.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 8:1 to 16:1 (3–4 stops) before development control
- **Colour temperature:** 5500K (daylight)
- **Time of day:** Early morning or late afternoon; midday for shells and studio work

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** 8×10 in view camera; contact print
- **Focal length:** 210mm to 300mm
- **Aperture:** f/45 to f/64
- **Shutter:** 1s to 8s
- **Film speed:** ISO 25 to 50
- **Focus:** Tilt the back and focus on a third of the way into the scene; the depth of field covers 0.5m to infinity at f/64.
- **Support:** Heavy tripod
- **Equipment:** 8×10 view camera · dark cloth · light meter with a 1° spot · red or yellow filter · contact printing frame

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Neutral black and white with a full scale and no toning.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Convert to monochrome with a neutral mix (R 30, G 59, B 11).
  - Set the black point at 0% and the white point at 99%.
  - Apply a −1 contraction on a flat scene or an expansion of +1 on a flat day, as for N−1 and N+1 development.
  - Dodge Zone III shadows by +0.3 stop and burn skies by −0.5 stop.
  - Sharpen at radius 0.8px, amount 80%.
  - Add no grain and no vignette.
- **Grain:** None: fine detail at 8×10 contact scale

## Subject & staging

- **Subjects:** rocks and tide pools · pepper and cabbage forms · dunes and trees · shells and driftwood · portraits and hands · industrial forms
- **Staging:** Minimal. The maker picks the form, the hour and the stop, and removes nothing from the scene.
- **Direction:** Sit a portrait in open shade for 2s to 4s; the sitter holds still.

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:**
  - Place shadows on Zone III by spot meter, and set the highlight zone by the time of development.
  - Develop each sheet by its own time (N−1, N, N+1).
  - Contact print on glossy paper at grade 2 with a printing frame.
  - Mount on white board; sign and date below the print.
- **Output:** A glossy contact print 8×10 in (20×25cm), mounted on white board.

## Techniques

### Tilt-shift (tool)

Lens or camera movements that keep vertical lines parallel and move the plane of focus; a building stands upright and sharp. Origin: photography, Vienna, 1904.

- Keep the back of the camera vertical and shift the lens up by 10 to 20 mm, so that vertical lines stay parallel.
- Tilt the lens by 2° to 8° to turn the plane of focus along the ground for depth at a small stop.
- Close the lens to f/11 to f/22 for full depth of field.
- In an editor, use a narrow band of focus and a strong blur outside it to fake a miniature.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Group f/64** (collective, San Francisco Bay Area, 1932–1935), launched the style. A group of seven photographers of the San Francisco Bay Area who shared a sharp, straight style. Ansel Adams and Willard Van Dyke organised it. It showed first at the de Young Museum in 1932. It dissolved by the end of 1935. It stood in part against pictorialism. The id keeps the old word exhibition.
