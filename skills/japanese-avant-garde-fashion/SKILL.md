# Japanese avant-garde fashion

> A garment is a space around the body, not a copy of it: it can be loose, torn, uneven and black, and still be beautiful.

The black, loose and torn clothes that Rei Kawakubo, Yohji Yamamoto and Issey Miyake showed in Paris from 1981. The clothes are oversized, asymmetric and unfinished, with no wish to show the body.

**Period:** 1981–1999 · **Era:** Power and subculture · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Japanese deconstruction, Hiroshima chic, Paris Japanese wave, Anti-fashion

4. Set a sleeve, a collar or a hem off the centre, so that the cut is asymmetric.

6. Place a lump or a fold on the body, so that the outline changes.
7. Treat the cloth as the first idea and cut the garment from it.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A large, loose and often asymmetric shape that stands away from the body: a long coat, a wide trouser or a draped dress.
- **Line:** A dropped shoulder, no waist, and a hem that is uneven or off the level.
- **Fit:** Very loose; the body is hidden and the garment moves round it with room to spare.
- **Proportion:** Garment 20 to 40 cm wider than the body. Hem at the calf or the ankle. A lopsided hem is 10 to 25 cm out of line.
- **Rules:** Remove the waist and the bust shaping. · Cut a coat or a dress as one large wrapped piece. · Leave a frayed or turned-out edge at the hem. · Use a hole, a twist or a fold to break the plain surface. · Pair the garment with a flat shoe.

## Fabrics & materials

- **Primary:** boiled-wool · jersey · gabardine · felt
- **Secondary:** calico · linen-cloth · worsted-suiting
- **Avoid:** brocade · taffeta · satin · velvet · jacquard
- **Rule:** Choose a cloth with texture and weight that hangs in folds: boiled wool, felt, plain jersey and black gabardine, in a dull finish.

## Construction

- **Methods:** flat pattern from one large piece · raw and frayed edges · seams turned to the outside · holes knitted into the cloth · heat-set garment pleating · off-centre openings and sleeves
- **Closures:** Large hidden snaps, ties and wrap closures, or a single button; no waist closure.
- **Details:** A single large pocket, a dropped sleeve, a seam on the outside, a hanging flap and a hole in the knit.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** fulled · heat-set-pleated · distressed · indigo-dyed · matte
- **Pattern:** Plain black, white or grey; texture from the weave; sometimes holes and lumps that make a pattern.
- **Gloss:** Wool and felt at 1–4 GU; cotton at 2–6 GU; the style avoids a high gloss.
- **Wear:** The style likes a worn and unfinished surface: a frayed edge, a faded black and a wrinkled cloth.

### Garment pleating (process)

A way to pleat cloth after the garment is cut and sewn, with a heat press, so that the pleats stay for good. Origin: fashion, Tokyo, Japan, c. 1988.

- Cut and sew the garment in a size that is larger than the finished piece.
- Fold the garment into pleats between sheets of paper.
- Press the folds in a heat press, so that the polyester holds them.
