# Expressionism

> A picture shows what the painter feels, not what the eye sees, so that form and colour are distorted until they carry the emotion.

Pictures that bend the world to show an inner state: harsh colour, jagged line, tilted space and mask-like faces. The city is a threat and the body is a nerve.

**Period:** 1905–1925 · **Era:** Avant-garde · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Die Brücke, Der Blaue Reiter, German Expressionism

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "expressionism", "expressionist", "kirchner", "kandinsky", "schiele", "die brucke", "munch scream style", "distorted figure", "harsh colour".

Good for: music, club and theatre posters, editorial art on city life and anxiety, album and book covers with a raw mood, film titles with a tense look.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a calm, polite or luxury mood.
- The picture must show true colour or fine product detail.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Expressionism, you MUST:

1. Distort the figure and the space: a long neck, a sharp angle, a floor that tilts at 10–25°.
2. Choose colour for the mood: a green face, a red street, a yellow sky, at full or near full chroma.
3. Draw with a hard, dark, angular line of 4–12mm and let the contour break and cut.
4. Flatten the depth to 1–2 layers and push the figures to the front edge.
5. Paint fast and rough: broad strokes of 20–50mm, with the brush marks and the scrapes in view.
6. Show the city, the night, the crowd, the nude and the portrait with a tense or empty gaze.
7. Simplify the face to a mask with large eyes and a hard mouth.

## Composition

- **Layout:** Figures pressed to the front, in a tilted, shallow space cut by sharp diagonals, with a dark contour and a few harsh colour fields.
- **Focal point:** The face or the pair of eyes, at one third from an edge, in the strongest colour clash.
- **Format:** A canvas at 4:5 to 3:2; figures fill 60–90% of the height and are cut by the edge.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| contrast | Set one clash of complementary hues at full chroma in the main area, with a value gap of 50% or more. |
| leading-lines | Cut the picture with 3–5 sharp diagonals at 20–60° from the horizontal, and let them point at the face. |
| layering-depth | Reduce the depth to 1–2 layers, and tilt the ground up so that the figures stand at the front edge. |
| scale | Enlarge the head or the figure to 60–90% of the picture height, and shrink the street behind it. |
| focal-point | Place the mask-like face at one third from an edge, with the darkest eyes and the hardest edge in the picture. |
| rhythm | Repeat a jagged angle or a thin figure 4–8 times at a step of 8–15% of the width, so that the crowd presses. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Harsh, saturated, unnatural colour set in clashes: acid green against red, yellow against violet, with a black or deep blue contour.
- **Distribution:** 2 clashing hues 50–60%, a third hue 20–25%, dark contour 10–15%, bare ground under 5%.
- **Ground:** A bare or thin ground, often left in view between the strokes.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint, thick and rough, with little medium; woodcut for the hard, cut line.
- **Support:** Canvas or board, often unprimed or thinly primed.
- **Brushwork:** Broad, fast strokes of 20–50mm; angular dashes and scrapes; a dark contour of 4–12mm.
- **Paint handling:** 1–2 layers; the paint is opaque in the clash areas and thin in the ground, and the marks are not blended.
- **Edges:** Hard, angular and broken; the contour cuts across the colour.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** Harsh and artificial: a street lamp or a flat glare, with no clear daylight source.
- **Modelling:** Minimal; a form turns by a jump of hue in 1–2 steps, and the face is a flat mask.
- **Shadow:** A coloured patch of green, violet or blue; a hard edge and no gradient.
- **Temperature:** A hot red or yellow against a cold green or blue, chosen for the mood.

## Subject

- **Typical:** The city street, the cafe, the cabaret, the nude in a studio, the self-portrait, the portrait with a mask-like face, the crowd.
- **Figure scale:** Large, at 60–90% of the picture height, cut by the edge.
- **Treatment:** Tense, raw and personal; the subject is an inner state shown by harsh colour and a hard line.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Matt and raw.
- **Texture:** Rough, scraped and ridged, with the canvas in view.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Lithography (process)

A print from a flat stone or plate on which a greasy drawing takes ink and water repels it; the artist draws as on paper. Origin: graphic & ui design, Munich, c. 1796.

- Draw on the stone with a greasy crayon or a tusche wash; the drawing takes ink and the rest stays wet.
- Pull the print in a press at a run of 20 to 200 copies for an artist print, or more for a poster.
- Use one stone for each colour; 3 to 5 stones give a poster.
- Keep the soft crayon grain and the dark, velvet black.

### Woodcut (process)

A print from a block of wood with the white parts cut away; the result is flat colour, hard edges and visible grain. Origin: graphic & ui design, Dunhuang, 868.

- Cut the block along the grain with a knife and a gouge; leave a line of 1mm to 3mm for the black.
- Use one block for each colour and register them within 1 mm.
- Print with a flat, opaque ink and leave the grain and the knife marks visible.
- Keep the shapes flat, with no more than 3 to 6 tones in a print.

## Media
