# Streamline Moderne product

> A mass-made object should look as if it moves through air: a smooth shell, round corners and long lines show progress and sell.

Smooth, rounded objects that look fast: toasters, radios, lamps and chairs in chrome, aluminium, Bakelite and enamel, with long horizontal lines and speed stripes, made in mass series.

**Period:** 1930–1955 · **Era:** Machine modern · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Streamline design, Streamlined product design, Machine-age streamlining

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "streamline moderne", "streamlined toaster", "1930s radio", "teardrop form", "speed stripes", "machine age chrome", "world's fair modern", "bakelite dial".

Good for: kitchen objects and lighting for a retro-modern interior, a brief for a reissued appliance, a still life of chrome and enamel objects, hardware schedules for a diner or a station.

Do not use it when:
- The room needs hand-made, rough or antique objects.
- The brief asks for visible fasteners or raw metal.

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Streamline Moderne product, you MUST:

1. Round each outside corner with a radius of 20 to 60 mm; no sharp corner shows on the body.
2. Draw long horizontal lines: two or three speed stripes, grooves or bands that run the full length of the object.
3. Wrap the moving parts in one smooth shell of pressed steel, cast aluminium or moulded Bakelite.
4. Use chrome, polished aluminium and white or cream enamel; they reflect the light and look clean.
5. Taper the form at the rear in a teardrop, so that the outline cuts the air.
6. Hide the joints and the fasteners under the shell; a seam is a single line or a chrome band.
7. Design for mass production: one die or one mould makes thousands of the same shell.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Carved or applied ornament.
- **NEVER:** Visible bolts, rivets and raw welds.
- **NEVER:** Sharp corners and stepped tiers.
- **NEVER:** Dark, heavy timber and a hand-made look.
- **NEVER:** Rough, textured or distressed surfaces.
- **NEVER:** Vertical emphasis with a tall, narrow form.

## Form

- **Geometry:** The ellipse, the teardrop, the long cylinder and the rounded box, with horizontal grooves and bands.
- **Silhouette:** A low, long, rounded outline that is higher at the front and tapers to the rear, like a train, a ship or an aircraft.
- **Edges:** Large, continuous radii of 20 to 60 mm on all outside corners; a fillet of 5 to 10 mm along each seam.
- **Proportion:** The length is 1.5 to 3 times the height; a radio case is about 35 cm wide and 20 cm high; a stripe is 5 to 10 mm wide.
- **Ornament:** Two to five parallel speed stripes, a chrome band or a fin; they cover 5 to 15% of the surface and run along the length.
- **Rules:**
  - No outside corner has a radius under 15 mm.
  - Each object has 2 to 5 parallel horizontal lines or grooves.
  - The body is one smooth shell with no visible fastener.
  - The length of the object is at least 1.5 times its height.
  - Chrome or polished aluminium covers 20 to 40% of the surface; the rest is enamel, Bakelite or glass.

## Materials

- **Primary:** chrome-plated-steel · aluminium · bakelite · enamelled-steel · chrome-plated-brass
- **Secondary:** stainless-steel · plastic-laminate · birch-plywood · vitreous-china · porcelain · opal-glass · frosted-glass · mirror-glass · clear-glass · wool · leather · abs-plastic · cotton
- **Avoid:** cast-iron · wrought-iron · terracotta · rattan · wicker · rosewood · mahogany
- **Rule:** Bright metal and enamel make about 60% of the visible surface; Bakelite, laminate and glass are smooth, and textile is plain.

## Finish & texture

- **Finishes:** polished · brushed · gloss-enamel · lacquered · moulded-gloss · silvered · piece-dyed
- **Textures:** mirror-smooth · satin-smooth · brush-line · ribbed · plain-weave · twill
- **Gloss:** 80–100 GU on chrome and enamel; 40–70 GU on brushed aluminium
- **Patina:** The style wants a new, smooth shine; a pit, a scratch or a rust spot is out of character.

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** chrome
- **Section:** Tube 20–32 mm, handles 15–25 mm, trim bands 5–10 mm wide
- **Joints:** A single chrome band or a flush seam; fasteners are hidden under the shell or under a cap.
- **Handles:** A round knob or a bar in Bakelite or chrome, set flush with the shell, with a section of 15 to 25 mm.
- **Switches and taps:** A large round Bakelite dial or a lever set in the shell; a tap with a smooth, rounded body and a chrome lever.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Speed lines (motif)

Three or more parallel horizontal bands in chrome or a raised moulding along a rounded body, so that a static object seems to move. Origin: industrial design, United States, c. 1930.

- Run three to five bands parallel to the long axis of the body.
- Space the bands 3 to 8mm apart and keep them the same length.
- Wrap the bands round a curved end, so that they follow the radius of the body.
