# Romanticism

> A picture should move the viewer by emotion and by awe, and so the painter puts feeling, nature and imagination above the cool rules of the academy.

Pictures of storm, sea, ruin, passion and distant lands, in which feeling comes before rule. Nature is vast and the person is small; colour and brush carry the mood.

**Period:** 1790–1850 · **Era:** Nineteenth century · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Romantic painting, The Sublime

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "romanticism", "romantic painting", "turner", "delacroix", "friedrich", "sublime", "stormy sea", "wanderer", "gothic landscape".

Good for: book and game cover art, film poster for epic or sea stories, travel, outdoor and expedition identity, music and festival art.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a calm, technical or minimal mood.
- The picture must show a small product in clear, even light.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Romanticism, you MUST:

1. Make nature large and the figure small: a person at 5–15% of the picture height against a sky, a sea or a peak.
2. Open a deep space with a strong diagonal or a vortex, and lead the eye to a glowing horizon or a storm centre.
3. Use a dark, heavy foreground and a bright, burning distance: a value jump of 50% or more between them.
4. Choose a strong, warm and cold colour pair: a red or gold fire against a blue-green storm.
5. Show the hand: loose, wet strokes of 10–40mm in the sky and sea, and a hard edge only at the main figure.
6. Add a back-turned figure (Rückenfigur) or a wreck, so that the viewer stands inside the feeling.
7. Take the subject from history, literature, the exotic or the weather, and show the moment of peak action or loss.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Cool, rational order with a calm, balanced layout.
- **NEVER:** Smooth, hidden brushwork and a polished finish.
- **NEVER:** A bright, even light with no drama.
- **NEVER:** Everyday, small and comfortable subjects.
- **NEVER:** Figures that dominate and block the landscape.
- **NEVER:** A narrow, low-contrast colour range.

## Composition

- **Layout:** A deep space with a strong diagonal or a swirl, a dark mass in the foreground, and a burning or stormy light near the horizon at 35–45% of the height.
- **Focal point:** A small figure, a wreck or a flare of light, set at one third from an edge, where the diagonal and the brightest value meet.
- **Format:** A wide or square canvas at 4:3 to 3:2; large for a history scene, small for a mood piece; the sky takes 40–60% of the height.

**Principles**

| leading-lines | Lead the eye by one strong diagonal or a vortex at 30–50° from the horizontal to the point of light. |

| layering-depth | Build 3 to 4 layers of depth: a dark front, a middle ground, and a far, hazy light, with a strong loss of detail at each step. |
| colour-distribution | Spend 60–70% of the area on a dark or stormy key, 20–30% on one glowing warm area, and 5–10% on a small accent. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A dark, stormy key of brown, grey-blue and green, with one burning area of gold, orange or red; strong contrast of warm against cold.
- **Distribution:** Dark and mid-tone mass 60–70%, glowing light area 20–30%, accent colour under 10%.
- **Ground:** A warm brown or red-brown ground that shows through the dark areas.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Oil paint with glazes and scumbles, and watercolour for rapid sketches of weather.
- **Support:** Canvas, often large, on a warm brown ground.
- **Brushwork:** Broad, wet strokes of 10–40mm in the sky and the sea, thicker dabs for the light, and fine strokes of 1–3mm at the figure.
- **Paint handling:** Thin dark glazes in the shadow, thick impasto of 2–5mm in the highlights; wet-in-wet blending of the sky.
- **Edges:** Soft and lost edges in the sky, mist and sea; a hard edge at the main figure and the wreck.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** A dramatic, low or burning source: a sunset, a storm gap, a fire or moonlight, set deep in the picture.
- **Modelling:** Strong, with 5–7 steps from dark to light; the form is shaped by the glow and loses detail in the dark.
- **Shadow:** Deep and large, 40–60% of the picture, with warm browns; the dark area is a mass with little detail.
- **Temperature:** A warm glow set against a cold storm; the warm area is small and the cold area is large.

## Subject

- **Typical:** Storm and wreck at sea, mountain and ruin, a lone wanderer, a lion hunt, a battle, a poem or a myth, and the east as an exotic scene.
- **Figure scale:** Small, at 5–15% of the picture height; in a history scene, large figures at 50–80%.
- **Treatment:** Emotional, vast and personal; nature is a force and the person feels small and moved.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Semi-gloss with a rich varnish; the dark areas are deep.
- **Texture:** Visible strokes in sky and sea, with thick, ridged light and smooth, thin shadow.
