# Goryeo celadon

> A pot is a clear green lens over a drawing: the glaze is almost transparent and the inlaid slip line shows through it like ink in water.

Korean celadon of the Goryeo kingdom. A thin, clear grey-green glaze covers a fine body, and potters inlay white and black slip into incised lines to make a pattern under the glaze.

**Period:** 1100–1350 · **Era:** Medieval glaze · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Korean celadon, Inlaid celadon, Sanggam celadon

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Goryeo celadon, you MUST:

1. Fire a fine grey stoneware to a thin, translucent glaze of green-blue, about 0.3 to 0.5 mm thick.
2. Cut the pattern into the leather-hard clay with a fine tool.
3. Fill the cuts with white slip and black slip, then scrape the surface clean (sanggam inlay).
4. Use cranes, clouds, willows, chrysanthemums and lotus as the main motifs.
5. Shape the body as a graceful maebyeong with a high shoulder, or a gourd-shaped ewer.
6. Mould a lid or a spout as a lion, a duck or a turtle-dragon, for the finest pieces.
7. Fire at 1200 to 1250 °C in reduction on small clay spurs.

## Guardrails

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Maebyeong plum vases, gourd-shaped ewers, incense burners, bowls and kundika water bottles.
- **Profile:** A high, curved shoulder tapering to a narrow base; the line is light and rises like a flower bud.
- **Foot and rim:** A small, tall foot ring and a short, flared lip with a thin edge.
- **Proportion:** Maebyeong height 25 to 40 cm, base diameter near half of the shoulder diameter, and a wall 3 to 6 mm thick.
- **Rules:**
  - Make the shoulder broad and the base narrow to give a rising line.
  - Keep the wall thin and the glaze clear for the inlay.
  - Use modelled animal forms only on lids, spouts and handles.
  - Leave the base ring bare with fine sand marks.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stoneware · porcelain
- **Avoid:** earthenware · bone-china · terracotta
- **Rule:** Use a fine grey-white stoneware that is low in iron. It must be pure enough that the glaze stays a clear, light green.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the wheel · slip inlay (sanggam) · carving and incising · mould forming of figures · slab joining
- **Joins:** Large vases are thrown in two parts and joined at the waist; moulded lids and spouts are luted with slip.
- **Details:** Fine sand or clay spur marks under the foot, and thin white and black lines of inlay that sit flush with the surface.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** celadon
- **Decorations:** slip-inlay · incised · relief-moulding · undecorated
- **Pattern:** Cranes in flight among clouds, willow and reed, lotus scroll and chrysanthemum, in white and black inlay, often in panels.
- **Gloss:** Clear and glassy, about 70 to 85 GU, with a thin transparent green.
- **Wear:** Fine crackle may show; the glaze shows brown dirt in the cracks, and the inlay stays crisp.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A translucent grey-green glaze over a pale body, with the inlay in white and black as the only contrast.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A wood-fired climbing kiln, one hundred or more metres long on a hillside, at Gangjin and Buan.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 900 °C · glaze dipping · glaze firing on small spurs in reduction · slow cooling
- **Scale:** Royal and local kilns made wares for the court and the temples; hundreds of kilns worked at Gangjin.
- **Signs:** Small spur marks under the foot, a pale green pool at the base, and sharp inlay lines under the glaze.

### Sanggam inlay (process)

Lines are cut into leather-hard clay and filled with white or black slip. The glaze then covers the surface, and the pattern shows below it. Origin: ceramics, Korea (Goryeo), c. 1150.

- Cut or stamp the pattern into the leather-hard clay.
- Fill the lines with white or dark slip.
- Scrape the surface flat.
- Cover with glaze and fire.
