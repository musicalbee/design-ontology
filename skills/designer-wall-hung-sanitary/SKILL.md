# Designer wall-hung sanitary ware

> A bathroom fitting is a designed object, so the ceramic has a clear, simple shape that floats off the floor.

Sanitary ware that a famous designer shapes as a clean object. A WC hangs on the wall, the cistern hides in the wall, and a basin sits on the counter as a thin, sharp bowl.

**Period:** 1994 to now · **Era:** Plural and contemporary · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Designer bathroom ceramics, Wall-hung WC and basin, Architectural sanitary ware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Designer wall-hung sanitary ware, you MUST:

1. Hang the WC on the wall at 400 mm seat height, and leave the floor free below it.
2. Hide the cistern in a frame of steel inside the wall, with a plate of 2 buttons.
3. Shape the bowl as a clear geometric form: an oval, a circle or a rounded square.
4. Make a counter-top basin with a wall of 6 to 10 mm and a straight, sharp edge.
5. Use one white glaze, or a satin glaze in a few quiet colours.
6. Keep the bowl rimless, so that the inside is easy to clean.
7. Match the shapes of the WC, the basin and the bidet in one family.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** A floor-standing pan with a visible cistern.
- **NEVER:** A moulded flower or a printed pattern on the bowl.
- **NEVER:** A bowl with a hard, sharp edge on the inside.
- **NEVER:** A mix of unrelated shapes in one set.
- **NEVER:** A loud, bright coloured glaze.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** A wall-hung WC and bidet, a counter-top bowl, a thin vanity basin and a flat shower tray, all in one design family.
- **Profile:** Clear geometry: a soft oval or a rounded square, with straight walls and a thin, sharp edge.
- **Foot and rim:** No foot; a hidden bracket on the wall, and a thin straight rim, 6 to 10 mm, on the basin.
- **Proportion:** WC 540 to 580 mm out from the wall and 360 mm wide; basin wall 6 to 10 mm; bowl diameter 400 to 600 mm.
- **Rules:** Keep one geometry through the set: for example one oval. · Hide every fixing, pipe and cistern behind the wall. · Keep the edge thin and straight on a counter basin. · Choose white, or a satin glaze in a quiet shade.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** vitreous-china
- **Secondary:** porcelain · stainless-steel · plastic · wood
- **Avoid:** fireclay · earthenware · terracotta · bone-china
- **Rule:** Use a dense, fine, white vitreous body that holds a thin wall of 6 mm without warping. Metal and wood are for the frame and the seat only.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pressure casting in resin moulds · slip casting of thin basins · robot fettling and spraying · laser measuring of the form · fitting of the frame and the plate
- **Joins:** The WC hangs on two steel studs in a frame inside the wall; the seat is a separate plastic part with a slow-close hinge.
- **Details:** A flat, ground back where the piece meets the wall, a flush glaze with no pinholes, and a very thin, even edge.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** sanitary-glaze · satin-glaze · coloured-gloss
- **Decorations:** undecorated · relief-moulding
- **Pattern:** No pattern. Form carries the design; at most a single, shallow groove or a soft ridge.
- **Gloss:** High gloss, about 90 GU, for the white pieces; satin pieces are about 30 to 40 GU.
- **Wear:** Almost none: the surface stays sharp, and only the seat and the buttons show use.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** White first, then a few quiet satin tones: grey, sand and black, with steel and glass as the only accent.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Concealed cistern frame (technology)

A steel frame inside the wall holds a flat cistern and two studs. The WC hangs on the studs, and only the bowl and a flush plate show in the room. Origin: industrial design, Switzerland, c. 1960.

- Fix a steel frame to the floor and the wall, with the studs at 180 or 230 mm centres.
- Hide a flat cistern of 6 to 9 litres and its pipes in the frame.
- Hang the bowl on the studs and seal it to the wall with a rubber boot.
- Cover the frame with a wall and fit a flush plate with 2 buttons.

### Warm-water bidet seat (technology)

A toilet seat with a heater and a hidden nozzle that sprays warm water. A small pump and a water heater inside the seat replace a separate bidet. Origin: ceramics, Japan, 1980.

- Heat the seat to about 36 °C with an electric element.
- Store a small volume of water and heat it to about 38 °C when needed.
- Hide the nozzle in a cover and rinse it with clean water before and after each use.
- Place the controls on the side of the seat or on a wall remote.
