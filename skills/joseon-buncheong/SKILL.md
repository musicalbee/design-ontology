# Joseon buncheong

> A plain grey stoneware gains life when a white slip is brushed, stamped or carved on it by a free hand with no fear of the rough.

Korean grey stoneware covered with white slip and a thin clear glaze. Potters stamp, carve, brush and paint the slip with bold and free patterns. The result is rustic, direct and lively.

**Period:** 1400–1600 · **Era:** Blue and white · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Punch'ong ware, Korean slip-decorated stoneware, Mishima ware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Joseon buncheong, you MUST:

1. Cover the grey body with a thick white slip, by dipping or by brush, before the glaze.
2. Stamp small repeated flower or circle marks into the leather-hard clay and fill them with white slip.
3. Scratch a design through the white slip to the dark body (sgraffito) for a bold line.
4. Brush the slip fast with a straw brush to leave rhythmic marks on the surface.
5. Paint iron-brown lines on the slip, for fish, flowers and leaves, in a loose manner.
6. Cover the piece with a thin clear or grey-green glaze and fire at 1180 to 1250 °C.
7. Keep the form heavy and strong: a flat bottle, a round jar or a wide bowl.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Flat and round bottles, wide jars, bowls, dishes, and large storage jars with heavy shoulders.
- **Profile:** A firm, full belly with a short neck; the shape is round and slightly uneven.
- **Foot and rim:** A low, thick foot ring and a plain rim; the foot is often bare, with sand grains stuck to it.
- **Proportion:** Height 20 to 40 cm, a wall 6 to 12 mm thick, and the belly wider than the base by one half.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the wall thick and the form strong, not light.
  - Cover only part of the surface with slip, so the dark body stays as a contrast.
  - Let the brush marks and stamps vary along the piece.
  - Leave the foot unglazed and sandy.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stoneware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · creamware
- **Rule:** Use a grey, iron-rich stoneware that stays coarse and dark. The white slip, not the body, makes the pale colour.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing on the wheel · stamping (inhwa) in leather-hard clay · sgraffito through slip · brush slip · iron painting · slip inlay
- **Joins:** A large jar is thrown in two parts and joined at the shoulder; handles and lugs are rare, and luted with slip when used.
- **Details:** Fast brush strokes in white slip, small round stamp marks, and sand or spur marks on the base.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** ash-glaze · clear-feldspathic
- **Decorations:** slip-inlay · sgraffito · iron-painting · incised · brushed-slip · stamped
- **Pattern:** Large peonies cut through the slip, fish and lotus in iron brown, rings of stamped flowers, and free brush swirls.
- **Gloss:** Satin to glossy, about 40 to 70 GU, with a thin grey-green or clear glaze.
- **Wear:** The slip may crack and show the dark body; the glaze may pool in the cut lines and bring out the pattern.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** White slip on a grey body, with iron brown and a thin clear glaze; no bright colour at all.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A wood-fired climbing kiln or a single-chamber kiln, with a loose reduction atmosphere.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:** throw and dry to leather-hard · stamp or cut the pattern · apply white slip · biscuit firing · dip in thin glaze · glaze firing
- **Scale:** Regional kilns in Gyeryongsan, Gwangju and Jeolla made ware for the court and for local government offices.
- **Signs:** Sand or spur marks on the base, brush marks in the slip, and a thin glaze that thins on the high parts.

### Sanggam inlay (process)

Lines are cut into leather-hard clay and filled with white or black slip. The glaze then covers the surface, and the pattern shows below it. Origin: ceramics, Korea (Goryeo), c. 1150.

- Cut or stamp the pattern into the leather-hard clay.
- Fill the lines with white or dark slip.
- Scrape the surface flat.
- Cover with glaze and fire.
