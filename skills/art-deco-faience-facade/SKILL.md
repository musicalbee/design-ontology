# Art Deco faience facade

> A glazed, washable slab turns a commercial building into a bright sign: colour and geometry attract the eye from the street.

Glazed terracotta slabs in bright colours and gold that clad cinemas, shops and towers of the 1920s and 1930s. The ornament is flat and geometric: zigzags, sunbursts and stepped lines.

**Period:** 1925–1940 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Art Deco glazed terracotta, Deco faience cladding, Streamline faience

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Art Deco faience facade, you MUST:

1. Clad the whole front with glazed slabs in one or two strong colours, with a gold or black band.
2. Mould the ornament in low relief: zigzags, sunbursts, chevrons, fountains and stepped forms.
3. Keep the slab flat, with a tight joint of 3 to 5 mm and a straight line of the grid.
4. Use a high-gloss or satin glaze that washes clean in the rain and in the street dirt.
5. Make the slab hollow and 30 to 60 mm thick, with a tie for the steel frame.
6. Light the facade at night; the glaze must reflect the light.
7. Place the tallest ornament over the entrance and at the top of the building.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Flat slabs for facades, shop fronts, cinemas and towers; piers with stepped caps, fountains and sunburst panels, with bands of relief ornament.
- **Profile:** A flat face with a low relief of 5 to 15 mm; the building steps back in tiers and the slab edge is sharp.
- **Foot and rim:** Each slab has a square, glazed edge and a ribbed back with a tie; a shop-front base has a thick, polished sill of black.
- **Proportion:** A slab is about 30 to 60 cm wide and 3 to 6 cm thick; a typical cinema front is 10 to 20 m wide and 6 to 12 m high.
- **Rules:** Use one bright colour with black and gold. · Mould zigzag and sunburst in low relief. · Keep joints thin and straight. · Make the slab hollow with a tie to the frame.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** terracotta
- **Secondary:** earthenware · steel-frame · concrete
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · stoneware · jasperware · parian · paper-clay
- **Rule:** Use a fine, even terracotta or a faience body that fires dense and light-coloured, so that the thin glaze shows a pure bright colour.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pressing in plaster moulds · hollow casting with ribs · machine extrusion of flat slabs · firing of glaze and body together
- **Joins:** Slabs are hung on a steel or concrete frame with metal ties and set in cement mortar with thin joints.
- **Details:** A ribbed back with a tie slot, a number or mark on the edge, and a thin line of glaze that pools at the joint.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** coloured-gloss · satin-glaze · matte-glaze
- **Decorations:** relief-moulding · gilding · undecorated
- **Pattern:** Zigzag, sunburst, chevron and stepped lines in low relief, in cream, black, blue-green and gold; plain glazed fields between the bands.
- **Gloss:** High gloss, about 80 to 95 GU, for the main colour; the black and the gold bands are satin or gloss.
- **Wear:** The glaze washes clean and keeps its colour; some slabs craze, and a few lose a corner at a sharp edge.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** One strong colour per building, usually cream, blue-green or black, with gold lines and one bright accent at the entrance.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A large tunnel or downdraught kiln of a terracotta works; slabs stand on edge on setters in saggars.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** pressing or casting of the slab · a bisque firing at about 1050 °C · spraying of a coloured glaze · a glaze firing at about 1050 to 1100 °C · hand gilding and a low-heat firing of the gold
- **Scale:** A few large works in New York and on the west coast supplied cinemas, shops and towers; a tower front used tens of thousands of slabs.
- **Signs:** A ribbed back, a thin glaze line at the joint and a bright, even gloss that mirrors the street.

### Architectural faience (material)

A hollow slab of fine clay with a coloured glaze on the visible face. It clads a wall with a bright, washable skin. Origin: ceramics, England, c. 1880.

- Press or cast the slab hollow, 30 to 60 mm thick, with ribs on the back.
- Fire the slab to a biscuit at about 1050 °C.
- Spray the visible face with a coloured glaze and fire it again.
- Tie the slab to the frame with metal ties and set it with thin joints.
