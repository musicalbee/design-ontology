# Web 2.0 Gloss

> A site is a friendly tool: make every control look glossy, round and easy to press.

Glossy rounded buttons, vertical gradients, reflections under logos, big friendly type, star badges and a 'beta' tag on a clean white page. Social and tool start-ups used it after 2004.

**Period:** 2004–2010 · **Era:** Postmodern & Early Digital · **Domain:** Internet · **Also called:** Web 2.0 style, Glossy buttons, Beta badge style

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "web 2.0", "glossy button", "beta badge", "reflection logo", "aqua gradient", "2005 start-up", "rounded corners", "ajax".

Good for: nostalgic start-up and tool pages, community and sharing sites, retro app landing pages, icon and badge sets, parody of the first social web.

Do not use it when:
- The brand needs a flat, modern or serious tone.
- The device needs a light, fast render with few images.
- The audience needs a premium, restrained look.

## System directives

When generating web pages, apps, memes, short video frames and other images of internet culture in Web 2.0 Gloss, you MUST:

1. Fill buttons with a vertical 2-stop gradient, for example #5B9BD5 to #2F6DB5, with a white 1px inner highlight at the top.
2. Set a gloss on the top half: a white overlay at 35% to 50% opacity that ends at 50% of the button height.
3. Round corners at 6px to 12px on buttons and cards, and use pill shapes for tags.
4. Place a reflection under the logo: a flipped copy at 30% opacity that fades to 0% over 40px.
5. Set big, rounded headlines at 36px to 48px in a friendly sans, and 14px body text on a white ground.
6. Add a star or burst badge of 60px to 90px with 'beta', 'new' or 'free' in bold white on orange (#FF8A00).
7. Use a soft drop shadow of 0 2px 4px at 20% black under cards and a 1px light grey line (#D0D7DE).

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Flat colour with no gradient.
- **NEVER:** Sharp 0px corners and hard bevels.
- **NEVER:** Dark moody grounds.
- **NEVER:** Small, dense text in tables.
- **NEVER:** Thin light type with no emphasis.
- **NEVER:** Cluttered tiled backgrounds.

## Spatial & grid logic

- **Grid mechanics:** A centred container of 960px on a 12-column grid with 20px gutters; a hero of 500px to 600px with a big call-to-action.
- **Whitespace ratio:** 30% to 40% of the page is empty; the spacing scale is 10, 20 and 40px.
- **Alignment rules:** Centred hero and logo; text blocks flush left; feature rows in 3 columns with icons.
- **Container geometry:** Cards and buttons with a 8px radius, pill tags, round avatars and badges.

## Typography

- **Classifications:** rounded sans · humanist sans
- **Tracking:** Display -0.01em; body 0em.
- **Leading:** Display 1.15; body 1.5.
- **Hierarchy rules:**
  - Use 14px for body text, 18px for lead text and 40px for the hero title.
  - Use bold and a darker blue for headings.
  - Use a link colour of blue with no underline until hover.

## Motion & interactivity

- **Born on screen:** yes
- **Transition physics:** Menus and panels fade and slide at 200ms to 300ms with ease-out; Ajax updates highlight in yellow (#FFF6A5) for 1s and fade out.
- **Easing:** cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.1, 0.25, 1)
- **Duration (ms):** 250
- **Hover and active:** Hover brightens the gradient by 8% and lifts the button by 1px; active state inverts the gradient and removes the shadow.

- **Icons:** duotone, stroke 2 on a 24px field, round caps, round joins, container: rounded. Use: `<svg class="icon"><use href="pack/icons.svg#home"/></svg>`. An icon takes `currentColor`.
- **Icon family:** the sprite is cut from Fluent UI System Icons (Filled, 24) by Microsoft Corporation, licence MIT. Reason: Rounded filled icons with two tones match the glossy tiles of the first social web. It is an open-source stand-in for a demo. For a product, use the icon library of the brand and choose the family by the rules below.
- **Display type:** weight 700, tracking -0.01em, leading 1.15, case: none.
- **Weights:** display 700, subhead 400, body 400, bold in text 700, labels and buttons 700. The style uses 2 weights (400, 700) and no other. The display is 300 heavier than the body. The accent is weight: a heavier word is the accent, so the steps between the weights are clear.
- **Body type:** 14px, leading 1.5.
- **Page:** hero split, center aligned, regular density, accent tilt 0°.
- **Ground:** sky. The ground is a pale sky gradient above a white page, as in a start-up landing page. Put the class `ground` on the first screen of the page; it takes `--ground`, a stack of gradients built from the colour tokens. The ground can run behind the copy, so put the class `ground-copy` on each text block of that screen: the block gets `--color-bg` and a soft edge. Keep the rest of the page on `--color-bg`.
- **Text colour:** `--color-text` is for the main reading field only. On each field, use its token: `--color-on-bg`, `--color-on-surface`, `--color-on-primary`, `--color-on-accent`.

### How to choose an icon pack

An icon pack is a typeface for pictures. Choose it with the same care as the text face. These rules hold for an open-source set and for a licensed library.

1. **The stroke matches the stem.** Set an icon next to the body text at text size. The line of the icon and the stem of a letter have the same weight.
2. **Terminals and corners follow the radius token.** Butt caps and mitre joins go with a radius of zero. Round caps and round joins go with a soft radius.
3. **The fill mode follows the technology of the era.** Print eras cut solid forms. Screen eras with depth fill and shade the form. Current product interfaces draw an outline.
4. **The metaphors are not younger than the style.** Each picture is an object that the era knew. An icon with a later metaphor breaks the period.
5. **One family, one grid.** All icons come from one family and one variant, drawn on one field. Do not mix families on one surface.
6. **The family covers the product.** List each concept that the product needs before you commit. A family that has no icon for one of them is the wrong family.
7. **The colour is a token.** An icon takes currentColor. A second tone comes from a token, not from the file.
8. **The licence permits the use.** Read the licence of the set. Give credit where the licence asks for it. A product with a brand uses its licensed library and applies the same rules to choose the family.

### Glossy gradient button (material)

A button with a vertical gradient, a white highlight on the top half and a soft shadow below, so that it looks like candy or glass. Origin: internet, United States, 2000.

- Fill the button with a vertical 2-stop gradient, light blue to dark blue.
- Add a white overlay at 35% to 50% opacity on the top half.
- Add a 1px inner highlight at the top edge and a shadow of 0 2px 4px below.
- Round the corners at 6px to 12px.
