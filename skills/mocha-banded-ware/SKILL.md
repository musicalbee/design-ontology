# Mocha and banded ware

> A machine can turn the bands of a dipped jug in one pass, and one drop of acid colour on a wet slip makes a tree that no hand can plan.

Cheap pale earthenware for the tavern and the farm kitchen. Bands of coloured slip are turned on a lathe, and a drop of acid colour spreads into a fern or a tree.

**Period:** 1770–1900 · **Era:** Industrial pottery · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Mocha ware, Dipped ware, Industrial slipware, Banded creamware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Mocha and banded ware, you MUST:

1. Throw or press the body in creamware or pearlware, with a wall 4 to 6 mm thick.
2. Dip the leather-hard pot in a slip of one colour, then turn bands in slip on a lathe.
3. Cut the bands 3 to 15 mm wide, in groups of 2 or 3, in contrast of fawn, blue, green or brown.
4. Make the mocha tree: touch the wet slip with a drop of tobacco, turpentine and iron-oxide colour; it feathers upward in 2 seconds.
5. Put the tree between two bands, once or three times round a mug.
6. Dip the whole piece in a clear lead glaze and fire it at about 1100 °C.
7. Keep the shapes plain: a mug or a jug should hold a half, one or two pints.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Cylindrical mugs and tankards, straight-sided jugs, bowls, measures and large mixing bowls made as pints and quarts.
- **Profile:** A straight or slightly flared wall on a flat base; a jug has a short neck and a strap handle with a foot.
- **Foot and rim:** A plain flat base with a small turned foot ring; the rim is a plain bead with a spout for the jug.
- **Proportion:** A pint mug stands 11 to 13 cm high; the wall is 4 to 6 mm thick, which is thin for earthenware.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the wall straight, so that the lathe can cut the bands in a clean line.
  - Place the tree in a band of one colour between two bands of another.
  - Glaze the inside and the outside.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** pearlware · creamware
- **Secondary:** earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · terracotta
- **Rule:** Use a pale earthenware that fires cream or pearl white, so that the slip colours show. Keep dark red and stoneware bodies out: they hide the bands.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing and jolleying on the wheel · dipping in slip · turning bands on an engine lathe · pouring a mocha colour on the wet slip · dipping in lead glaze
- **Joins:** Handles are moulded or pulled and fixed with slip; the rim is turned smooth with a metal rib.
- **Details:** A turned band as sharp as a line, a tree that feathers up to a band, and a small foot ring cut by the lathe.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** lead-glaze
- **Decorations:** mocha-dendritic · hand-banding · agateware
- **Pattern:** Fine turned bands in two or three colours, wide bands with a mocha tree, and a seaweed or cat's-eye pattern; some pots show marbling.
- **Gloss:** A clear gloss of about 55 to 80 GU, with a slight yellow cast.
- **Wear:** The slip bands wear at the rim and the foot; the tree stays fresh because the glaze covers it.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Earth tones in bands on a pale ground: fawn, brown, ochre, blue and green, with a dark tree in one band.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A coal-fired bottle kiln; biscuit, slip decoration and a second firing for the glaze.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 1100 °C · dipping in slip · turning the bands on a lathe · adding the mocha tree on wet slip · glaze firing at about 1080 °C
- **Scale:** Pubs in England and America used pottery measures in the thousands; the ware was made by the million between 1790 and 1880.
- **Signs:** Sharp lathe bands, a mocha tree that feathers, and a base that has a turned ring.

### Engine turning (skill)

A lathe with a cutting tool cuts a regular pattern of fine lines in a flat metal face. The pattern catches the light and shows no tool marks. Origin: fashion, Europe, c. 1600.

- Fix the metal blank on the rose engine, and set the cam that gives the pattern.
- Cut each line at a depth of 0.05 to 0.1 mm, and keep the pitch the same.
- Polish the cut lines, or fill them with clear enamel.
