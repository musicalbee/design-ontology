# Music video

> The song is the script, so images are cut to its beat and structure and a loud, memorable look sells the artist.

A picture built to a song. The cut falls on the beat, the set is a stage, and the grade is loud. Story and place are cues that give way to a hook, a dance and a look.

**Period:** 1981–today · **Era:** Tape, cable and the blockbuster · **Domain:** Film & Video · **Also called:** MTV style, Promo video, Pop clip

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "music video", "mtv style", "pop video", "clip on the beat", "lip-sync video", "dance video", "promo video", "visualiser".

Good for: music releases and lyric videos, fashion and brand films, short-form vertical clips on a track, video prompts for a dance or a performance.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a documentary tone or a quiet, long take.
- The piece has no music track to cut to.

## System directives

When planning, shooting, prompting, editing or grading moving images in Music video, you MUST:

1. Cut on the beat: at 120 bpm a beat lasts 0.5 s, so cut on every beat or every second beat, and change the shot on each chorus.
2. Build the clip in three layers: a performance, a story or a concept, and a graphic inset; keep each for 3 to 8 s.
3. Shoot the artist in a close-up for 30% or more of the runtime, with a lip-sync at 24 fps and the song at playback speed.
4. Use a strong key colour per section: one saturated hue at 40% or more of the frame, and swap it at the chorus.
5. Move the camera at the tempo: a push-in over 4 beats, a spin over 8 beats, a whip at the drop.
6. Use a wide lens of 14mm to 24mm for dance and a long lens of 85mm to 135mm for a face, with a shallow focus at f/1.8 to f/2.8.
7. Place one signature visual in the first 10 s and repeat it at the final chorus.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** A static, single, long take with no reference to the beat.
- **NEVER:** A realistic, un-graded, muted palette.
- **NEVER:** A plain, unlit location with no concept.
- **NEVER:** Dialogue and diegetic sound that mask the song.
- **NEVER:** A scene that is cut for continuity and not for the music.
- **NEVER:** A face that does not look into the lens.
- **NEVER:** A look with no signature visual.

## Composition

- **Framing:** A bold, wide-lens frame in 16:9 or 2.39:1, with the artist large and central, and a graphic shape or a colour wall behind.
- **Staging in depth:** A stage of 3 planes: a foreground prop or a dancer, the artist in the middle and a coloured set or a screen behind.
- **Subject placement:** The artist on the centre or a third line; the dancers in a row or a ring about them, and the lens at a low or a Dutch angle.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule | Value |
|---|---|---|

## Camera & movement

- **Movement:** A push-in, a spin, a crane drop or a whip pan timed to the beat; the camera is a dancer of the piece.
- **Support:** Steadicam, jib, crane, dolly and a motion-control rig
- **Camera height:** 0.3 to 2.5 m; low for a power shot and high for a choreography
- **Move speed:** Push-in over 4 beats (about 2 s at 120 bpm); spin 180° to 360° over 8 beats; whip 180° per second

## Lens & format

- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 (1.78:1) or 2.39:1; 4:3 for the 1980s clips; 9:16 for a platform version
- **Frame rate:** 24 fps; 48 to 120 fps for slow motion; 30 fps for video
- **Shutter angle:** 180° (1/48 s); 90° for a strobed, sharp look
- **Focal length:** 14mm to 24mm (35mm format) for dance; 85mm to 135mm for a face
- **Aperture:** f/1.8 to f/2.8 for a shallow face; f/5.6 for choreography
- **Resolution:** 35mm or 4K to 6K digital
- **Focus:** A shallow focus on the artist, with a rack to the dancers on the drop.
- **Equipment:** Steadicam · jib · motion control · a strobe and LED wall · green screen

## Light

- **Quality:** Hard, coloured, theatrical light with a haze, beams, a strobe and a rim light on the artist.
- **Direction:** A back light and side light for a rim, a top light for a hero shot and a flat front light for a pop face.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 6:1 for a dramatic clip; 2:1 for a pop clip
- **Colour temperature:** A mix of 3200K and gels from 2000K to 9000K, with a coloured key
- **Source:** LED panels, gelled spots, a strobe and a haze machine

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** A loud, saturated, stylised grade that changes by section: one dominant hue, deep blacks and a clean rim light.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Set the black point at 1% and keep the blacks deep and clean.
  - Push the saturation by +30% to +50% and shift the midtones to the section's hue by 10% to 20%.
  - Add a contrast curve of +30% with a hard shoulder at 92%.
  - Add a glow of 8% to 15% on the highlights and a chromatic split of 1 to 2 px at the edge.
  - Change the hue at each chorus, and keep one look for each section.
- **Texture:** Clean or stylised: either a smooth digital image or a deliberate grain of 10% to 20%

## Editing & rhythm

- **Average shot length:** 1.5 to 3 s (estimate; check Cinemetrics for each title)
- **Shot length range:** 0.2 s for a flash cut to 15 s for a performance shot
- **Cutting logic:** Cut to the beat and the lyric. Change the picture at each section of the song, and use a repeat or a loop at the chorus.
- **Transitions:** Hard cut on the beat, a flash frame, a whip, a match cut on a movement and a speed ramp.
- **Runtime:** 3 to 5 min

## Sound

- **Approach:** The song is the only sound, in full range and at playback speed; effects are rare and added on the beat.
- **Dialogue:** None, or a spoken intro of 5 to 15 s at the start of a clip.
- **Music:** The full track, cut to a video version of 3 to 5 min, with an intro and an outro edit.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, with a true peak of −1 dBTP for a platform release

## Titles & graphics

- **Treatment:** The artist and the song title in a lower third in the first 10 s; lyrics or graphics as a kinetic type on the beat.
- **Type:** A bold display or a condensed sans-serif in a saturated colour, 2 to 5 s for the title card.

## Techniques

### Jump cut (montage)

A cut inside one continuous shot that removes time and leaves the jump in view. Origin: film & video, Paris, 1960.

- Remove 0.3 to 2 seconds inside one shot and keep the framing within 10% of the frame width, so that the jump shows.
- In a talk to camera, cut on the word and not on the action: one cut every 2 to 4 seconds.
