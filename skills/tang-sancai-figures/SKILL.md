# Tang sancai figures

> A tomb figure shows the luxury of the living court in clay, and a lead glaze in three colours makes the animal or the lady glow.

Tomb figures of white earthenware under lead glazes in amber, green and cream that run into each other. Court ladies, Sogdian grooms, camels, horses and guardians show the life of the Silk Road.

**Period:** 700–907 · **Era:** Medieval glaze · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Tang three-colour figures, Tang tomb figures, Tang camels and horses

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Tang sancai figures, you MUST:

1. Make the figure from a white or buff earthenware that is pressed in moulds, in parts.
2. Fire the body once to about 1050 °C before any glaze touches it.
3. Splash amber, green and cream lead glazes on the biscuit, and let them run and mix in the second firing at 800 to 900 °C.
4. Leave the face, the hands and sometimes the hair free of glaze, and paint them cold after the firing.
5. Give a horse a strong neck, a small head and a tied tail, and a camel a raised head that is open to call.
6. Model Sogdian grooms and musicians with beards, high caps and wide eyes, as the traders of the Silk Road.
7. Give a guardian a fierce face, a raised fist and a rock base, and a height of 70 to 100 cm.
8. Keep the glaze off the base, which stays bare and dry, so that it does not stick to the kiln.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** A single flat colour of glaze over the whole figure.
- **NEVER:** A translucent white porcelain body with thin walls.
- **NEVER:** Neat printed or evenly painted glaze patterns.
- **NEVER:** A small cute figure with a round child-like face.
- **NEVER:** A blue cobalt glaze in the main area.
- **NEVER:** A rough brown unglazed terracotta, like a soldier of Qin.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Horses, Bactrian camels, court ladies, grooms, musicians, officials and guardian kings, usually 20 to 80 cm tall, hollow and made in parts.
- **Profile:** A horse stands with arched neck and a straight back, a camel stands with its head thrown back, and a lady stands in a long S-curve with full sleeves.
- **Foot and rim:** A flat rectangular base, left bare of glaze, with a vent hole under the belly of the animal or the figure.
- **Proportion:** A horse is about 60 to 80 cm high and a camel 50 to 80 cm; the wall is 5 to 10 mm thick, and a lady has a small head and a full body.
- **Rules:**
  - Press the figure in parts, then join and finish the parts by hand.
  - Let the three glazes meet and run in streaks.
  - Leave the face and hands bare and paint them cold.
  - Fit a vent hole in the hollow figure for the kiln.
  - Show the load on a camel: bags, a cooking pot and a hanging duck.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Secondary:** terracotta
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · bone-china · parian
- **Rule:** Use a white to buff kaolin-rich earthenware that fires to a soft, pale body under the glaze. Keep it porous, and keep porcelain out.

## Forming

- **Methods:** press moulding in two-piece clay moulds · hand modelling of details · slab building of bases · luting of parts with slip · carving of the leather-hard surface
- **Joins:** The front and back halves are pressed in moulds and joined with slip; the legs, head and ears are added or cast apart and fitted.
- **Details:** A vent hole under the belly, a seam hidden by hand finish, and three small stilt marks where the figure stood in the kiln.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** sancai · lead-glaze · bare-clay
- **Decorations:** cold-painted-pigment · relief-moulding · gilding
- **Pattern:** Splashed and dripped amber, green and cream glaze, in streaks and blotches. Some pieces add a deep blue from cobalt, and details are cold-painted in red and black.
- **Gloss:** Glossy, about 60 to 90 GU on the glaze; the cold-painted face is matt.
- **Wear:** The lead glaze turns iridescent and silver in the ground. The cold paint and gilt flake off, and the bare clay of the face shows.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Three warm colours on a pale ground: amber, leaf green and cream, with a rare blue and cold red or black on the face.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A small updraught or horseshoe kiln, fired with coal or wood, and two firings for each figure.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 1050 °C · brushing of lead glaze colours · glaze firing at 800 to 900 °C · cold painting of the face and hair
- **Scale:** Workshops at Gongxian in Henan and near Chang'an made large runs for the tombs of officials and nobles.
- **Signs:** Glaze that runs down the body and pools at the base, three stilt marks, and a bare face with traces of cold paint.

## Techniques

### Glaze splashing (process)

Lead glazes of different colours are brushed, dripped or splashed on a fired body. They run together in the second firing and make streaks of amber, green and cream. Origin: ceramics, Henan and Shaanxi, Tang China, c. 700.

- Fire the pressed body to about 1050 °C.
- Brush or drip the coloured lead glazes on the biscuit.
- Leave the base bare of glaze.
- Fire again at 800 to 900 °C, so that the glazes run and mix.
