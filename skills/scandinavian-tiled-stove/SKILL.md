# Scandinavian tiled stove

> A white tiled stove is both a machine for heat and a piece of furniture: the glaze reflects the light of a dark northern room.

A tall stove clad in white glazed tiles that stores the heat of a short wood fire in a room. The Swedish kakelugn has thin tiles, a plain white face and a soft frame of mouldings.

**Period:** 1750–1930 · **Era:** Industrial pottery · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Kakelugn, Swedish tile stove, Nordic faience stove

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Scandinavian tiled stove, you MUST:

1. Clad a brick core with flat glazed tiles about 20 cm by 25 cm, and 15 to 20 mm thick.
2. Make the stove tall and slim, 2 to 3 m high, so that it fits the corner of a room.
3. Glaze the tiles in a plain white; add a few lines of blue or grey painting for decoration.
4. Finish the top with a cornice, a crown or an urn, and the base with a plinth.
5. Cut the flue in long windings of 5 to 8 m, so that the brick stores the heat of the fire.
6. Use a small iron door and a damper of brass, and keep the metal parts in a simple form.
7. Mould the cornice and the frames in the same clay, so that the tiles and the mouldings match.
8. Repeat the neoclassical motifs, the garland, the urn and the fluted column, in low relief.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** A tall corner stove with a flat white face, a moulded cornice, a crown, an urn or a flame, a small door, and a plinth; and some fireplace and frieze tiles.
- **Profile:** A narrow, upright body with a slight taper; the cornice and plinth make horizontal shadow lines.
- **Foot and rim:** The stove stands on a plinth tile and ends in a crowned cornice that meets the ceiling line.
- **Proportion:** Height 2 to 3 m, width about 60 to 80 cm, depth about 50 to 60 cm; each face tile is about 20 cm by 25 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the face plain white and show the joints as thin lines.
  - Put all ornament in the cornice and the crown.
  - Match the stove to the proportion of the room: one stove for each room.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware · creamware
- **Secondary:** fired-brick
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · vitreous-china
- **Rule:** Use a pale earthenware tile with a white glaze, and later a creamware body. Use fired brick for the core. Keep porcelain out: it is too dense for the heat cycles.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pressing the tile in a plaster mould · drying the tile in a rack · moulding the cornice in sections · building the brick core with winding flues · setting the tiles with a wire and clay mortar
- **Joins:** Iron wire and clay hold each tile to the brick core, and a thin joint of clay and sand fills the gaps.
- **Details:** A fine crackle in the glaze, thin joints, a tile number on the back and a small iron door with a brass damper.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** tin-glaze · lead-glaze
- **Decorations:** undecorated · underglaze-cobalt · underglaze-polychrome · relief-moulding · gilding
- **Pattern:** A plain white face with a blue or grey garland, a moulded cornice and a crown, with a little gilding on the best stoves.
- **Gloss:** Soft gloss, about 50 to 75 GU, with a slight crackle.
- **Wear:** The glaze crazes in time with the heat and shows a fine network of lines; the white turns a little cream.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A white or pale cream face with a little blue, grey or green; a very small amount of gold.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A coal or wood-fired kiln for the tiles; a stove maker builds the stove on site.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** a biscuit firing of the pressed tile · dipping in a white glaze · a glaze firing · building the stove on site
- **Scale:** In 1882 one Stockholm factory made 6,400 stoves, and the tiled stove was the usual heater of an urban flat.
- **Signs:** A crazed glaze, a number on the back of each tile and a stamp of the factory.

### Tiled stove (technology)

A stove of fired clay tiles holds a hot fire for an hour and gives out its heat for a day. Long flues inside the stove take heat from the smoke. Origin: ceramics, Stockholm, Sweden, 1767.

- Build a firebox and long inner flues of fireclay.
- Cover the body with glazed tiles of 20 to 30 cm.
- Burn one hot load of wood in 1 to 2 hours and close the damper.
- Let the tile mass give out heat for 12 to 24 hours.
