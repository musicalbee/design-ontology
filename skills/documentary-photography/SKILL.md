# Documentary photography

> A photograph can be evidence and an appeal at once, if the maker stays honest about what happened and with whom.

Photographs that bear witness to people, work and events. The maker gets close, earns trust and shows what the sitter needs the viewer to see.

**Period:** 1930s–today · **Era:** The small camera · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** Social documentary, Photojournalism

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "documentary photography", "photojournalism", "photo essay", "dorothea lange", "walker evans", "social documentary", "reportage".

Good for: editorial essays, NGO and report imagery, oral-history projects, image prompts for honest portraits at work.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a staged or glossy ideal.
- The subjects have not agreed to be shown, or their safety is at risk.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in Documentary photography, you MUST:

1. Use a 35mm or 50mm lens at 1 to 2.5m, so that the sitter, the room and the work share the frame.
2. Shoot in available light at ISO 400 to 1600, with a shutter of 1/60s or faster, and do not add flash.
3. Show the face and the hands, and the place that explains them.
4. Make 100 or more frames for one story, and edit down to 8 to 20 pictures.
5. Print with a full but natural range: no crushed blacks and no clipped skies.
6. Caption with who, where and when, in plain words.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Staging a scene or rearranging people.
- **NEVER:** Heavy HDR or composite skies.
- **NEVER:** Cropping out context that changes the meaning.
- **NEVER:** Victim pose and pity as the only note.
- **NEVER:** Beauty filters or skin smoothing.
- **NEVER:** Captions that invent facts.

## Composition

- **Framing:** Medium and wide; the frame holds the person and the place that explains them.
- **Viewpoint:** Eye height, handheld, 1 to 2.5m from the subject.
- **Geometry:** Window frames, doorways and tools make the geometry; people sit inside layers of the room.
- **Subject placement:** Off centre with the eyes or the hands on a third line; context fills the rest.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| focal-point | Let 1 face or pair of hands take 25–40% of the frame, with the place around it. |
| layering-depth | Hold 3 planes: foreground object at 1m, person at 2m and room or street at 5m. |
| rule-of-thirds | Put the eyes within 0.05 of the upper third line, at 0.33 of the height. |
| framing | Use a door, window or tool to frame the person with a margin of 5–15%. |
| balance | Keep informal balance: person on one side, work or object on the other, at a ratio of 0.7–1.4. |

## Light

- **Quality:** Available light, as found: window light, overcast sky or a single lamp.
- **Direction:** From a window or door to the side; the face gets a soft modelling of 2:1 to 4:1.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 2:1 to 4:1 (1–2 stops)
- **Colour temperature:** 5000–6500K for daylight; 2800–3200K for tungsten
- **Time of day:** Any; early and late light preferred

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** 35mm SLR or rangefinder; digital full frame as a modern equivalent
- **Focal length:** 35mm to 50mm
- **Aperture:** f/4 to f/8
- **Shutter:** 1/60s to 1/500s
- **Film speed:** ISO 400 (up to 1600 pushed)
- **Focus:** Zone focus at 2m at f/8, or focus on the eyes at f/4.
- **Support:** Handheld
- **Equipment:** 35mm camera body · 35mm and 50mm primes · notebook for captions

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Natural black and white with moderate grain, or true colour with no creative grade.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Convert to monochrome with a neutral mix (R 40, G 50, B 10).
  - Set the black point at 2% and the white point at 97%.
  - Apply +15% midtone contrast.
  - Recover skies by −0.5 stop with a graduated filter; do not composite.
  - Add film grain, amount 15%, size 30%, as for Tri-X at ISO 400.
  - Crop by no more than 5%, and keep a caption with every frame.
- **Grain:** Tri-X 400: medium grain, visible at A4

## Subject & staging

- **Subjects:** workers · families at home · migrants and refugees · farms and factories · protests · children at school
- **Staging:** None. The maker spends days or weeks with the subject and photographs what happens.
- **Direction:** Ask for consent and for time; do not ask for a pose. Show the sitter the pictures when it is possible.

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:**
  - Make a contact sheet of every frame and mark the strongest.
  - Edit the series down to 8 to 20 pictures that run as a story.
  - Print in a natural range on grade 2 paper.
  - Write captions with name, place and date; check every fact.
- **Output:** A photo essay of 8 to 20 pictures, with captions, in print, in a book or online.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### 35 mm camera (technology)

A small, quiet camera that takes 36 frames of 24 x 36 mm on cinema film, so that the photographer can work by hand in the street. Origin: photography, Wetzlar, Germany, 1925.

- Use a 28 to 50 mm lens and focus by scale, not through the finder.
- Shoot at 1/125 to 1/500 s, from the hand.
- Show the full frame; keep the 2:3 format.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Life** (magazine, New York, 1936–2007), published the style. The weekly photo magazine that Henry Luce started in 1936 from an older humour magazine of the same name. It made the photo essay a main form of documentary photography.
- **Magnum Photos** (studio, Paris and New York, 1947–now), made the style. A cooperative that its photographers own. Its members made documentary and street photography for magazines.
