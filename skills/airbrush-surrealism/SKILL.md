# Airbrush Surrealism

> A dream can be rendered as a polished object: the airbrush gives an impossible scene the smooth, lit surface of a photograph.

Surreal airbrushed illustration: supersaturated colour, floating or juxtaposed objects, chrome surfaces, lightning and X-ray cut-aways. It lived on album covers, pulp science fiction and fantasy, and pin-up chrome robots.

**Period:** Late 1960s – Mid 1980s · **Era:** Avant-garde · **Domain:** Painting · **Also called:** Airbrush fantasy, Airbrush illustration, Chrome-and-lightning airbrush

## When to load this skill

Good for: music covers and posters, science fiction and fantasy book covers, game and film key art, editorial and merch illustration.

Do not use it when:
- The brief asks for a loose, painterly or hand-made look.
- The subject is a quiet, realistic scene with no impossible element.

## System directives

When making, prompting or art-directing a picture: a painting, an illustration, an image-generation prompt or a cover in Airbrush Surrealism, you MUST:

1. Build every form with airbrushed gradients and no visible stroke: a sphere or a tube holds a smooth tonal run of 8 to 12 steps from highlight to core shadow.
2. Render chrome and metal with a sharp horizon line in the reflection: a sky band over a dark ground band, split at 50% of the form height.
3. Place 2 to 4 unrelated objects in one scene, juxtaposed or floating, with a clear gap of 5 to 15% of the picture between them.
4. Raise saturation to the top of the range in the sky and in glows: use 3 to 5 hues, such as electric blue, magenta and orange, at full chroma.
5. Cut hard edges with masking film: each shape has a crisp edge of 0.5mm or less, and each glow has a soft edge of 20 to 60mm.
6. Add one light effect, such as a lightning bolt, a lens flare or a halo, that crosses 30 to 50% of the picture.
7. Show the inside of a body or a machine in one X-ray cut-away, with the outer skin stopped at a clean line.
8. Set the horizon at 1/3 from the bottom, in a flat plain or a sea, so that the objects stand in a deep, empty space.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Visible brush strokes and impasto in the paint.
- **NEVER:** Muted, earthy or tonal palettes.
- **NEVER:** Soft, hazy focus with no crisp mask edge.
- **NEVER:** A single, realistic scene with no impossible element.
- **NEVER:** Neon grid, wireframe and digital glow from later computer imagery.
- **NEVER:** Hand-drawn contour lines of uniform weight around each form.
- **NEVER:** Flat poster colour with no gradient on the forms.

## Composition

- **Layout:** A centred or slightly off-centre hero object on a deep plain or sea horizon, with secondary objects floating in the sky at 2 to 3 scales.
- **Focal point:** The brightest highlight or the lightning strike, placed at 1/3 from the top and 1/3 from one side.
- **Format:** A square 30 by 30cm album sleeve (1:1), a pulp cover of 2:3, or a landscape 3:2; the main object may be cut by the edge by up to 10%.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| focal-point | Put the highest contrast and the sharpest highlight on one object, which fills 25 to 40% of the picture. |
| layering-depth | Show 3 to 4 planes: a foreground object, a mid-ground object, a horizon band and a sky gradient of 40% of the height. |
| contrast | Set a dark core shadow (below 15% lightness) against a specular highlight (above 90%) on the same form. |
| negative-space | Leave 25 to 35% of the picture as open sky or sea, filled by a smooth gradient and not by detail. |
| scale | Make the scale wrong on purpose: an object at 3 to 10 times its natural size next to a small one. |

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Supersaturated gradients: a deep ultramarine or black sky that glows into magenta, orange and cyan at the horizon, with chrome that reflects the same hues.
- **Distribution:** Dark sky and ground 40–50%, saturated gradient glow 25–30%, chrome and highlight neutrals 15–20%, one hot accent under 10%.
- **Ground:** Smooth white illustration board or a gessoed panel, sealed so that the spray lies flat and even.

## Brushwork & medium

- **Medium:** Liquid acrylic, gouache and dye, sprayed through an airbrush at 20 to 40 psi, with masking film and frisket to hold the edges.
- **Support:** Illustration board or gessoed hardboard, from 30 by 30cm to 60 by 90cm; a final fix with a clear varnish.
- **Brushwork:** No brush stroke shows. The spray builds in thin layers of 10 to 30 passes; a fine brush adds only specular dots of 1 to 2mm.
- **Paint handling:** Paint goes on thin and transparent, and each mask is cut, sprayed and removed in turn; tone builds by repeat passes and not by thick paint.
- **Edges:** Crisp, knife-cut edges on solid objects and soft, 20 to 60mm spray edges on glow, cloud and haze; the two meet in each picture.

## Light & modelling

- **Light:** A strong, single key light with a rim light of a second colour; the highlights are specular and clipped at white.
- **Modelling:** Form comes from smooth gradients, with a sharp reflection line on metal and a glossy highlight on every curved surface.
- **Shadow:** Core shadows run to near black; cast shadows are soft ovals under floating objects at 20 to 30% opacity.
- **Temperature:** Cool blues in the sky and shadows against warm orange and magenta in glows; chrome picks up both.

## Subject

- **Typical:** Album covers, pulp science fiction and fantasy, pin-up chrome robots, floating eyes and planets, lightning, skulls, spacecraft and technical cut-aways.
- **Figure scale:** The hero object fills 25–50% of the height; a figure or robot may reach 70–90% on a pin-up or a pulp cover.
- **Treatment:** Idealised and polished: bodies and machines are smooth, symmetrical and glossy, with no wear and no visible texture.

## Surface

- **Finish:** Glossy and seamless, as a photograph; varnish adds a uniform sheen.
- **Texture:** No texture: a fine spray grain visible only at 10 times magnification.

### Airbrush (tool)

A pen-sized spray gun that lays paint in soft, even tones; with masks, it makes hard edges and smooth gradients side by side. Origin: photography, United States, 1879.

- Build tone in 3 to 6 thin passes; each pass adds 20% to 40% of the final density.
- Cut a hard mask for each edge that must stay sharp; leave the light soft.
- Put one specular highlight of pure white on each chrome form.
