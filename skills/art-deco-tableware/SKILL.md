# Art Deco tableware

> A cheap white earthenware becomes modern when a painter puts strong flat colour in sharp geometry on a form with angles and cones.

Bright, hand-painted earthenware in angles, cones and bands. Painters in the potteries of Stoke-on-Trent put bold flat colour on a white ground, and the shapes copy the stepped and conical forms of the Paris fair of 1925.

**Period:** 1928–1939 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Jazz Age ceramics, Bizarre ware, 1930s geometric tableware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Art Deco tableware, you MUST:

1. Form the piece in a sharp geometry: a cone, a step, a square or a triangle handle.
2. Paint in flat enamel colours: orange, yellow, red, green and black, with no shading.
3. Divide the surface with bands 3 to 10 mm wide, in bold stripes or in steps.
4. Paint a motif in a few strokes: a crocus, a sun ray, a tree, or a house, and repeat it.
5. Use a white or cream ground, and a thin clear glaze that gives a bright gloss.
6. Let the hand show: each stroke has a small variation, though the pattern is a fixed design.
7. Give each pattern a bold name and put it on the base with the maker mark.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Conical sugar shakers and cruets, plates with a square or stepped rim, angular jugs and tureens, and stacked bowls.
- **Profile:** A cone, a cube or a stepped profile; the handle is a flat triangle or a solid ring.
- **Foot and rim:** A flat, plain base; the rim is a sharp edge or a stepped edge, with a painted band.
- **Proportion:** Plate diameter 18 to 25 cm and wall 4 to 6 mm thick; the colour bands cover about two thirds of the surface.
- **Rules:**
  - Build the form from 2 or 3 simple solids, such as a cone on a disc.
  - Make the handle a flat triangle or a solid ring, with no curl.
  - Paint the colour in flat bands that follow the form.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Secondary:** bone-china
- **Avoid:** stoneware · terracotta · red-stoneware
- **Rule:** Use a white earthenware that takes a bright flat colour. Bone china is for the costly lines. Do not use a brown stoneware: the colour needs a white ground.

## Forming

- **Methods:** slip casting of angular forms · press moulding of plates · jiggering of rounds · hand painting with a brush in flat colour · banding with a wheel · lithographic transfer for the cheap lines
- **Joins:** Triangle handles are cast with the body or luted with slip; stacking lids sit on a rebated seat.
- **Details:** A small run of glaze at a band edge, a pattern name in the base, and a slight difference of stroke between pieces.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** lead-glaze · coloured-gloss
- **Decorations:** overglaze-enamel · hand-banding · decal · gilding
- **Pattern:** Crocus flowers, sunrays, orange trees, houses and diamond shapes in flat enamel colour, with broad bands and stripes.
- **Gloss:** Bright gloss, about 80 to 95 GU.
- **Wear:** The enamel can scratch; the glaze may craze in a fine net, and the colour stays strong.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** A white ground with orange, yellow, red, green and black in flat blocks; the contrast between the colours is high.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A coal or gas-fired bottle kiln; a biscuit firing, a glaze firing and a low enamel firing.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 1150 °C · dipping in glaze · glaze firing at about 1050 °C · painting in enamel · enamel firing at about 750 °C
- **Scale:** A painting shop of some hundreds of young women, who painted the same pattern to a fixed design.
- **Signs:** A flat enamel colour with a slight edge, a pattern name in the base, and a small run at the band.

### Sunburst (motif)

Rays from a disc or a half disc: a sign of light and of the new, on a facade, a gate, a mirror or a clock. Origin: architecture, Paris, 1925.

- Draw 12 to 36 rays from one centre; alternate long and short rays, or two finishes.
- Keep the centre disc at 15% to 30% of the full diameter.
- In a room, use one sunburst as the focal point of one wall.
