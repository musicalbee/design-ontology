# Are-bure-boke

> A photograph cannot give a clear and certain view of the world, so the picture shows the fragments that a moving eye catches.

A rough form of street photography from Japan: grain, blur and loss of focus on purpose. The picture shows a wandering glance, not a clear fact. The Provoke magazine of 1968 used the look. Nakahira and Moriyama had tried it before.

**Period:** From 1968 (the Provoke magazine) · **Era:** The small camera · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** Grainy, blurred, out of focus, Provoke style

Good for: album covers and music posters, fashion campaigns with a raw mood, photo books, image prompts for night cities.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs a clear record of an event or a face.
- The subject needs a clean, calm or premium look.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in Are-bure-boke, you MUST:

1. Shoot by hand and do not stop to focus; let blur and motion stay in the frame.
2. Use fast film at ISO 800 to 3200 and push it, so that the grain becomes coarse and visible.
3. Change the development on purpose: alter the exposure time, the temperature and the print, as Provoke did.
4. Print with high contrast, so that shadows fill and the mid-tones fall away.
5. Tilt the camera and cut the frame at odd places; keep frames that other photographers would discard.
6. Print on newsprint or with a coarse halftone screen, and accept the loss of detail.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Sharp, clear pictures that state one fact.
- **NEVER:** Tripod, level horizon and careful framing.
- **NEVER:** Fine grain and smooth tonal range.
- **NEVER:** Polished retouching of grain or blur.
- **NEVER:** A caption or an argument that explains the picture.
- **NEVER:** Posed people who look at the lens.

## Composition

- **Framing:** Tilted, cropped hard and often unbalanced; the edge cuts objects at random.
- **Viewpoint:** Handheld at walking pace, close to the subject and often at a slant.
- **Geometry:** Diagonals and smears of motion; shapes break into dark and light patches.
- **Subject placement:** Off centre or half out of the frame; the subject is partly lost in shadow or blur.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| framing | Tilt the horizon 5 to 20 degrees and cut the main subject at the frame edge. |
| contrast | Push the print to a shadow-to-highlight difference of 6 stops or more, with no mid-tone detail in the dark half. |
| balance | Leave the frame unbalanced: put 70% of the visual weight on one side. |
| focal-point | Give no sharp focal point; one patch of light holds the eye for a moment. |

## Light

- **Quality:** Harsh and found: street lamps, flash, shop windows and hard sun.
- **Direction:** Any direction; backlight and lamps in the frame give flare and burnt highlights.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 8:1 or more (3 stops or more)
- **Colour temperature:** 2800–5500K; do not correct mixed light
- **Time of day:** Night and dusk, or hard midday sun

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** Small handheld camera; 35 mm film, or a compact digital camera
- **Focal length:** 24mm to 35mm
- **Aperture:** f/2.8 to f/8, not set with care
- **Shutter:** 1/15s to 1/250s; slow speeds let motion blur show
- **Film speed:** ISO 800–3200, pushed
- **Focus:** None set; shoot by feel and let the focus miss.
- **Support:** Handheld; no tripod
- **Equipment:** compact or 35 mm camera · wide lens · fast black and white film

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Black and white with heavy grain and crushed blacks.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Convert to monochrome with a neutral mix (R 33, G 33, B 33).
  - Set the black point at 8% and the white point at 92%.
  - Apply +60% midtone contrast with a steep S-curve.
  - Add film grain, amount 60%, size 60%, as for fast film pushed two stops.
  - Add a slight blur of 2 to 6 pixels to the moving parts of the frame.
  - Do not crop to straighten; keep the tilt.
- **Grain:** Fast film at EI 1600 or higher: coarse, clumped and visible at A5

## Subject & staging

- **Subjects:** night streets · passers-by in motion · shop windows · car interiors · signs and posters · a nude in a hotel room
- **Staging:** None. The photographer walks and shoots without a plan.
- **Direction:** Nobody poses; people pass through the frame and blur.

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:**
  - Push the film by two stops, or change the developer time and temperature.
  - Make a contact sheet and pick frames that other people would discard.
  - Print on hard paper, or screen the picture with a coarse halftone.
  - Print to the edge of the page with no margin, or place two pictures side by side.
- **Output:** A dark, grainy print or a magazine page with halftone, in a square or 2:3 format.

### Halftone (process)

Tone printed as dots of different size or spacing; at a distance the dots merge into grey or colour. Origin: print, New York, 1879.

- Set the screen at 10 to 30 lines per cm for a print grain, or enlarge the dots to 4 to 8mm for a Pop effect.
- Turn each colour screen to its own angle: cyan 15°, magenta 75°, yellow 0°, black 45°.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Provoke** (collective, Japan, 1968–1970), shaped the style. A Japanese group of photographers, a critic and a writer. It made a small magazine of three issues. Its pictures were grainy, blurred and out of focus. The group worked against the realism of Magnum. Sources date its end to 1969 or to 1970.
