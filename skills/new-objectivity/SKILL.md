# New Objectivity photography

> The lens is exact and unsentimental, so the honest picture shows the structure of a thing at close range and in full detail.

Sharp, close, frontal pictures of plants, tools, faces and machines, made in Germany between the wars. The camera sees more than the eye, and it names things without comment.

**Period:** 1920s–1930s · **Era:** The small camera · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** Neue Sachlichkeit photography, Neues Sehen

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "new objectivity", "neue sachlichkeit", "blossfeldt", "renger-patzsch", "august sander", "specimen photograph", "typology photograph".

Good for: product catalogues with a scientific feel, botanical and industrial plates, portrait series by trade, image prompts for specimen grids.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs warmth, mood or a story in one frame.
- The subject is a moving crowd.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in New Objectivity photography, you MUST:

1. Fill the frame with one object, shot at 0.3 to 1m, so that its structure becomes the pattern.
2. Use a 135mm to 210mm lens on a 9×12cm or 13×18cm plate, stopped down to f/32 for full sharpness.
3. Light from one side in open shade or a window, with a key-to-fill ratio of 2:1.
4. Place the subject frontal and centred, or in strict rows, with a neutral grey or white ground.
5. Print on glossy paper at grade 2 and show the whole tonal scale without toning.
6. Repeat the same view for a whole series of 20 to 100 plates, so that the differences become the content.

## Guardrails

## Composition

- **Framing:** Tight and frontal; the object touches or leaves the frame edges.
- **Viewpoint:** Level with the subject, 0.3 to 1m away, camera square to the plane.
- **Geometry:** Rows, spirals and repeats; the plant or tool forms its own grid.
- **Subject placement:** Centred, with a plain ground that takes 10–20% of the frame.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| focal-point | Let 1 object fill 80–95% of the frame; nothing else takes more than 5%. |
| symmetry-axis | Put the axis of the plant or tool at 0.5 of the width, within 0.03. |
| rhythm | Repeat a leaf, rib or tooth at a constant pitch, with 8 to 20 repeats across the frame. |
| negative-space | Keep 10–20% of the frame as plain grey or white ground. |
| unity-variety | Show 20 to 100 plates of one view and one light, and change only the specimen. |

## Light

- **Quality:** Soft, even and directional: open shade or a north window.
- **Direction:** From one side, 60° to the lens axis, with a white card on the other side.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 2:1 (1 stop)
- **Colour temperature:** 5500K (daylight)
- **Time of day:** Midday, in open shade

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** Plate or sheet film, 9×12cm to 13×18cm; digital medium format as a modern equivalent
- **Focal length:** 135mm to 210mm (large format)
- **Aperture:** f/32 to f/45
- **Shutter:** 1/2s to 2s
- **Film speed:** ISO 25 to 50 (orthochromatic plate)
- **Focus:** Focus on the nearest edge of the structure and tilt nothing; the depth of field covers 0.3m or more.
- **Support:** Heavy tripod
- **Equipment:** view camera · lens hood · white reflector card · focusing cloth

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Neutral black and white with a full tonal scale, no toning and no grain.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Convert to monochrome with a neutral mix (R 30, G 59, B 11).
  - Set the black point at 2% and the white point at 97%.
  - Apply +10% midtone contrast with a gentle S-curve.
  - Sharpen at radius 0.8px, amount 80%, to show the fine structure.
  - Add no grain and no vignette.
  - Do not crop; keep the edge of the plate.
- **Grain:** None: a fine-grain plate, sharp at 10× enlargement

## Subject & staging

- **Subjects:** plants and seed heads · machine parts · tools · workers' faces · portraits of trades · industrial towers
- **Staging:** Minimal. The object stands on a plain ground; sitters face the lens and hold the pose for 1 to 2s.
- **Direction:** Say nothing about meaning. Ask the sitter to look at the lens with a neutral face.

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:** Develop in a fine-grain developer, for a gamma of 0.7. · Contact print or enlarge to grade 2 on glossy paper. · Order the plates in a sequence by form. · Print the series at one size.
- **Output:** A sharp glossy print of one view, shown with its siblings as a series or a book plate.

## Techniques

### Serial repetition (process)

One motif or one frame, repeated with small changes in a set of works, so that the viewer reads the change and not the object. Origin: painting, Paris, 1891.

- Fix the motif, the format and the viewpoint; change one variable only, such as the hour, the colour or the stripe width.
- Make at least 5 works in the set and show them in one row at one height.
- Keep the spacing between works equal to a fixed unit, such as 10 cm or one panel width.
- Remove the hand: use a mechanical edge, a stencil or a plain lens.
