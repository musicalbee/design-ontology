# Bauhaus modern tableware

> A service is a tool for the table: it is a few simple solids, in white, that fit one another and give good use.

Plain white porcelain made for a factory, in simple solid forms. The designers drop the ornament and the gold, and shape a service that stacks, pours well and costs little to make.

**Period:** 1930–1960 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Functional porcelain, Neue Sachlichkeit tableware, Modernist white porcelain service

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Bauhaus modern tableware, you MUST:

1. Build each piece from a few simple solids: a cylinder, a sphere, a flat disc.
2. Keep the surface white and undecorated, and let the glaze and the form carry the look.
3. Make the pieces stack and nest, so that a service takes little room in a cupboard.
4. Set the handle and the spout by use: the hand fits the handle, and the lip does not drip.
5. Reduce the service to 20 to 30 shapes that serve all meals.
6. Cast the piece in a mould so that each one is the same as the next.
7. Print or band only a single thin line in a colour when the buyer asks for it.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Flat plates with a narrow rim, straight-sided bowls, cylinder tureens, and stacking sauceboats and egg cups.
- **Profile:** A flat floor and a short, straight rim; each piece is a simple solid with a sharp, clean edge.
- **Foot and rim:** A flat base with a small foot ring or no ring; the rim is a plain edge about 10 mm wide.
- **Proportion:** Plate diameter 20 to 26 cm and wall 2.5 to 3.5 mm thick; the height of a bowl equals half its width.
- **Rules:**
  - Use one outline for the whole service, in circle and straight line.
  - Make the pieces nest and stack in columns.
  - Keep the rim plain and narrow, with no moulded border.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** european-hard-paste
- **Secondary:** glass
- **Avoid:** earthenware · creamware · bone-china
- **Rule:** Use a hard-paste porcelain that casts well and fires white and dense. Do not use earthenware: the service has to be hard and thin.

## Forming

- **Methods:** slip casting in plaster moulds · jiggering of plates · turning the model in plaster · fettling of seams · glaze dipping
- **Joins:** Handles and spouts are cast with the body or luted with slip, and fettled to a clean line.
- **Details:** A clean mould seam that the worker removes, a plain maker mark in the base, and an even white glaze.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** clear-feldspathic · biscuit
- **Decorations:** undecorated · hand-banding
- **Pattern:** No pattern; at most a thin line or band in one colour on the rim.
- **Gloss:** Bright to satin gloss, about 70 to 90 GU, on a pure white ground.
- **Wear:** The glaze stays hard and shows a fine mark of cutlery; there is no gold or enamel to wear.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A gas- or coal-fired tunnel kiln with saggars; a biscuit firing and a glaze firing at high heat.
- **Atmosphere:** reduction
- **Processes:** slip casting and fettling · biscuit firing at about 900 °C · dipping in glaze · glaze firing at about 1400 °C in reduction
- **Scale:** A state factory that made services by the tens of thousands, from one set of master moulds.
- **Signs:** A faint mould seam, a factory mark in the base, and the same white on every piece.

### Primary shapes (motif)

The circle, the square and the triangle in flat colour, used as the whole picture or as a sign. Origin: painting, Petrograd, Russia, 1915.

- Use 1 to 3 shapes at large size; let one shape fill 30% to 60% of the frame.
- Fill each shape with one flat colour; no gradient and no outline.
- Put the shapes on a plain ground with 30% or more empty space.
