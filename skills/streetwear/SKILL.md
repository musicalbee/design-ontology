# Streetwear

> Dress comes from the street and the club: the wearer joins a group by a plain, comfortable garment that carries a mark.

The casual dress that grew from surf, skate, hip-hop and sport clubs from 1980. It has the graphic T-shirt, the hoodie, the baggy jean, the nylon trouser and the cap, sold in small drops.

**Period:** 1980–present · **Era:** Power and subculture · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Street style, Skate and surf wear, Hype wear, Urban casual

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Streetwear, you MUST:

1. Cut the T-shirt and the hoodie boxy and 5 to 10 cm wider than the body.
2. Print a mark, a name or a graphic on the chest in one to four flat colours by screen print.
3. Choose heavy cotton jersey of 200 to 300 g/m² and brushed-back loopback terry for the hoodie.
4. Cut the jean or the trouser wide and straight, and let the hem break on the shoe.
5. Add one sport or work element: a nylon panel, a zip pocket or a drawcord.
6. Release the piece in a small run, so that few people own it.
7. Pair the garment with a cap and a sneaker.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A loose, boxy rectangle on top with a wide, straight leg, finished by a cap and a thick-soled shoe.
- **Line:** A dropped shoulder, a hem at the hip, and a trouser hem that rests on the shoe.
- **Fit:** Loose and comfortable; the garment hangs free of the body and is easy to move in.
- **Proportion:** T-shirt 5 to 10 cm wider than the chest. Hoodie hem at the hip. Jean leg 22 to 26 cm at the hem. Cap crown 10 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Drop the shoulder seam 4 to 8 cm below the natural line.
  - Keep the hoodie hem and cuff in a rib knit.
  - Cut the jean or trouser wide and full from the hip.
  - Place the mark on the chest or the back, at 10 to 25 cm in width.
  - Add a cap or a beanie and a sneaker.

### Screen printing (process)

Ink pushed through a fine mesh with a squeegee; a stencil on the mesh blocks the parts that do not print. Origin: print, Manchester, 1907.

- Stretch a silk or polyester mesh of 40–120 threads a centimetre on a frame.
- Make the stencil by hand cut film or by a photo emulsion exposed through a film positive.
- Pull thick, opaque ink across the screen with a rubber squeegee in one even stroke.
- Print each colour from its own screen and let each layer dry before the next.
