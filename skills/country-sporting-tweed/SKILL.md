# Country sporting tweed

> Dress for the weather, and put the sport first. A thick wool twill sheds rain, hides dirt and blends with heather, so it does the work of a coat. Age makes it better.

The rough wool dress of the landowner in the field. A loose tweed jacket, breeches or plus-fours, a knitted jumper and heavy boots, cut for the hill, the moor and the river.

**Period:** 1860–1939 · **Era:** Victorian dress · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Highland sporting dress, Estate tweed, Shooting and fishing dress

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Country sporting tweed, you MUST:

1. Cut the jacket 5 to 8 cm full at the chest, so that the arm can lift a gun.
2. Pleat the back from the yoke to give 6 cm of play at the shoulder blade.
3. Set the knee of the breeches 10 cm below the knee joint for a plus-four, to give room for the stride.
4. Choose a cloth of 450 to 650 g per square metre that sheds a day of rain.
5. Match the check to the ground: a dark, small check for the wood, a pale, bold check for the open hill.
6. Add a patch pocket and a flap on each side; use leather at the elbow and cuff.
7. Use leather or horn buttons that hold under strain.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A boxy, easy jacket with a high button stance. A belted or pleated back, over breeches that end below the knee and a long sock.
- **Line:** A natural shoulder, a high collar that turns up against the wind, and a hem at mid-seat.
- **Fit:** Cut loose in the body and the arm, so that the wearer can lift a gun or cast a line. Cut close at the cuff and the knee.
- **Proportion:** Jacket length to mid-seat; breeches end 10 cm below the knee; roughly 1 to 1.4 chest to hip.
- **Rules:**
  - Cut the jacket full across the back and give it a box pleat or an action pleat.
  - Raise the collar so that it closes at the neck against rain.
  - Gather the trouser at the knee in a buckled band, over a patterned sock.
  - Put a deep game pocket inside the skirt of the jacket.
  - Wear a tweed cap or a deerstalker with the suit, in the same cloth.

## Techniques

### Tweed weaving (process)

A rough woollen twill, woven from yarn that is dyed in the wool. A check or a heather mix that comes from the fleck of colours. Origin: fashion, Scottish Borders, c. 1830.

- Dye the wool in the fleece in several heather colours and blend the colours in the card.
- Spin a coarse woollen yarn and weave it in a 2/2 twill on a loom.
- Full and finish the cloth in water, so that it shrinks, thickens and sheds rain.

### Wax proofing (process)

A dressing of oil or wax worked into a dense cloth so that it sheds water; the owner renews it each year. Origin: fashion, North Sea coasts, c. 1800.

- Warm the wax blend until it is liquid.
- Work the wax into the cloth by hand or by machine, and press it into the seams.
- Let the cloth cure, and renew the wax when water no longer beads on the face.
