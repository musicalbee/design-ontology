# Liuli glazed roof tile

> A roof tile is a sign of rank: its colour, its ridge figures and its curved line tell the viewer which hall is for the emperor.

Glazed roof tiles in yellow, green and blue that the imperial kilns made for the palaces and temples of Beijing. Rank and meaning decide the colour: imperial yellow covers the main halls of the Forbidden City.

**Period:** 1420–1911 · **Era:** Blue and white · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Chinese glazed roof tile, Imperial yellow tile roof, Liuli

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Liuli glazed roof tile, you MUST:

1. Glaze the main halls in yellow, the princes' houses in green and the Temple of Heaven in blue.
2. Cover the roof with half-round tiles and flat tiles in rows, and finish the eave with a round disc tile.
3. Place a row of glazed animals on each hip ridge; use up to 9 figures for the highest hall.
4. Mould each ridge piece, finial and dragon in a mould, and glaze it in the colour of the roof.
5. Curve the eaves up at the corner, so that the tile line follows a soft S shape.
6. Press the tile from a fine, pure clay and fire it twice: once bare, once with the lead glaze.
7. Build a glazed screen wall with moulded relief tiles, as in the Nine-Dragon Wall.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Half-round cover tiles and flat pan tiles, eave discs with a dragon or a flower, ridge pieces, ridge-end beasts and moulded relief tiles for wall panels.
- **Profile:** A long curved tile with a gentle taper; the pan tile is a shallow arc and the cover tile a half cylinder that sits over the joint.
- **Foot and rim:** The eave tile ends in a round disc or a drop-shaped apron with a moulded face; the ridge ends in a large beast with a back hole for the fixing.
- **Proportion:** A pan tile is about 40 to 60 cm long and 2 to 3 cm thick; a main ridge end for a great hall stands about 3 m high.
- **Rules:** Use one glaze colour on one hall. · Put the same animals along each hip in a set order. · Mould the ornament; do not carve it after the glaze. · Seal the joint of the tiles with a cover tile.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Secondary:** fired-brick · terracotta
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · stoneware · parian · jasperware
- **Rule:** Use a fine, pale clay that stays pure of iron and grit, so that the lead glaze keeps a clean yellow. The tile must be hard, but it must stay porous.

## Forming

- **Methods:** pressing into plaster and wooden moulds · wire cutting of the half-round tile · hand modelling of ridge beasts · slow drying under cover
- **Joins:** Tiles lap over each other and sit in a bed of lime mortar on the roof; ridge pieces are stacked on an iron rod and fixed with mortar.
- **Details:** A small hole for the nail on each eave disc, thick glaze on the underside of the curve, and a dark body edge on the cut face.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** lead-glaze
- **Decorations:** relief-moulding · undecorated · tile-mosaic
- **Pattern:** Plain yellow, green or blue roofs, with a row of glazed dragons, phoenixes and lions on the ridge and tile reliefs of dragons on the screen walls.
- **Gloss:** High gloss, about 80 to 95 GU; the lead glaze is thick, bright and a little wavy on the half-round tile.
- **Wear:** The glaze keeps its colour for centuries; at an edge it crazes and shows the pale body; ridge beasts lose a small horn or tail.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Imperial yellow, jade green and peacock blue as flat colour fields; the colour marks rank, and the white-grey wall and red column below frame it.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A brick kiln that burns wood or coal; the tile sits in saggars or in stacked rows, with a bisque firing first and a glaze firing second.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:**
  - pressing and drying of the tile
  - a bisque firing at about 1000 °C
  - dipping or brushing with lead glaze with a copper, iron or cobalt colour
  - a glaze firing at about 900 to 1000 °C
- **Scale:** Imperial kilns near Beijing made tiles for the Forbidden City, with more than 9,000 rooms; each roof used thousands of pieces.
- **Signs:** A maker stamp on the back of an eave tile, glaze drips at the lower edge, and a small gap in the colour where the tile rested in the kiln.

### Glazed roof tile (material)

A roof tile of fine clay with a coloured lead glaze. The glaze makes the roof bright, hard and watertight, and its colour shows the rank of the building. Origin: ceramics, China, c. 500.

- Press the tile from a fine, pale clay and fire it to a biscuit at about 1000 °C.
- Dip or brush the biscuit with a lead glaze that has copper, iron or cobalt in it.
- Fire the glaze at 900 to 1000 °C.
- Lay half-round cover tiles over flat pan tiles in a bed of lime mortar.
