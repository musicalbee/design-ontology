# Roman terra sigillata

> A fine slip, not a glaze, gives the red shine: the potter sorts the clay by particle size, paints the thin top layer on the pot, and fires it in clean air.

Red-gloss tableware of the Roman world. Potters at Arezzo press fine clay into stamped moulds, coat the pieces with a slip of very small particles, and fire them to a glossy coral red.

**Period:** 30 BC–AD 300 · **Era:** Ancient clay · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Arretine ware, Samian ware, Roman red-gloss tableware

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Roman terra sigillata, you MUST:

1. Settle the clay in water and keep only the finest top layer, with particles under 1 micron, for the slip.
2. Coat the leather-hard pot with 1 or 2 thin layers of slip and polish it with a soft cloth.
3. Press the bowl into a fired clay mould that carries the relief in reverse; the mould keeps the design sharp.
4. Stamp the maker name in a small cartouche in the well of the plate.
5. Give each form a fixed number in a catalogue, so that a buyer in any province can match the set.
6. Fire the ware in clean oxidising air at about 1000 °C, so that the iron in the slip turns red.
7. Stack the pieces in a large kiln in columns, with a ring of clay between them, to fire 10 000 or more at once.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Plain and moulded plates, platters, bowls with relief friezes, and small cups with a lipped rim.
- **Profile:** A plate has a flat floor and a low wall that rises at an angle; a moulded bowl has a rounded body with a frieze of figures and a plain rim band.
- **Foot and rim:** A low ring foot, turned sharp on the wheel; the rim is a plain band or a thin rolled lip, often with a groove.
- **Proportion:** Plate diameter 15 to 30 cm and wall 3 to 5 mm thick, which is thin for earthenware.
- **Rules:**
  - Keep the wall thin and even, so that the ware stacks and fires with little loss.
  - Divide a moulded bowl into one wide frieze and one narrow rim band.
  - Repeat the same form in 3 or 4 sizes, so that the set nests.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** earthenware
- **Secondary:** terracotta
- **Avoid:** porcelain · stoneware · creamware
- **Rule:** Use a fine, iron-rich clay that fires to a dense red body at 1000 °C. Do not use porcelain or stoneware: the style is low-fired and unglazed.

## Forming

- **Methods:** throwing plain plates on the wheel · press moulding bowls in a fired clay mould · turning the foot when leather-hard · stamping the maker name with a small die
- **Joins:** Handles and applied figures are luted with slip; a bowl comes out of its mould when the clay has dried a little.
- **Details:** A sharp foot ring, a circular maker stamp, and a slight seam where the frieze meets the rim band.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** terra-sigillata · bare-clay
- **Decorations:** relief-moulding · incised · undecorated · sprigging
- **Pattern:** Friezes of dancers, plants, hunters and mythic scenes in low relief, with stamped palmettes and rosettes; plain plates carry only a maker stamp.
- **Gloss:** Soft sheen to gloss, about 25 to 45 GU; the shine comes from the polished slip.
- **Wear:** The slip scratches and wears at the foot and rim, and the buff or orange body shows under the red.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** One coral-red slip on an orange body, with a darker red in the deep relief.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A large updraught kiln with a stack of ware in columns, fired with wood or charcoal.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** slip coating on leather-hard ware · polishing the slip · single firing in clean oxidising air · cooling in the closed kiln
- **Scale:** A workshop at Arezzo made thousands of pieces in one firing; the potteries of Gaul sent samian ware to the whole empire.
- **Signs:** A maker stamp in the well, a small ring of kiln-stack marks on the foot, and an even red from rim to base.

### Press moulding in a bowl mould (process)

Soft clay is pressed into a fired clay mould that carries a design in reverse. The bowl keeps a sharp relief when it comes out of the mould. Origin: ceramics, Athens, Greece, c. 250 BCE.

- Stamp or carve the design into a bowl-shaped mould of clay.
- Fire the mould hard.
- Press soft clay into the mould and let it dry a little.
- Lift out the bowl, then turn the rim and the foot on the wheel.
