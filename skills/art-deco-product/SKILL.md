# Art Deco product

> Luxury can be geometric: a rare material, a precise machine line and a bold pattern make an object that looks rich and modern.

Rich, geometric objects of the jazz age: stepped forms, fans and sunbursts in chrome, ebony, lacquer, mirror and Bakelite, made by hand and by machine for a city luxury.

**Period:** 1925–1940 · **Era:** Machine modern · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Art Deco object, Style Moderne object, Jazz Age object

Good for: lighting and hardware for a hotel or a cocktail bar, a brief for a deco reissue, a still life with mirror and chrome, hardware schedules for a period flat.

Do not use it when:

- The brief asks for a matt, raw or recycled finish.

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Art Deco product, you MUST:

1. Build each form from stepped tiers, a fan, a sunburst or a chevron; repeat it three to five times.
2. Use rare, dark wood such as Macassar ebony and rosewood with a high French polish.
3. Pair a dark ground with a bright metal: chrome, nickel or gilt brass on ebony, black glass or lacquer.
4. Cut glass and mirror in facets; etch or frost glass in a geometric pattern.
5. Keep the outline symmetrical about a vertical axis, with a strong base and a stepped top.
6. Use Bakelite and other new plastics for knobs and cases, moulded in a sharp, faceted form.
7. Set each pattern in a rhythm of 3 to 5 equal parts, and make each surface a mirror or a deep gloss.

## Form

- **Geometry:** Stepped pyramids, fans, sunbursts, chevrons and zigzags, built on a strict symmetrical axis.
- **Silhouette:** A stepped, symmetrical outline with a wide base and a narrow top, like a skyscraper in small scale.
- **Edges:** Crisp, faceted edges and bevels with a radius of 0 to 3 mm; a few large rounded corners of 20 to 50 mm on a case.
- **Proportion:** A sideboard is about 1.8 times wider than high; a table lamp is about 40 cm high, with a base and a shade of equal width.
- **Ornament:** Geometric inlay, fluting, sunburst and chevron; it covers 15 to 40% of the surface and repeats in 3 to 5 steps.
- **Rules:**
  - Each object is symmetrical about one vertical axis.
  - A stepped form has 3 to 5 tiers, and each tier is about 60 to 80% of the width of the tier below it.
  - A pattern repeats in equal parts: a fan has 5 to 9 rays.
  - A dark ground and a bright metal meet at a sharp line.
  - Surfaces are polished to a mirror or a deep gloss.

## Finish & texture

- **Finishes:** french-polished · polished · silvered · clear-gloss · gilded · acid-etched · moulded-gloss · lustre-pile
- **Textures:** mirror-smooth · figured-grain · fluted · etched · pressed-pattern · pile · cast-relief
- **Gloss:** 80–100 GU on wood, lacquer and metal
- **Patina:** The style wants a new, deep shine; a worn or dull surface loses the effect.

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** chrome
- **Section:** Rods and stems 10–20 mm, handles 12–25 mm, base discs 60–120 mm
- **Joints:** Fine, tight joints; a polished metal band or a flush mount hides each seam, and a screw head is flush or covered.
- **Handles:** A stepped bar, a ring or a faceted knob in chrome, ebony or Bakelite, with a section of 12 to 25 mm.
- **Switches and taps:** A faceted or stepped Bakelite push button; a tap with a ribbed, faceted or stepped lever in chrome.

## Palette

## Manufacture

- **Processes:** veneer and marquetry · chrome plating · mirror silvering and bevelling · Bakelite compression moulding · acid etching of glass
- **Scale:** Luxury workshops made single pieces and small series; large firms made the same forms in machine series for the shop and the home.
- **Signs:** Tight veneer matches in a mirror image, sharp bevels, a chrome band at each edge and a mould parting line that is polished away.

## Techniques

These techniques started in one domain and moved into this style. Apply the rules as they are written; the full record is in [`TECHNIQUES.md`](../TECHNIQUES.md).

### Sunburst (motif)

Rays from a disc or a half disc: a sign of light and of the new, on a facade, a gate, a mirror or a clock. Origin: architecture, Paris, 1925.

- Draw 12 to 36 rays from one centre; alternate long and short rays, or two finishes.
- Keep the centre disc at 15% to 30% of the full diameter.
- In a room, use one sunburst as the focal point of one wall.

## Organisations
