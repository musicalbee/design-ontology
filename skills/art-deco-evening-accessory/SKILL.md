# Art Deco evening accessory

> An evening accessory is a jewel that holds a powder compact, and its geometry shows modern taste.

The small, hard, bright accessories of evening dress between the wars. They include enamelled and engraved cases, the minaudière, beaded satin pouches and long kid gloves, in geometric forms of black, silver and a jewel colour.

**Period:** 1925–1939 · **Era:** Interwar modern · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Deco minaudière and evening bag, 1920s–30s vanity case

1. Cut the form in a clean geometry: a rectangle, a circle or a stepped edge.
2. Keep the case small, 12 to 20 cm wide, so that the hand holds it as a jewel.
3. Set a bold contrast: black enamel with silver, or jade green with gold.
4. Fit the inside with a mirror, a powder compact and a lipstick holder.
5. Repeat angular motifs in rows: a sunburst, a chevron or a stepped line.
6. Finish the clasp with a row of small stones or a bar of enamel.
7. Use a chain or a cord of 20 to 40 cm so that the case is carried in the hand or on the wrist.
8. Pair the case with long kid gloves and a satin dress.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A small rigid box or a flat pouch with a geometric outline and a bright clasp.
- **Line:** A straight top line, a stepped or rounded corner and a bold bar clasp.
- **Fit:** The case is held in the hand, tucked under the arm or hung from the wrist on a short chain.
- **Proportion:** Case 12–20 cm wide, 8–12 cm high and 2–4 cm deep; wrist chain 20–40 cm.
- **Rules:**
  - Divide the face of the case into bold panels, each a single colour or a single metal.
  - Set the clasp as the one point of detail, with stones or a contrast bar.
  - Keep the case flat enough to hold on the hip under a close-fitting dress.
  - Match the metal of the case to the jewellery of the evening.

### Vitreous enamelling (material)

A thin coat of glass that is fused to iron or steel at about 800 °C. It makes a hard, bright, washable surface that resists heat and rust. Origin: industrial design, Central Europe, c. 1850.

- Clean the metal and coat it with a ground layer of glass powder and water.
- Fire the part at 760 to 840 °C until the glass melts into one skin.
- Apply a top coat of 0.1 to 0.3 mm in white or a single colour and fire it again.
- Leave the edges rolled: the enamel chips at a sharp edge and shows the dark metal.
