# Ulm and Braun functionalism

> A product is a system: it is as little design as possible, it explains its use, and it fits with its neighbours on a common grid.

Systematic household products of the Ulm school and the Braun firm: grey and white cases of aluminium and plastic, built on a grid, with controls that explain the object.

**Period:** 1955–1995 · **Era:** Post-war modern · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Ulm model, Rams functionalism, Good design (Ulm school)

- The brief asks for curves, colour or fun.

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Ulm and Braun functionalism, you MUST:

1. Build each product on a grid; set all parts to a module of 5 or 10 mm.
2. Make the case a plain box with radius 1 to 4 mm, in white, grey or black.
3. Put the controls in a clear group, and mark each with a small, grey or white symbol.
4. Use aluminium, steel or matte plastic; no surface has a decorative texture.
5. Keep colour for one function only: a green, red or yellow key or a mark.
6. Make each object part of a family that shares size, grid and colour.
7. Draw for long use: choose a form that stays right after 10 years.
8. Test the object for use, noise and repair before the form is final.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Yearly changes of form that follow fashion.
- **NEVER:** Fake wood and fake chrome.
- **NEVER:** Round, soft pod forms and bubble shapes.
- **NEVER:** Surface pattern and engraved decoration.
- **NEVER:** Many colours with no function.
- **NEVER:** Hidden screws that make repair hard.

## Form

- **Geometry:** The rectangular box, the cylinder and the flat plane on a square grid; each edge lines up with the next.
- **Silhouette:** A flat, rectangular block with one clear front; a row of dials and keys inside a grid.
- **Edges:** A small radius of 1 to 4 mm on a plastic case; a sharp, clean arris on aluminium.
- **Proportion:** A shelf module is 36 cm wide and 22 or 32 cm deep; a radio body is about 4 times as wide as it is high.
- **Ornament:** None. The only decoration is the function: a printed scale, a dial ring or a key row.
- **Rules:** Every part is placed on a grid of 5 or 10 mm. · The case is a box or a cylinder with a radius of 1 to 4 mm. · The body has one main colour: white, light grey, or black. · Each key or dial has one job and one mark. · A colour accent covers less than 5% of the front.

- **Textures:** satin-smooth · brush-line · orange-peel · mirror-smooth · plain-weave · fine-grain

## Hardware & joints

- **Metal tone:** stainless
- **Section:** Rails and rods 8–20 mm; keys and dials of 10–30 mm diameter
- **Joints:** Screws and clips in a regular row; the case opens for repair and the fixings are visible.
- **Handles:** A recessed finger grip, a flat bar or a ribbed dial of black plastic.
- **Switches and taps:** A square rocker switch in white plastic; a rotary dial with a white dot and a grey scale.

### Modular grid (process)

A page or screen divided into equal columns and rows; each element fits a cell or a block of cells, so that the whole layout repeats one measure. Origin: graphic & ui design, Zurich, c. 1958.

- Divide the page into 4 to 12 equal columns with a gutter of 1/10 to 1/5 of a column width.
- Cut the page into rows of the same module height; align each image and text block to the lines.
- Set text on a baseline grid of one line height, and use 1 to 2 type sizes for each level.
- Scale an image by a whole number of modules.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Braun** (company, Frankfurt, 1921–now), made the style. A German maker of radios, record players and small appliances. Its design team under Dieter Rams made the calm, functional products that later shaped interface design.
- **Ulm School of Design** (school, Ulm, 1953–1968), taught the style. A school of design that taught system, grid and method. Its teachers worked with Braun and with corporate identity programs.
