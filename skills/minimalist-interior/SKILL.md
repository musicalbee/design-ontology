# Minimalist

> Remove every thing that is not necessary, so that space, light and material can be seen.

The room reduced to space, light and surface. White or pale stone walls, one floor material throughout, hidden storage and flush details leave only one or two pieces in view; daylight on a plain wall is the main event.

**Period:** 1990–present · **Era:** Global minimal · **Domain:** Interior · **Also called:** Minimalist interior, Minimal architecture interior, White minimalism

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "minimalist", "minimal interior", "white minimalism", "pawson", "less is more", "handleless", "clean lines", "empty room".

Good for: galleries and showrooms, luxury retail, spa bathrooms, hotel rooms, homes of collectors of one object.

Do not use it when:
- The users have many things and little storage space.
- The brief asks for warmth from pattern or collections.

## System directives

When planning, describing or rendering a room in Minimalist, you MUST:

1. Hide all storage behind flush, handleless doors that read as wall.
2. Use one floor material without thresholds through all rooms.
3. Show no more than 3 loose pieces of furniture in a room.
4. Use a palette of white, pale stone and one pale wood; no pattern.
5. Make the window the focal point: a large opening with a thin or hidden frame.
6. Detail every junction flush: shadow gaps in place of skirtings and architraves.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Visible cables, handles or switches in groups.
- **NEVER:** Decorative objects on open shelves.
- **NEVER:** Pattern or texture for decoration.
- **NEVER:** More than 3 colours in one room.
- **NEVER:** Curtains with folds and pelmets.
