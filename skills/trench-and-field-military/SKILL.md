# Trench and field military

> A uniform earns each strap, flap and pocket from a task in the mud: keep the rain out, carry the kit, and show rank.

The dress of the officer and the soldier in the field. A double-breasted gabardine trench coat with a storm flap and a belt, and a khaki cotton field jacket with four pockets.

**Period:** 1914–1945 · **Era:** Belle Époque and the Great War · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Trench coat, Officer's field dress, Khaki field dress

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Trench and field military, you MUST:

1. Cut the coat 10 to 15 cm below the knee, so that it covers the thigh in a crouch.
2. Double the front with a 10 cm overlap and two rows of six buttons.
3. Add a storm flap over the right shoulder, to throw rain from the front.
4. Add a gun flap over the chest; use a belt of the same cloth with a D-ring.
5. Use a rain cloth that sheds water and lets the body breathe.
6. Put a button-in lining in the coat so that the wearer can change it with the season.
7. Colour the cloth in khaki, stone or olive, the colours of dust and mud.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A long, double-breasted coat that falls to the knee, with a wide skirt, a belt at the waist. A collar that closes at the throat.
- **Line:** A wide, strapped shoulder, a belted waist and a straight hem at the knee.
- **Fit:** Roomy over a tunic and a greatcoat layer; the belt pulls the body in at the waist.
- **Proportion:** Hem 10 to 15 cm below the knee; roughly 1 to 1.2 waist to hip with the belt closed.
- **Rules:**
  - Double-breast the front with a deep lapel that closes at the throat.
  - Put a storm flap on the right shoulder and a gun flap on the chest.
  - Add a cuff strap and a buckle at each sleeve.
  - Use a self belt with a D-ring buckle at the waist.
  - Back the yoke with a second layer of cloth that sheds water.

## Construction

- **Methods:** steep cotton twill woven from proofed yarn · double-breasted front with a storm flap · button-in wool lining · strapped cuff and belt with a D-ring · taped and stitched seams for rain · metal eyelets under the arm for air
- **Closures:** Two rows of horn or leather buttons, a throat latch, a buckled belt with D-rings and a strap at each cuff.
- **Details:** A storm flap, a gun flap, shoulder epaulettes, two deep slash pockets and a vent at the back for a horse or a march.

## Surface & finish

- **Finishes:** matte · wax-proofed · napped · distressed
- **Pattern:** Plain khaki, with a check lining in the officer's coat; no print on the face.
- **Gloss:** Gabardine at 3–10 GU, a low sheen; rubberised cloth at 20–40 GU.
- **Wear:** The coat takes mud and creases at the elbow and the belt. The colour fades to a pale stone, and the wearer keeps it for years.

### Yarn proofing (process)

A rain cloth made from yarn that is proofed before it is woven, so that the cloth sheds water and the body can still breathe. Origin: fashion, Basingstoke, England, 1879.

- Proof the cotton or wool yarn with a water-repellent finish before the weave.
- Weave the yarn in a steep, tight twill.
- Dye the cloth in khaki or stone, and cut it into a coat.
