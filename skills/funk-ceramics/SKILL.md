# Funk ceramics

> Clay is a rude, funny material, so a sculptor can make a toilet, a typewriter or a bust of himself and treat high art as a joke.

Hand-built ceramic sculpture from California that turns toilets, typewriters, self-portraits and bad taste into loud, crude and funny figures. Bright low-fire glazes and drips keep the clay rude.

**Period:** 1961–1985 · **Era:** Modern ceramics · **Domain:** Ceramics · **Also called:** Funk art ceramics, California funk ceramics, Bay Area figurative ceramics

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting fired clay: tableware, teaware, kitchen and storage ware, vases and display vessels, figures, tiles, architectural ceramics and sanitary and technical ware in Funk ceramics, you MUST:

1. Build the figure by hand with coils and slabs, and leave the thumb and the tool marks.
2. Take the subject from daily life or from the artist's own face: a toilet, a typewriter, a pet, a bust.
3. Make the form lumpy, thick and a little wrong, with a big head and small hands.
4. Cover the clay with loud low-fire glazes and overglaze china paints, in hot pink, yellow and green.
5. Let the glaze run, drip and pool, and leave blisters and pinholes.
6. Add a joke in the title or in a small detail: a text, a pun or a crude word on the base.
7. Make the piece 30 to 120 cm tall, as a single piece or a small group on a plain base.
8. Fire the piece in several runs, and add paint and glaze in layers until the surface is rich.

## Form & profile

- **Shape:** Hand-built objects and figures: a toilet, a typewriter, a bust, a head with a tongue, a dog, a bottle, a hand with a cigarette.
- **Profile:** A lumpy, thick outline with a big head, small hands and a slumped body; the form leans, sags and bulges.
- **Foot and rim:** A thick, flat or uneven base that rests on a slab, or a plain block of clay; edges are rough and torn, with no turned foot.
- **Proportion:** Height 30 to 120 cm; the wall is 10 to 25 mm thick, and the head is about a third of the height for a bust.
- **Rules:** Build by hand and leave the thumb marks. · Pick a daily subject or a self-portrait. · Distort the form: swell the head, shrink the hands. · Use loud, mixed glaze and overglaze colours. · Put a joke in the title or the detail.

## Clay bodies

- **Primary:** stoneware · earthenware
- **Avoid:** porcelain · bone-china · parian · european-hard-paste · creamware
- **Rule:** Use a grogged stoneware or earthenware that stands up when it is hand-built in a large size. Keep the surface rough, and keep fine porcelain out.

## Forming

- **Methods:** coil building · slab building · pinching · hand modelling of details · assemblage of cast and found parts
- **Joins:** Parts are scored, wetted and pressed together by hand. Cracks and joins are left open and show as part of the form.
- **Details:** Thumb and tool marks, cracks at the joins, drips of glaze on the base, and small words or numbers cut into the clay.

## Glaze & decoration

- **Glazes:** coloured-gloss · lava-glaze · bare-clay · matte-glaze
- **Decorations:** overglaze-enamel · decal · underglaze-polychrome · incised
- **Pattern:** Glaze in pools and drips of hot pink, yellow and green, with a china-paint line and sometimes a decal or a text. Parts of the clay stay bare.
- **Gloss:** Gloss and satin, 40 to 95 GU; blistered and bubbled areas break the light.
- **Wear:** The low-fire glaze chips at the edges and crazes in a net. The overglaze colours are bright at first and rub with handling.

## Palette

- **Strategy:** Loud, clashing hot colours on a rough clay: hot pink, lemon yellow, acid green and turquoise, with black and a patch of bare clay.

## Firing

- **Kiln:** A university or studio electric kiln, with a biscuit firing, a low-fire glaze run and a china-paint run.
- **Atmosphere:** oxidation
- **Processes:** biscuit firing at about 1000 °C · glaze firing at 1000 to 1200 °C · overglaze china-paint firing at about 750 °C · repeat runs for layers of colour

- **Signs:** Thumb marks, drips and blisters of glaze, cracks at the joins and the signature or title cut into the base.

### Hand building (skill)

A vessel or a figure is built from coils, slabs or pinched lumps of clay, with no wheel. The maker joins the parts with slip and keeps the marks of the hand. Origin: ceramics, China and Japan, c. 18000 BCE.

- Roll coils or cut slabs of clay.
- Score the edges and press the parts together with slip.
- Smooth the joins, or leave them as part of the form.
- Dry the piece slowly, then fire it.
