# Pop foam furniture

> A soft foam can take any shape, so a piece of furniture can be a picture of an everyday thing at full size, and the joke is part of its use.

Seats, sofas and stands cast in soft polyurethane foam as one big object: lips, grass blades, a cactus. They are Pop art jokes that a person can sit on or hang a coat on.

**Period:** 1968–present · **Era:** Post-war modern · **Domain:** Industrial design · **Also called:** Gufram foam multiples, Polyurethane pop seating, Radical foam furniture

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting the objects that furnish a room: taps, switches, lamps, curtain rods, storage, seats, tables, kitchen objects, frames, vases and pots in Pop foam furniture, you MUST:

1. Cast each body as one soft block of foam, so that the object is its own picture.
2. Choose one familiar subject for each piece: a mouth, a plant, a stone, a classical capital.
3. Draw the subject at a size larger than life, so that the body is also a seat or a stand.
4. Give each piece one colour on 90% of its surface, in a flat and strong tone.
5. Keep the surface smooth with no joint, no stitch line and no applied trim.
6. Make the foam give a little under the hand, so that the piece reads as soft before anyone touches it.
7. Show the piece alone on a plain floor, like a sculpture, with a long shadow.

## Form

- **Geometry:** A large single volume copied from a known thing, such as a pair of lips, a clump of grass or a cactus, with the seat or the arm cut into it.
- **Silhouette:** The outline of the subject itself, with no frame or leg: it sits on the floor as a mass or on a short stem.
- **Edges:** All edges are soft with a radius of 30 mm or more; the foam body has no hard corner.
- **Proportion:** A seat is 70 to 110 cm wide and 60 to 90 cm high; a coat stand is about 170 cm high; a blade of grass is 8 to 12 cm thick.
- **Ornament:** None applied. The shape of the subject and one flat colour are the whole surface.
- **Rules:** Each piece reads as one named subject from 3 m away. · Each exterior corner has a radius of 30 mm or more. · One colour covers 90% or more of the surface. · No visible frame, leg or fixing on the main body. · The subject is larger than life by a factor of 2 or more.

### Polyurethane foam moulding (process)

Two liquids react in a closed mould and swell into a soft foam. The foam takes the shape of the mould and keeps it. Origin: industrial design, Leverkusen, Germany, 1937.

- Mix the polyol and the isocyanate with a blowing agent in the ratio of the supplier.
- Pour the mix into the mould and close it before the foam rises.
- Open the mould after the cure and pull out the foam body.
- Trim the flash at the parting line and coat the body with a flexible paint or a fabric cover.

## Organisations

The groups, studios, companies, schools, channels and backers that carried this style.

- **Gufram** (company, Piedmont, Italy, 1966–now), made the style. An Italian furniture maker. From 1966 it worked with artists and architects of Turin and made furniture in polyurethane foam. Its products helped to shape Radical design.
- **Studio 65** (collective, Turin, 1965–now), made the style. A group of architects, designers, poets and artists in Turin. It took an essential part in Italian Radical design and drew the Bocca sofa and the Capitello chair for Gufram.
