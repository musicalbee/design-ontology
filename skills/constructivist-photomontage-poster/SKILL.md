# Constructivist photomontage poster

> A poster made of real photographs, set on strong diagonals and printed in a few hard colours, speaks as a fact and not as a picture.

Cut-out photographs are pasted with drawn shapes and big type, then copied by photo-lithography or by halftone blocks in two to four flat inks. The diagonals, the red and black blocks and the sans-serif capitals come from the montage and the spot colour.

**Period:** 1923–1938 · **Era:** Industrial press · **Domain:** Print · **Also called:** Soviet photomontage poster, Конструктивистский фотомонтажный плакат

- The design uses no photographs and no strong diagonal.

## System directives

When specifying, prompting or art-directing a printed piece or its look on screen: a poster, a plate, a book page, a zine, a label or a print-look illustration in Constructivist photomontage poster, you MUST:

1. Cut each photograph to a hard edge and paste it on the layout; do not feather or blend it.
2. Set the main axis on a diagonal of 15–45° and let the figures lean along it.
3. Change the scale of the photographs sharply: a face as large as the sheet beside a crowd as small as a thumb.
4. Use 2–4 flat inks: red, black, cream paper and, in some sheets, ochre. Put the red in blocks and wedges.
5. Make the photographs with a coarse halftone of 50–80 lines an inch so that they print on cheap paper.
6. Set the slogan in heavy sans-serif capitals, in red or black, and give it a block of its own.
7. Use geometric shapes (circle, bar, wedge) as frames and as pointers, and leave no ornament.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Soft blends and drop shadows between photographs.
- **NEVER:** A centred, symmetrical layout with no diagonal.
- **NEVER:** Ornament, script type and floral borders.
- **NEVER:** A fine halftone that looks like a photograph in a magazine.
- **NEVER:** Pastel colours and gradients.

## Process

- **Family:** planographic
- **Matrix:** A photo-lithographic plate or a zinc halftone block, made from the pasted montage.
- **Press:** A flatbed lithographic press or a letterpress machine at a state printing house.
- **Image making:** The designer cuts photographs and paper shapes and pastes them on a board. A camera copies the montage through a screen, and the colour plates are drawn by hand.
- **Edition:** Thousands of sheets, all the same; the print is a machine copy of a hand-made master.

## Inks

- **Strategy:** Opaque spot inks: a red for the blocks, a black for the photographs and the type, and sometimes an ochre, on a cream sheet.
- **Ink count min:** 2
- **Ink count max:** 4

### Halftone (process)

Tone printed as dots of different size or spacing; at a distance the dots merge into grey or colour. Origin: print, New York, 1879.

- Set the screen at 10 to 30 lines per cm for a print grain, or enlarge the dots to 4 to 8mm for a Pop effect.
- Turn each colour screen to its own angle: cyan 15°, magenta 75°, yellow 0°, black 45°.

### Photomontage (montage)

Fragments of photographs, cut and joined on one sheet, so that the joins make a statement that no single photograph makes. Origin: graphic movements, Berlin, 1918.

- Join 3 to 12 photographic fragments on one ground and keep the cut edges hard and visible.
- Vary the scale of the fragments by 4:1 or more, so that the joins read as a claim and not as a scene.
- Set type as one more fragment, turned 0° to 30°, in the same stack of layers.
