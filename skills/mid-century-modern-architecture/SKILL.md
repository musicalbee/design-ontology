# Mid-century Modern

> Modern architecture can be warm: the free plan and the glass wall serve everyday life, and the house belongs to its garden.

A low house of post-and-beam timber or steel, with glass walls to the garden, a flat or low-pitched roof and a warm palette. It made the International Style domestic and suited it to climate, family and the car.

**Period:** 1940s–1960s · **Era:** Modern Movement · **Domain:** Architecture · **Also called:** Californian Modern, Case Study House style, Usonian-influenced modern

Good for: house and villa renders, real-estate staging, residential interiors, film and set design.

Do not use it when:

- The brief asks for density, high-rise or monumental scale.

## System directives

When designing, describing or rendering buildings and interiors in Mid-century Modern, you MUST:

1. Build on a post-and-beam frame with a module of 1.2m (4ft) or 1.5m, so that walls, doors and glass fit the grid.
2. Keep the house low: one storey, or two with a split level, with a ceiling height of 2.4 to 2.7m.
3. Open one long side to the garden with floor-to-ceiling glass or sliding doors, 50 to 80% of that wall.
4. Run the roof as a wide overhang, 0.9 to 1.5m, with a flat or low-pitch form of 1:12 to 3:12, so that the shade is part of the glass wall.
5. Use warm natural materials inside and outside: wood ceilings, brick or stone feature walls, and slate or terrazzo floors.
6. Place a solid core, the fireplace and the service block, at the centre, and let the living spaces wrap round it.

## Guardrails

- **NEVER:** Applied historical ornament or colonial trim.

- **NEVER:** Cold, all-white and all-glass surfaces with no timber or stone.
- **NEVER:** Heavy raw concrete at monumental scale.
- **NEVER:** Tall, narrow, vertical proportions.

## Massing & form

- **Form:** Low horizontal pavilions, L- or U-shaped around a court, often with a detached carport or a split-level section.
- **Proportion:** The building is 3 to 6 times longer than its height; ceiling height 2.4 to 2.7m, with a higher ceiling in the living room.
- **Roof:** Flat or low-pitched butterfly or shed roof with deep eaves of 0.9 to 1.5m; clerestory strips sit between roof planes.
- **Relation to ground:** The floor is at ground level or 0.3 to 0.6m above it, so that terraces and patios continue the room outdoors.

## Structure

- **System:** A timber post-and-beam frame or a light steel frame, with a concrete slab on grade.
- **Span and module:** Posts at 3.6 to 4.8m centres on a 1.2m module; glass panels, doors and ceiling boards are sized to the module.
- **Expression:** Exposed beams carry through the glass line to the eaves, so the frame is the one decoration of the house.

## Materials

- **Primary:** timber (redwood, cedar, walnut) · plate glass · brick or stone feature wall
- **Secondary:** painted steel · terrazzo and slate · plywood · cork
- **Finish:** Natural and satin: oiled wood, matte paint, honed stone. Surfaces show the grain but they are not rough.

## Facade

- **Composition:** A horizontal plane of roof, glass and solid wall; a closed, private street front and an open garden front.
- **Openings:** Floor-to-ceiling sliding glass on the garden side, high clerestories and a small number of windows to the street; the window-to-wall ratio is 15% at the street and 50 to 80% at the garden.
- **Solid to void:** Street side 85% solid; garden side 20–50% solid.

## Light & space

- **Daylight:** Light enters from the garden side and from clerestories; deep eaves cut the summer sun and let in the winter sun.
- **Spatial sequence:** A low, narrow entrance leads to an open living, dining and kitchen space centred on a fireplace; bedrooms are in a wing.
- **Interior character:** Wood ceilings, a brick or stone fireplace wall, built-in storage, and furniture by Eames, Nelson and Saarinen in simple, curved forms.

## Ornament

- **Attitude:** No applied ornament; the grain of the wood, the pattern of the structure and one piece of art carry the interest.
- **Details:** exposed post-and-beam joints · brick or stone fireplace wall · breeze-block screens · slatted wood screens · sunken conversation pit
