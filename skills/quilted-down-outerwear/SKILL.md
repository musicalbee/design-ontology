# Quilted down outerwear

> Warmth comes from trapped air. Sew the down into baffles that stop it from sliding, and the jacket gives the most heat for the least weight.

The jacket that holds a warm cloud of goose down in rows of stitched cells; Eddie Bauer made the first one in Seattle in 1936.

**Period:** 1936–now · **Era:** Interwar modern · **Domain:** Fashion · **Also called:** Down jacket, Puffer jacket, Baffled down coat

## System directives

When specifying, sourcing or prompting dress and what goes with it: garments, outdoor wear, underwear, shoes, accessories, jewellery, watches and the styling of cars in Quilted down outerwear, you MUST:

1. Sew the cells in rows 8 to 15 cm wide, so that the down cannot move to the bottom.
2. Fill each cell with 90 to 230 g of goose down, by the cold that the jacket must meet.
3. Choose a shell of 20 to 70 g per square metre that is tight enough to hold the down.
4. Bind the cuff and the hem with elastic, to seal the warm air in.
5. Cut the hood and the collar to close over the chin.
6. Add a zip with a storm flap that covers the draught.
7. Stitch each baffle through to a wall, not to the shell, to remove cold spots.

## Silhouette & form

- **Shape:** A round, padded jacket with horizontal or vertical rows of puffed cells, a high collar and a snug cuff.
- **Line:** A rounded, soft shoulder, a body that puffs in rows and a hem at the hip.
- **Fit:** Roomy over a jumper; the elastic cuff and hem keep the warm air in.
- **Proportion:** Jacket to the hip bone or to the thigh; roughly 1 to 1.2 chest to hip with the fill.
- **Rules:**
  - Divide the body into baffles in even rows.
  - Put a high collar and a fixed or a rolled hood on the jacket.
  - Close the cuff and the hem with elastic or a drawcord.
  - Cover the zip with a flap held by studs.
  - Keep a pocket at the chest and at the hip, lined with brushed cloth.

## Fabrics & materials

- **Primary:** ripstop-nylon · goose-down · poplin
- **Secondary:** synthetic-insulation · canvas · flannel · ballistic-nylon · shearling
- **Avoid:** tweed · velvet · serge · brocade · satin
- **Rule:** Choose a light, tight shell of cotton poplin or ripstop nylon, and fill it with goose down. The down gives the warmth, the shell holds it.

## Construction

- **Methods:** box-baffle sewing in even rows · goose down fill in each cell · tight ripstop or poplin shell · elastic cuff and hem · storm flap over the front zip · quilted stitch lines through two layers
- **Closures:** A full-length zip with a storm flap, stud snaps over the flap, and a drawcord at the hem.
- **Details:** A baffled body, a high collar, a hood with a drawcord, two hand pockets and a chest pocket.

- **Gloss:** A matte shell at 5–15 GU; a polished nylon shell at 40–80 GU.
- **Wear:** The loft falls with wet and with crush; the owner washes and tumbles the jacket to bring the down back.

### Box-baffle quilt (process, invented here)

A quilt with a wall of cloth between the two faces of each cell. Loose down stays in its place and gives even warmth. Origin: fashion, Seattle, Washington, 1936.

- Sew strips of cloth between the outer shell and the inner layer to form cells.
- Blow a measured weight of down into each cell.
- Close each cell and sew a bar tack at the end of each row.
