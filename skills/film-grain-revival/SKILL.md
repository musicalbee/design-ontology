# Film grain revival

> Grain and imperfection give a picture a body, so the viewer believes it was lived and not rendered.

Photographs with visible grain, soft halation and warm colour, made on 35mm film or graded to look like it. The look says that a real moment passed through a real roll.

**Period:** 2015–today · **Era:** Digital and the grade · **Domain:** Photography · **Also called:** Analogue look, Shot on film

## When to load this skill

Load it when the user asks for: "film grain", "shot on film", "portra", "halation", "analogue look", "35mm colour", "film emulation".

Good for: lifestyle and travel imagery, music and fashion portraits, wedding and event books, image prompts for a warm analogue look.

Do not use it when:
- The brief needs product accuracy or exact colour.
- The image must stay clean for small screens or print at high resolution.

## System directives

When making, briefing, prompting or grading photographs in Film grain revival, you MUST:

1. Shoot on 35mm colour negative film at ISO 400, such as Portra 400, Gold 200 or Ektar 100, and scan at 4000 dpi.
2. Use a 35mm or 50mm prime at f/2 to f/4, with a shutter of 1/125s or faster.
3. Expose 1 stop over for the negative; let highlights roll off with a soft shoulder.
4. Add halation around highlights by a red glow of 3 to 10px at 20% amount.
5. Allow light leaks, dust and an uneven frame edge in no more than 10% of frames.
6. Keep the grade warm at 5200–5600K with lifted blacks at 4–6%.

## Guardrails

Each line below breaks the style. Check your output against the list before you return it.

- **NEVER:** Fake grain at uniform size and no halation.
- **NEVER:** Heavy digital sharpening with film grain on top.
- **NEVER:** Pushed saturation and a teal-orange split.
- **NEVER:** Perfect clean skin.
- **NEVER:** Date stamps and borders over every frame.
- **NEVER:** Hyper-sharp digital lenses with a film preset.

## Composition

- **Framing:** Casual and honest; the frame holds a moment, not a layout.
- **Viewpoint:** Eye height, handheld, 1 to 4m from the subject.
- **Geometry:** Loose and natural; a horizon or a table edge shapes the frame.
- **Subject placement:** Off centre, with the face or object at a third and some of the surroundings left in.

**Principles**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| rule-of-thirds | Put the subject within 0.05 of 0.33 or 0.67 of the width. |
| framing | Leave a margin of 5–15% and allow the frame to cut a hand or a plate. |
| negative-space | Leave 30–50% of the frame as sky, wall or blur. |
| layering-depth | Use 2 to 3 planes at f/2.8: a soft foreground, a sharp subject and a blurred background. |
| contrast | Keep a scene range of 4 to 6 stops with lifted blacks at 4–6%. |

## Light

- **Quality:** Natural and warm: sun, window or lamp, with a soft roll-off in highlights.
- **Direction:** Side or backlit by low sun, with flare allowed.
- **Key to fill ratio:** 3:1 to 4:1 (1.5–2 stops)
- **Colour temperature:** 5200–5600K for daylight; 3000K for lamp
- **Time of day:** Golden hour or soft overcast

## Lens & camera

- **Format:** 35mm SLR or compact; digital with a film emulation as a hybrid
- **Focal length:** 35mm to 50mm
- **Aperture:** f/2 to f/4
- **Shutter:** 1/125s to 1/500s
- **Film speed:** ISO 400 (Portra 400); ISO 200 for Gold
- **Focus:** Manual or zone focus; accept some missed frames.
- **Support:** Handheld
- **Equipment:** 35mm film camera · 50mm f/1.8 lens · 3 rolls of ISO 400 colour negative · lab scan

## Colour & grade

- **Treatment:** Warm, soft colour with lifted blacks, creamy highlights and visible grain.
- **Grade recipe:**
  - Set the white balance at 5400K, tint +4.
  - Lift the black point to 5% and the white point to 96%.
  - Apply a soft S-curve with a gentle shoulder: highlights −15, shadows +15.
  - Shift greens toward yellow by −10 hue and drop blue saturation by −8%.
  - Add halation: red glow 6px, amount 20%, on highlights above 85%.
  - Add film grain, amount 35%, size 50%, roughness 55%.
- **Grain:** Portra 400: fine, even and organic at A4; Gold 200: tighter and warmer

## Subject & staging

- **Subjects:** friends at a table · portraits in window light · road trips and travel · parties and domestic life · summer beaches · street and city dusk
- **Staging:** Light. Choose the place and the light; let the moment happen, and shoot 1 roll per outing.
- **Direction:** Do not ask for poses; ask people to carry on and shoot 3 frames in a row.

## Post-processing

- **Workflow:** Send the roll to a lab and scan at 4000 dpi. · Correct dust and dirt by hand, and keep 90% of the grain. · Grade only in a global warm curve; avoid local brightening. · Add light leaks only where they were real.
- **Output:** A warm, grainy file at 4:5 or 3:2, for feeds, books or small prints.

### 35 mm camera (technology)

A small, quiet camera that takes 36 frames of 24 x 36 mm on cinema film, so that the photographer can work by hand in the street. Origin: photography, Wetzlar, Germany, 1925.

- Use a 28 to 50 mm lens and focus by scale, not through the finder.
- Shoot at 1/125 to 1/500 s, from the hand.
- Show the full frame; keep the 2:3 format.
