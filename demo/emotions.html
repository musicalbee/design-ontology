Emotions across the styles

The layer has 30 terms: 21 from the AESTHEMOS scale and 9 culture-bound words. Each term has a point on three axes: pleasure, arousal and dominance. A style carries a term through an edge. Each edge has evidence of one kind: measured (a study with people), stated (an open source says it) or inferred (read from the keywords of the style). A sourced edge ranks above an inferred one.

How to read this page

AESTHEMOS

The Aesthetic Emotions Scale. Schindler, Hosoya, Menninghaus and others made it in 2017 to measure the emotions that people feel about art, design, film, music and places. It has 21 subscales, and each subscale is one term on this page.

This layer puts the terms in four groups: prototypical (beauty, awe, being moved), epistemic (interest, insight), pleasing (joy, relaxation) and negative (ugliness, boredom, anger). The 9 culture-bound words, such as koselig and saudade, are not in the scale. Each one has its own source.

Schindler et al. 2017, PLOS ONE, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0178899

PAD: pleasure, arousal, dominance

Three axes for an emotional state. Mehrabian and Russell made the model in 1974 for rooms and surroundings. Each axis goes from −1 to +1:

Pleasure: unpleasant to pleasant. Arousal: calm and sleepy to excited and alert. Dominance: small and controlled by the place to in control of it.

Russell's circumplex (1980) puts the emotions on a circle of pleasure and arousal. Section 1 shows that plane, and a second plot shows dominance. The points are authored estimates, not measured values.

Mehrabian and Russell 1974, An Approach to Environmental Psychology, MIT Press · Russell 1980, doi:10.1037/h0077714

Munsell value and chroma

Two scales of the Munsell colour system. Value is lightness: 0 is ideal black and 10 is ideal white, in steps that look equal to the eye. Chroma is the strength of the colour: 0 is grey, and strong paint colours reach about 14. The third scale, hue, is not used by a cue on this page.

In the cues, a higher value means a lighter colour. Valdez and Mehrabian (1994) found that lighter colours are more pleasant and calmer, and that colours with more chroma are more arousing.

Munsell colour system, en.wikipedia.org · Valdez and Mehrabian 1994, J. Exp. Psych.: General 123(4)
