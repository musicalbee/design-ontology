Joseon buncheong

ceramics · 1400–1600 · also: Punch'ong ware, Korean slip-decorated stoneware, Mishima ware

Korean grey stoneware covered with white slip and a thin clear glaze. Potters stamp, carve, brush and paint the slip with bold and free patterns. The result is rustic, direct and lively.

Core thesis

A plain grey stoneware gains life when a white slip is brushed, stamped or carved on it by a free hand with no fear of the rough.

Design principles

  • Cover the grey body with a thick white slip, by dipping or by brush, before the glaze.
  • Stamp small repeated flower or circle marks into the leather-hard clay and fill them with white slip.
  • Scratch a design through the white slip to the dark body (sgraffito) for a bold line.
  • Brush the slip fast with a straw brush to leave rhythmic marks on the surface.
  • Paint iron-brown lines on the slip, for fish, flowers and leaves, in a loose manner.
  • Cover the piece with a thin clear or grey-green glaze and fire at 1180 to 1250 °C.
  • Keep the form heavy and strong: a flat bottle, a round jar or a wide bowl.

Anti-patterns

  • A thick opaque white glaze
  • Delicate fine painting with a hard border
  • A thin refined body that shows translucency
  • A symmetrical machine-like pattern
  • A bright glossy glaze with coloured enamel
