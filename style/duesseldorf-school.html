Düsseldorf School

photography · 1976–today · also: Becher school, Typological photography

Large, cool, frontal colour pictures of buildings, rooms, crowds and landscapes in fine detail. The method comes from the Bechers' typologies, and the scale comes from the museum wall.

Core thesis

Seen without drama and in great detail, an ordinary place becomes a map of the way we live.

Design principles

  • Use a 4×5 in or digital medium format camera, with a lens of 90mm to 150mm.
  • Photograph in flat grey light with a shadow ratio of 1.5:1 or less, and with no people posed.
  • Keep the camera level and square to the subject, so that verticals stay vertical.
  • Use a viewpoint raised by 3 to 20m, or a straight-on view at 1.5m, and hold it for the whole series.
  • Print large: 1.5m to 3m on the long edge, with sharpness from edge to edge.
  • Work in series of 20 or more, so that the type is the subject.

Anti-patterns

  • Dramatic skies and golden-hour light
  • Tilted horizons and wide-angle distortion
  • Saturation boosted past the scene
  • Picturesque framing that invites a story
  • One-off hero images with no series
  • Heavy vignette
