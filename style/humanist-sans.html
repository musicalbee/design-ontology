Humanist sans

typeface · 1916–present · also: Humanist lineale, Calligraphic sans, Gill style

A sans serif on the skeleton of the Renaissance roman: open apertures, varied widths, a little stroke modulation and an angled cut on the terminals. It reads well at small sizes and in poor conditions, and sounds warm.

Core thesis

A sans serif reads best when it keeps the proportions and the open forms of the written roman.

Design principles

  • Open the apertures of c, e, a and s: the gap of the c is at least 0.35 of its height.
  • Keep a slight stroke modulation of 1.1–1.6:1, with thinner joins where curves meet stems.
  • Cut the terminals at an angle, as a pen stroke ends.
  • Give the letters classical widths: a wide O and M, a narrow E, S and r.
  • Hold the x-height at 0.66–0.78 of the cap height.
  • Use a true italic with cursive forms, not a sloped roman.

Anti-patterns

  • Closed apertures with horizontal terminals
  • Pure geometric circles for o and a single-storey a
  • Even, uniform letter widths
  • A sloped roman as the italic
  • Very tight tracking in text sizes
