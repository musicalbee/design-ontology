Etching

print · 1513–1900 · also: Painter-etching, Radierung, Eau-forte, Hard-ground etching

The artist draws with a needle through a wax ground on a metal plate, and acid bites the lines. The line is free like a pen line. Callot made it exact; Rembrandt made it dark and alive, with tone from hatching and from ink left on the plate.

Core thesis

A needle that moves through wax as easily as a pen gives the artist a free line, and the acid gives it depth.

Design principles

  • Draw with a free, even line of the needle; the width comes from the time in the acid, not from the pressure of the hand.
  • Make the dark parts by more layers of hatching and by a longer bite: stop out the light lines and bite the dark ones again.
  • Use hatching in 1–4 directions; let the lines be loose and a little irregular.
  • Leave a film of ink on the plate (plate tone) for a soft dark where the image needs atmosphere.
  • Keep a wide margin and a clear plate mark.
  • Print in a warm black or a brown-black on a cream or a Japanese paper.

Anti-patterns

  • Mechanical hatching with an equal pitch everywhere
  • Lines that swell like a burin line
  • Flat colour or a halftone screen
  • A hard, black edge round every form
