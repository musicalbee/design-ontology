Food photography

photography · 1950s–today · also: Editorial food, Dark and moody food

Photographs of dishes made to look edible and honest. A window, a small set and a short lens give shape, steam and texture, and a few props tell where the meal sits.

Core thesis

Food is a sensation, so the picture shows texture, steam and light before it shows the plate.

Design principles

  • Use a 50mm to 105mm macro lens at f/2.8 to f/5.6, with a shallow plane that falls on the front edge of the dish.
  • Light from the side or behind with one window or a 60cm soft box at 90° to the lens axis; fill with a white card at 1 stop under.
  • Shoot at 30° to 45° above the plate for a plated dish, or at 90° for a flat lay.
  • Put 3 to 5 props in the frame, and cut the dish by 10–20% at an edge.
  • Keep a warm, natural colour at 5000–5600K; protect reds and greens.
  • Shoot each dish within 5 minutes of plating, and use a stand-in for the first test frames.

Anti-patterns

  • Flash on the front of the dish
  • Cold blue casts that kill appetite
  • Too many props that hide the dish
  • Dry food with no sheen or steam
  • Greasy retouch and over-sharpening
  • Plastic or inedible-looking fake food that is claimed as real
