Qin terracotta warriors

ceramics · 221–206 BC · also: Terracotta Army, Qin tomb figures, Bingmayong

Life-size grey terracotta soldiers, horses and officials that the workshops of the First Emperor made for his tomb. Each body is built from standard parts, and the head is finished by hand.

Core thesis

An emperor takes an army of clay into the grave, and a factory system of standard parts and hand finish makes each soldier a different man.

Design principles

  • Make each figure life-size: 175 to 190 cm for a soldier and up to 200 cm for a general.
  • Build the legs as solid columns and the torso as a hollow coil-built tube, then join the parts with slip.
  • Press the head in a two-piece mould and add ears, nose, hair and beard by hand to the leather-hard clay.
  • Give each face its own mouth, brow and moustache, so that no two heads match.
  • Put the rank in the dress: a general wears armour with a tied bow and a high cap, and an archer kneels.
  • Fire the grey body in reduction at 950 to 1050 °C, and leave it unglazed.
  • Paint the fired figure with lacquer and mineral pigments, in red, green, purple and blue.
  • Stamp or incise the name of the workshop and the master on the body, as a record of who made it.

Anti-patterns

  • A glossy lead glaze over the whole figure
  • One mould face that repeats on every soldier
  • A small tabletop figure at half size or less
  • A white porcelain body with fine enamel painting
  • Soft rounded armour with no plates or ties
  • A bright metallic gold finish on the figure
