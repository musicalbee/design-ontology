Scandinavian modern tableware

ceramics · 1950–1975 · also: Nordic modern ceramics, Finnish and Swedish studio-industry tableware, Stackable Nordic dinnerware

Plain, stackable tableware from the factories of Finland and Sweden. Artists on the staff of the factory design simple forms in a few shapes, glaze them in satin or matt colours, and add a quiet hand mark.

Core thesis

A few plain shapes that stack, a warm satin glaze and a quiet hand mark make tableware that is made in a factory but feels human.

Design principles

  • Design a service of a few shapes that are fit for every meal, and let them stack in a column.
  • Make each piece a plain solid, and leave the surface to the glaze.
  • Glaze in a satin or matt colour: white, sand, grey, olive or a deep blue.
  • Add a small relief of ribs or a thin incised line, and no more.
  • Let an artist, not a stylist, design the service, and put the name in the base.
  • Make the factory a studio: the artist works on the line and on one-off pieces.
  • Choose a wall 4 to 6 mm thick, which is strong enough for daily use and stacking.

Anti-patterns

  • A gilt rim and a floral pattern
  • A glossy bright colour in the manner of the American diner
  • A heavy, rustic wall with a rough glaze
  • A sharp, cold geometric shape with no warmth
