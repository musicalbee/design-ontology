Victorian boot

fashion · 1851–1914 · also: Button boot, Elastic-sided boot, Balmoral boot

The ankle boot of Victorian dress. It has a kid or cloth upper, a row of buttons or a lace front, an elastic side gusset, and a small stacked heel.

Core thesis

A Victorian woman covers the ankle, so the boot is the shoe of every day and the cloth upper shows the cut of the last.

Design principles

  • Cover the ankle: raise the shaft 12 to 20 cm above the heel.
  • Close the shaft with 8 to 14 small buttons, or with a lace front of 8 to 10 eyelets.
  • Set a 2 to 4 cm stacked heel under the waist of the sole.
  • Cut a narrow, slightly pointed toe on a last made for a left and a right foot.
  • Add a toe cap of patent leather or kid in a second colour for day wear.
  • Insert an elastic gusset of vulcanised rubber webbing in each side for a slip-on boot.
  • Stitch the sole to the upper by welt or by pegs; do not glue it.

Anti-patterns

  • A bare ankle in town dress
  • A flat, round-toed shoe with a single strap
  • A heel taller than 5 cm on a day boot
  • Rubber and foam soles on a dress boot
  • Visible plastic buttons or zips
