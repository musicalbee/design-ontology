Streamline Car

fashion · 1933–1950 · also: Streamliner, Streamline Moderne car, Aerodynamic car

The car as a teardrop. A smooth, rounded body with the mudguards merged into the sides, a sloped tail, a raked grille and bands of chrome that show speed.

Core thesis

A fast car looks like a drop of water: one smooth shell with no break in the line, from the grille to the tail.

Design principles

  • Round the nose, and set the grille at a rake of 10 to 20 degrees.
  • Merge the mudguards into the body, so that the flank has one unbroken curve.
  • Slope the tail in a long taper, or end it in a short fastback of about 30 degrees.
  • Hide the headlamps in the wings or behind the grille, and cover the rear wheel with a skirt.
  • Run two or three horizontal chrome bands along the body, to show speed.
  • Divide the windscreen in two and slope it back by 25 to 30 degrees.
  • Set the cabin with a dashboard of Bakelite and painted metal, and mohair cloth on the seats.

Anti-patterns

  • Upright radiators and separate mudguards
  • Square corners and a flat roof
  • A bright wire wheel with no cover
  • Tail fins on the rear wings
  • Hard, flat, unrounded panels
