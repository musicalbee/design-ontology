Underwear as Outerwear

fashion · 1984–1999 · also: Corsetry as dress, Lingerie dressing, Bustier and corset top

Designers of the 1980s and 1990s took the corset, the cone bra and the slip out of the wardrobe and put them on the street. The bodices were boned satin and wool, worn as tops, jackets and dresses.

Core thesis

What was hidden is now the dress: a corset, a bra or a slip shown in the open gives the body a built, public form.

Design principles

  • Cut the corset from historical patterns, with 10 to 20 boned panels and a busk or a laced back.
  • Boil down the idea to one garment: a boned bodice that stands as a top, a jacket or a dress.
  • Use outer cloth for the underwear shape: satin, wool tweed, leather or cotton drill.
  • Cone the cup in satin and set it at the bust, with a metal-cone bra for an extreme version.
  • Pair the corset with a plain skirt, a tailored jacket or trousers, so that the contrast shows.
  • Show the garment fully: a visible busk, a visible lace and a visible strap.
  • Add a slip dress in silk satin, 2 to 3 mm of seam and a bias cut.

Anti-patterns

  • A corset hidden under a plain dress
  • A soft, shapeless lingerie set
  • A plain cotton bra with no structure
  • A costume pastiche with no tailoring
  • A hem and a neck that cover the garment
