Rococo porcelain figures

ceramics · 1731–1770 · also: Meissen figures, Kändler figures, Commedia dell'arte porcelain, Dresden figures

Small hand-painted porcelain figures of lovers, shepherds, harlequins and monkeys, made for the dessert table of the court. They are white, glossy and light, in S-curves with lace, flowers and gilt.

Core thesis

A porcelain figure is a toy for the table of a prince: white, glossy and light, it turns a scene of court play into a small, bright piece of theatre.

Design principles

  • Model the figure in a twisting S-curve, with the head turned against the hip.
  • Keep the figure 10 to 30 cm high, with a base that is a small mound or a scrolled plinth.
  • Cast the figure from many small piece moulds, and join the parts with slip.
  • Dip real lace in slip and drape it on the dress, so that it fires to a stiff white lace.
  • Add flowers, small birds and leaves by hand to the base and the hair.
  • Paint the figure with overglaze enamels in clear, light colours, and fix them at 700 to 800 °C.
  • Add gilt scrolls on the base and the hem, and burnish them after the third firing.
  • Choose a scene from the stage or the court: Harlequin, Columbine, a gallant, a shepherdess, a monkey in a wig.

Anti-patterns

  • A stiff upright figure with both feet flat and the arms at the sides
  • A matt unglazed biscuit surface with no colour
  • A heavy earthenware body with a thick glaze
  • A mass of cold, dark and muddy colours
  • A figure larger than 50 cm
  • Flat decal faces that look printed
