Dong Ho folk woodblock painting

print · c.1600 (date not certain)–1950s · also: Tranh Đông Hồ, Tranh khắc gỗ dân gian Đông Hồ, Dong Ho painting

Villagers print a black outline block, then add flat colour blocks one by one on do paper that carries a coat of scallop-shell powder. The colours come from plants and minerals, so they are bright and flat.

Core thesis

A village can print a whole folk art from plant and mineral colour, one flat block at a time, on paper that shines with ground shell.

Design principles

  • Print the black outline block first. Keep the line coarse and 1–3 mm wide.
  • Add 4–5 flat colour blocks one by one, in order from light to dark, with no gradation.
  • Use opaque colour: sophora yellow, leaf green, red and shell white.
  • Use motifs for the New Year (Tet): a carp, a pig with chicks, a rooster and a wedding of rats.
  • Cut a caption in the block, or write it by hand, as a proverb or a wish.
  • Let the colour shift 1–2 mm from the outline. Keep the wood grain in the large fields.
  • Print on do paper that has a coat of scallop-shell powder, so the sheet sparkles.

Anti-patterns

  • Gradient shading or soft modelling
  • Fine hatching or a thin, delicate line
  • Halftone dots or a photograph
  • A glossy, smooth finish
  • A colour set that no plant or mineral gives
