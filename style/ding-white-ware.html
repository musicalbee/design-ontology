Ding white ware

ceramics · 960–1279 · also: Ding yao, Northern Song white porcelain, Ding ware

Thin ivory-white porcelain of the Northern Song court. The potters of Ding carve or mould fine plant designs under a clear glaze and fire the bowls rim down, so they bind the bare rim with metal.

Core thesis

A white body and a clear ivory glaze are enough: the beauty is the thin wall, the carved line and the quiet tone.

Design principles

  • Throw the bowl with a wall 2 to 3 mm thick and turn it on the outside to a fine profile.
  • Cut the design in the leather-hard body with a comb or a knife, in lines 1 to 2 mm wide.
  • Press a moulded design of peony, lotus or fish into the inside of a dish for a sharp relief.
  • Cover the piece in a clear glaze with a trace of iron, so that it fires to an ivory white.
  • Stack the bowl rim down on a ring support to fire many pieces in one kiln.
  • Leave the rim bare after firing and bind it with a band of silver or copper.
  • Allow a few tear-like runs of glaze on the outside; they are a mark of the kiln.

Anti-patterns

  • A blue-white glaze like the ware of Jingdezhen
  • Painted colour under or over the glaze
  • A thick wall with a heavy foot
  • Bright enamel or gilt panels across the surface
