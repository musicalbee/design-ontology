Pointillism

painting · 1884–1891 · also: Divisionism, Neo-Impressionism, Chromoluminarism

A picture made of small, separate dots of pure colour that the eye mixes at a distance. The scene is still and ordered, built by colour theory and not by blending.

Core thesis

Colour is a science: pure dots set side by side mix in the eye with more light than a mix on the palette, and a picture can be built by rule.

Design principles

  • Lay each dot as a separate touch of pure colour, 2–5mm across, with a ground that shows between them.
  • Mix by juxtaposition: set complementary pairs, such as orange and blue, next to each other, so that the eye fuses them.
  • Build each area from 3 elements: local colour, light (warm) and shadow (cool complement), plus a reflected colour.
  • Hold the scene in still, simple masses: a frieze of figures in profile and in full face, in a clear, ordered space.
  • Plan the picture on a grid or a golden-section armature, with strong verticals and horizontals.
  • Add a painted border in the colour that contrasts with the edge of the scene.
  • Use a matt, chalky surface so that the dots stay pure and bright.

Anti-patterns

  • Blended or glazed colour that hides the dot
  • Spontaneous, quick and uneven strokes
  • Dark, black or brown shadows
  • Soft, lost edges that run into the next form
  • Movement, blur and a fleeting moment
  • Large flat areas of one mixed colour
