Vintage Coachbuilt Car

fashion · 1919–1939 · also: Coachbuilt classic, Vintage luxury car, Pre-war grand car

The pre-war luxury car. A long bonnet on a bare chassis, with a body that a coachbuilder made to order in aluminium over wood. The cabin is leather, walnut and cloth.

Core thesis

A fine car is two works. The maker builds the chassis. The coachbuilder makes the body and the cabin to the taste of one buyer.

Design principles

  • Make the bonnet 40 to 50 percent of the car length, with the radiator upright at its front.
  • Build the body on an ash frame and clad it in hand-beaten aluminium panels.
  • Keep the mudguards separate from the body, in a long sweep from each wheel.
  • Fit the cabin with burr walnut veneer on the dashboard and the door caps.
  • Trim the seats in hide, and the rear compartment in wool cloth or silk damask.
  • Chrome or nickel plate the lamps, the radiator and the bumpers, and keep the plate bright.
  • Paint in two tones, one for the body and one for the mudguards or the roof, with a coach line between.

Anti-patterns

  • A short, stubby bonnet
  • Mudguards that merge into the body
  • A moulded plastic dashboard
  • Vinyl in place of hide
  • A mass-made body with no hand work
