Spaghetti western

film · 1964–1975 · also: Italian western, Euro-western, Leone western

A western shot in Spain and Italy on a wide 2.35:1 frame. A face fills the screen in an extreme close-up of the eyes, then the frame opens to a vast desert. Time stretches, the cut is slow, and the score is a part of the scene.

Core thesis

An extreme close-up and an extreme wide shot, with a long wait between them, make a duel into a ritual and the land into a stage.

Design principles

  • Shoot in Techniscope at 2.35:1 with a 2-perf frame and shoot in colour on a hot, dry location.
  • Cut between an extreme close-up of the eyes (a 75mm to 150mm lens) and an extreme wide shot (a 20mm to 28mm lens).
  • Hold the build to a duel for 30 to 90 s before the first shot is fired.
  • Use a stylised, sparse set with sun, dust, stubble and sweat on every face.
  • Light with hard sun at a high angle, and keep the faces in deep, dark eye shadow.
  • Dub the sound after the shoot: loud boots, spurs, wind, one shot and a long silence.
  • Write the score first for the key scenes, and let the music set the length of the take.

Anti-patterns

  • A tidy studio western set
  • A hero in a clean white hat
  • Fast cutting through a duel
  • A soft, polite long-shot only coverage
  • Natural, balanced mid-shot staging
  • A moral speech before the fight
