Four-shade handheld

game · 1989–1998 · also: Game Boy look, DMG green, Four-shade monochrome

A 160×144 screen with four shades of one olive-green hue. Every house and room is drawn from 8×8 tiles in light, two mid tones and dark, so form lives only in value and pattern.

Core thesis

Four values of one colour are enough for a world, if the pattern and the outline carry the form.

Design principles

  • Use exactly 4 shades: lightest for the ground, darkest for outlines.
  • Draw at 160×144 pixels on an 8×8 tile grid.
  • Separate materials with dither and hatch patterns, not with hue.
  • Keep large areas in the lightest shade so the screen stays readable.
  • Outline every object in the darkest shade, 1 px.
  • Make a character 16×16 px, with a top-down or side view.

Anti-patterns

  • Any second hue
  • Gradients or more than 4 values
  • Large dark areas that smear on the slow screen
  • Detail smaller than 1 px
  • Perspective views
