Art Deco product

industrial · 1925–1940 · also: Art Deco object, Style Moderne object, Jazz Age object

Rich, geometric objects of the jazz age: stepped forms, fans and sunbursts in chrome, ebony, lacquer, mirror and Bakelite, made by hand and by machine for a city luxury.

Core thesis

Luxury can be geometric: a rare material, a precise machine line and a bold pattern make an object that looks rich and modern.

Design principles

  • Build each form from stepped tiers, a fan, a sunburst or a chevron; repeat it three to five times.
  • Use rare, dark wood such as Macassar ebony and rosewood with a high French polish.
  • Pair a dark ground with a bright metal: chrome, nickel or gilt brass on ebony, black glass or lacquer.
  • Cut glass and mirror in facets; etch or frost glass in a geometric pattern.
  • Keep the outline symmetrical about a vertical axis, with a strong base and a stepped top.
  • Use Bakelite and other new plastics for knobs and cases, moulded in a sharp, faceted form.
  • Set each pattern in a rhythm of 3 to 5 equal parts, and make each surface a mirror or a deep gloss.

Anti-patterns

  • Soft, curved plant motifs
  • Rough, hand-hewn timber and visible tool marks
  • Plain white, bare steel with no pattern
  • Rattan, wicker and natural fibre
  • Faded, distressed surfaces
  • Plastic that looks cheap, in a toy colour
