Yuan and Ming blue-and-white porcelain

ceramics · 1330–1644 · also: Jingdezhen blue and white, Qinghua porcelain, Chinese blue-and-white

White porcelain painted in cobalt blue under a clear glaze. Jingdezhen potters made it in the Yuan dynasty and refined it for the Ming court into the best-known ceramic of the world.

Core thesis

Cobalt blue painted straight on white porcelain and sealed under a clear glaze gives a lasting picture that cannot rub or fade.

Design principles

  • Paint cobalt oxide on the dry, unfired body with a soft brush, then dip in a clear glaze.
  • Fire once at 1280 to 1330 °C in a strong flame, so that the porcelain turns white and dense.
  • Divide the surface into bands: a main field, a shoulder band, and a foot border of lappets or waves.
  • In the Yuan style, fill the field with dense bands; in the Ming style, leave more white ground.
  • Draw dragons, lotus scrolls, phoenixes, flowers and landscapes in outline and wash.
  • Use a deep blue with dark spots in the early work, and a clean soft blue in the later Ming work.
  • Write the reign mark in six characters in a ring or a square under the base.

Anti-patterns

  • Brown or red earthenware showing at the foot
  • Bright overglaze enamels as the main colour
  • A thick opaque white tin glaze
  • A grey or cloudy body from a weak firing
  • Transfer-printed pattern with identical repeats
