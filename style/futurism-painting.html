Futurism

painting · 1909–1918 · also: Italian Futurism, Dynamism

Pictures of speed, noise and the machine city. Forms repeat and break into lines of force, so that a dog, a train or a crowd seems to move through the picture.

Core thesis

Modern life is speed and energy, so a picture should show movement itself: the form is cut and repeated in time and fused with its setting.

Design principles

  • Repeat the moving form 5–20 times along its path, with a step of 3–10% of the width.
  • Draw lines of force: long diagonals and arcs that run through the form and out to the edge.
  • Break the object into shards and let the shards cross the ground, so that no edge holds the body.
  • Choose the subject from the modern world: a train, a car, a street, a factory, a crowd, a light.
  • Use a bright, divided colour, with a hard complementary pair in the shards, and keep the key high.
  • Give each form a hard, facet-like edge of 2–6mm and let the colour run between the shapes.
  • Fuse the figure and the space so that the street enters the head and the head enters the street.

Anti-patterns

  • A still, posed figure in a calm, closed space
  • Soft, blended, atmospheric haze
  • A single viewpoint at a single moment
  • A pastoral, historical or sentimental subject
  • Muted earth colours with no energy
  • A symmetric, balanced layout
