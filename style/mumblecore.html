Mumblecore

film · 2002–2012 · also: Low-budget talk cinema, Slackavetes

A no-budget film about young people who talk, fail to say what they mean and drift. It uses a small digital camera, friends as actors, real rooms and half-improvised speech. The picture is plain and the mood is awkward.

Core thesis

A cheap digital camera and a few friends are enough to film the small, awkward moments of a relationship, so the film trusts the talk and not the polish.

Design principles

  • Shoot with a small digital camera and a crew of 2 to 5 people, with a budget under 100,000 USD.
  • Cast friends and non-professional actors, and write an outline with 50% or more of the lines improvised.
  • Film in real flats, cars and cafés with the available light, and add no lamp.
  • Frame in 16:9 or 4:3 with a 28mm to 50mm lens, and keep the camera at eye level.
  • Hold a take for 30 to 120 s, and let the pauses and the stammer stay in the cut.
  • Zoom or re-frame in the take, and let the camera hunt for the person who speaks.
  • Use a few quiet songs by friends and no score, and keep the sound of the room.

Anti-patterns

  • A polished studio light kit
  • A famous actor in a styled role
  • A score that tells the viewer how to feel
  • A tidy, written line without a pause
  • A smooth dolly or a crane shot
  • A heavy colour grade
