Modernist Studio Jewellery

fashion · 1940–1969 · also: Studio jewellery, Modern handmade jewellery, Sculptural jewellery of the 1940s and 1950s

Small sculptures to wear, made by one artist at the bench. The materials are silver, brass wire and cut crystal, in abstract forms from modern art, with no regard for the value of the metal.

Core thesis

A jewel is a small sculpture. An artist makes it by hand from an idea about form, and the metal and the stones serve the idea.

Design principles

  • Begin with an abstract form from modern art: a mobile, a line, a plane or a biomorphic shape.
  • Make each piece by hand at the bench, and make no more than a few of each.
  • Use a modest metal such as sterling silver, brass or copper, and choose it for its form, not its value.
  • Cut a stone to a new design, for example a rock crystal with an optical line cut under the surface.
  • Bend and join wire into an open line, and let the empty space count as part of the design.
  • Keep the piece to one gesture and to two or three materials.
  • Leave the hand marks, and sign each piece with a mark.
  • Design the piece for the body: a pin that sits on the lapel, a cuff that follows the arm.

Anti-patterns

  • Rows of equal diamonds in a platinum frame
  • Ribbon and bow forms
  • Cast, repeated costume parts
  • Mourning jet and hair
  • Heavy ornament in all directions
  • A closed, symmetrical, formal design
