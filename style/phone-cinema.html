Phone cinema

film · 2015–today · also: Shot on iPhone, Smartphone filmmaking, Mobile cinema

A feature film or a brand film shot on a phone. The lens is wide and close. The camera slips into places a big rig cannot reach. Gimbal, clip-on lens and app give a controlled look at small cost.

Core thesis

A phone is light, cheap and always present, so a crew of 3 can shoot a feature in real streets and fix the look with an app and a grade.

Design principles

  • Shoot on a phone with a fixed, manual setting: 24 fps, 1/48 s shutter, low ISO and a locked white balance.
  • Use the main lens at 24mm to 26mm equivalent, and a clip-on anamorphic adapter for a 2.39:1 crop.
  • Shoot in log or the highest bit-depth mode, and grade in post to match a cinema look.
  • Mount the phone on a gimbal at 0.5 to 1.5 m/s, or hold it still on a clamp.
  • Use available light and small LED panels, and avoid the night noise with a 0.5 to 1 stop push only.
  • Fix the focus and the exposure on the face and lock them before the take.
  • Record the sound on a separate lavalier or recorder, and sync it in post.

Anti-patterns

  • A phone at auto exposure
  • A shaky, unstabilised handheld clip
  • The default phone colour with a heavy sharpening
  • A vertical frame for a cinema release
  • A sensor crop that digital zooms
  • Phone microphone sound for the dialogue
