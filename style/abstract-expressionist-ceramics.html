Abstract Expressionist ceramics

ceramics · 1954–1975 · also: Otis group ceramics, Voulkos school, Clay as sculpture

American ceramics after 1954 in which the potter treats the vessel as a sculpture. Peter Voulkos threw large forms, cut, tore and stacked them, and painted them with the gesture of action painting.

Core thesis

The pot is a place for a gesture: a thrown cylinder can be cut, torn and stacked like paint on a canvas, and the use of the vessel no longer sets the form.

Design principles

  • Throw large cylinders of 40 to 100 cm, in 3 to 5 parts, and join them while they are soft.
  • Cut, tear, paddle and puncture the thrown wall so that the action stays visible.
  • Stack the parts into a tall, uneven form, with open holes that show the inside.
  • Paint the clay with thick slip and iron in broad gestures, as an action painter paints a canvas.
  • Use thick, raw glazes: black iron, ash and tenmoku, with runs and bare clay patches.
  • Fire in a gas kiln in reduction at 1200 to 1300 °C; accept the cracks and the warps.
  • Leave a vessel opening, a lid or a spout as a sign of the pot, even when the pot cannot hold liquid.
  • Work fast and in a group, and call the studio a place of work, not a pottery.

Anti-patterns

  • A smooth, perfect and useful table pot
  • A small pot with a pretty painted flower
  • A precise geometric outline
  • A glossy even glaze
  • Rustic craft that is made to look rough
