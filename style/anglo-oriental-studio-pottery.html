Anglo-Oriental studio pottery

ceramics · 1920–1975 · also: Leach tradition, St Ives pottery, Anglo-Japanese studio ware

British studio pottery that Bernard Leach started at St Ives in 1920. A potter made high-fired stoneware by hand, with ash and iron glazes after Chinese, Korean and Japanese pots.

Core thesis

A good pot joins the plain beauty of East Asian stoneware with the English craft of one person who makes, glazes and fires it.

Design principles

  • Make each pot by one potter from clay to kiln, and sign it with a seal.
  • Throw the form on the wheel in a plain shape: a round belly, a firm foot and a thick rim.
  • Cut the foot with a clear bevel of 3 to 5 mm and leave part of it bare.
  • Use ash, tenmoku and celadon glazes, fired high in reduction at 1280 to 1300 °C.
  • Paint the decoration with iron or cobalt in a few brush strokes: a leaf, a fish, a bird.
  • Fire in a wood-fired climbing kiln with a long reduction; accept the variation that results.
  • Keep the colour to browns, greens and greys of the earth.
  • Make useful pots first: bowls, bottles and jars for the table.

Anti-patterns

  • Perfect, machine-even form
  • Bright, glossy or pastel colour
  • Gilt line or printed pattern
  • Thin porcelain with a clear glaze
  • Pottery made only to look rustic, with no skill
