Victorian Corsetry

fashion · 1840–1899 · also: Victorian corset, Steel-busk corset, Hourglass corset

The factory-made corset of the Victorian age. It has coutil cloth, a steel busk, metal eyelets and spring-steel bones, shaped with gussets into an hourglass. It was worn under the crinoline and the bustle.

Core thesis

The dress is only as good as the foundation: a firm, shaped corset gives the hourglass that the outer cloth then covers.

Design principles

  • Cut the corset in 8 to 12 panels, and add a gusset at the bust and at the hip to give the curve.
  • Close the front with a steel busk of two flat plates, and lace the back through metal eyelets.
  • Set a bone or a steel strip in each seam, and one at each side of the busk and of the eyelets.
  • Make the body of coutil, a tight cotton herringbone twill that does not stretch.
  • Reduce the waist 5 to 10 cm below the natural waist; use a long busk of 30 to 35 cm.
  • Add a satin or a broché cover for show, with a narrow lace edge at the top.
  • Keep a plain cotton chemise under the corset and a corset cover over it.

Anti-patterns

  • A soft bodice with no bone or busk
  • The flat, conical front of the eighteenth century
  • Stretch cloth that moves with the body
  • A bare, unlined cotton with no coutil
  • A short brassiere with no waist control
