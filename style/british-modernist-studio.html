British modernist studio ceramics

ceramics · 1946–1990 · also: Rie and Coper ceramics, Albion Mews pottery, Modern British studio pottery

Fine studio ceramics from London after 1946. Lucie Rie and Hans Coper worked in a small studio and made thin bowls, bottles and sculptural forms with a quiet, textured surface.

Core thesis

A modern pot is a precise form with a quiet surface, where the glaze and the clay do the work that ornament did before.

Design principles

  • Throw the form thin and exact: a porcelain wall of 2 to 3 mm and a stoneware wall of 3 to 6 mm.
  • Keep the profile simple: a cylinder, a cone, a sphere or a combination of them.
  • Join two or three thrown parts with a visible rim, to make a form that is not a single throw.
  • Coat the stoneware with a thin manganese or a white slip, and leave it matt and unglazed.
  • Make the glaze pitted, volcanic or thick, in one colour, such as pink, yellow, white or bronze.
  • Scratch a line through a slip or a glaze with a pointed tool, in a pattern of 1 to 2 mm lines.
  • Fire in an electric kiln, in oxidation, at 1200 to 1280 °C.
  • Make a small number of pieces and a small range of forms.

Anti-patterns

  • Painted flowers and gilt
  • A rustic, wood-fired look
  • Shiny, even, white glaze
  • Pieces that repeat a mould with no variation
  • A form that is large and loud
