Art Deco tableware

ceramics · 1928–1939 · also: Jazz Age ceramics, Bizarre ware, 1930s geometric tableware

Bright, hand-painted earthenware in angles, cones and bands. Painters in the potteries of Stoke-on-Trent put bold flat colour on a white ground, and the shapes copy the stepped and conical forms of the Paris fair of 1925.

Core thesis

A cheap white earthenware becomes modern when a painter puts strong flat colour in sharp geometry on a form with angles and cones.

Design principles

  • Form the piece in a sharp geometry: a cone, a step, a square or a triangle handle.
  • Paint in flat enamel colours: orange, yellow, red, green and black, with no shading.
  • Divide the surface with bands 3 to 10 mm wide, in bold stripes or in steps.
  • Paint a motif in a few strokes: a crocus, a sun ray, a tree, or a house, and repeat it.
  • Use a white or cream ground, and a thin clear glaze that gives a bright gloss.
  • Let the hand show: each stroke has a small variation, though the pattern is a fixed design.
  • Give each pattern a bold name and put it on the base with the maker mark.

Anti-patterns

  • A soft pastel painting in the Rococo manner
  • A gilt rim and a botanical flower
  • A printed pattern in blue on white
  • A round, curvy and pale organic form
