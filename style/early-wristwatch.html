Early wristwatch

fashion · 1904–1930 · also: Wristlet, Trench watch, Early Santos wristwatch

The small watch on a leather strap that replaced the pocket watch between 1904 and the 1920s. It has a 28 to 35 mm case with wire lugs, a white enamel dial and luminous numerals. The trench version has a wire guard over the glass.

Core thesis

Time must be on the wrist, where a pilot, a soldier or a driver can read it with one glance and no free hand.

Design principles

  • Make the case 28 to 35 mm wide, so that it fits under a cuff and on a thin wrist.
  • Fix the strap to the case with wire or fixed lugs, so that the strap does not slip.
  • Print large Arabic numerals on a white enamel dial for a quick read on a moving wrist.
  • Paint the numerals and hands with luminous paint, so that the dial shows in the dark.
  • Cover the glass of a field model with a wire guard that turns a blow.
  • Seal the case with a screw back and a screw bezel, so that sweat and dust stay out.
  • Fit a small movement, 25 to 29 mm wide and not more than 8 mm thick.
  • Cut a long, narrow strap of calf leather with a plain pin buckle.

Anti-patterns

  • A pocket watch case with a bow, pushed on a strap
  • A large dress dial that jams under a cuff
  • An engraved lid that hides the dial
  • A thick domed case that catches on cloth
  • Tiny numerals that blur on a moving wrist
  • A fabric strap with beads or jewels
