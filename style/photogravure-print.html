Photogravure

print · 1878–1930 · also: Heliogravure (French), Photogravur (German), Klic process

A carbon-tissue positive is transferred to a copper plate, which is etched through a dusted aquatint grain. The plate prints continuous photographic tone as deep, rich ink on thin tissue, with no visible screen.

Core thesis

A photograph etched into copper through an aquatint grain prints as continuous tone in a deep ink. The photograph then reads as an original print.

Design principles

  • Expose a carbon tissue under a positive, transfer it to a copper plate and etch through 3–5 iron-chloride baths.
  • Etch the plate through a dusted aquatint grain, so that the tone shows no dots.
  • Print in one warm black or brown-black, wiped by hand.
  • Print on thin Japan tissue or laid paper, and keep the plate mark.
  • Keep the full tone scale: a deep velvet black, soft mid-tones and a clean light.
  • Mount the tissue on a larger sheet, with a margin of 20–40 mm.

Anti-patterns

  • Visible halftone dots
  • Glossy photographic paper
  • Saturated colour
  • Sharp, flat digital contrast
  • A hard, even line
