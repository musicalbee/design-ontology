Party flash

photography · 2008–today · also: Flash snapshot, Direct flash, On-camera flash

A night-out picture lit by one hard flash on the camera. The subject glows against a falling-off, dark background, skin shines and colour is bold; the image looks candid, tipsy and slightly rude.

Core thesis

The harsh direct flash is the honest light of a real night, and an image that looks unplanned and unflattering reads as truthful and as cool.

Design principles

  • Put one flash on the camera axis, at 1.5 to 3m from the subject, with the flash at full or one stop under.
  • Let the background fall off by 2 to 3 stops, so that it drops to black or to a dark ambient colour.
  • Shoot at 28mm to 35mm (35mm format), f/2.8 to f/4, 1/60s or 1/30s to let some ambient light in (slow sync).
  • Keep the flash at 5500K against a warm tungsten ambient of 2800 to 3200K, and let the orange and blue split stay.
  • Frame the moment loosely: tilted, cut-off, with a friend half in and red eyes allowed.
  • Grade for punch: clip the highlights at 100%, crush the shadows to 0–5% and raise contrast by +30%.

Anti-patterns

  • Bounce flash, softboxes and diffused fill
  • A clean, even exposure of the room
  • Perfect skin, retouched shine and corrected red eye
  • Careful composition with a clear subject on a third
  • Pastel or low-contrast grading
  • A tripod or a posed group in rows
