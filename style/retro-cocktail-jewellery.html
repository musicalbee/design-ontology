Retro Cocktail Jewellery

fashion · 1935–1950 · also: Retro Modern jewellery, 1940s gold jewellery, Cocktail jewels

Big, curved jewels in warm yellow and rose gold, set with large coloured stones. The forms are bows, ribbons, flowers and bracelets of links. They were made in the years of war and wartime metal rules.

Core thesis

A jewel is a bold, warm sculpture in gold: large, curved forms and bright coloured stones that gain from the dress and the cocktail hour.

Design principles

  • Use gold, yellow and rose, as the main metal; platinum is scarce in the war years.
  • Sculpt the metal into bows, ribbons, scrolls, flowers and folds that look like cloth.
  • Set large coloured stones, such as aquamarine, citrine, amethyst and topaz, in a simple frame.
  • Add small rubies, synthetic rubies or sapphires as a point of colour.
  • Make the piece big. A cocktail ring is 2 to 3 cm, a brooch is 6 to 9 cm and a bracelet is 2 to 4 cm wide.
  • Build a bracelet of curved links like a tank track, a gas pipe or a woven strap.
  • Make a clip brooch in pairs that can be worn as one piece on the lapel or the neckline.
  • Polish the gold bright, and set it in contrast to a few matte or woven areas.

Anti-patterns

  • Flat black and white geometry
  • Platinum lace and milgrain
  • Small, discreet jewels
  • Hammered silver
  • Foil-backed paste
  • Soft Victorian jet and hair
