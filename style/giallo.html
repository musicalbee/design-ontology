Giallo

film · 1963–1985 · also: Italian thriller, Italian murder mystery

An Italian murder film in saturated colour. A killer in black gloves is seen only as a hand and a blade. The camera moves as the killer sees, and the set glows in red, blue and green gels.

Core thesis

Saturated gel light, a killer's-eye camera and a stylised set make the murder a piece of design, so fear comes from the look more than from the plot.

Design principles

  • Light with coloured gels in red, blue and green, and mix 2 saturated colours in one frame.
  • Show the killer as a gloved hand, a coat and a blade, and keep the face off screen until the last reel.
  • Put the camera at the killer's point of view, and move it by 0.3 to 1 m/s through a door or a window.
  • Shoot each murder as a set piece of 60 to 180 s, with a wide lens of 18mm to 24mm and a slow build.
  • Use a bold set, with a wallpaper, a statue, a mirror or a painting in the frame.
  • Hold a close-up of an eye, a key or a glass for 1 to 3 s as a clue.
  • Dub all sound after the shoot and let the score run loud over the murder.

Anti-patterns

  • A neutral, realistic set
  • Flat natural colour
  • A killer shown in the open from the start
  • A quiet, silent murder
  • A tidy police procedural
  • A plot that explains all the clues
