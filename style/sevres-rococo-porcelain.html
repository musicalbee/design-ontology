Sèvres rococo porcelain

ceramics · 1756–1775 · also: Sèvres soft-paste porcelain, Vincennes and Sèvres rococo, Pompadour porcelain

Soft-paste porcelain of the royal factory at Sèvres. A glossy ground colour, such as bleu céleste or rose, frames white reserves that carry flowers and birds, with fine gilt edges. Louis XV and Madame de Pompadour were its patrons.

Core thesis

A soft, glassy body takes a deep, even ground colour that no hard paste can match; the painter then leaves white windows for the flowers.

Design principles

  • Fire a glassy soft paste with a lead-rich glaze at about 1100 °C.
  • Lay a ground colour, such as a sky blue or a rose, in an even layer over the glaze.
  • Leave white reserves in the shape of a cartouche, and paint flowers, birds or scenes in them.
  • Outline each reserve with a fine band of gold, and add small gold dots and scrolls.
  • Model the form in curves: a double curve at the rim, a scroll handle, a shell foot.
  • Mark the base with two crossed L letters and a date letter, so that each piece can be dated.
  • Leave the figures in unglazed biscuit, with a soft, matt surface like marble.

Anti-patterns

  • A hard white body with a dry, bright glaze
  • Plain white ware with no ground colour
  • A printed outline filled with colour
  • A strict straight line and a Greek key border
