West German fat lava

ceramics · 1960–1980 · also: Fat lava ceramics, West German pottery, WGP vases

Vases and bowls from West German factories between 1960 and 1980. A thick, bubbled and cratered glaze runs over a moulded earthenware body, in orange, brown, green or yellow.

Core thesis

A thick glaze that is allowed to run, bubble and crater gives a mass-made vase the look of cooled volcanic rock.

Design principles

  • Mould the earthenware body in a bold, round or flared shape with a wide opening.
  • Apply the lava glaze in a layer of 1 to 3 mm, and add a second thinner glaze over it.
  • Let the glaze run and crater, so that no two vases are the same.
  • Choose a bright ground: orange, yellow, green or brown, with a white or black drip.
  • Make large floor vases of 40 to 60 cm and table vases of 15 to 30 cm.
  • Add a relief of rings, bands or bubbles in the mould, to give the glaze a place to pool.
  • Fire at 1000 to 1100 °C in a tunnel kiln.
  • Mark the base with the factory name, a model number and a height in cm.

Anti-patterns

  • A thin even glossy glaze
  • A painted flower or a printed pattern
  • Porcelain or fine bone china
  • Muted museum earth colours
  • A dull, round, plain form
