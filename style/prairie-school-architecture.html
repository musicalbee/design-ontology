Prairie School

architecture · 1900–1917 · also: Prairie style, Wrightian, Chicago Prairie

Long low houses with broad overhanging roofs, ribbon windows and a central hearth. Frank Lloyd Wright and the Chicago architects built for the flat Midwest: the horizontal line rules, and rooms flow into each other.

Core thesis

A house should belong to its land: low horizontal lines, a free plan around the hearth and honest materials make an American architecture of the prairie.

Design principles

  • Stress the horizontal: low hipped roofs of 1:6 to 1:4 pitch, eaves that project 1 to 2.5m and long unbroken lines of sill and band.
  • Plan around a central masonry hearth, and let the living rooms flow into each other in a cross-shaped or L-shaped plan.
  • Group the windows in bands of casements with art-glass geometry, 0.8 to 1.2m above the floor, so that the rooms feel sheltered.
  • Make the entrance low and indirect, 2.0 to 2.2m high, to compress the visitor before the open living room of 3 to 3.5m.
  • Use long thin Roman brick with deep raked horizontal joints and flush vertical joints, to stress the horizontal line.
  • Design the whole house as one work: built-in furniture, lamps, glass and planters follow one geometric motif.

Anti-patterns

  • Tall vertical proportions and steep pitched roofs
  • Closed boxlike rooms separated by corridors
  • Applied historical ornament or classical columns
  • A grand central front entrance on the street axis
  • Painted smooth surfaces that hide the brick and timber
  • Window openings set high and narrow in vertical strips
