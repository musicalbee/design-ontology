Hong Kong action

film · 1984–1997 · also: Heroic bloodshed, Gun fu, Kung fu comedy action

Action built in the cut and in the body: slow motion, wide anamorphic frames and a stunt shot in full view. Gunfights are choreographed like dance, and fights are made of clear, fast phrases.

Core thesis

An action scene is a piece of choreography shot and cut for its rhythm, so the stunt is shown whole and the cut gives it speed and weight.

Design principles

  • Shoot the stunt in a wide or medium shot that shows the whole body, and keep the performer's head and feet in frame for 60% or more of the fight.
  • Cut at an average shot length of 1.5 to 3 s and build a fight from 4 to 8 short phrases of 2 to 5 s each.
  • Use slow motion at 48 to 96 fps for 1 to 3 s at the peak of a leap, a fall or a shot, then return to 24 fps.
  • Frame in 2.35:1 anamorphic with a 28mm to 50mm lens, and keep the camera at 0.5 to 1.5 m, moving at 1 to 3 m/s in a push, a whip or a spin.
  • Light with hard, coloured sources: a blue-green or sodium key at 4:1, with a smoke haze and neon in the back.
  • Do the dangerous stunt on set, with wire work or a real fall, and repeat the strike from two angles at 0.3 to 0.5 s apart.
  • Score with a synthesizer and a guitar theme, and add a punch, a whoosh and a shell casing to every hit.

Anti-patterns

  • A static, wide master shot of a fight with no cut
  • A shaky, blurred close-up that hides the stunt
  • A stunt double hidden by an edit
  • A fight with no rhythm or slow-motion peak
  • A realistic, quiet sound track with no foley
  • A single, drab, desaturated grade
  • A hero with no code of honour
