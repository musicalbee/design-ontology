Breguet classical watch

fashion · 1780–1830 · also: Breguet style, Classical dress watch, Guilloché dress watch

The plain, exact gold watch that Abraham-Louis Breguet made in Paris from 1780. It has an engine-turned silver dial, blued steel hands with an open tip and a coin-edge case. Its movement set the rule for the dress watch.

Core thesis

A fine watch shows its worth by exact work and a quiet face, so each part is plain, thin and cut by hand.

Design principles

  • Turn the silver dial on a rose engine in a tight pattern of fine lines, checks or barley corns.
  • Print slim Arabic or Roman numerals and keep the minute track thin.
  • Blue the steel hands in a flame and cut each tip as an open ring, the Breguet hand.
  • Cut a fine coin edge on the case band, so that the thumb finds the edge.
  • Keep the case thin and round: the bow, the pendant and the crown carry no extra ornament.
  • Put the small seconds dial off centre, at 6 or 10 o'clock, and leave the middle of the dial clear.
  • Add a secret signature near the 12 that shows only in raking light.
  • Bend the end of the hairspring in an overcoil, so that the spring opens and closes in an even circle.

Anti-patterns

  • A crowded dial with bold block numerals
  • A chrome or steel case with a sport bezel
  • Printed fake engine turning on a flat dial
  • Bright coloured hands or a coloured dial
  • A thick case with large lugs and a heavy crown
  • Machine-stamped patterns that copy the look of hand turning
