Swiss modernist corporate mark

logo · 1955–1985 · also: International Style trademark, Corporate identity mark, Modernist logo

A mark for a whole organisation: a reduced symbol on a grid, one or two colours, a neo-grotesque or a custom sans logotype, and a manual that fixes every size, space and colour. Railways, airlines and state agencies led the style.

Core thesis

A mark is a tool of a system: it must be objective, reproducible in any medium, and the same at 5 mm and at 5 m.

Design principles

  • Draw the symbol on a square grid of 8–16 units; every edge lands on a unit line or on a 45° step.
  • Use strokes of one weight, at 12–20% of the symbol height, with no contrast.
  • Limit the mark to 1–2 flat colours: one strong brand colour and black or white.
  • Set the logotype in a neo-grotesque or a custom sans, with a cap height of 35–50% of the symbol height.
  • Fix the lock-up of symbol and logotype in a manual, with the gap between them equal to one grid unit times 2–4.
  • Define clear space as a module of the mark: often the stroke width times 2, or half the symbol height.

Anti-patterns

  • Pictorial or illustrative symbols
  • Ornament, shading or texture
  • More than 2 colours in the master mark
  • Serif or script lettering
  • A mark redrawn by hand for each use
