Racing chronograph

fashion · 1957–now · also: Motorsport chronograph, Tachymeter chronograph, Driver's watch

The watch for the stopwatch era of motor racing. It has a steel case, a dark dial with two or three sub dials, two pushers and a tachymeter scale. The Speedmaster of 1957 and the Carrera of 1963 set the type.

Core thesis

A racing chronograph is a stopwatch on the wrist. Its dial shows elapsed time and average speed in one glance, and the case stays slim enough to fit under a cuff.

Design principles

  • Fit two pushers at 2 and 4 o'clock, so that the driver starts, stops and resets the timer with one hand.
  • Print a tachymeter scale from 60 to 500 on the bezel or on the dial rim. It gives the speed over one measured mile or kilometre.
  • Arrange two or three sub dials in a balanced pattern, each 8 to 10 mm wide, for the running seconds, the minutes and the hours.
  • Use a matt dial in black, grey or white, with high contrast against the hands and the sub dials.
  • Cut the case 38 to 42 mm wide and 12 to 14 mm thick, so that the watch fits under a driving glove.
  • Add a stepped sapphire or domed acrylic crystal that keeps the glare low.
  • Choose a perforated leather strap, so that the wrist stays cool in a car.
  • Use a red or orange seconds hand and one signal colour on the dial.

Anti-patterns

  • A single sub dial with no timing scale
  • A tachymeter in tiny print that no one can read
  • A thick case that does not go under a cuff
  • A gold case with a pale enamel dial
  • Pushers that are too small for a glove
  • A dial with six colours and a busy pattern
