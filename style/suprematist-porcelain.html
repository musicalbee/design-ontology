Suprematist porcelain

ceramics · 1923–1935 · also: Suprematist ceramics, Malevich teapot, Soviet avant-garde porcelain

Tea and coffee ware in white porcelain from the State Porcelain Factory in Petrograd. Malevich and his pupils turned the plain shapes of Suprematist painting into pots, cups and jugs in blocks, planes and bars.

Core thesis

A pot is a Suprematist object when its form is built of cylinders, cubes and slabs, and the paint on it is a flat plane of colour.

Design principles

  • Build the form from simple solids: a cylinder, a block, a slab and a cone.
  • Keep the surface white, so that the shape shows first.
  • Paint flat planes in red, black and one other colour, with sharp edges.
  • Place the planes at an angle to the form, as in the painting.
  • Make the spout, the handle and the lid a part of the structure, not a decoration.
  • Cast the form in plaster moulds in a repeatable series.
  • Use no flowers, no gilding and no relief.
  • Fire the porcelain hard to about 1400 °C for a dense white wall.

Anti-patterns

  • A flower spray or a bird as the main decoration
  • A soft round bulb shape with a curved handle
  • A gilt rim or a scalloped edge
  • Colours that blend or fade at the edge of the plane
