Iznik tiles

ceramics · 1550–1700 · also: Ottoman court tiles, İznik çini, Iznik wall tiles

Painted wall tiles that the Iznik kilns made for the Ottoman court. A white quartz body takes a white slip, a free painting of flowers in cobalt, turquoise, green and raised red, and a clear glaze.

Core thesis

A court tile is a garden in glaze: the painter shows tulips, carnations and leaves in clear colours on a white ground, and the glaze gives them depth.

Design principles

  • Cover the quartz body with a white slip, then paint the design on the slip under a clear glaze.
  • Draw the outline in black or cobalt first, then fill the shapes with flat colour.
  • Paint the flowers of the court garden: tulip, carnation, hyacinth, rose and the long saz leaf.
  • Put the raised red, made of bole clay, on a flower only after about 1560; keep it 0.1 to 0.3 mm thick.
  • Add emerald green after 1567; before that use only cobalt, turquoise, purple and black.
  • Set the tiles in a square repeat of 25 cm and fit a border and a corner tile to each panel.
  • Cover a whole wall or lunette over a window, with the design running over the joints.
  • Leave a white ground between the shapes; let the white carry about one third of the surface.

Anti-patterns

  • A body of ordinary red clay under the slip
  • A flat printed pattern with no brush line
  • Gold and lustre in the painting
  • A dull red that does not stand in relief
  • Figures of people and animals on the tile
