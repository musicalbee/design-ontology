Dogme 95

film · 1995–2005 · also: Dogma 95, Vow of Chastity

A film made under a written vow: handheld camera, real locations, available light and the sound of the scene. The rules remove the tools that fake the world, so the actors and the room carry the film.

Core thesis

A fixed set of prohibitions removes illusion, polish and the director's taste, so that the truth of the scene remains.

Design principles

  • Shoot on location with the props that are already there; bring in no set piece.
  • Hold the camera in the hand for every shot; keep the camera at 1.4 to 1.7 m and let it search, with reframes every 3 to 8 s.
  • Light only with the available light; if the room is too dark, fix one lamp to the camera and nothing else.
  • Shoot in colour at 4:3 or Academy 1.37:1, at 25 fps (PAL) or 24 fps, with a 180° shutter and no filter.
  • Record sound with the picture; add no music unless it plays in the room where the scene is shot.
  • Cut at an average shot length of 4 to 8 s (estimate; check Cinemetrics for each title) with jump cuts that stay in the film.
  • Use no genre and no superficial action: no murder, no weapon; keep the time and place of the story in the here and now.
  • Leave the director out of the credits.

Anti-patterns

  • A tripod, a dolly or a crane
  • Added music or sound effects laid under a scene
  • Studio lighting, a lit set or a coloured filter
  • Optical effects and a post-produced colour grade
  • A genre film with set-piece action
  • A director credit and a signature style
  • A period setting
