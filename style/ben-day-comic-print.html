Ben-Day comic print

print · 1896–1975 · also: Ben-Day dots, Four-colour comic, Newsprint comic, Sunday funnies

A comic or a newspaper picture printed on cheap newsprint in four flat inks, with tints made of fixed dots from Benjamin Day's films. The Sunday supplement of the 1890s and the comic book of the 1930s–1960s made black lines, primary colours and dots a mass look.

Core thesis

Four inks, a black line and a few fixed tints of dots can colour a whole world for a cent.

Design principles

  • Draw every form with a firm, black brush or pen line; the colour only fills.
  • Use only cyan, magenta and yellow at 0, 25, 50 and 100 %, alone or mixed; the colourist has no other tones.
  • Make each tint with a regular field of dots of one size, not a photographic screen.
  • Put flat primary colour on the main figures and a light dot tint on the background and the skin.
  • Accept a misregister of 1–2 mm and a slight spread of the ink on the soft paper.
  • Use speech balloons and captions in hand lettering, all capitals.

Anti-patterns

  • Smooth gradients or soft airbrush
  • A photographic halftone with dots of many sizes
  • Thin or broken outlines
  • A palette of muted or pastel colours
