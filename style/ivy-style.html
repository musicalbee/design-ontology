Ivy style

fashion · 1950–1969 · also: Ivy League look, American trad, Natural-shoulder style, Eastern establishment dress

The dress of the American college man: an unpadded, natural-shouldered sack jacket, an oxford button-down shirt, chinos and flannels, worn with ease.

Core thesis

A man's dress is plain, a little worn and made to last; it shows that he belongs and does not show that he tries.

Design principles

  • Cut the jacket on a straight sack line with a natural shoulder and no pad.
  • Use a 3-roll-2 front with a soft lapel, and a single vent at the back.
  • Wear an oxford cloth shirt with a button-down collar that rolls in a soft curve.
  • Pair the jacket with flannel grey trousers or cotton chinos with a plain front and a cuff of 4 cm.
  • Choose tweed, flannel or seersucker by the season, and cotton oxford in pale stripes.
  • Add a knit tie, a Shetland jumper or a repp-striped tie in two or three colours.
  • Keep shoes in brown leather with a thick sole, and let them age.

Anti-patterns

  • Padded, square shoulders
  • A tight, cropped jacket
  • Shiny or stretch suiting
  • A collar with no roll
  • A bold logo or a loud print
  • Spotless, new, stiff clothes
