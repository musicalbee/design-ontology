California cuisine

cuisine · 1971–present · also: Market-driven cuisine, Farm-to-table

The kitchen of the local farm and the single daily menu. Alice Waters opened Chez Panisse in 1971 and cooked simple French dishes with produce from named growers, in season, with little change to the ingredient.

Core thesis

The best cooking starts with the best produce: buy ripe food from known growers in season, and then do as little to it as you can.

Design principles

  • Build the menu from what the farms bring that week, and write it the same day.
  • Offer one set menu each evening, with the same dish for each guest.
  • Name the farm, the farmer or the field on the menu.
  • Cook over a wood fire or in a wood oven where the dish allows it.
  • Keep each dish to three or four parts and keep the cooking simple.
  • Serve fruit, salad and vegetables at their ripest, with olive oil and herbs.
  • Plate in a rustic way on a plain plate: the food is the decoration.
  • Take ideas from the French and Italian country table, and use Californian produce.

Anti-patterns

  • Out-of-season produce flown in for the menu
  • A heavy sauce that hides the ingredient
  • A tall tower of food with a decorative garnish
  • A long menu with a choice for every guest
  • Frozen or canned produce
  • A plate with a smear and a foam
