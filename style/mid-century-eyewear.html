Mid-century eyewear

fashion · 1937–1965 · also: Cat-eye and browline glasses, 1940s–50s spectacle frames

The glasses and sun glasses of the years after 1937. Frames in celluloid and acetate took the cat-eye, browline and round panto shapes, beside the thin gold-filled aviator. Glasses became a fashion item and not only a medical aid.

Core thesis

Glasses are a part of the face, and the frame gives the wearer a character.

Design principles

  • Cut the frame in plastic, 3 to 5 mm thick, with a keyhole or a flat bridge.
  • Raise the outer corner of the lens by 10 to 20 degrees for the cat-eye.
  • Join a heavy brow bar of plastic to a thin metal rim for the browline.
  • Choose a tortoiseshell pattern or a solid colour in the plastic.
  • Set rhinestones or metal studs in the upper corner of a ladies' frame.
  • Fix the temples with 5-barrel hinges on a core wire.
  • Make the lens 48 to 54 mm wide.
  • Offer one frame in many colours, so that the wearer owns more than one pair.

Anti-patterns

  • Rimless frames with no visible border
  • Thin titanium wire with a sports finish
  • Oversized wrap shields
  • Digital screen glasses
  • Bright neon plastic of the 1980s
