Modernist cuisine

cuisine · 2011–present · also: Modernist cooking, Science-led cuisine, Precision cuisine

A kitchen that cooks by measurement and shows how it works. The six-volume book Modernist Cuisine of 2011 set out the physics of sous-vide, pressure, gels and freezing. Grant Achatz at Alinea in Chicago turned the method into a tasting menu.

Core thesis

A cook who knows the physics of heat, water and texture can control the result to the degree, the second and the gram, and can then make food that was not possible before.

Design principles

  • Measure the temperature and the time of each dish, and write both in the recipe.
  • Cook in a sealed bag in a water bath so that the whole piece reaches the same exact core temperature.
  • Use a tool of the laboratory when it gives a better result than a tool of the old kitchen: a centrifuge, a pressure cooker, a vacuum.
  • Show the inside: cut the food in half and photograph it, so that the guest and the reader see the structure.
  • Build a dish as a sequence of senses: smell, sound, temperature and touch as well as taste.
  • Share the method in a book, so that any cook can test it.
  • Serve a long menu of exact, small courses in a room that feels like a theatre.

Anti-patterns

  • A time or a temperature that the cook only guesses
  • A rustic, uneven plate
  • A recipe that stays a secret
  • Technique as a trick, with no gain in taste or texture
  • A heavy sauce that hides the exact cooking of the main piece
  • A menu that does not change from year to year
