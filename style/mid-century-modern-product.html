Mid-century modern product

industrial · 1945–1975 · also: Atomic-age design, American modernism, Italian post-war design

Post-war household objects of moulded plywood, fibreglass, steel rod and enamel, in organic curves and bright accents, made by American furniture firms and small Italian workshops.

Core thesis

New materials from the war economy make a new kind of object: light, curved, bright and cheap enough for the new suburban home.

Design principles

  • Mould plywood, fibreglass or plastic in a compound curve that fits the body.
  • Put each shell on a slim base of steel rod, dowel or tapered wood, 10 to 25 mm in section.
  • Use organic outlines: the kidney, the boomerang, the egg and the disc.
  • Combine warm walnut or teak with a cool part of chrome, brass or enamel.
  • Add one saturated colour, in orange, turquoise, mustard or red, to each room set.
  • Make each object a module of a system that a customer can combine and extend.
  • Design the object for series production in a factory, and show the production method in the form.
  • Use a gloss finish of 40 to 80 GU on moulded and enamelled parts.

Anti-patterns

  • Period revival ornament and carved legs
  • Heavy dark upholstery with a button back
  • Raw, unfinished wood without a clear shape
  • Stacks of small ornaments on a sideboard
  • A full range of pastel and grey only
  • Thick square legs and boxy frames
