Music video

film · 1981–today · also: MTV style, Promo video, Pop clip

A picture built to a song. The cut falls on the beat, the set is a stage, and the grade is loud. Story and place are cues that give way to a hook, a dance and a look.

Core thesis

The song is the script, so images are cut to its beat and structure and a loud, memorable look sells the artist.

Design principles

  • Cut on the beat: at 120 bpm a beat lasts 0.5 s, so cut on every beat or every second beat, and change the shot on each chorus.
  • Build the clip in three layers: a performance, a story or a concept, and a graphic inset; keep each for 3 to 8 s.
  • Shoot the artist in a close-up for 30% or more of the runtime, with a lip-sync at 24 fps and the song at playback speed.
  • Use a strong key colour per section: one saturated hue at 40% or more of the frame, and swap it at the chorus.
  • Move the camera at the tempo: a push-in over 4 beats, a spin over 8 beats, a whip at the drop.
  • Use a wide lens of 14mm to 24mm for dance and a long lens of 85mm to 135mm for a face, with a shallow focus at f/1.8 to f/2.8.
  • Place one signature visual in the first 10 s and repeat it at the final chorus.

Anti-patterns

  • A static, single, long take with no reference to the beat
  • A realistic, un-graded, muted palette
  • A plain, unlit location with no concept
  • Dialogue and diegetic sound that mask the song
  • A scene that is cut for continuity and not for the music
  • A face that does not look into the lens
  • A look with no signature visual
