Shaker object

industrial · 1790–1900 · also: Shaker craft, Shaker utility design

The plain household goods of the Shaker communities: ladder-back chairs, peg rails, oval boxes and chests of pine, maple and cherry, built for use with no applied ornament.

Core thesis

An object that serves its use well is beautiful; the maker adds no ornament, shows each joint and makes each piece with honest care.

Design principles

  • Make each object for its use; remove each part that has no job.
  • Use thin, light parts: a chair post of 25 to 32 mm and a rail of 12 to 20 mm.
  • Show the joint: a dovetail, a mortise or a tenon stays in view.
  • Use wood in a plain finish: oil, wax or a thin matte paint in red, yellow, blue or green.
  • Hang each chair on a peg rail of 7 to 9 cm pegs, so that the floor is free for the broom.
  • Make a box or a drawer in 3 to 7 sizes that nest, with swallowtail fingers of thin maple.
  • Keep the line straight and the turning simple: a ball finial and a taper are the only decoration.

Anti-patterns

  • Carved or inlaid ornament
  • Gilding and bright metal
  • Fringes, tassels and buttoned upholstery
  • Dark, thick varnish
  • Veneer that hides the joint
  • Patterned fabric
