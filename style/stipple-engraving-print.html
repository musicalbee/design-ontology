Stipple (crayon-manner) engraving

print · c.1770–1840 · also: gravure au pointille (French), Punktiermanier (German)

The plate is built up from etched and engraved dots and short flicks, so tone has no hatching. The print shows soft skin and flesh with pale dotted shading, and publishers often printed in red-brown (sanguine).

Core thesis

A plate built from dots and short flicks, with no hatching, gives flesh and drapery a soft tone that imitates a chalk drawing.

Design principles

  • Build every tone from etched dots and short flicks; draw no hatching in the figure.
  • Make the dots small and close in the shadows, 80–120 an inch, and wide apart in the lights.
  • Print in sanguine red-brown for a chalk drawing look, or in warm black for a portrait.
  • Leave the edge of the figure soft and fade it into the paper, in an oval or a vignette.
  • Add a light outline only where a form needs a firm edge, such as a hat or a sleeve.
  • Keep a plate mark round the sheet.

Anti-patterns

  • A hard outline round every form
  • Halftone dots in a regular grid
  • Photographic tone
  • Saturated colour
  • Dense cross-hatching in the flesh
