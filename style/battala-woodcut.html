Battala woodcut

print · c.1816–c.1900 · also: Battala print, Bat-tala woodcut, Calcutta bazaar woodcut

A cheap woodcut sheet from the Battala bazaar quarter of Calcutta. Local craftsmen cut a bold black outline, often to copy a painted original, and printed it with type on thin country paper.

Core thesis

A cheap wood block with a bold line can put a sacred or comic story in the hands of a bazaar crowd.

Design principles

  • Cut a strong, even outline in wood and keep the line 0.5–1.5 mm wide.
  • Fill large shapes with a flat colour, applied by hand or by a second block.
  • Show gods, village scenes and city satire, with the main figure at the centre.
  • Print the verse or the title in Bengali type on the same sheet.
  • Use thin country paper; do not hide its fibre.
  • Keep the hatch short and sparse, in the style of a European wood engraving that the cutter had seen.
  • Accept a thick line, a blot and a shift of 1–2 mm between the black and the colour.

Anti-patterns

  • Smooth oil-like blend of the oleograph kind
  • Halftone dots
  • Fine engraved line with deep shadows
  • Gloss and varnish
  • A refined, balanced European layout
