Punk Zine

internet · 1976–1986 · also: Cut-and-paste zine, Xerox style, Ransom-note style

Photocopied black and white, cut-and-paste letters, typewriter text, tape and hand scrawl. It came from the punk scenes of London and New York and shows that anyone can make a page.

Core thesis

Anyone can publish: make the page with scissors, glue and a copier, and show the seams on purpose.

Design principles

  • Set the page in black on off-white (#F1EEE4) with one spot colour (fluorescent pink #FF2E88 or yellow) on less than 10% of the surface.
  • Mix 3 to 5 letter styles in a headline, each letter cut as a block with a 2px to 4px offset and a 1° to 6° tilt.
  • Set body text in a typewriter face at 15px to 16px with uneven word spacing; underline or strike through words by hand.
  • Add strips of tape (80px to 160px long, 20% opacity grey) at the corners of images and boxes.
  • Photocopy every image: greyscale, contrast 150% to 200%, a visible grain at 10% to 20%.
  • Rotate boxes and images by -4° to 4° and overlap them by 8px to 40px.
  • Add hand-drawn marks (arrows, X, scribbles) in marker with a 3px to 5px stroke.

Anti-patterns

  • Clean alignment to a grid
  • Glossy gradients and soft shadows
  • Perfect, uniform type in one face
  • Rounded UI components
  • Smooth vector illustration
  • More than one spot colour
  • Corporate stock photography

Palette

  • Newsprint #F1EEE4 background
  • Toner #111111 text
  • Hot Pink #FF2E88 spot colour
  • Copier Grey #BDBAB0 tape and shadows
  • Highlighter #F5F000 alternate spot colour
