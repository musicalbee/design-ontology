Expressionism

painting · 1905–1925 · also: Die Brücke, Der Blaue Reiter, German Expressionism

Pictures that bend the world to show an inner state: harsh colour, jagged line, tilted space and mask-like faces. The city is a threat and the body is a nerve.

Core thesis

A picture shows what the painter feels, not what the eye sees, so that form and colour are distorted until they carry the emotion.

Design principles

  • Distort the figure and the space: a long neck, a sharp angle, a floor that tilts at 10–25°.
  • Choose colour for the mood: a green face, a red street, a yellow sky, at full or near full chroma.
  • Draw with a hard, dark, angular line of 4–12mm and let the contour break and cut.
  • Flatten the depth to 1–2 layers and push the figures to the front edge.
  • Paint fast and rough: broad strokes of 20–50mm, with the brush marks and the scrapes in view.
  • Show the city, the night, the crowd, the nude and the portrait with a tense or empty gaze.
  • Simplify the face to a mask with large eyes and a hard mouth.

Anti-patterns

  • A calm, true-to-life colour and light
  • Smooth, blended modelling and a polished surface
  • Deep, correct perspective and exact proportion
  • Soft, round, gentle contours
  • A cheerful, decorative mood
  • A neutral, detached view of the subject
