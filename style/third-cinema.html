Third Cinema

film · 1968– · also: Tercer Cine, Militant documentary, Cinema of liberation

A militant film that works as a tool, not as a show. It uses a light 16mm camera, black and white stock, real streets and factories, archive clips, slogans on screen and direct address. The picture is rough and the cut is an argument.

Core thesis

A film made outside the studio and outside the art house is a weapon for a people in a struggle, so each image must serve an argument and each screening must start a debate.

Design principles

  • Shoot with a light 16mm camera and a crew of 2 to 6 people, in the streets, the factories and the villages.
  • Cast real workers and activists as themselves, and let them speak to the camera.
  • Cut live footage against newsreel, photographs, statistics and printed slogans, to make a claim.
  • Split the film into chapters with title cards of 2 to 6 words and a clear question.
  • Use handheld takes of 5 to 20 s and a hard cut, and hold a face when it speaks.
  • Lay a voice-over or a song over the image, and leave gaps for the audience to answer.
  • Show the film in a meeting room or a union hall, and stop it for a debate.

Anti-patterns

  • A studio set or a star actor
  • A score that decorates the image
  • A soft, glossy colour grade
  • A scene that entertains and makes no claim
  • A neutral voice that hides the maker's side
  • A tidy ending that closes the argument
