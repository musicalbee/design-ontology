Victorian millinery

fashion · 1840–1900 · also: Victorian hats and bonnets, Poke bonnet and trimmed hat

The women's hats and bonnets of Queen Victoria's reign. A wire and buckram frame carried silk, velvet, felt or straw, trimmed by hand with ribbon, flowers and feathers. The shape followed the dress, from deep poke bonnet to small tilted hat.

Core thesis

A hat completes a lady's dress, and the trim shows the skill of the milliner.

Design principles

  • Build the shape on a wire and buckram frame, then cover it with cloth cut on the bias.
  • Cut the brim of a poke bonnet 8 to 12 cm deep in the 1840s, so that the face looks out of a tunnel.
  • Shrink the bonnet to a small hat at the back of the head by the 1860s, as the hair is dressed higher.
  • Tie the strings under the chin in a silk bow 4 to 6 cm wide.
  • Mass the trim at one side or at the front: flowers, ribbon loops, a plume or a bird.
  • Match the cloth of the trim to the dress of the wearer.
  • Use felt for winter, silk for the evening and plaited straw for summer.
  • Sew the lining and the head band by hand.

Anti-patterns

  • A bare hat with no trim
  • A flat crown in the modern shape
  • Sports felt, baseball caps and visors
  • Plastic flowers and synthetic net
  • Machine-sprayed colour on the crown
