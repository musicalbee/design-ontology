Deconstructivism

architecture · 1980s–2000s · also: Deconstruction, Deconstructivist architecture

Skewed, fractured volumes that collide at sharp angles, with a facade that seems to tear itself apart. Gehry, Hadid, Libeskind and Coop Himmelb(l)au used distortion to disturb the pure box of the Modern Movement from inside.

Core thesis

The pure form is already flawed: distort the grid, collide the volumes and let the building show that order is unstable.

Design principles

  • Collide two or more orthogonal volumes at 10 to 40° skews, so that no wall is parallel to its neighbour.
  • Fragment the form into 5 to 15 shards or bars that overlap, tilt and cantilever.
  • Tilt walls and floors off the vertical by 3 to 15°, and let beams and columns slope.
  • Cut slot windows and sharp gashes at angles that do not follow the floor levels.
  • Show the stress: bracing, folded planes and structure that seems to carry the load without a clear path.
  • Wrap the shards in one material, usually metal sheet, zinc or titanium, and let the joints draw the geometry.

Anti-patterns

  • Regular orthogonal grids and repeated bays
  • Symmetric compositions around an axis
  • Soft rounded blob forms with no hard edges
  • Historical quotation or applied ornament
  • A plain box with one decorative sloped wall
  • Windows aligned with floor levels and a clear horizontal order
