Greek red-figure pottery

ceramics · c. 530–320 BC · also: Attic red-figure, Red-figure vase painting

Greek vases in which the background is black gloss and the figures keep the red of the clay. The painter draws the inner lines with a brush, so the figures look rounder and freer.

Core thesis

A vase is a field for the drawn human body: the figure stays the colour of the clay and the brush line gives it weight, movement and feeling.

Design principles

  • Reserve the figures in the red clay and paint the whole ground in black gloss.
  • Draw the inner lines with a brush in relief lines, thick and raised, for the main contours.
  • Use thinner dilute lines for muscles, hair and folds of cloth.
  • Show the body in three-quarter view, twisted and foreshortened.
  • Frame the picture with a meander or a palmette border, and a plain black wall below.
  • Place one or two figures in the tondo of a cup so that the circle holds the pose.
  • Fire in three stages, oxidation, reduction and re-oxidation, to fix black and red.

Anti-patterns

  • Black silhouettes with incised detail on a red ground
  • Flat profile figures that do not turn
  • Thick opaque added colour over the large areas
  • A crowded frieze with no black field
  • A glazed surface with clear gloss over the red
