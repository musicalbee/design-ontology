Grotesque sans

typeface · 1816–1930 · also: Grotesk, Gothic, Early sans serif, Lineale grotesque

The first sans serifs: plain, heavy letters for posters, with a little contrast, some quirks of width and a two-storey g. Later text cuts such as Akzidenz-Grotesk and Franklin Gothic gave the class a sober, workmanlike voice.

Core thesis

A letter without serifs is plain, and the plain letter is the strongest one at a distance.

Design principles

  • Keep the stroke contrast at 1.1–1.6:1, with a slight thinning where curves meet stems.
  • Use a two-storey g or a g with a spur, a spurred G and an R with a straight or curved leg.
  • Let the widths vary: a wide M and W, a narrow S and E, with no strict rule.
  • Cut the terminals of c, e and s at an angle or close to horizontal, with a moderate aperture.
  • Hold the x-height at 0.68–0.78 of the cap height.
  • Set headlines in bold or condensed bold; the grotesque is at its best in heavy weights.

Anti-patterns

  • Pure geometric circles and triangles in the letters
  • Calligraphic stress or swelling strokes
  • A uniform, optically perfect width for all capitals
  • Light hairline weights for text
  • Serifs or slabs on any letter
