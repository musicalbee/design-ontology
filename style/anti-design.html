Anti-Design

internet · 2016–now · also: Anti-design web, Ugly design, Broken grid

Broken grids, clashing type, overlap, odd colour and deliberate roughness. It answers the polish of templated product sites and asks the visitor to notice the design.

Core thesis

Polish is a lie: a page that breaks the rules on purpose shows that people made it.

Design principles

  • Break the grid on purpose: shift at least 3 elements by 12px to 80px off any column and overlap 2 or more blocks by 8px to 48px.
  • Mix at least 3 type families or 4 styles on one page and scale type by a ratio of 3 or more between neighbours.
  • Use 4 to 6 clashing colours, including 1 acid colour (#C6FF00 or #FF3B00), with saturation above 80%.
  • Rotate 2 to 5 elements by 3° to 15° and let text run at an angle or vertically.
  • Set at least one headline at 120px or larger and crop it by the viewport edge.
  • Place a raw or default element (system button, plain table) beside a styled one.
  • Make the navigation odd: a label in the wrong place, an icon with no text, or a link inside the headline.

Anti-patterns

  • Centred symmetric layouts on a tidy grid
  • A single safe typeface and a safe palette
  • Cards of equal size with equal padding
  • Soft gradients and glass effects
  • Stock photography in neat frames
  • Predictable SaaS section order
  • Pastel colour

Palette

  • Chalk #F2F0E8 background
  • Black #0A0A0A text and borders
  • Acid #C6FF00 accent
  • Signal #FF3B00 second accent
  • Electric #3B2BFF third accent
  • Pink #FF7AD9 fourth accent

Lineage
