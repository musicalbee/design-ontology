New Hollywood

film · 1967–1980 · also: Hollywood Renaissance, American New Wave

A director's cinema inside the studios: location shooting, long lenses, a zoom, a wide anamorphic frame and flawed heroes. A story that ends without a lesson. The style looks lived in and tough.

Core thesis

The studio picture is used for a director's personal, sceptical view, so the heroes are flawed, the places are real and the endings stay open.

Design principles

  • Shoot on real streets, roads and rooms, with practical lamps in the frame, and let the light fall off to dark in the corners.
  • Frame in 2.35:1 anamorphic or 1.85:1 and use a long lens of 85mm to 200mm to compress a street into a flat, crowded plane.
  • Use a slow zoom of 2 to 5 s into or out of a face, and a handheld follow of 1 to 1.5 m/s at a key moment.
  • Cut at an average shot length of 5 to 8 s and let a dialogue scene run in a single take of 30 to 90 s.
  • Print with a low-key grade, a lifted contrast of +20% and a desaturated palette of earth and brown.
  • Use source music from a car radio or a jukebox as the score, at −18 to −20 dB under the dialogue.
  • End on a freeze, an unresolved image or a bleak close, with no moral summing up.

Anti-patterns

  • A studio back lot with a painted sky
  • A glamour key with a soft filter on the star
  • A heroic, resolved ending
  • An orchestral score that tells the viewer what to feel
  • A clean, saturated Technicolor palette
  • Back projection in a car scene
  • A hero with no moral flaw
