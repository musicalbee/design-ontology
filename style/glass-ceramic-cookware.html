Glass-ceramic cookware

ceramics · 1958–1990 · also: Corning Ware, Pyroceram cookware, Freezer-to-oven ware

Oven and flame ware made of a white glass-ceramic, a glass that is crystallised by heat. The body takes the gas flame and the freezer, and a blue cornflower decal marks the early range.

Core thesis

A cook wants one pot that goes from the freezer to the flame to the table, so the body is a glass that heat turns into a fine crystal ceramic.

Design principles

  • Melt a lithium-aluminosilicate glass with a nucleating agent, and form it as glass at about 1500 °C.
  • Heat the formed glass to about 750 to 1100 °C in a second step, called ceramming, so that fine crystals grow in it.
  • Leave the surface smooth, glossy and white; no separate glaze is added.
  • Use a wall 3 to 6 mm thick; keep the thickness even, so that the heat spreads.
  • Print a blue cornflower on the side with a decal in a low firing.
  • Fit a plastic handle or a glass lid with a rim that sits in the wall.
  • Make the shapes plain and round: a casserole, a saucepan, a skillet and a baking dish.

Anti-patterns

  • A thick, uneven stoneware wall
  • A hand-painted pattern
  • A deep glossy glaze with pooling
  • A red clay body
  • A rim that is not reinforced
