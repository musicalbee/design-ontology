Trench and field military

fashion · 1914–1945 · also: Trench coat, Officer's field dress, Khaki field dress

The dress of the officer and the soldier in the field. A double-breasted gabardine trench coat with a storm flap and a belt, and a khaki cotton field jacket with four pockets.

Core thesis

A uniform earns each strap, flap and pocket from a task in the mud: keep the rain out, carry the kit, and show rank.

Design principles

  • Cut the coat 10 to 15 cm below the knee, so that it covers the thigh in a crouch.
  • Double the front with a 10 cm overlap and two rows of six buttons.
  • Add a storm flap over the right shoulder, to throw rain from the front.
  • Add a gun flap over the chest; use a belt of the same cloth with a D-ring.
  • Use a rain cloth that sheds water and lets the body breathe.
  • Put a button-in lining in the coat so that the wearer can change it with the season.
  • Colour the cloth in khaki, stone or olive, the colours of dust and mud.

Anti-patterns

  • A bright, shiny rain cloth
  • A short, thin coat that soaks the knee
  • Decorative straps that carry nothing
  • A tight sleeve that blocks the rifle arm
  • A fashion colour of pink or white
