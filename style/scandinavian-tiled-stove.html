Scandinavian tiled stove

ceramics · 1750–1930 · also: Kakelugn, Swedish tile stove, Nordic faience stove

A tall stove clad in white glazed tiles that stores the heat of a short wood fire in a room. The Swedish kakelugn has thin tiles, a plain white face and a soft frame of mouldings.

Core thesis

A white tiled stove is both a machine for heat and a piece of furniture: the glaze reflects the light of a dark northern room.

Design principles

  • Clad a brick core with flat glazed tiles about 20 cm by 25 cm, and 15 to 20 mm thick.
  • Make the stove tall and slim, 2 to 3 m high, so that it fits the corner of a room.
  • Glaze the tiles in a plain white; add a few lines of blue or grey painting for decoration.
  • Finish the top with a cornice, a crown or an urn, and the base with a plinth.
  • Cut the flue in long windings of 5 to 8 m, so that the brick stores the heat of the fire.
  • Use a small iron door and a damper of brass, and keep the metal parts in a simple form.
  • Mould the cornice and the frames in the same clay, so that the tiles and the mouldings match.
  • Repeat the neoclassical motifs, the garland, the urn and the fluted column, in low relief.

Anti-patterns

  • Tiles in strong colours and heavy patterns
  • A cast-iron stove with a thin tile on the face
  • An open fire with no winding flue in the stove
  • A thick tile with a rough glaze
  • Loud baroque scroll work on the face
