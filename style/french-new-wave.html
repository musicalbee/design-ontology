French New Wave

film · 1959–1967 · also: Nouvelle Vague

Light 35mm cameras, fast black-and-white stock and real streets. A handheld camera, a jump cut and a director who shows the hand. The film talks about film.

Core thesis

A film is a personal essay shot on location with light gear, so the director's eye and the street replace the studio and the script department.

Design principles

  • Shoot on location with a handheld or wheelchair-dolly 35mm camera, at 24 fps and a 180° shutter (1/48 s).
  • Use available light with a bounce from a ceiling or a wall, at a key-to-fill ratio of about 2:1, on stock pushed 1 to 2 stops (ISO 400 to 1600 equivalent).
  • Frame at 1.37:1 or 1.66:1 with a 28mm to 50mm lens, and keep the camera at eye level, 1.4 to 1.7 m.
  • Cut at an average shot length of 5 to 8 s (estimate; check Cinemetrics for each title) and use a jump cut that removes 1 to 2 s inside one shot.
  • Let one tracking shot run 20 to 60 s along a street or a corridor, and let the actor look into the lens for 1 to 2 s.
  • Record sound on location or post-dub it, and let the music stop or switch in mid-bar, with no fade.
  • Cite a film or a book in a shot or a line, and break the fourth wall once in each sequence.

Anti-patterns

  • A studio set with a lit back wall
  • Invisible continuity editing that hides every cut
  • Three-point lighting at 4:1 on a star
  • A score that swells under each emotion
  • A tidy, literary plot with all questions closed
  • A camera on a crane for decoration
  • A glossy, saturated colour grade
