Luxury steel sports watch

fashion · 1972–now · also: Integrated bracelet sports watch, Steel luxury watch, Octagonal steel watch

A sports watch that costs more than a gold dress watch. It has a steel case with exposed screws, a patterned dial and a bracelet that grows from the case. The Royal Oak of 1972 and the Nautilus of 1976 set the type.

Core thesis

Steel can be luxury if the maker finishes it like gold, so the price comes from the hand work, not from the metal.

Design principles

  • Fuse the case and the bracelet into one flowing line, with no lug and no gap at the join.
  • Fix the bezel with visible screws or with a porthole shape, so that the case looks like a ship's window.
  • Alternate brushed and polished surfaces on every link, bevel and edge.
  • Cut a pattern in the dial, for example a tapisserie grid with 1 mm squares or horizontal lines.
  • Keep the case 7 to 10 mm thick and 39 to 42 mm wide, to wear like a dress watch.
  • Make the watch water resistant to 50 or 100 m, as a sports watch is.
  • Print baton markers and hands in the same tone as the case, with luminous fill.
  • Make the bracelet taper from 28 mm at the case to 18 mm at the clasp.

Anti-patterns

  • A leather strap on an integrated case
  • A dial with a plain flat finish and a cheap print
  • A case with round lugs that stand out from the line
  • A thick diving case with a rotating bezel
  • A bracelet with a thin hollow link
  • A decorated case with gemstones and enamel
