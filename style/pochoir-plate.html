Pochoir plate

print · 1908–1935 · also: Pochoir, Stencil colouring, Art Deco fashion plate, Gazette du Bon Ton style

A plate coloured by hand through a set of stencils, each one for a colour, in gouache and watercolour on a printed key line. Iribe and Lepape made the Poiret albums in 1908 and 1911; Barbier and the Gazette du Bon Ton made it the look of Art Deco fashion.

Core thesis

A stencil and a brush give a flat, opaque and luminous colour that no press can match.

Design principles

  • Print a thin key line in black or grey, then put each colour on by hand through its own stencil.
  • Use 4–30 flat, opaque colours of gouache, with gold and silver for accents.
  • Keep each colour area flat and clean, with a soft edge where the brush meets the stencil.
  • Use a slim, elongated figure in a simple pose against a large area of bare paper.
  • Let one bright colour (a rose, a jade, a lacquer red) carry the plate.
  • Add a decorative pattern only in the dress or the background, never in both.

Anti-patterns

  • A halftone dot or a gradient
  • Thick, dark outlines like a comic
  • A full-colour background that leaves no bare paper
  • Muddy colours mixed from overprints
