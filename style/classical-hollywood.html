Classical Hollywood

film · 1930–1960 · also: Studio-era Hollywood, Continuity style, Golden age Hollywood

A studio craft in which the camera, the light and the cut serve a clear story. Three-point light, a master shot with coverage and a smooth match cut. The style hides itself.

Core thesis

A film serves its story, so every device (light, angle, cut and score) is invisible and keeps the viewer's eye on the character and the action.

Design principles

  • Light each face with a three-point setup at a key-to-fill ratio of 3:1 to 4:1, a back light at 1.5 to 2 times the key and a soft eye light.
  • Shoot a master shot and cover each scene with a medium shot and a close-up from 3 to 5 set-ups, and keep the 180° line.
  • Cut for continuity on action, at an average shot length of 8 to 12 s, with the match cut hidden inside a movement.
  • Frame in 1.37:1 (1.33:1 to 1.37:1) at eye level, a 40mm to 75mm lens, with the star in a medium close-up in the centre third.
  • Use a dolly or a crane move of 0.3 to 0.8 m/s only to follow a character, and finish it on a stop.
  • Score under the scene with a leitmotif per character at −18 to −22 dB below the dialogue, and a rising cue before an act break.
  • Resolve the plot in three acts of 25%, 50% and 25% of the runtime and end on a closed, legible image.

Anti-patterns

  • A jump cut that shows the edit
  • A handheld shake with no motive
  • An open ending with no resolution
  • Available light with no fill
  • A camera that looks into the lens
  • A shot that breaks the 180° line without a reason
  • A location sound track with unclear dialogue
