New Objectivity photography

photography · 1920s–1930s · also: Neue Sachlichkeit photography, Neues Sehen

Sharp, close, frontal pictures of plants, tools, faces and machines, made in Germany between the wars. The camera sees more than the eye, and it names things without comment.

Core thesis

The lens is exact and unsentimental, so the honest picture shows the structure of a thing at close range and in full detail.

Design principles

  • Fill the frame with one object, shot at 0.3 to 1m, so that its structure becomes the pattern.
  • Use a 135mm to 210mm lens on a 9×12cm or 13×18cm plate, stopped down to f/32 for full sharpness.
  • Light from one side in open shade or a window, with a key-to-fill ratio of 2:1.
  • Place the subject frontal and centred, or in strict rows, with a neutral grey or white ground.
  • Print on glossy paper at grade 2 and show the whole tonal scale without toning.
  • Repeat the same view for a whole series of 20 to 100 plates, so that the differences become the content.

Anti-patterns

  • Soft focus, mist or hand-worked negatives
  • Dramatic angles for effect alone
  • Moody low-key lighting
  • Decorative props or backgrounds
  • Sepia or any toning
  • Captions that tell the viewer how to feel
