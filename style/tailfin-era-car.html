Tailfin-Era Car

fashion · 1948–1963 · also: Jet-age car, Fifties American car, Detroit chrome

The big American car of the jet age. A long, low body with tail fins, a wide chrome grille, a wrap-around windscreen and two-tone paint. The cabin has bright vinyl and cloth.

Core thesis

After the war a car shows plenty: each year it is longer, lower and brighter, with a fin and chrome that copy the jet aircraft.

Design principles

  • Raise a tail fin at the rear wing, from 5 cm in 1948 to 30 cm in 1959.
  • Widen the grille to the full width of the car, in a bar of chrome with bright teeth.
  • Curve the windscreen round the corners to give a wrap-around view.
  • Paint the body in two tones, split by a chrome spear along the flank.
  • Fit bumpers with bullet-shaped guards, and set the tail lamps in the fin.
  • Make the interior bright, with vinyl in a woven pattern, nylon cloth and an aluminium dashboard trim.
  • Change the body each year, so that a new model looks longer and lower than the last.

Anti-patterns

  • A plain, upright body with no fin
  • Small, thin bumpers
  • A narrow grille
  • A body in one dull colour
  • Hand-made panels with no chrome
