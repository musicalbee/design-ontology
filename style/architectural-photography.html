Architectural photography

photography · 1930s–today · also: Building photography, Modernist architecture photography

Photographs of buildings with upright lines, clean light and a chosen hour. The picture shows the form, the material and the way a building sits in its place.

Core thesis

A building is judged in a picture, so the picture must keep its lines true and choose the light that shows its form.

Design principles

  • Use a tilt-shift lens of 24mm to 45mm, or a view camera, and keep the camera back parallel to the facade, so that verticals stay at 0°.
  • Stop down to f/11 to f/16 at ISO 100, on a tripod, with a shutter of 1/15s to 2s.
  • Choose the hour: low sun at 15° to 30° for a facade, or blue hour with interior lamps lit for a glow.
  • Shoot at 1.5m height for a plan view of the facade, or raise to 3m to 6m for a clean top line.
  • Keep people small or absent; use a figure at 1/20 of the frame height for scale.
  • Grade to neutral colour: keep the true tone of the material, and keep shadow detail.

Anti-patterns

  • Converging verticals from a tilted camera
  • Wide-angle distortion below 17mm
  • Flat grey light with no shadow modelling
  • Heavy HDR halos
  • Cars and signs that block the form
  • Colour casts that change the material
