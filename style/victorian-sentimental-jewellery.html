Victorian Sentimental Jewellery

fashion · 1837–1901 · also: Victorian mourning and love jewellery, Sentimental jewels of the Victorian era

Jewels that hold a person or a feeling: lockets, hair work, cameos, snakes, hearts, and black jet for mourning. They were made in gold, jet and pearl in Britain after 1837.

Core thesis

A jewel carries a memory: it holds a lock of hair, a name or a likeness close to the body.

Design principles

  • Give each piece a hollow or a glass front for a lock of hair, a portrait or a monogram.
  • Carve jet or conch shell by hand into a flower, a hand, a cross or a head in relief.
  • Make mourning jewellery in black jet for the first year and the first two years of widowhood, with pearls as tears.
  • Shape the piece as a symbol: a heart, a snake with a tail in its mouth, a clasped hand, an anchor or a forget-me-not.
  • Engrave a date, a name or a motto on the back of the piece.
  • Wear a locket on a long chain or a short velvet ribbon, at the centre of the chest or the throat.
  • Set small stones in a ring so that the first letters of the stones spell a word, such as REGARD.
  • Fit a brooch with a safety catch and a hinged back, for a pin that holds a shawl or a collar.

Anti-patterns

  • Platinum and white diamond lines of 1905
  • Bare, plain metal with no message
  • Bright plastic or paste costume sparkle
  • Abstract forms with no symbol
  • Machine-bright chrome finish
  • Large cocktail stones
