Memphis product

industrial · 1981–1990 · also: Memphis Milano objects, Memphis design furniture, Postmodern laminate furniture

The objects of the Memphis group: bookcases, tables, lamps and ceramics built from bright laminate, terrazzo and coloured glass, in stacked geometric shapes with loud patterns.

Core thesis

An object is a toy for the mind: it joins cheap, bright materials with symbols and jokes, and it refuses the rule that good taste is quiet.

Design principles

  • Build each piece from 3 to 7 simple solids: a cube, a cone, a sphere, a slab and a bar.
  • Cover a flat plane with plastic laminate in a bold pattern of dots, stripes, squiggles or speckles.
  • Use 4 or more contrasting colours on one object; set a pastel next to a saturated hue.
  • Make the form asymmetrical: a bookcase has arms, steps and angled shelves.
  • Join cheap and rich materials: laminate with terrazzo, or enamel with coloured glass.
  • Put a visible joint or a colour change on every junction where two solids meet.
  • Give each object a name and a joke; use it as a sign more than as a tool.

Anti-patterns

  • One neutral colour on the whole object
  • Symmetrical forms with plain faces
  • Natural wood left raw and unpatterned
  • Hidden joints and flush panels
  • Chrome tube functionalism
  • Quiet grey and beige palettes
