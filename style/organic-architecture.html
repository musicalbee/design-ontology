Organic Architecture

architecture · 1905–present · also: Organic design, Wrightian organic, Usonian organic

A building grows from its site like a plant. Wright, Aalto and Goff set the plan, the structure and the material in one flowing whole that opens to the land, with no fixed façade or box.

Core thesis

A building should grow from its site and its use as a living form: the whole and the parts belong together, and the plan flows with the land.

Design principles

  • Fit the building to the site: set the floor levels to the slope and let rocks, trees and water keep their place.
  • Plan as a continuous flow with few doors, and open each room to 2 or more sides of the landscape.
  • Use the cantilever, the curve and the spiral, in spans of 3 to 6m, to make the structure and the form one thing.
  • Use few materials, 3 or 4, left in their natural state, and run them from the inside to the outside.
  • Compress the entry to 2.0 to 2.3m, then release it into a space of 3.5 to 5m.
  • Design the built-in furniture, the light and the garden as parts of the same form.

Anti-patterns

  • A closed box set on a levelled site
  • Applied historical ornament
  • A rigid orthogonal grid that ignores the site
  • Glossy synthetic surfaces and painted-over materials
  • Rooms cut off by corridors and doors
  • A building that dominates the landscape
