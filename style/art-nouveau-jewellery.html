Art Nouveau Jewellery

fashion · 1895–1914 · also: Jugendstil jewellery, Lalique-style jewels, Plique-a-jour jewellery

Jewels of the whiplash line. They show women, insects, orchids and dragonflies in enamel, glass, horn and opal. The design counts for more than the price of the stones.

Core thesis

A jewel is a work of art: the design, the enamel and the line count for more than the value of the diamonds.

Design principles

  • Draw the piece in one flowing, asymmetrical line that comes from a stem, a wave or a lock of hair.
  • Choose a subject from nature or from a woman's body: a dragonfly, an orchid, a poppy, a swan, a head with loose hair.
  • Use enamel in many thin layers, and use plique-a-jour with no back plate, so that light passes through the colour.
  • Mix precious and cheap materials by their look: horn, glass and opal beside gold and diamonds.
  • Keep diamonds small and use them as a point of light, not as the main form.
  • Set a large opal, a moonstone or a piece of carved glass at the centre of the piece.
  • Make each piece a unique work and sign it on the front or the back.
  • Hang a pendant on a fine chain so that the piece moves with the body.

Anti-patterns

  • Rows of equal stones in a symmetrical frame
  • Closed foil-backed paste
  • Heavy black mourning jet
  • Straight geometric lines of the 1920s
  • Large bright diamonds as the main form
  • Cold white platinum open work
