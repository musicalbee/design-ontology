Nishiki-e multi-block colour woodblock print

print · 1765–1900s · also: Nishiki-e, 錦絵, Brocade picture, Edo colour woodblock print

A drawing is pasted on a cherry plank and cut by a carver. A printer rubs water-based pigment from one block for each colour onto damp washi with a baren. The colours are flat and soft, and the wood grain shows.

Core thesis

A set of wood blocks, one for each colour, can make a full-colour picture when a printer rubs thin water-based colour into damp paper and keeps each block in register by hand.

Design principles

  • Print a sumi key block first. Keep its line 0.2–0.5 mm wide and unbroken, so the carver can follow it.
  • Give each flat colour its own block. Use 3–12 blocks, and let the inks mix where two colours overlap.
  • Cut two L-shaped register marks (kento) on each block, so each colour lands on the key line within 0.5 mm.
  • Make a gradation (bokashi) by wiping the ink on the block with a damp cloth. Place it at the sky, the water and the hem of a robe.
  • Leave the wood grain in the large flat areas. Do not hide it with extra ink.
  • Press a bare, inkless block into the sheet (karazuri) where you need a raised line, for example on snow or a collar.
  • Print on thin damp washi, and rub the back with a baren in a circle until the colour sinks into the fibre.

Anti-patterns

  • A thick, even outline round each shape
  • A smooth digital gradient with no hand-wipe edge
  • A halftone dot or a photographic tone
  • Glossy, opaque, uniform ink
  • Perfect register with no kento shift or grain
