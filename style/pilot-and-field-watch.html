Pilot and field watch

fashion · 1931–now · also: Flieger watch, Observation watch, Military field watch

The legible tool watch for the cockpit and the field. It has a steel case, a matt black dial with large white luminous numerals, a large crown for gloves and a long strap. The Hour Angle watch of 1931 and the German observation watches of the 1930s set the type.

Core thesis

A tool watch must be read at a glance, in poor light and with a glove on. Each part serves legibility and nothing else.

Design principles

  • Print white Arabic numerals on a matt black dial, and mark the 12 with a triangle and two dots.
  • Cut the hands as bold batons or swords filled with luminous paint, and make the minute hand reach the track.
  • Make the crown 7 to 9 mm wide, as an onion or a fluted wheel, so that a gloved hand can wind it.
  • Make a field watch 34 to 38 mm wide, and a pilot's watch 40 to 55 mm wide that goes over a flight sleeve.
  • Fit a soft-iron cage round the movement, so that the magnetic fields of aircraft instruments cannot move the balance.
  • Keep the dial matt, so that the glare stays low in a cockpit.
  • Cut the strap 200 to 250 mm long, so that it goes over a flight jacket, and rivet it to the lugs.
  • Leave the dial free of ornament, logos and extra scales.

Anti-patterns

  • A gold or enamel dial
  • Small numerals and thin hands that do not glow
  • A mirror-polished case that flashes in the sun
  • A dial that is crowded with sub dials and windows
  • A thin dress case that a knock can bend
  • A short strap that fits only a bare wrist
