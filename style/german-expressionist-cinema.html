German Expressionist cinema

film · 1920–1927 · also: Caligarism, Expressionist film

A studio cinema of painted sets, hard single-source light and distorted perspective. The frame shows the mind of the character, not the street. Black-and-white prints are tinted by scene.

Core thesis

The set, the light and the body show inner fear, so the picture is built as a state of mind and not recorded as a place.

Design principles

  • Light each scene with one hard key at a key-to-fill ratio of 8:1 or more, and paint or cast shadows onto walls and floor so that the shadow is a shape in the set.
  • Build sets with no true right angle: lean walls, doors and stairs 5° to 20° off the vertical, and make doorways taper by 10% to 30%.
  • Frame in 1.33:1 and place the figure in the lower third, with 50% or more of the frame left to a looming shadow or a steep diagonal.
  • Cut at an average shot length of 6 to 10 s and hold a silhouette or a stare for 4 s or more before the cut.
  • Open and close scenes with an iris of 0.5 to 1 s, and use a slow dissolve of 1 to 2 s between a place and a memory.
  • Tint the print by scene: amber for lit interiors, blue for night, green for the uncanny, and keep each tint for the whole scene.
  • Direct the actor in held, stylised gesture: 1 to 2 s to a pose, hands and eyes wide, no naturalistic business.

Anti-patterns

  • Location photography of real streets and rooms
  • Even, soft studio light with fill above 3:1
  • Straight vertical walls and a level horizon
  • Naturalistic acting at everyday speed
  • Rapid cutting under 3 s average shot length
  • A single neutral grey grade over the whole film
  • Handheld camera shake
