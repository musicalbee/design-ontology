Malletier luggage

fashion · 1854–1939 · also: Trunk-maker's luggage, Flat-topped travel trunk

The travel trunks of the French malletier. A light wooden frame carries coated canvas or leather, bound with brass and fitted to its use. The flat lid stacks in a ship's hold or on a train.

Core thesis

A trunk is a small room that a traveller trusts, and each part of it serves a use.

Design principles

  • Make the lid flat, so that trunks stack 3 high in a hold or a luggage van.
  • Build the frame of poplar wood, 8 to 12 mm thick, light and strong.
  • Cover the frame in canvas coated with grey lacquer, so that it sheds rain.
  • Bind the edges and corners with brass and secure them with 4 to 6 mm nails.
  • Fit a tumbler lock and two or four latches in brass.
  • Fit the inside to the use: a wardrobe trunk with rails, a bed trunk, a hat trunk, a tray for linen.
  • Line the inside with striped linen cloth, and add initials by hand.
  • Repeat a stripe, a check or a monogram on the canvas, so that a porter knows the trunk at a glance.

Anti-patterns

  • A soft bag with a zip
  • Moulded plastic shells
  • A round, domed lid that cannot stack
  • Chrome hardware
  • Bright nylon covers
