Neo-grotesque sans

typeface · 1957–present · also: Neo-grotesk, Swiss sans, Realist sans, Helvetica style

The neutral sans of the grid: even widths, horizontal terminals, closed apertures, a large x-height and almost no contrast. It lets the layout speak and is the default voice of interfaces and corporate systems.

Core thesis

A typeface is a neutral vessel: it carries the message and adds nothing of its own.

Design principles

  • Keep the stroke contrast at 1.0–1.4:1, measured on the o.
  • Cut the terminals of c, e, s and a horizontal, so the apertures close.
  • Even the widths: the capitals are close to one width, and the round letters are wide.
  • Hold the x-height at 0.70–0.78 of the cap height.
  • Use a single-storey g and a two-storey a; the R has a vertical or curved leg.
  • Track tighter as the size grows: 0 em at 16 px, −0.02 em at 48 px, −0.03 em above 96 px.

Anti-patterns

  • Visible calligraphic stress
  • Quirky, uneven letter widths
  • Decorative swashes or alternates
  • Wide positive tracking on lower-case headlines
  • Mixing more than two families with it
