Bistronomie

cuisine · 1992–present · also: Bistro gastronomique, Neo-bistro

A chef of a palace kitchen cooks at the price of a bistro. Yves Camdeborde opened La Régalade in Paris in 1992 with a fixed menu, honest produce, regional dishes and big portions, in a plain room with no linen and no ceremony.

Core thesis

Good cooking does not need a palace: a trained chef can serve fine produce and exact technique in a simple room at a fair price.

Design principles

  • Fix a menu of three courses at a low price, with a choice of a few dishes for each course.
  • Write the menu on a blackboard, and change it with the market.
  • Cook the dishes of the French provinces with the skill of a hotel kitchen: confit, terrine, braise and stew.
  • Serve a generous portion that fills a hungry guest.
  • Put a terrine or a pot of rillettes on the table at the start, and let the guests help themselves.
  • Use cheap cuts and offal with care: cheek, trotter, tongue and shoulder.
  • Plate on a plain plate or in the cooking pot, and use no formal service.
  • Seat guests close together in a room with a bar, bare tables and a short wine list.

Anti-patterns

  • A white tablecloth and a waiter in a jacket
  • A tiny portion on a large plate
  • A long tasting menu at a high price
  • A dish made with foam, gel or liquid nitrogen
  • A menu that does not change with the market
  • A garnish of micro herbs placed with tweezers
