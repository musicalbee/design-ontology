Memphis postmodern ceramics

ceramics · 1981–1990 · also: Memphis Milano ceramics, Postmodern ceramic vessels, Italian postmodern pottery

Ceramic vessels from the Memphis group of Milan after 1981. They combine plain geometric solids with loud colour and patterns of stripes, dots and terrazzo, and they ignore good taste.

Core thesis

A vase is a toy, a totem and a joke at the same time: its form is a stack of simple solids, and its surface is a pattern in loud colour.

Design principles

  • Build the form from 3 to 5 simple solids: a cone, a sphere, a cylinder, a cube and a ring.
  • Use 4 or more bright colours on one piece, with black and white as colours too.
  • Cover the surface with stripes, dots, zigzags or terrazzo flecks in a regular repeat.
  • Put two patterns on one object, and let them clash.
  • Make the glaze flat and glossy, so that the form and the colour read as one graphic.
  • Name each piece after a place or a myth, and sign it with the name of the designer.
  • Print the pattern by screen print or decal, and fix it in a second firing at 750 to 850 °C.
  • Treat the vessel as a statement, not as a holder: a vase may have no opening that works.

Anti-patterns

  • Natural earth colour and a hand-made look
  • A single tone and a plain surface
  • Symmetry that is quiet and balanced
  • Traditional ornament such as scrolls and flowers
  • A thin, elegant, classical profile
