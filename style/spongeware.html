Spongeware

ceramics · 1840–1930 · also: Spatterware, Sponge-decorated earthenware, Sponged export ware

Cheap white earthenware that potters dabbed with a cut sea sponge dipped in colour. The soft blue or green mottle fills a bowl, a jug or a canister with little skill.

Core thesis

A sponge turns a cheap white pot into a coloured one in a few seconds, so that a worker without training can decorate a hundred pieces in an hour.

Design principles

  • Make a plain, white-bodied pot in a mould or on a jigger, with a wall 4 to 6 mm thick.
  • Dab the colour on the biscuit with a cut sponge dipped in a stain; use 1 to 4 colours.
  • Stamp the sponge in a pattern of dots, or dab it in a loose mottle.
  • Keep a plain white band at the rim and the foot for contrast.
  • Use blue first, then green, brown and red; add a hand-painted line or a flower in some pieces.
  • Dip the pot in clear glaze and fire it at about 1050 to 1100 °C.
  • Make useful shapes: a mixing bowl, a jug, a butter dish and a canister.

Anti-patterns

  • A neat engraved pattern
  • A thick hand-trailed slip
  • A deep, glossy glaze with pooling
  • A unified white ground with no colour
  • Gold or lustre
