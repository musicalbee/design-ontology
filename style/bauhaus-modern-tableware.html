Bauhaus modern tableware

ceramics · 1930–1960 · also: Functional porcelain, Neue Sachlichkeit tableware, Modernist white porcelain service

Plain white porcelain made for a factory, in simple solid forms. The designers drop the ornament and the gold, and shape a service that stacks, pours well and costs little to make.

Core thesis

A service is a tool for the table: it is a few simple solids, in white, that fit one another and give good use.

Design principles

  • Build each piece from a few simple solids: a cylinder, a sphere, a flat disc.
  • Keep the surface white and undecorated, and let the glaze and the form carry the look.
  • Make the pieces stack and nest, so that a service takes little room in a cupboard.
  • Set the handle and the spout by use: the hand fits the handle, and the lip does not drip.
  • Reduce the service to 20 to 30 shapes that serve all meals.
  • Cast the piece in a mould so that each one is the same as the next.
  • Print or band only a single thin line in a colour when the buyer asks for it.

Anti-patterns

  • A gilt rim with scrolls and flowers
  • A modelled relief of leaves and shells
  • A hand-painted floral pattern in many colours
  • A thick, hand-thrown wall with a visible tool mark
