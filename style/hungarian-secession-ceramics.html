Hungarian Secession ceramics

ceramics · 1893–1914 · also: Zsolnay architectural ceramics, Lechner ceramic roofs, Hungarian national romantic ceramics

Colourful architectural ceramics of the Hungarian Secession. Ödön Lechner and the Zsolnay factory made frost-proof pyrogranite tiles and iridescent eosin glazes for roofs, domes and facades.

Core thesis

A national style needs a national material: frost-proof, bright clay tiles with Hungarian folk flowers and Eastern patterns make a roof into the face of a building.

Design principles

  • Cover the roof with glazed tiles in 3 or 4 colours, laid in zigzag and diamond patterns.
  • Mould the tiles from pyrogranite, a clay fired at a very high heat that resists frost.
  • Draw the ornament from Hungarian folk art: tulips, pomegranates, peacocks and stylised leaves.
  • Use the iridescent eosin glaze on the finials, the crown pieces and the wall panels.
  • Frame the arches and the gates with bands of relief and a ceramic mosaic.
  • Fire the tiles in a single, hot firing so that the colours do not fade.
  • Set a bright ridge, a finial and a tower top on the roof line.

Anti-patterns

  • A plain grey roof of slate or metal
  • Gothic or classical ornament with no folk pattern
  • A matt single-colour roof tile
  • Thin, delicate porcelain in place of a hard tile
  • Fine details in a pale, even glaze
