New Colour

photography · 1970s–early 1980s · also: New Color, Colour as subject, Dye-transfer vernacular

Colour photography of the plain American scene: a car park, a kitchen, a tricycle. Saturated dye-transfer and slide colour carries the picture, and the composition looks casual and exact at the same time.

Core thesis

Colour is the subject and not a decoration, and the everyday world is rich enough to need no drama.

Design principles

  • Choose the subject for its colour relationships, and keep to three or four dominant hues in the frame.
  • Shoot on slide or dye-transfer stock at ISO 25 to 64 for deep, saturated colour with a fine grain.
  • Use a 35mm to 50mm lens at f/5.6 to f/11 and a low or oblique viewpoint at 1 to 3m.
  • Accept the light as found: midday sun, shop windows and fluorescent tubes, and let the mixed colour casts show.
  • Keep the composition casual but exact: put the subject off centre, with a strong colour mass in balance.
  • Show the ordinary: cars, diners, kitchens and car parks, with no hero and no punchline.

Anti-patterns

  • Black and white conversion or desaturated grading
  • Golden hour glow and a colour cast added in the lab
  • Centred hero subjects with a clear message
  • Picture-postcard scenery
  • Shallow depth of field that blurs the colour of the background
  • A uniform filter look over the whole frame
