Bias-cut glamour

fashion · 1927–1939 · also: Bias-cut gown, Vionnet cut, 1930s Hollywood gown

The long, close evening gown of the 1930s: silk satin cut on the cross grain, so that it drapes and follows the body.

Core thesis

Silk cut across the grain drapes by itself, so that the body shows a line with no boning and no pads.

Design principles

  • Cut the cloth at 45 degrees to the grain, so that it stretches up to 20 per cent.
  • Drape the pattern on a small wooden doll, 80 cm high, before the full-size cut.
  • Keep seams to a minimum: a gown needs two to four main pieces.
  • Let the waist return to the natural line, and let the gown run in a smooth curve over the hip.
  • Hang the skirt for 24 hours before the hem is sewn, so that the bias settles.
  • Choose a heavy crêpe or a satin of 70 to 100 g per square metre.
  • Finish with a cowl neck, a low back or a halter, and no other trim.

Anti-patterns

  • A boxy 1920s tube with a low waist
  • Stiff boned bodices and petticoats
  • Cloth cut on the straight grain
  • A heavy beaded surface
  • Thick, ribbed or napped cloth
  • A short, cropped hem line
