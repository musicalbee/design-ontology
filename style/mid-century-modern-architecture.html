Mid-century Modern

architecture · 1940s–1960s · also: Californian Modern, Case Study House style, Usonian-influenced modern

A low house of post-and-beam timber or steel, with glass walls to the garden, a flat or low-pitched roof and a warm palette. It made the International Style domestic and suited it to climate, family and the car.

Core thesis

Modern architecture can be warm: the free plan and the glass wall serve everyday life, and the house belongs to its garden.

Design principles

  • Build on a post-and-beam frame with a module of 1.2m (4ft) or 1.5m, so that walls, doors and glass fit the grid.
  • Keep the house low: one storey, or two with a split level, with a ceiling height of 2.4 to 2.7m.
  • Open one long side to the garden with floor-to-ceiling glass or sliding doors, 50 to 80% of that wall.
  • Run the roof as a wide overhang, 0.9 to 1.5m, with a flat or low-pitch form of 1:12 to 3:12, so that the shade is part of the glass wall.
  • Use warm natural materials inside and outside: wood ceilings, brick or stone feature walls, and slate or terrazzo floors.
  • Place a solid core, the fireplace and the service block, at the centre, and let the living spaces wrap round it.

Anti-patterns

  • Steep pitched roofs with small gables
  • Applied historical ornament or colonial trim
  • Closed, compartmented rooms with no view of the garden
  • Cold, all-white and all-glass surfaces with no timber or stone
  • Heavy raw concrete at monumental scale
  • Tall, narrow, vertical proportions
