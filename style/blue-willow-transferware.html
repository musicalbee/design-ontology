Blue willow transferware

ceramics · 1790–1900 · also: Willow pattern, Blue and white transfer-printed earthenware, English blue printed ware

White earthenware that carries a blue pattern in the Chinese manner, printed from an engraved copper plate. The willow pattern, with a bridge, a tree, a boat and two birds, is the best known of the many designs.

Core thesis

A copper plate puts a Chinese-style blue picture on cheap white earthenware, so that every household owns the pattern that once only a rich house could buy.

Design principles

  • Engrave the pattern on a copper plate in fine lines, and print it with cobalt ink on thin tissue.
  • Rub the wet tissue on the biscuit ware, so that the blue lines pass to the surface.
  • Fix the print in a low firing, then dip the piece in clear glaze, and fire it again.
  • Arrange the picture in a ring: a border, a main scene, and a small repeat on the rim.
  • Draw the willow scene with a bridge, a tree, a pagoda, a boat, a fence and two birds in the sky.
  • Use one tone of blue that is deep and even, and a clear glaze that stays white.
  • Put the pattern name and the maker mark in the base, so that a customer can order again.

Anti-patterns

  • A hand-painted line with a free, uneven brush
  • A thick opaque tin glaze
  • A multi-colour overglaze print
  • A moulded relief with no printed pattern
