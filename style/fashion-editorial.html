Fashion editorial photography

photography · 1930s–today · also: Magazine fashion, High-fashion editorial

Planned pictures of clothes, bodies and attitude for a magazine page. Light, set, pose and edit are all designed, so that the image sells a world and not only a garment.

Core thesis

Fashion sells a feeling before it sells a thing, so the photograph builds the whole world around the clothes.

Design principles

  • Use an 85mm to 135mm lens at f/5.6 to f/11, so that the body keeps its proportion and the clothes stay sharp.
  • Light with one large source: a 1.2m to 2m soft box or a beauty dish, with a key-to-fill ratio of 3:1 to 8:1.
  • Pose from the hip and the neck: make long lines, and give the hands a task.
  • Choose a set, a location or a colour sweep that carries the story, and place the model in it with 40–60% negative space.
  • Retouch skin to a natural texture, and grade for a clear mood: pastel, hard flash or film noir.
  • Shoot 300 to 1000 frames for 6 to 12 pages.

Anti-patterns

  • Flat catalogue light with a stiff front pose
  • Heavy beauty-filter skin and plastic textures
  • Shots with no set, no story and no direction
  • Stock poses that ignore the garment
  • Wide-angle distortion of the body
  • Competing props that hide the clothes
