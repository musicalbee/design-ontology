Planimetric symmetry

film · 1996–today · also: Dollhouse frame, Centred tableau, Flat staging

A frame built like a diagram: one central axis, a flat back wall, pastel colour blocks and a camera that tracks or pans at right angles. Every object is placed. The look is a miniature world with a dry joke.

Core thesis

A film is a constructed object, so the frame is composed as a flat, symmetrical tableau and the camera moves only at right angles to it.

Design principles

  • Put the key subject on the vertical axis of the frame and mirror the set about it, so that 90% or more of the visual weight is balanced left to right.
  • Shoot head-on to the back wall, with the lens axis at 90° to it, and keep the horizon and all verticals true.
  • Move the camera only on the three right-angle axes: a track along the wall at 0.5 to 1.5 m/s, a pan of 90° or 180° at a steady rate, or a straight push-in over 3 to 6 s.
  • Limit the palette to 4 to 6 flat colours, one dominant at 40% to 50% of the frame, with the rest in blocks of 5% to 15%.
  • Stage the actors in a line or a pyramid on one or two planes, facing the lens, and give a deadpan line with no overlap.
  • Cut at an average shot length of 3 to 5 s and use a whip pan or a title card of 1 to 3 s as a chapter break.
  • Fix the frame size in advance for each part: 1.37:1, 1.85:1 or 2.39:1, and change it only at a chapter.

Anti-patterns

  • A handheld camera that follows the actor
  • An off-axis, canted or random angle
  • A natural, un-graded, muddy colour palette
  • A cluttered frame with unplaced objects
  • Overlapping, naturalistic dialogue
  • Shallow depth of field with a blurred background
  • A lens flare or a bokeh effect for mood
