Industrial utility product

industrial · 1910–present · also: Factory and workshop fixtures, Industrial vernacular, Utility hardware

Fixtures made for work, not for show: enamel shades, cage lamps, cast iron, steel lockers, conduit and brass ship hardware. The form follows the duty, and the finish is hard and repairable.

Core thesis

A tool-like object earns its form from the job it does and the strain it takes; any surface that does no work is left off.

Design principles

  • Make each part for one duty: a shade throws light down, a cage guards the bulb, a hook holds a load.
  • Choose cast iron, mild steel, aluminium and glass for strength; use 2 to 4 mm of steel for a shade or a locker.
  • Coat steel in vitreous enamel or powder paint that is hard, washable and 0.1 to 0.3 mm thick.
  • Show every bolt, rivet and weld; a worker must be able to repair each part with a spanner.
  • Use a simple dome, a cylinder or a box with a radius of 3 to 10 mm on the edges.
  • Use stock sizes: standard tube, pipe, sheet and bar, in a few colours such as white, black and grey.
  • Add a guard, a cage or a rib only where the object needs it, and keep the guard open.

Anti-patterns

  • Applied ornament or a revival motif
  • Hidden fasteners and a smooth, sealed body
  • Soft textile, velvet and silk
  • Marble and gilt
  • Pastel and decorative colour
  • A thin, fragile section that fails in use
