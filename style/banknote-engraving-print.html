Steel-engraved banknote and stamp

print · 1819–1990 · also: Stahlstich (German), taille-douce de securite (French), line-engraved security print

Hardened steel plates carry portraits, vignettes and geometric rosettes, printed from a transfer press. Dense fine lines and machine-turned patterns are hard to copy. The print has a tight frame of line work in green or black, with a coloured tint.

Core thesis

Fine engraved line and machine-turned rosettes on steel are hard to copy. The print is then the proof that a note or a stamp is real.

Design principles

  • Engrave the portrait and the vignette with fine parallel lines, 150–200 lines an inch.
  • Cut the border with a rose engine, so that the guilloche lines interlace with a constant pitch.
  • Print the key plate in intaglio black or green, and a tint plate behind it in a lighter colour.
  • Print the serial numbers and the seal in a second colour, by letterpress.
  • Frame the picture with a closed border, with a title in engraved script.
  • Print from a transferred steel plate, so that many plates carry the same die.

Anti-patterns

  • A halftone or a photographic portrait
  • Brush texture or soft wash
  • Flat vector shapes
  • A neon or fluorescent colour
  • A coarse line that a copier can reproduce
