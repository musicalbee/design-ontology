Jasperware (Neoclassical)

ceramics · 1775–1830 · also: Wedgwood jasper, Jasper ware, Etruria jasper

A fine, dense, unglazed stoneware that Josiah Wedgwood made from 1775. It is stained in pale blue, green or lilac and carries white relief figures in the classical manner.

Core thesis

A matt coloured stoneware with white Greek and Roman reliefs lets an English factory make the antique world for the library table.

Design principles

  • Stain the whole body, not only the surface, with cobalt, so that the blue shows in each chip.
  • Leave the surface unglazed and fine-polished, so that it has a velvet matt look.
  • Press each white relief in a pitcher mould, 1 to 3 mm thick, and fix it to the damp body with water.
  • Take the figures from Greek vases and Roman gems: dancing women, sacrifices, Cupid and muses.
  • Use the solid jasper for fine pieces and the jasper dip, a thin coloured skin on a white body, for cheaper pieces.
  • Divide the surface into bands: a laurel or key-pattern border, a main frieze and a plain foot.
  • Fire the body once to full density at about 1200 to 1250 °C.
  • Mark the base with the factory name impressed in the clay.

Anti-patterns

  • A glossy glaze over the relief
  • Reliefs painted on in colour
  • A coarse or speckled body
  • Rococo scrolls and asymmetric forms
  • Reliefs that are thick and soft, with no sharp edge
