Chicago School terracotta

ceramics · 1885–1930 · also: Chicago terracotta cladding, Sullivanesque terracotta, Skyscraper terracotta

Thin, fireproof terracotta cladding on the steel frames of early skyscrapers. Architects in Chicago used it for flat, white or cream walls and for flowing plant ornament.

Core thesis

When a steel frame carries the load, the wall becomes a skin: a thin clay tile protects the steel from fire and carries the ornament.

Design principles

  • Hang a thin hollow terracotta tile on the steel frame; the tile carries no load.
  • Keep the wall flat and divided in a grid that shows the frame behind it.
  • Use a cream, buff or white glaze, so that the clay shows the same colour from the street.
  • Model the ornament as flowing plants and geometric knots, in low relief, over the piers and the spandrels.
  • Fire the tile hard so that it protects the steel from fire and rain.
  • Make each piece in a mould, and fit it to a number on the plan.
  • Cover the whole facade with one material, from the base to the cornice.

Anti-patterns

  • A thick, load-carrying stone wall
  • Heavy classical ornament with no link to the frame
  • A dark brick wall with no cladding
  • Glossy, bright colour on the whole facade
  • Hand-carved stone details
