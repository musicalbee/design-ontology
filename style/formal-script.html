Formal script

typeface · 1740–present · also: Copperplate script, English roundhand, Script typeface, Engraver's script

Joined, sloped letters from the pointed pen of the eighteenth-century writing master. Pressure makes the downstrokes swell and the upstrokes become hairlines. It speaks of invitations, ceremony and romance, and reads well only in short lines.

Core thesis

A letter is one movement of a flexible pointed pen: pressure on the downstroke, a hairline on the upstroke, and every letter joined to the next.

Design principles

  • Slope the letters 50–55° from the baseline, at one constant angle.
  • Swell the downstrokes and keep the upstrokes and the joins as hairlines.
  • Join each lower-case letter to the next with a hairline that leaves from the baseline or the x-height.
  • Keep the x-height small, 0.40–0.60 of the cap height, so the loops of the ascenders and descenders have room.
  • Make the capitals large, with oval loops and one or two flourishes, and never set a word in them.
  • Set the text with no letter spacing, so the joins meet.

Anti-patterns

  • Words set in all capitals
  • Positive tracking that breaks the joins
  • Long paragraphs of body text
  • Fake slope made by the software on an upright face
  • Script and blackletter in one line
