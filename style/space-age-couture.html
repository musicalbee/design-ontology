Space Age couture

fashion · 1964–1970 · also: Space Age fashion, Courrèges look, Cosmos line, Moon Girl look

The Paris couture of 1964 to 1970 that took its look from the space race. It uses white and silver, short A-line dresses and flat trousers. It also has vinyl, plastic and metal discs, and low boots with a visor or helmet hat.

Core thesis

Dress for the future shows clean, geometric lines and new materials. The body moves free in a short, flat, hard-edged shape of white, silver and plastic.

Design principles

  • Cut the hem 10 to 15 cm above the knee on a day dress, and keep the skirt in a plain A-line.
  • Make each seam a visible line, with a flat welt or a top-stitched seam in a contrast colour.
  • Choose a stiff cloth with body, such as heavy gabardine, that holds the A-line away from the leg.
  • Use white, silver and one bright colour, and keep the plain areas wide.
  • Cut a window, a circle or a square into the dress, so that the shape is geometric.
  • Join plastic or metal discs of 5 to 8 cm with small rings in place of thread.
  • Finish with flat boots, a visor or a helmet cap, and tinted glasses.

Anti-patterns

  • Soft ruffles and lace
  • A fitted waist with a full petticoat skirt
  • Floral prints and pastel tones
  • Heavy ornament and fringe
  • A high heel and a long skirt
  • Visible hand sewing and a worn, aged cloth
