Film noir

film · 1941–1958 · also: Noir, Classic noir

A black and white crime film lit from one hard source. Venetian-blind bars, rain and smoke cut the frame. The camera sits low and tilts. A tired man tells the story in voice-over and the plot closes on him.

Core thesis

One hard lamp, a low camera and a narrow strip of light turn a city into a trap, so the viewer feels the fate of the hero before the hero does.

Design principles

  • Light the set with 1 hard key and no fill, and let 40% to 60% of the frame fall to black.
  • Shoot in black and white at 1.37:1 with a 25mm to 35mm lens and deep focus on 3 planes.
  • Put the camera 0.5 to 1.0 m low, and tilt it 5° to 15° off the level for a tense scene.
  • Throw bars, rain and smoke shadows across a face with a gobo or a slatted blind.
  • Cut at an average shot length of 6 to 10 s and use a long take for a talk in a car or a room.
  • Tell the story through a voice-over by the hero, and let it run at 5% to 15% of the runtime.
  • Set the scene at night in a city, in a bar, a flat or an office with a lit sign outside.

Anti-patterns

  • Flat, even high-key light
  • A sunny day exterior
  • A bright happy ending with a kiss
  • A fixed, level, neutral camera
  • A bright colour print
  • Soft diffusion on the heroine's face
