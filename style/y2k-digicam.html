Y2K digicam

photography · 1999–2009 · also: Early digital compact, CCD compact look

The look of a 2 to 5 megapixel CCD compact: deep focus from a tiny sensor, blown highlights, a hard on-camera flash, over-sharpened edges and clumsy white balance. It is a record of a device and now a deliberate nostalgia.

Core thesis

The flaws of a cheap early sensor are the style: they mark a moment in time and read as honest.

Design principles

  • Emulate a small sensor (1/2.7 in, crop factor about 6) with a real focal length of 5.8mm, which is 35mm in 35mm format, and a deep focus from 0.5m to infinity.
  • Clip the highlights: set the white point at 100% and let skies and skin go to pure white.
  • Use the pop-up flash on every indoor shot, with a ratio to ambient of 6:1 and a hard, flat face.
  • Limit the image to 1600×1200 px (2MP) or 2048×1536 px (3MP), saved as a compressed JPEG at quality 60–75.
  • Add the sensor faults: chroma noise at ISO 200 and above, purple fringing at high-contrast edges and a slight green or magenta cast.
  • Sharpen with a coarse radius of 1.5 to 2px at amount 150%, as the camera's own processor did.

Anti-patterns

  • Shallow depth of field and creamy bokeh
  • Clean, high-dynamic-range tonal rendering
  • Natural, accurate white balance
  • Medium or large file sizes with fine detail and smooth gradients
  • Film grain instead of sensor noise
  • A carefully composed, evenly lit frame
