Cuisine classique

cuisine · 1884–1965 · also: Escoffier cuisine, Classic French cuisine, Hotel cuisine

The kitchen of the grand hotel as Auguste Escoffier set it down. A brigade of stations cooks a menu of courses from a fixed system of stocks and mother sauces, and serves each dish on its own plate. Le Guide culinaire of 1903 holds about 5,000 recipes of the system.

Core thesis

Simplify Carême and make the kitchen a system: every dish comes from a small set of bases, cooked by a station that does one task.

Design principles

  • Keep a white stock, a brown stock and a fish fumet ready each day; every sauce starts from one of them.
  • Derive each sauce from one of five mother sauces: espagnole, velouté, béchamel, tomato and hollandaise.
  • Split the kitchen into stations: the saucier, the rôtisseur, the poissonnier, the entremetier, the garde-manger and the pâtissier.
  • Serve à la russe: one course after another, each on a plate, in the order of the menu.
  • Cut the number of garnishes, so that the main ingredient is the subject of the plate.
  • Name a dish after its garnish or a patron: Rossini, Melba, Véronique, Jeannette.
  • Finish each sauce with butter off the heat for a gloss.
  • Use the same cut for each piece of a garnish: julienne, brunoise, tournée to 7 faces.

Anti-patterns

  • A sauce thickened with bread or ground almonds
  • Every dish of a meal on the table at once
  • An edible architecture of sugar and pastry on the table
  • A garnish that hides the main ingredient
  • Raw fish, raw meat or undercooked vegetables
  • A sauce made to order without a stock
