Unicode Emoji

internet · 2010–now · also: Emoji set, Emoji keyboard, Emoji glyphs

A set of small colour pictures that sit in text as characters: yellow faces, hands, food and flags. Japanese phone makers drew the first sets; Unicode 6.0 added them to the standard in 2010.

Core thesis

A picture becomes a character: the same small yellow face can enter any text on any device.

Design principles

  • Draw each glyph on a square field with a 1:1 ratio and set it at the line height of the text, 1.2em.
  • Use a round yellow face (#FFC83D) with a 1px darker rim, two simple eyes and a mouth of one curve.
  • Fill shapes with flat colour and one soft highlight; keep the outline to 1px or none.
  • Show a skin tone with 5 swatches from #FADCBC to #3D2B1F as a modifier row under a hand glyph.
  • Set the glyph in a picker grid of 8 columns with 8px gutters and a tab row of 9 categories.
  • Keep the text font neutral and round, so that the glyph is the only colour in the line.
  • Let the glyph be 1.0em to 1.2em in text, and 32px to 48px in a picker.

Anti-patterns

  • Glyphs drawn at a different size from the line
  • Photographic emoji with realistic skin and lighting
  • Heavy outlines of 3px or more
  • Emoji set on a dark gradient that hides the yellow
  • Custom emoji with no shared base shape
  • Mixed glyph sets from different vendors in one line

Palette

  • Page White #FFFFFF background
  • Cream Card #FFF6D6 surface
  • Ink Blue #1F2430 text
  • Face Yellow #FFC83D primary
  • Link Blue #2F6FED accent
  • Slate #5D6472 muted text
  • Rim Sand #E3D9B5 border
