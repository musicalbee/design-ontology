Symbolism

painting · 1886–1910 · also: Symbolist painting, Fin de siècle dream

Pictures of dream, myth, death and desire, in which a figure or a form stands for an idea. The mood is quiet, strange and inward; line and colour are chosen to suggest, never to explain.

Core thesis

A picture should suggest an inner idea by symbol and mood, and should turn from the plain surface of the visible world toward dream and the spirit.

Design principles

  • Show an idea by an image: a sphinx, a flower with a face, a floating head, a veiled woman, a dark pool.
  • Keep the space shallow and still, with a flat, decorative field of 40–60% behind the main figure.
  • Set the figure in the centre or on a clear axis, and hold it motionless, with a closed or far-off gaze.
  • Use a limited, dim, jewel-like palette: violet, deep blue, gold and green, with pale flesh and a dark ground.
  • Draw a long, soft, flowing line of 2–6mm and let the contour curve like a stem or a hair.
  • Blur the edges in mist and dusk, and blend the light into a glow without a source.
  • Leave one thing unexplained: a title, a gaze, or an object that has no place in the scene.

Anti-patterns

  • A plain, literal record of daily life
  • Harsh, bright daylight with strong cast shadows
  • Fast, loud, active strokes and a busy crowd
  • A saturated, cheerful colour scheme
  • A clear story that the title explains in full
  • A deep, open landscape with a wide horizon
