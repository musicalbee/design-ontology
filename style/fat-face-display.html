Fat face display

typeface · 1810–present · also: Fat face, Display face, Poster Didone, Ultra-bold modern

The first type made only for display: a Didone pushed to extreme weight, with stems as thick as half the letter and hairlines kept fine. It shouts from posters and covers, and it fails in text.

Core thesis

A letter for a poster is as black as the stem can be while the hairlines keep it readable.

Design principles

  • Make the stems 0.35–0.5 of the cap height wide and keep the hairlines and serifs fine.
  • Keep the stroke contrast at 6:1 or more, measured on the o.
  • Set the stress vertical, as in a Didone, with ball terminals on a, c, f, r and y.
  • Make the counters small and the round letters wide.
  • Use it only at 30 pt and more, for a few words.
  • Set tight: −0.01 to −0.03 em at large sizes, so the black stems form one shape.

Anti-patterns

  • Running text in a fat face
  • Bracketed or slab serifs
  • Wide tracking on the lower case
  • Two fat faces on one page
  • Light weights from the same family in place of a text face
