Ottoman ebru marbled paper

print · c.1500–1900 · also: Ebru, Ebrû, Turkish marbling, Water marbling

Pigment floats on a thick bath of water. The marbler combs it into a pattern and lays a sheet on it for one monotype. Each sheet is unique, with flowing veins, combed curls and floral drops.

Core thesis

A pattern that the water and the comb make together, and that no plate can repeat, gives a sheet of paper a living surface.

Design principles

  • Make each sheet as one pull: float the colours, comb them, then lay the paper on them.
  • Use 2–8 mineral and plant pigments, and float each one on the bath with a brush.
  • Draw with a stylus, a comb and a fan; keep the veins in smooth flowing curves.
  • Let a drop spread to a ring and add a smaller drop inside it, for a flower.
  • Use a ground of indigo, red or black and leave the veins in ochre, white and red.
  • Cut or frame the sheet as a border, an endpaper or a calligraphy margin.
  • Accept uneven colour density and a soft blur at the edge of each vein.

Anti-patterns

  • A repeating tile or a mirrored pattern
  • A crisp vector outline
  • A halftone dot or a photographic tone
  • Plate mark or relief in the paper
  • A uniform flat colour with no veins
