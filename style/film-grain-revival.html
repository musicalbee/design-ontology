Film grain revival

photography · 2015–today · also: Analogue look, Shot on film

Photographs with visible grain, soft halation and warm colour, made on 35mm film or graded to look like it. The look says that a real moment passed through a real roll.

Core thesis

Grain and imperfection give a picture a body, so the viewer believes it was lived and not rendered.

Design principles

  • Shoot on 35mm colour negative film at ISO 400, such as Portra 400, Gold 200 or Ektar 100, and scan at 4000 dpi.
  • Use a 35mm or 50mm prime at f/2 to f/4, with a shutter of 1/125s or faster.
  • Expose 1 stop over for the negative; let highlights roll off with a soft shoulder.
  • Add halation around highlights by a red glow of 3 to 10px at 20% amount.
  • Allow light leaks, dust and an uneven frame edge in no more than 10% of frames.
  • Keep the grade warm at 5200–5600K with lifted blacks at 4–6%.

Anti-patterns

  • Fake grain at uniform size and no halation
  • Heavy digital sharpening with film grain on top
  • Pushed saturation and a teal-orange split
  • Perfect clean skin
  • Date stamps and borders over every frame
  • Hyper-sharp digital lenses with a film preset
