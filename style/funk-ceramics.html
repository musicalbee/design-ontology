Funk ceramics

ceramics · 1961–1985 · also: Funk art ceramics, California funk ceramics, Bay Area figurative ceramics

Hand-built ceramic sculpture from California that turns toilets, typewriters, self-portraits and bad taste into loud, crude and funny figures. Bright low-fire glazes and drips keep the clay rude.

Core thesis

Clay is a rude, funny material, so a sculptor can make a toilet, a typewriter or a bust of himself and treat high art as a joke.

Design principles

  • Build the figure by hand with coils and slabs, and leave the thumb and the tool marks.
  • Take the subject from daily life or from the artist's own face: a toilet, a typewriter, a pet, a bust.
  • Make the form lumpy, thick and a little wrong, with a big head and small hands.
  • Cover the clay with loud low-fire glazes and overglaze china paints, in hot pink, yellow and green.
  • Let the glaze run, drip and pool, and leave blisters and pinholes.
  • Add a joke in the title or in a small detail: a text, a pun or a crude word on the base.
  • Make the piece 30 to 120 cm tall, as a single piece or a small group on a plain base.
  • Fire the piece in several runs, and add paint and glaze in layers until the surface is rich.

Anti-patterns

  • A thin, symmetrical vase in a quiet glaze
  • A polished, factory-smooth surface with no tool marks
  • A refined classical pose in white biscuit
  • A pretty pastel pattern in a floral print
  • A serious, solemn tone with no joke
  • A copy of an old master in porcelain
