Electronic and digital watch

fashion · 1969–1989 · also: Quartz watch, LED watch, LCD watch, Digital watch

The watch with no hands and no spring. A quartz crystal keeps the time, and a red light or a grey liquid crystal shows the digits. The Seiko Astron of 1969 and the Pulsar of 1972 set it.

Core thesis

A watch is an electronic instrument, so it shows the time as digits. A crystal with no moving parts keeps the time.

Design principles

  • Keep the time with a quartz crystal that vibrates 32,768 times each second.
  • Show the time as digits in red LED or in black on a grey LCD, with a seven segment shape for each digit.
  • Make the case in steel, gold or plastic, as a flat, bold shape with a square or a cushion form.
  • Hide the display until the user presses a button on the case side, for the early LED models.
  • Add functions in rows of buttons: date, alarm, stopwatch and a second time zone.
  • Make the case 30 to 45 mm wide and 8 to 12 mm thick, with a flat top and a clean face.
  • Use a black or a dark red display glass with no hands and no markers.
  • Price the watch by its function, so that a watch with more functions costs more.

Anti-patterns

  • A hands dial with a printed quartz label
  • A case with engraved, chased or enamelled work
  • A display with a decorative font
  • A thick case with a heavy bezel
  • A face with markers that have no use
  • A leather strap with a fancy stitched edge
