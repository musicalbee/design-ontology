Kyo-yaki overglaze

ceramics · 1650–1750 · also: Kyō ware, Kyoto ware, Ninsei ware, Kyoto enamelled ware

Fine pottery from Kyoto that Nonomura Ninsei made at the kiln of Omuro. A thin crackle glaze takes enamel colours and gold in painted designs of flowers, hills and seasons.

Core thesis

A Kyoto potter can make tea ware as rich as a screen painting: a thin body, a pale crackle glaze, enamel and gold.

Design principles

  • Throw a thin, smooth form on the wheel, with a wall of 3 to 5 mm.
  • Cover the form with a pale crackle glaze that is fired first at about 1100 °C.
  • Paint the design in enamel colours and gold on the fired glaze, and fire again at 800 °C.
  • Place the design to fit the form: wisteria down a tall jar, peony round a water jar.
  • Keep part of the glaze plain, so that the painted area has room.
  • Use the Kyoto court palette: green, blue, red, purple, black and gold.
  • Sign the base with the maker's seal, as the Omuro kiln did.
  • Make each piece one of a small set, for a tea master or a temple.

Anti-patterns

  • A thick rough glaze with fire marks and no overglaze
  • A single plain colour with no painted design
  • A print or a transfer pattern in place of the brush
  • A heavy wall with a bent, wild form
