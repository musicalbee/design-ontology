Delft tiles

ceramics · 1620–1800 · also: Dutch wall tiles, Delftse tegels, Dutch blue and white tiles

Small square tin-glazed tiles that Dutch potteries made by the million for walls, kitchens and fireplaces. Each tile holds one small picture in blue or purple and a corner motif, and a set of tiles makes a wall.

Core thesis

A cheap square tile and a quick brush make a clean, washable wall that still holds a small picture in each unit.

Design principles

  • Keep the tile square, 13 cm by 13 cm, and 5 to 8 mm thick, so that all tiles fit one wall.
  • Paint one small subject in the centre: a ship, a child at play, a flower, an animal or a soldier.
  • Add a corner motif of 4 parts, the ox head, the spider or the fleur-de-lis, to join four tiles in a pattern.
  • Paint in cobalt blue on the raw tin glaze; use manganese purple for a second colour in a few sets.
  • Draw the subject with a pricked paper pattern, then fill the shapes with a loose wash.
  • Set the tiles in a grid with joints under 2 mm, and add a plinth tile of 13 by 6.5 cm at the floor.
  • Use biblical scenes on fireplace walls, and bright flowers on the walls of the kitchen and the dairy.
  • Fire each tile in a stack with stilts; accept the small marks of the stilts on the edge.

Anti-patterns

  • A large single picture that ignores the tile grid
  • A translucent porcelain body
  • A printed pattern with a uniform line
  • A bright polychrome palette in the early work
  • A thick tile with a square factory edge
