Impressionism

painting · 1872–1886 · also: Plein-air painting, Impressionist painting

Painting of the first look: quick, broken strokes of pure colour that record changing light on a modern scene. The painters work outdoors, drop the outline and the black shadow, and let the viewer's eye mix the colour.

Core thesis

A picture records the impression of light on a surface at one moment, not the object as it is known to be.

Design principles

  • Paint the scene in one session of 2 hours or less, so that the light is the same across the canvas.
  • Apply colour in separate, visible touches of 5–20mm and let them mix in the eye at a distance.
  • Paint shadow in colour: use blue, violet or green at the same value as the lit ground, and use no black.
  • Set the value range narrow: most of the picture sits within 3 or 4 steps of a 10-step scale.
  • Put complementary pairs side by side (orange and blue, red and green) to make the vibration of light.
  • Crop as a snapshot: let the edge cut figures and objects, and place the horizon high or low.
  • Choose modern leisure as the subject: boulevards, rivers, gardens, stations and cafés.

Anti-patterns

  • Black outlines or hard contours around forms
  • Black or brown studio shadows
  • A smooth, blended, invisible surface
  • Dark, low-key ground and a single dramatic beam of light
  • Mythological or historical narrative subjects
  • Detailed, sharp rendering of faces and leaves
