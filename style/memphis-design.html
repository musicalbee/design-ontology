Memphis Design

internet · 1981–1988 · also: Memphis Group, Memphis Milano, 80s Memphis

Clashing primary and pastel fields, squiggles, confetti dots and black-and-white pattern on irregular geometric forms. Ettore Sottsass and the Memphis group launched it in Milan in 1981 as a joke against good taste.

Core thesis

Good taste is a convention, and a loud, cheap, playful object can carry as much meaning as a serious one.

Design principles

  • Combine at least four saturated hues on one surface, and put no two neighbouring fields in the same hue.
  • Build every composition from primitive shapes: circles, triangles, half-circles, arches and 45 degree bars at sizes between 24px and 320px.
  • Cover one or two large areas with a repeating black-and-white pattern such as stripes of 8px, dots of 6px at 16px pitch, or a zigzag.
  • Scatter 6 to 20 small accents (squiggles, confetti dots, short bars) over the empty ground, and rotate each by an angle between -30 and 30 degrees.
  • Set the display type in a heavy geometric sans, and let one word break the baseline or tilt by 5 to 12 degrees.
  • Give shapes hard 3px black outlines and hard offset shadows with no blur.
  • Place blocks off axis and overlap them by 10% to 30%, so that no edge lines up with the page edge.

Anti-patterns

  • A single accent colour on a white page
  • Soft gradients, blur and realistic shadows
  • Strict centred or modular grids with equal margins
  • Thin hairline strokes under 2px
  • Muted earth tones and desaturated greys as the main palette
  • Symmetrical layouts with one shape repeated at one size
  • Photographic backgrounds behind the pattern layer

Palette
