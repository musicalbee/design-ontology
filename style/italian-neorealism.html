Italian Neorealism

film · 1943–1952 · also: Neorealismo, Postwar Italian realism

Ruined streets, ordinary people and a story with no hero. Real places, non-professional actors and light that the day provides. The camera stays at human height and records the time a thing takes.

Core thesis

A film earns its truth by going out of the studio and showing ordinary lives in the real place, at the real pace, with no star and no neat plot.

Design principles

  • Shoot on location in real streets, tenements and fields, with no built set and no back projection.
  • Cast non-professional actors for 50% or more of the roles and let them speak in their own dialect.
  • Light with daylight and a bounce, at a key-to-fill ratio of 2:1 to 3:1, and avoid a hard glamour key.
  • Frame in 1.37:1 at eye level with a 28mm to 50mm lens and keep depth in the shot, with deep focus at f/8 to f/11.
  • Hold a long take of 10 to 30 s on a walk or a wait, and cut at an average shot length of 8 to 12 s.
  • Use a slow pan or a short tracking shot of 0.5 to 1 m/s to follow a person through a street, with no crane.
  • Dub the sound after the shoot, and keep the score sparse: a theme of 20 to 60 s used 3 times at most.

Anti-patterns

  • A studio back lot and a painted backdrop
  • A star with a glamour key light
  • A tidy plot with a heroic resolution
  • Invisible continuity with a shot and reverse shot in every scene
  • A swelling score under each feeling
  • Back projection for the street
  • A soft, diffused, flattering grade
