Novoandina and Nikkei cuisine

cuisine · 1986–present · also: Novoandina, Nikkei cuisine, Modern Peruvian cuisine, Cocina novoandina

A kitchen of Lima that takes the potatoes, grains and chillies of the Andes and the fish of the Pacific, and gives them French and Japanese technique. Gastón Acurio, Virgilio Martínez and Mitsuharu Tsumura made it a world cuisine.

Core thesis

Peru holds the sea, the desert, the mountain and the jungle, and the cooks of Lima join them with the knives of Japan and the sauces of France to make one new national kitchen.

Design principles

  • Start from the native ingredient: the potato, the maize, the quinoa, the chilli and the fish of the cold sea.
  • Cure raw fish for a few minutes in lime juice, and serve it at once with its juice.
  • Join the Japanese cut and the Peruvian chilli: a thin slice of fish with soy, lime and aji.
  • Use many chilli kinds for fruit and colour as much as for heat.
  • Bring the produce of the altitudes into the menu, from the sea to the high Andes.
  • Give the cook and the farmer a share of the credit, and help the small producer.
  • Keep the plate bright, with sour, hot, sweet and salty tastes in one bite.

Anti-patterns

  • Fish that is cured for hours until it is dry
  • A heavy cream sauce on a ceviche
  • A dish with no chilli or acid
  • A copy of a European menu with a Peruvian name
  • Imported ingredients when a native one is in season
  • A flat, one-colour plate
