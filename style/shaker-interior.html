Shaker

interior · 1820s–1860s · also: Shaker style, Shaker plain style

The rooms of the United Society of Believers in Christ's Second Appearing in New England, New York and Kentucky. Built-in storage, a peg rail around the walls, light wood furniture and bare floor give a room that is ordered for work and easy to clean.

Core thesis

A room is beautiful when each object in it is useful, well made and in its place.

Design principles

  • Build the storage into the wall: one case of drawers and cupboards from floor to ceiling on one wall, with no free-standing chests.
  • Run a peg rail around the room at 180–190 cm above the floor, with a turned peg each 30 cm, and hang chairs, clothes and tools on it.
  • Place doors, windows and pairs of furniture in mirror pairs about the centre line of the room.
  • Leave 70–80% of the floor bare, so that the floor is easy to sweep.
  • Use one wood species and one paint colour for all woodwork in a room.
  • Make each drawer in a case 10–15% taller than the drawer above it.
  • Keep each piece light: a ladder-back chair weighs 4–5 kg and one person can lift it onto the peg rail.

Anti-patterns

  • Carved, gilded or veneered ornament on furniture or trim
  • Hung pictures, mirrors with frames or display shelves of ornaments
  • Printed wallpaper or patterned upholstery
  • Heavy upholstered sofas and armchairs that stay on the floor
  • Furniture placed at an angle to the walls
  • More than one accent paint colour in one room
