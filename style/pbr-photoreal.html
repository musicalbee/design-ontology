PBR photoreal

game · 2013–now · also: Physically based realism, Current-gen photoreal, Unreal photoreal

Materials follow physics: albedo, roughness and metalness answer one light model, so wood, stone and metal look right in any light. Scanned surfaces, global illumination and ray-traced reflections push game worlds close to architectural renders.

Core thesis

If every material obeys the same physics of light, the world looks real from any angle and at any hour.

Design principles

  • Author every material as albedo, roughness, metalness and normal in calibrated ranges.
  • Use scanned or measured surfaces for stone, wood and fabric.
  • Light with physical units: a sun in lux, lamps in lumens, a real sky.
  • Use global illumination so light bounces between surfaces.
  • Grade the frame with a filmic tone curve, not a heavy colour cast.
  • Add small imperfections: dust, smudges, uneven wear.

Anti-patterns

  • Light painted into textures
  • Albedo values outside the physical range
  • Uniform gloss on every surface
  • Heavy colour grading
  • Stylised proportions
