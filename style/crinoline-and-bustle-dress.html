Crinoline and bustle dress

fashion · 1856–1889 · also: Cage crinoline dress, Victorian bustle dress, Second Empire dress

The dress of the Second Empire: a vast bell skirt on a steel cage from 1856, then a bustle from 1870, in strong silk.

Core thesis

The skirt is the dress: a steel cage and then a bustle carry yards of silk, and the couture house sets the shape each season.

Design principles

  • Carry the skirt on a cage of 12 to 20 steel hoops; the hem is up to 3 m across.
  • Cut the bodice close to the body, with a pointed front and a waist at 56 to 63 cm.
  • Use 10 to 20 m of silk for a day skirt, and up to 40 m for a ball gown.
  • In the 1870s, move the fullness to the back on a bustle pad of wire and horsehair.
  • Drape the overskirt in swags at the back, and add a train for evening.
  • Trim the skirt with flounces, pleats, bows and braid in rows.
  • Use the new strong dyes, so that the colour is deep and bright.

Anti-patterns

  • A skirt that hangs straight from the waist
  • A soft waist with no stays
  • Pale muslin with no lining
  • A bare, plain skirt with no trim
  • A short hem that shows the leg
  • Stretch cloth or thin jersey
