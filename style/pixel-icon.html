Pixel icon

icon · 1981–present (revival from 2015) · also: Bitmap icon, Pixel-art icon

Icons drawn on a hard pixel grid, one square at a time. Each pixel is on or off, edges are stairs, and the set has 1 to 16 colours. The style began with low screen resolution and returns now as a retro choice.

Core thesis

An icon is a set of square pixels on a fixed grid, and the grid is part of the look, not a limit to hide.

Design principles

  • Draw on a grid of 16 × 16 or 32 × 32 px, and place every edge on a whole pixel.
  • Use straight runs and 1:1, 1:2 or 2:1 steps for diagonals; a curve is a sequence of these steps.
  • Keep the outline 1 px wide at 16 px and 2 px wide at 32 px.
  • Use 1 to 4 colours for a UI glyph and at most 16 for an app icon, with no anti-alias.
  • Scale only by whole multiples (2×, 3×, 4×) with nearest-neighbour sampling.
  • Leave a 1 px clear margin inside the grid on each side.

Anti-patterns

  • Anti-aliased, soft edges
  • Pixels at half-pixel positions
  • Smooth vector curves on a pixel grid
  • Gradients and soft shadows
  • Scale by a fraction such as 1.5×
