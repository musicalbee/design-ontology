8-bit tile graphics

game · 1983–1992 · also: 8-bit pixel art, NES-era graphics, Third-generation console look

A world drawn from 8×8 pixel tiles on a 256×240 screen, with 3 colours plus transparency in each sprite and a fixed hardware palette of about 54 colours. Black outlines and flat fills make each house and tree a small icon.

Core thesis

A small grid and a small palette force each object into its clearest silhouette, so a house in 16 pixels reads at once.

Design principles

  • Build every background from 8×8 or 16×16 tiles that repeat on a grid.
  • Give each sprite 3 colours plus transparent; outline it in black or a dark hue.
  • Use flat fills; show volume with one highlight row and one shadow row, never a gradient.
  • Keep the screen at 256×240 pixels and scale it up with hard nearest-neighbour edges.
  • Use a sky of one flat colour, often light blue or black.
  • Make a character 16 to 32 pixels high, with a large head.

Anti-patterns

  • Smooth gradients or anti-aliased edges
  • More than 4 colours in one 16×16 area
  • Sub-pixel detail or filtered scaling
  • Perspective that breaks the tile grid
  • Photographic texture
