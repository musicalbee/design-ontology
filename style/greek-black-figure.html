Greek black-figure pottery

ceramics · c. 700–480 BC · also: Attic black-figure, Corinthian black-figure

Greek vases in which the painter draws figures in a black gloss slip on the red clay and cuts the details with a sharp point. A three-stage firing makes the black and the red.

Core thesis

A pot is a surface for story: black silhouettes on red clay, made sharp with incised lines, tell a myth in frieze after frieze.

Design principles

  • Paint each figure as a solid black silhouette in gloss slip on the red clay.
  • Incise the inner lines of the figures with a sharp point to show the red clay below.
  • Add purple-red and white slip for flesh of women, hair, and clothing accents.
  • Divide the pot into panels and friezes that run round the body in horizontal bands.
  • Fire in three stages: oxidation, reduction and re-oxidation, at 800 to 950 °C.
  • Keep the main scene on the shoulder or the belly, and a lotus and palmette band below the handles.
  • Write the name of the painter or the potter in a short line on the vase, when it is signed.

Anti-patterns

  • Red figures on a black ground
  • Soft shading or colour blends inside the figure
  • A glaze that covers the whole pot with a clear gloss
  • Perspective depth and overlapping landscapes
  • A thick wall with no foot and no clear profile
