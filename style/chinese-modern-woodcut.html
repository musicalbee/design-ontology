Chinese modern woodcut

print · 1931–1960 · also: New Woodcut Movement, 新兴木刻运动, Modern woodcut movement

In 1931 Lu Xun urged young artists to learn the European expressionist woodcut of Kollwitz and Masereel. They cut black-and-white prints on cheap wood and printed many on thin paper, bold and rough.

Core thesis

A cheap block of wood and one knife can give a poor and angry public a picture of its own life and its own fight.

Design principles

  • Cut the block yourself, with a knife, in black ink on white, or in black and one flat colour.
  • Build the image from heavy black masses and white knife lines, and keep the contrast high.
  • Choose the people as the subject: workers, farmers, soldiers, a crowd, a portrait of a hero.
  • Cut the line rough and angular, 0.5–2 mm wide. Leave the knife edge visible.
  • Allow some cross-cut hatching in the shadow, but keep most tones as a mass or a white line.
  • Print many copies on thin paper by hand rubbing with a spoon or a baren, so the print is cheap to share.
  • Add a short caption or a date, and keep the sheet small enough for a magazine page or a wall.

Anti-patterns

  • A soft colour gradient or a pastel set
  • Halftone dots or a photograph
  • A thin, delicate, decorative line
  • A clean vector edge with no cut mark
  • A pretty or neutral subject with no social point
