Roman terra sigillata

ceramics · 30 BC–AD 300 · also: Arretine ware, Samian ware, Roman red-gloss tableware

Red-gloss tableware of the Roman world. Potters at Arezzo press fine clay into stamped moulds, coat the pieces with a slip of very small particles, and fire them to a glossy coral red.

Core thesis

A fine slip, not a glaze, gives the red shine: the potter sorts the clay by particle size, paints the thin top layer on the pot, and fires it in clean air.

Design principles

  • Settle the clay in water and keep only the finest top layer, with particles under 1 micron, for the slip.
  • Coat the leather-hard pot with 1 or 2 thin layers of slip and polish it with a soft cloth.
  • Press the bowl into a fired clay mould that carries the relief in reverse; the mould keeps the design sharp.
  • Stamp the maker name in a small cartouche in the well of the plate.
  • Give each form a fixed number in a catalogue, so that a buyer in any province can match the set.
  • Fire the ware in clean oxidising air at about 1000 °C, so that the iron in the slip turns red.
  • Stack the pieces in a large kiln in columns, with a ring of clay between them, to fire 10 000 or more at once.

Anti-patterns

  • A glass-like glaze that fuses with the body and hides the clay
  • Black or grey patches from smoke in the kiln
  • Painted scenes with an outline brush on the red ground
  • A thick, wide-rimmed form with a heavy wall
