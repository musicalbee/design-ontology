Radical architecture

architecture · 1960s–1980s · also: Architettura radicale, Radical design

Architecture that lives on paper, in collage and in film. Groups in London, Florence and Moscow drew endless grids, walking cities and impossible houses to attack the market and the state.

Core thesis

The drawing is the act: if the real city serves the market, the architect must draw a city that no market can build.

Design principles

  • Draw the project as a collage, a film still or an etching, not as a plan.
  • Set one huge, simple form against an ordinary landscape.
  • Use the endless grid as a neutral frame for life.
  • Borrow images from pop culture, machines and advertising.
  • Make the project a critique that readers must decode.
  • Keep the scale impossible: cities that walk or monuments that circle the earth.

Anti-patterns

  • A buildable, budget-ready scheme
  • Warm, handmade detail
  • Historical ornament
  • A single, finished object with a client brief
  • Careful realism in the image
  • A pitched roof or a house-like shape
