Georgian porcelain tea service

ceramics · 1745–1830 · also: English soft-paste tea service, Georgian tea set, Bow and Worcester tea ware, English porcelain tea service

The first English porcelain tea services. A tea set of handleless bowls, a pot, jugs and slop bowls in soft-paste or bone china, with blue painting, enamel flowers and gilt rims.

Core thesis

English potters make a full tea table in porcelain for the new tea drinkers: a set of matched pieces, with a Chinese or a European look.

Design principles

  • Make a set of 12 to 40 pieces in one body and one pattern.
  • Make the tea bowls handleless at first and add handles after about 1790.
  • Paint flowers, birds and Chinese figures in blue or in enamel colours.
  • Pick one border for the whole set: gilt line, blue band or scalloped edge.
  • Add gilding to the rim, the handle and the knob of the pot.
  • Make the pot rounded on a low foot, with a straight or curved spout and a knob lid.
  • Use the soft glassy glaze that pools in the corners and shows the tint of the body.
  • Copy Chinese and Meissen models first, and then design a new English form.

Anti-patterns

  • A thick, heavy stoneware wall in a sandy colour
  • A single piece with no match in the set
  • A flat modern glaze with no gilding
  • A plain white pot with no border
