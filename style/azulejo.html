Azulejo

ceramics · 1560–present · also: Portuguese tile, Spanish painted tile, Azulejos

Painted tin-glazed tiles that cover the walls of churches, palaces and streets in Portugal and Spain. A painter works in blue or in many colours on a white glaze, and one scene runs across many tiles.

Core thesis

A wall becomes a picture when many square tiles are painted as one scene on a white tin glaze.

Design principles

  • Paint the scene on the raw tin glaze, so that each stroke dries at once and cannot be changed.
  • Plan the scene across a grid of square tiles, about 13 to 15 cm each, and number the tiles on the back.
  • Use cobalt blue alone for narrative panels; use many colours for pattern carpets.
  • Repeat a pattern tile in a 2 by 2 or 4 by 4 group to make a carpet of stars and flowers.
  • Frame a large panel with painted volutes, shells, putti and a cornice.
  • Cover the wall to the ceiling, or to a dado of about 1.5 m, with no bare patch.
  • Keep the tile body thin and the glaze thick, about 0.5 mm.
  • Use a bold blue and yellow in the early pattern tiles and a soft blue in the 18th-century panels.

Anti-patterns

  • A printed pattern with no brush line
  • A glossy porcelain body
  • A bare grey grout line between tiles
  • A photograph printed on the tile
  • A scene that stops at the edge of one tile
