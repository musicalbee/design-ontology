English slipware

ceramics · 1670–1800 · also: Staffordshire slipware, Slip-decorated earthenware, Wrotham ware

Red earthenware that English country potters covered with a clear lead glaze and trailed with cream and brown slip. Large dishes, posset pots and jugs carry bold pictures and rhymes.

Core thesis

A country potter needs no costly colour: red clay, white slip from a quill, and a lead glaze give a bold picture.

Design principles

  • Throw the body of a red-firing local clay; the wall is 5 to 8 mm thick.
  • Cover the front with a white slip when the clay is leather-hard.
  • Trail liquid slip from a quill pipe in lines 2 to 4 mm wide and in dots.
  • Draw a picture on a large dish: a king, a mermaid, a pelican or a flower.
  • Write a name, a date or a rhyme in capital letters round the rim.
  • Dip the piece in a clear lead glaze; the slip turns cream and the clay turns amber to brown.
  • Fire the glaze at 1000 to 1080 °C in one firing after the biscuit.

Anti-patterns

  • A white glaze that hides the red clay
  • Neat printed outlines
  • Thin porcelain walls
  • Cold blue and grey colours
  • Gold on the rim
