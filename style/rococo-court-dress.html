Rococo court dress

fashion · 1720–1789 · also: Robe à la française, Louis XV court dress, Watteau-back gown

The court dress of Louis XV: a wide-hipped silk gown with Watteau back pleats, lace ruffles and flower brocade, copied at the courts of Europe.

Core thesis

A court gown shows rank by the width of the skirt, the weight of the silk and the hours of hand work in the trim.

Design principles

  • Hold the skirt out on side hoops 1 m across for day and up to 3 m across for court.
  • Boned stays shape the torso into a stiff cone; lace the stays tight so that the back stays straight.
  • Cut the back as two box pleats that fall free from the neck to the floor (the Watteau pleat).
  • Open the front of the skirt over a petticoat in the same silk or a contrast silk.
  • Fill the V of the bodice with a stomacher, and cover it with bows or embroidery.
  • End the sleeve at the elbow in two or three tiers of lace ruffles.
  • Choose small flower sprays on a pale ground; keep the brocade pattern in scale with the skirt.

Anti-patterns

  • A straight, narrow skirt with no hoops
  • Heavy, dark, plain wool for a court gown
  • A high neckline with no lace at the elbow
  • Stiff, large, geometric prints
  • A waist at the natural line with no point at the front
  • Visible machine seams
