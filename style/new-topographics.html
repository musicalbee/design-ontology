New Topographics

photography · 1975–1985 · also: Deadpan landscape, Man-altered landscape

Flat, neutral, frontal views of suburbs, car parks and industrial sites, with no drama and no opinion. It shows the built landscape as a catalogue of facts, in a cool, exact, almost clerical style.

Core thesis

The ordinary, man-made landscape deserves the formal care that the sublime wilderness once received, and the photographer's voice should stay out of it.

Design principles

  • Shoot frontally from a fixed height of 1.5m with the camera level, so that verticals stay parallel.
  • Wait for flat overcast or hazy light at a ratio of 2:1 or less, so that no shadow leads the eye.
  • Stand 10 to 50m from the subject, and fill 60% of the frame with ordinary built structure.
  • Use a view camera at f/22 to f/45 so that everything from the kerb to the horizon is sharp.
  • Keep the tonal scale narrow and neutral: black point at 5%, white point at 95%, with no toning.
  • Treat the subject as a type: photograph the same kind of object in a series, from the same distance and angle.

Anti-patterns

  • Dramatic skies, golden hour and sunset colour
  • Wide-angle distortion and tilted horizons
  • Picturesque framing that prettifies a car park or a tract house
  • Human emotion or an ironic gesture inside the frame
  • Heavy burning and dodging that adds mood
  • Saturated colour and decorative texture
