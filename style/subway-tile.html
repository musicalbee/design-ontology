Subway tile

ceramics · 1904–present · also: Metro tile, White brick tile, Bevelled white tile

A small white rectangular wall tile with a glossy glaze and a bevelled edge, laid in a brick bond. It began as a clean, washable lining for tunnels and stations. It is now the default tile of the kitchen and the bathroom.

Core thesis

A white, shiny, small tile makes a wall that is clean, bright and easy to wash.

Design principles

  • Make the tile a 2:1 rectangle, 3 by 6 inches, or about 7.5 by 15 cm.
  • Use a white glaze with a high gloss, 85 to 100 GU, so that the wall reflects light.
  • Bevel the edge by 1 to 2 mm, so that each tile has a soft line and the grout is not flat.
  • Lay the tiles in a running bond with a half-tile offset, and keep the joints to 2 to 3 mm.
  • Fill the joint with a light or a dark grout in one colour to set the strength of the grid.
  • Add a trim set: a bullnose, a cap, a base and a corner piece in the same glaze.
  • Keep the wall plain; use one band of colour or one border at most.
  • Fire the tile hard enough that the glaze does not craze and stains do not enter.

Anti-patterns

  • A rough, hand-made surface with a wavy edge
  • A printed pattern on each tile
  • A square tile in a stack bond for a classic look
  • A deep relief that holds dirt
  • A dull matt glaze with large pores
