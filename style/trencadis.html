Trencadís

ceramics · 1900 to the present · also: Broken tile mosaic, Pique assiette on architecture, Shard mosaic

A surface of broken tile and china that is set in mortar on a curved wall, bench or roof. Antoni Gaudí and Josep Maria Jujol made it a free, colourful skin for Barcelona buildings.

Core thesis

A broken tile is not waste: a wall of shards takes any curve, and the glaze colour of each piece joins into one bright surface.

Design principles

  • Break glazed tiles and plates with a hammer into pieces of 2 to 10 cm.
  • Set the pieces in a bed of wet mortar on a curved wall, a bench or a chimney.
  • Mix many colours in one field; place the pieces at random and keep the pattern loose.
  • Add shards of plates, bottle glass and cups, so that a few printed motifs appear.
  • Keep the glaze face outward; the sharp edges of the shards stay below the grout line.
  • Fill the gaps with a thin mortar joint of 3 to 5 mm and wipe the face clean.
  • Use the mosaic on a form that has a curve: a serpentine bench, a lizard, a dome or a chimney.

Anti-patterns

  • A flat grid of square tiles in one colour
  • A regular, printed tile pattern with no breaks
  • Smooth, uncut ceramic with no shard edge
  • A sharp-edged tile that cuts the hand
  • Pieces of one colour that give a plain surface
