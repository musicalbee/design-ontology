Found footage

film · 1999–2020 · also: Faux-documentary horror, Camcorder horror

A film that claims to be real recordings of a camera, left behind by people who vanished. The camera is in the hand of a character. It shakes, loses focus, runs out of light and records the fear in real time.

Core thesis

If a character holds the camera, the frame shows only what that person can see, so the viewer cannot look away from the dark edge of the picture.

Design principles

  • Give the camera to a character, and let the character say why they film.
  • Shoot handheld with a consumer camera at 4:3 or 16:9 with a 1/50 s shutter, and keep every shot as a long take of 30 to 180 s.
  • Light with the lamp on the camera, a torch or a night-vision mode, and let 50% or more of the frame go dark.
  • Keep the sound to what the camera microphone hears, with wind noise, clipped peaks and breath.
  • Hold the monster off screen or at the frame edge until the last 1 to 3 min.
  • Show the camera's own signs: a date stamp, a battery icon and a tape glitch.
  • Add no score and no cut that the character could not make.

Anti-patterns

  • A score laid over the scene
  • A tripod and a smooth dolly
  • A perfect, well-lit frame
  • A cut to a view that no camera has
  • A visible monster in the first reel
  • A polished studio colour grade
