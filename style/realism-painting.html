Realism

painting · 1848–1880 · also: Courbet realism, Naturalism

Ordinary people, work and country in plain light and earth colour. The painter shows life as it is, at life size, with no myth, no ideal and no sweetness.

Core thesis

A picture should record the real world of its own time, and ordinary work and ordinary people are as worthy a subject as a king or a god.

Design principles

  • Choose a subject from daily life: a peasant, a worker, a stonebreaker, a farm, a road.
  • Paint the figures at life size, or large against the picture, at 60–100% of its height.
  • Keep the light plain and from one side: an overcast day or a window, with no divine beam.
  • Hold the palette to earth colour: brown, ochre, grey, olive and a dull black, with one muted red or blue.
  • Build the form with a palette knife or a heavy brush in thick, matt paint, and leave rough edges.
  • Compose with the figures in a plain, near-frieze arrangement, off-centre, and cut by the edge as in life.
  • Add no moral, no idealised body and no story; show weight, dirt, wear and posture.

Anti-patterns

  • Gods, saints, heroes and exotic fantasy
  • Idealised, smooth and graceful bodies
  • Theatrical light and a dramatic sky
  • Bright, sweet or pastel colour
  • A tidy, symmetrical, heroic layout
  • Sentimental poses and a moral message
