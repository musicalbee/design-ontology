American art pottery

ceramics · 1880–1940 · also: Rookwood standard glaze ware, Grueby matte ware, American Arts and Crafts pottery

Art pottery from American potteries after 1880. Rookwood painted slip under a clear glaze, and Grueby made thick matt green glazes for vases with leaf forms.

Core thesis

A pottery of artists can make a vase a painting or a leaf: one line paints in slip under glaze, and the other line builds the form in matt colour.

Design principles

  • Paint the design in coloured slip on damp clay, so that the colours blend like a watercolour.
  • Cover the painted piece with a clear glossy glaze, the Standard glaze, and fire it once.
  • Grade the ground from dark brown to orange, olive or yellow with an atomiser spray.
  • Paint one flower, fish or portrait on each vase; the painter signs with a monogram.
  • For a matt line, use a thick glaze of 1 to 2 mm in a single colour, green above all.
  • Mould the leaf and the stem in the wall, and make the glaze pool in the hollows.
  • Mark each piece on the base with a stamp, a date code and a shape number.
  • Fire in oxidation at about 1100 to 1250 °C for earthenware and 1250 °C for stoneware.

Anti-patterns

  • A white body with a printed transfer pattern
  • Glossy flat colour with no tone
  • Gilt rims and Victorian scrolls
  • A thin, even, machine-made wall
  • A mark that is missing or only a number
