Brass-Era Car

fashion · 1886–1918 · also: Veteran car, Edwardian motor car, Horseless carriage

The first motor cars. A tall, upright carriage body on wood-spoke wheels, with open seats, a folding hood, and lamps, radiator and horn of polished brass.

Core thesis

The first car is a carriage that has lost its horse. It keeps the wood, the leather and the brass of the coach. It shows its engine as a proud machine.

Design principles

  • Set the body high on the chassis, with the seat 90 to 110 cm above the road.
  • Build the body on an ash frame, and clad it in thin sheet metal or in wood panels.
  • Fit wood-spoke wheels, 12 to 16 spokes each, with a thin rim and a narrow tyre.
  • Polish the lamps, the radiator shell, the horn and the door handles in brass, and keep them bright.
  • Fix the hood as a folding canvas frame on iron bows, with leather straps.
  • Stuff the seats deep with horsehair, and button the leather in a diamond pattern.
  • Leave the engine, the chain or shaft and the springs in view.

Anti-patterns

  • Chrome plate on the lamps and the bumpers
  • Low, wide, closed bodies with a boot
  • Moulded plastic knobs and switches
  • A smooth, enclosed underside
  • Thick balloon tyres with a bright white wall
