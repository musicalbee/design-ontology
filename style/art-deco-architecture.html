Art Deco

architecture · 1920s–1930s · also: Style Moderne, Streamline Moderne (late phase), Zigzag Moderne

Stepped towers, zigzag and chevron relief, and rich materials in a symmetric frame. It came from the Paris exhibition of 1925 and made the skyscraper and the cinema into the pictures of the machine age.

Core thesis

Modern technology deserves luxury: the machine age should look glamorous, symmetric and expensive.

Design principles

  • Step the silhouette back in setbacks of 1.5 to 4m as the tower rises, and end it with a spire, a crown or a lantern.
  • Stress the vertical: run piers or fins unbroken from the base to the crown, with spandrels recessed 100 to 300mm behind them.
  • Compose the front on one central axis and balance both sides; the entrance is the richest point.
  • Use geometric ornament: zigzag, chevron, sunburst, fluting and stylised flora in low relief of 25 to 75mm.
  • Mix rich and industrial materials: polished stone, chrome, aluminium, bronze and glass in one elevation.
  • Give the lobby the most ornament of the building: a tall hall with a patterned floor, metal doors and a lit ceiling.

Anti-patterns

  • Smooth undecorated planes with no relief
  • Curved, whiplash or plant-like line work
  • Raw concrete or visible formwork marks
  • Asymmetric, informal composition with no axis
  • A flat-topped box with no crown or setback
  • Historical orders copied without simplification
