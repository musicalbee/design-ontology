Victorian sanitary ware

ceramics · 1870–1914 · also: Victorian lavatory ware, Transfer-printed sanitary ware, Late Victorian bathroom ceramics

The first ceramic bathroom of the home. Basins, pans and cisterns of earthenware and early vitreous china carry blue, brown or polychrome transfer prints, moulded flowers and gilt lines.

Core thesis

A new sanitary fitting must look like fine china, so that a family sees a clean, rich object in a room that was once hidden.

Design principles

  • Cover each visible face with a transfer print in one to three colours, such as blue, brown or green.
  • Mould a raised flower, a shell or a dolphin into the rim, the handle and the pedestal.
  • Glaze the bowl white and thick, 0.5 to 1 mm, so that it is easy to clean.
  • Fit the wash-down pan with a plain curved trap, so that 9 litres of water clear the bowl.
  • Shape the basin with a wide rim, 3 tap holes and a small overflow slot at the back.
  • Mount the cistern high on the wall with a pull chain of brass and a porcelain handle.
  • Add gilt lines on the rim of a fine basin only, as the last firing.

Anti-patterns

  • A plain white bowl with no print and no moulded detail
  • A bright coloured glaze all over the body
  • A thin sheet that cracks at the rim
  • A wall-hung pan with a hidden cistern
  • A matte surface that holds dirt
