Rhenish salt-glaze stoneware

ceramics · 1400–1700 · also: Rhenish stoneware, Steinzeug, Bartmann jugs and Westerwald ware

Hard grey stoneware that the potters of the Rhineland fired to about 1250 °C and glazed with salt. The jugs, bottles and storage jars are tough, waterproof and ready for the cellar.

Core thesis

A dense stoneware pot needs no lining, because a handful of salt thrown into the hot kiln gives it a skin that keeps out water and does not taste of lead.

Design principles

  • Throw the body of a local stoneware clay and fire it to 1200 to 1300 °C until it does not take up water.
  • Throw rock salt into the firebox at the peak of the firing; the vapour forms a glaze 0.1 mm thick with an orange-peel texture.
  • Press a face mask, a coat of arms or a rosette in relief on the neck or the belly of a jug.
  • Keep the neck narrow and the belly round, so that the jug holds 1 to 5 litres and pours well.
  • Pull a strap handle and fix it with slip at the neck and at the belly.
  • Colour the surface in the fire: tan to brown with iron in the clay; after 1590 add cobalt blue and manganese purple in the Westerwald.
  • Stack the pots in the kiln with a gap of 2 to 3 cm, so that the salt vapour reaches every face.

Anti-patterns

  • A smooth, glossy, even glaze with no texture
  • A white or cream body that shows through the glaze
  • Thin printed lines with no relief
  • Bright overglaze colours on the surface
