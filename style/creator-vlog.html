Creator vlog

film · 2005–today · also: YouTube vlog, Jump-cut vlog, Talking-head vlog

A person talks to the lens in a room or on the move. Every pause is cut out, and a title, a sound or a cut-away lands every few seconds. The creator is the set, the brand and the grade.

Core thesis

A direct address from one person, tightened by the jump cut, builds more trust than a produced film, so the creator's pace is the style.

Design principles

  • Frame the face at arm's length, 0.4 to 0.7 m, with a 16mm to 24mm lens (35mm format), in 16:9 at 24 or 30 fps, 180° shutter.
  • Hook inside the first 5 to 10 s with the promise of the video, and show the payoff before 30 s.
  • Cut out each pause longer than 0.3 s; keep an average shot length of 3 to 5 s with a jump cut or a punch-in of 10% to 20%.
  • Add a cut-away or a b-roll shot of 1 to 3 s every 6 to 10 s, and a text or a sound effect on each change of topic.
  • Light the face with a soft key at 45° and 5600K, at a key-to-fill ratio of 2:1, with a window or a panel at 1 m.
  • Record the voice at 0.3 m with a lavalier or a shotgun, mix at −14 LUFS integrated, and sit the music bed 18 to 24 dB below the voice.
  • Grade for warm, clean skin: saturation +10%, a gentle S-curve, a white balance of 5300K to 5600K.

Anti-patterns

  • Long pauses and filler words left in
  • A tripod shot with a dead static frame for 60 s
  • A cinematic letterbox with a heavy teal grade
  • A voice recorded on the camera microphone at 3 m
  • A slow intro with a logo before the hook
  • Music louder than the voice
  • A script read from a teleprompter with no eye contact
