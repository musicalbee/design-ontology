Hippie bohemian dress

fashion · 1966–1975 · also: Hippie fashion, Flower power dress, Boho dress, Summer of Love dress

The loose, layered dress of the counterculture from 1966 to 1975. It has long cotton and velvet, Indian prints, embroidery, tie-dye, flared jeans, suede waistcoats and the Afghan coat.

Core thesis

Dress is free of the factory and of the rules of the shop: handmade, worldly, loose on the body and rich in colour.

Design principles

  • Cut the dress loose from the shoulder, with no darts, and let it fall to the ankle or the mid-calf.
  • Cut the flare of the trouser from the knee, so that it measures 25 to 30 cm at the hem.
  • Choose soft natural cloth, such as Indian cotton muslin, velvet or suede, and not a stiff synthetic.
  • Layer three or more pieces, such as a waistcoat, a blouse and a long skirt or a dress.
  • Dye by hand with a tie-dye or an indigo bath, so that no two pieces match.
  • Decorate with embroidery, beads, patches and block-printed motifs from India and the Middle East.
  • Wear the piece worn and mended; add bells, a headband and bare feet or sandals.

Anti-patterns

  • Sharp tailoring with padded shoulders
  • A short, rigid mod dress
  • Plain corporate colours of grey and navy
  • Synthetic shine and vinyl
  • A new, pressed and factory-clean look
  • Hard black and white geometry
