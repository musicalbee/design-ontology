Country sporting tweed

fashion · 1860–1939 · also: Highland sporting dress, Estate tweed, Shooting and fishing dress

The rough wool dress of the landowner in the field. A loose tweed jacket, breeches or plus-fours, a knitted jumper and heavy boots, cut for the hill, the moor and the river.

Core thesis

Dress for the weather, and put the sport first. A thick wool twill sheds rain, hides dirt and blends with heather, so it does the work of a coat. Age makes it better.

Design principles

  • Cut the jacket 5 to 8 cm full at the chest, so that the arm can lift a gun.
  • Pleat the back from the yoke to give 6 cm of play at the shoulder blade.
  • Set the knee of the breeches 10 cm below the knee joint for a plus-four, to give room for the stride.
  • Choose a cloth of 450 to 650 g per square metre that sheds a day of rain.
  • Match the check to the ground: a dark, small check for the wood, a pale, bold check for the open hill.
  • Add a patch pocket and a flap on each side; use leather at the elbow and cuff.
  • Use leather or horn buttons that hold under strain.

Anti-patterns

  • A smooth, shiny town-suit cloth
  • A tight, short cut that blocks the arm
  • Bright, clear colours that show against the hill
  • Thin cloth that soaks through in the first shower
  • A fashion check with no link to the ground
