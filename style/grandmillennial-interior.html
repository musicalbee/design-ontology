Grandmillennial

interior · 2019–present · also: Granny chic, New traditional, Grandmillennial style

A young generation takes back the rooms of its grandparents. Chintz, ruffles, needlepoint, skirted tables, wicker and blue-and-white porcelain return with a fresh, light palette and a sense of humour; pattern meets pattern and inherited pieces meet new ones.

Core thesis

Comfort and memory beat the cool, empty room: inherited, pretty and patterned things make a home.

Design principles

  • Mix 3–5 patterns in one room: a floral, a stripe or check, and a small print, held in one colour family.
  • Keep the walls light and fresh, or cover them in a small floral paper.
  • Use soft traditional forms: skirted tables, pleated lampshades, ruffled cushions and scalloped edges.
  • Mix inherited or vintage pieces with a few new ones.
  • Display collections: blue-and-white porcelain, plates on the wall, needlepoint cushions.
  • Keep the arrangement traditional and symmetric about the hearth or the bed, then break it with one witty piece.

Anti-patterns

  • Flat-pack furniture of one new range
  • Grey and white rooms with no pattern
  • Chrome or glass furniture
  • Bare walls and bare tables
  • Dark, heavy Victorian colours
