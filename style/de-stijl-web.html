De Stijl (web)

graphic movement · 1917–1931 · also: Neoplasticism, Mondrian grid

Horizontal and vertical black lines, flat fields of red, yellow and blue, and white space. It came from the Dutch group around Mondrian and Van Doesburg and reduces form to its basic parts.

Core thesis

Reduce form to the straight line and the primary colour, and a universal, harmonious order appears.

Design principles

  • Build each layout from straight horizontal and vertical lines only; no angle other than 0° or 90°.
  • Draw black lines of 6px to 12px between all colour fields.
  • Use only red (#D8261C), yellow (#F4C719), blue (#1D4FA8), black, white and a light grey (#E8E8E4).
  • Fill 60% to 75% of the page with white; colour fields cover at most 25% and one large field takes the most weight.
  • Place colour fields off centre and keep them unequal in size; the ratio between two adjacent fields is 1:1.6 to 1:2.4.
  • Set text in a geometric sans-serif at 16px; let headlines sit in a white field in lower case.
  • Let the lines run to the edge of the viewport and cut off at the border.

Anti-patterns

  • Diagonal lines and curves
  • Gradients, textures and shadows
  • Secondary colours such as green, orange or purple
  • Symmetric compositions with fields of equal size
  • Decorative illustration and ornament
  • Rounded corners on any element
  • Photographs with no flat field frame

Palette

  • White #FFFFFF background
  • Black #111111 lines and text
  • Red #D8261C primary field
  • Yellow #F4C719 accent field
  • Blue #1D4FA8 second accent field
  • Grey #E8E8E4 surface

Lineage
