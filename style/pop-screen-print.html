Pop screen print

print · 1962–1985 · also: Silkscreen, Serigraph, Pop art screen print, Warhol print

A photograph or a found image pulled through a silk screen in flat, bright inks, often with the black photo layer off register. Warhol made it a studio process in 1962; Corita Kent printed text and colour; the look stands for pop art.

Core thesis

A mechanical image, repeated and coloured by hand in loud inks, makes the familiar new.

Design principles

  • Start from a found photograph, cut to high contrast or a coarse halftone, in a black ink.
  • Put 2–5 flat, opaque, bright colours under the black, cut by hand and a little loose from the photo.
  • Let the black layer miss the colour by 2–5 mm, so the hand shows.
  • Repeat the same image in a grid with different colour sets.
  • Use colours from advertising: hot pink, lemon, turquoise, orange.
  • Keep the ink films thick and the edges a little uneven where the squeegee dragged.

Anti-patterns

  • A soft photographic tone with no contrast
  • A muted or natural palette
  • Exact register that hides the process
  • A painterly brush texture over the whole sheet
