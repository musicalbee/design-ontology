Mass Timber

architecture · 1995–present · also: New wood architecture, Engineered timber architecture, CLT architecture

Buildings of engineered wood. Cross-laminated and glued timber panels and beams make floors, walls and frames, and tall buildings of 6 to 25 storeys with warm interiors and a low carbon footprint.

Core thesis

A building can be made of engineered timber panels and beams, and so store carbon, build fast off site and show the warm grain of its own structure.

Design principles

  • Use cross-laminated timber panels of 0.1 to 0.3m for floors and walls, and glulam beams and columns on a grid of 6 to 9m.
  • Show the timber: leave the soffit, the beam and the column visible, with fire-char allowances of 40 to 60mm.
  • Prefabricate in the factory, and lift the panels to the site in a plan with a tolerance of 2 to 5mm.
  • Keep the grid regular and repeating, because the panel and the truck size set the module.
  • Protect the timber from water: use a deep eave, a rainscreen and a ventilated cavity of 20 to 40mm.
  • Combine with a concrete core or hybrid floor for stiffness and fire when the building passes 8 storeys.

Anti-patterns

  • Timber hidden behind plasterboard on every surface
  • A grid so irregular that each panel is unique
  • Open timber end grain exposed to the rain
  • Fake wood-pattern cladding on a steel frame
  • Long untreated timber spans without fire design
  • Dark opaque finishes that hide the grain
