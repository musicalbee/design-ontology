New Look

fashion · 1947–1957 · also: Corolle line, Dior New Look, Post-war couture silhouette

The couture line that Christian Dior showed in February 1947. It has round shoulders, a small built waist and a full calf-length skirt in metres of wool and silk. It answered the rationed, square dress of the war.

Core thesis

After rationing, a woman's dress shows plenty: a small waist, a full skirt and the hand work of the couture house.

Design principles

  • Cut the waist 5 to 8 cm smaller than the natural waist, and hold it with a boned inner belt.
  • Set the hem 30 to 40 cm above the floor, at mid-calf.
  • Give a day skirt a hem of 10 to 25 m, and an evening skirt up to 80 m.
  • Round the shoulder with no pad; let the line run down from the neck in one curve.
  • Pad the hip with a basque so that the waist looks smaller.
  • Build the shape inside the dress with canvas, boning and a tulle petticoat.
  • Finish each seam and each hem by hand.

Anti-patterns

  • Square padded shoulders of wartime utility dress
  • Short straight skirts that save cloth
  • A loose, dropped waist
  • Thin, limp cloth that cannot hold a pleat
  • Visible machine top-stitching on a couture piece
