Isometric icon

icon · 1995–present · also: Isometric pictogram, 2.5D icon

Icons drawn as small objects in a parallel isometric view: three faces at once, edges at 30° to the horizontal, no vanishing point. Flat colour fills give each face its own tone.

Core thesis

An icon can show an object as a solid in space and keep it exact, because parallel projection keeps every edge at a true angle.

Design principles

  • Draw on an isometric grid with edges at 30° and 150° to the horizontal and vertical edges at 90°.
  • Show three faces: a top face in the light tone, a left face in the mid tone and a right face in the dark tone.
  • Use 3 tones of one hue for each object, 15–25% apart in lightness.
  • Keep all parallel edges parallel; no vanishing point and no foreshortening.
  • Fit the object into a hexagonal keyline that fills 80–90% of the grid.
  • Use a 1–1.5 px stroke at 48 px only when the faces need separation.

Anti-patterns

  • Vanishing-point perspective
  • Front-only flat views
  • Faces that share one tone
  • Edges off the 30° grid
  • Photographic texture
