Canvas court sneaker

fashion · 1917–present · also: Plimsoll, Canvas high-top, Gym shoe

The cotton canvas shoe on a vulcanised rubber sole. It is a white or navy high-top or low-top with a toe cap and a thin foxing strip. It was first made for basketball in 1917.

Core thesis

A court shoe is cheap cloth on a cured rubber sole, so it grips the floor and costs little; its plainness is the style.

Design principles

  • Cut the upper in cotton canvas of 400 to 600 gsm and stitch it to a rubber foxing strip.
  • Cure the sole with the upper in a vulcanising oven, so that the rubber bonds to the canvas.
  • Cap the toe in rubber, and set a round ankle patch on the inside of a high-top.
  • Use 8 pairs of eyelets with a flat cotton lace.
  • Print one small mark on the ankle patch; keep the rest plain.
  • Set the sole at 2 to 3 cm, flat, with a diamond or herringbone tread.
  • Leave the canvas free of coating, so that it can fade and crease.

Anti-patterns

  • A padded, cushioned midsole with air units
  • A leather and mesh upper
  • A thick heel flare
  • Large printed logos across the side
  • A shiny, patent finish
