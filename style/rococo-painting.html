Rococo

painting · 1720–1780 · also: Late Baroque, Louis XV style

Light, playful pictures of love, leisure and ornament, in pale pastel colour and curving lines. The weight and drama of the Baroque give way to charm, intimacy and a soft, feathery touch.

Core thesis

A picture should give pleasure: it is a refined, light and intimate object that charms the eye, and it does not preach or overwhelm.

Design principles

  • Build the layout on S-curves and C-curves, and run the main line in a long, lazy diagonal from one corner to the other.
  • Set the palette in pale pastel: pink, powder blue, cream, soft green and gold, with 70% of the area above value 60%.
  • Paint small, intimate scenes of 0.5 to 1.5m: lovers, a swing, a boudoir, a garden party.
  • Surround the figures with soft, leafy and cloud-like masses that frame them like a shell.
  • Keep the paint thin and the touch light: feathery strokes of 2–8mm with a visible, quick flick in the foliage.
  • Light the picture from a high, soft, golden source with weak shadows that stay warm and pale.
  • Add ornament: ribbons, flowers, shells and gilt, in small amounts at the edge of the figure.

Anti-patterns

  • Heavy, dark ground and strong chiaroscuro
  • Solemn, heroic or religious subjects with a moral
  • Hard, straight architectural lines and strict symmetry
  • Deep, saturated primary colour
  • Rough, thick or dramatic paint handling
  • Large, monumental figures that fill the picture
