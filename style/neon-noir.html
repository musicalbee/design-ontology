Neon noir

film · 1981–today · also: Neo-noir, Synth noir

A night crime film in colour. A lone driver or a replicant moves through a wet city of signs. Magenta and cyan light hits glass, rain and chrome. The pace is slow, the score is synthesised and the symmetry is cold.

Core thesis

Coloured signs and a synth score turn the noir trap into a glowing night city, so the viewer reads loneliness in a clean, still, stylised frame.

Design principles

  • Light the night with neon in magenta and cyan, and let one sign be the key and a second sign the rim.
  • Compose in 2.39:1 with the figure small and central, in a symmetric street or a corridor.
  • Wet the ground so it mirrors the signs, and add haze to show the beam.
  • Move the camera on a slow dolly or a car mount at 0.2 to 0.6 m/s, or hold it still.
  • Hold a take for 6 to 15 s, with a pause before and after each line and a long close shot on the face.
  • Use a synthesiser score with a steady pulse, and keep the dialogue short.
  • Keep the hero silent and the violence short, sudden and clear.

Anti-patterns

  • Flat daylight
  • A warm, busy colour comedy
  • A handheld, shaky camera
  • A rush of fast cuts
  • A full orchestral score
  • A talkative hero who explains the plot
