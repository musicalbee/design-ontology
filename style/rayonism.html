Rayonism

painting · 1910–1914 · also: Rayism, Luchism

Pictures built from lines that stand for rays of light thrown back by objects. The rays cross in sharp, coloured beams, and the object breaks into a spray of lines in a Cubist and Futurist space.

Core thesis

What the eye sees is the sum of the rays of light that objects throw back, so the painter draws the crossing of the rays and not the objects.

Design principles

  • Draw the rays as straight, narrow lines of 3–15mm, in a fan or a bundle that crosses the picture.
  • Cross the rays at 10–45°, and let the form appear where two or more rays meet.
  • Set the ray lines in contrasting colours, one warm against one cool, over a ground of broken colour.
  • Break the object into 8–20 shards with a sharp edge, and keep the shards in motion along the rays.
  • Keep the space flat, with 2–3 layers of overlapping beams, and avoid a vanishing point.
  • Show a little of the subject, such as a forest, a street or a fish, so that the eye finds it in the beams.
  • Paint with a dry, quick stroke so that the beams have a feathered edge.

Anti-patterns

  • Round, smooth modelling of volume
  • A single viewpoint and a calm still life
  • Soft, atmospheric haze
  • Flat colour patches with a thick outline
  • A tidy grid or a circle motif of the Delaunays
  • A deep scene with a clear horizon
