Cubism

painting · 1907–1920s · also: Analytic Cubism, Synthetic Cubism

A picture that shows an object from several sides at once. Analytic Cubism breaks the subject into faceted planes in a narrow brown-grey key; Synthetic Cubism rebuilds it from flat shapes, pasted paper and brighter colour.

Core thesis

A picture is an object in its own right, and it shows what a thing is by combining several viewpoints on one flat surface.

Design principles

  • Break the subject into 20–60 planes with mostly straight edges, and show each plane from a different viewpoint.
  • Keep the space shallow: stack planes in 2–4 layers that lean forward and never open a deep perspective.
  • In the analytic phase, limit the palette to 3–4 hues of ochre, grey, brown and olive.
  • Make the contour pass through the form: let edges open, so that figure and ground exchange places.
  • Set a grid of verticals and horizontals as the armature, and tilt the planes 5–20° from it.
  • In the synthetic phase, use flat shapes in solid colour, and add real texture: pasted paper, sand, a stencilled letter.
  • Choose a plain, close subject that stands on a table: a guitar, a bottle, a glass, a newspaper, a head.

Anti-patterns

  • Deep single-point perspective with a receding floor
  • Smooth modelling that turns a form as one solid volume
  • Soft atmospheric edges and blended colour
  • A single viewpoint and a full, undistorted face
  • Strong cast shadows from one light source
  • Sentimental or narrative subjects that need a story
