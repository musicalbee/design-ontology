Fiesta colourware

ceramics · 1936–1973 · also: American solid-colour dinnerware, Mix-and-match colour tableware, Fiesta

Solid-colour dinnerware for the American home. A round Art Deco shape with concentric rings is glazed in one strong colour, and the buyer mixes the colours of the open stock. The first red glaze held uranium.

Core thesis

One simple, ringed shape in a set of strong glaze colours lets every buyer build a table of their own, one piece at a time.

Design principles

  • Use one shape family with concentric rings in relief on the rim and the foot.
  • Glaze each piece in one solid colour, and offer 5 or more colours in the first year.
  • Sell the line as open stock: each piece is sold by itself, and the buyer picks the colour.
  • Make the glaze thick and semi-opaque so that the colour covers the body.
  • Fire a red glaze with uranium oxide to get an orange-red.
  • Leave the base unglazed in a ring where the piece stood in the kiln.
  • Add a new colour from time to time to keep the line new.

Anti-patterns

  • A printed or painted pattern on the face
  • A white porcelain body with a thin glaze
  • A gilt rim and a floral band
  • A pastel matt colour with a soft, uneven tone
