Cyberdelia

internet · 1988–1996 · also: Cyber-psychedelia, Cyberdelic, Rave cyberpunk

The psychedelic side of early cyberculture. It joins cyberpunk with acid colour: fractals, kaleidoscopes, posterised Photoshop collage, early 3D type and rave flyers. It takes its name from the club in the film Hackers (1995).

Core thesis

The computer is a consciousness machine: use acid colour, fractal repetition and raw early software effects to show a mind that has gone into the network.

Design principles

  • Fill the ground with a fractal or kaleidoscope tile that repeats 6 or 8 times around a centre, at 40% to 60% opacity.
  • Set a palette of 4 acid hues (#FF2BD6, #19F0FF, #B6FF1A, #FF8A00) on a deep violet ground (#12002B).
  • Use gradients with hard bands: posterise to 5 to 7 steps, and never blend smoothly.
  • Set display type in extended, outlined or 3D-extruded letters at 72px or larger, with a 4px offset copy in a second hue.
  • Collage cut-out photographs with a 2px white keyline and a posterised colour wash at 60% opacity.
  • Rotate accent elements by 3° to 12° and overlap layers by 16px to 48px.
  • Keep body text on a solid dark block of 90% opacity at 16px with contrast of 7:1 or more.

Anti-patterns

  • Muted, balanced palettes
  • Smooth photographic gradients
  • Strict symmetric grids with wide margins
  • Corporate sans-serif display type
  • Flat vector icons with 1px strokes
  • Calm, minimal pages with one image
  • Beige and grey hardware panels

Palette

  • Deep Violet #12002B background
  • Acid Magenta #FF2BD6 primary
  • Electric Cyan #19F0FF accent
  • Acid Lime #B6FF1A second accent
  • Rave Orange #FF8A00 highlight
  • Panel Plum #2B0A5E surface
