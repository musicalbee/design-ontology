Pacific Rim fusion

cuisine · 1983–2005 · also: East-west fusion, Euro-Asian cuisine, Hawaii Regional cuisine

French technique and Californian produce with the sauces and the fish of Asia. Chinois on Main (1983), Roy's in Honolulu (1988) and Matsuhisa and Nobu from 1987 put soy, ginger, miso and raw fish on a plated menu.

Core thesis

A chef from one tradition can use the pantry and the flavours of another, if the cook keeps the balance of each dish and the precision of the technique.

Design principles

  • Cook with French method and season with the sauces of Asia: soy, ginger, miso, chilli and citrus.
  • Sear or wok-cook the main piece quickly and leave the centre rare.
  • Pair raw fish with a hot oil or a sharp dressing of yuzu and soy.
  • Build a sauce from a reduction of stock, a butter and an Asian seasoning, for example a soy and mustard butter sauce.
  • Serve a mix of small plates in the middle of the table, or an arranged plate of one main item.
  • Use produce of the Pacific: tuna, shellfish, tropical fruit and local greens.
  • Set the food on a bright plate with a clear shape, and dress it with a herb or a few strips of vegetable.

Anti-patterns

  • A heavy cream sauce on Asian food
  • A dish with five unrelated spices
  • Overcooked fish
  • A tall tower with a garnish
  • A flat copy of one national dish
  • Canned or frozen sauce
