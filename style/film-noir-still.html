Film noir still photography

photography · 1940s–today · also: Noir portrait, Chiaroscuro still

Black-and-white stills with one hard light, deep shadow and a figure caught in a graphic frame. The look comes from the 1940s crime film, and it stays alive in fashion, posters and portraits.

Core thesis

Darkness holds the story, so the picture shows only as much light as the mood needs.

Design principles

  • Use a single hard source, a spot or a bare bulb, at 45° to 90° from the lens axis.
  • Hold a key-to-fill ratio of 16:1 (4 stops) or more, and let half the face fall into black.
  • Cast a pattern on a wall: blinds, a lattice or a doorway, with 3 to 7 bars.
  • Use a 50mm to 85mm lens at f/2.8 to f/5.6 and shoot slightly from below or at a Dutch tilt of 5° to 15°.
  • Pose the figure with a hat, a cigarette or a gun, in a doorway or on stairs.
  • Print on a hard grade with true black at 0% and a single highlight at 95–100%.

Anti-patterns

  • Soft, even light with open shadows
  • Bright, pastel or saturated colour
  • Cheerful expressions
  • Clean modern sets with no texture
  • Digital noise that looks like a filter
  • Cartoon props that break the mood
