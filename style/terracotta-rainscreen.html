Terracotta rainscreen

ceramics · 1990 to the present · also: Ventilated terracotta facade, Extruded terracotta cladding, Baguette terracotta facade

A modern wall skin of extruded, hollow terracotta panels that hang on rails with an air gap behind them. The clay face is matt, flat and in earth colours, with a thin glaze or an engobe.

Core thesis

A wall of fired clay can be a light, ventilated skin: the panel keeps the rain from the insulation, and the air behind it dries the wall.

Design principles

  • Extrude the panel hollow in a die; the wall of the panel is 15 to 30 mm thick.
  • Hang each panel on a rail of stainless steel or aluminium, with an air gap of 20 to 50 mm.
  • Keep the joint open, 6 to 10 mm, so that the wall can drain and dry.
  • Use a matt face in earth colours: red, brown, buff, grey and white.
  • Make the panel in a width of 300 to 600 mm and a length up to 1.5 m, to match the grid of the building.
  • Fire the panel once; the engobe or the glaze fires together with the body.
  • Use baguettes or fins for a facade with depth and shadow.

Anti-patterns

  • A thick, solid terracotta block laid in mortar
  • A glossy, mirror-like glaze on the panel
  • A closed joint with no air gap
  • Heavy, carved ornament
  • A bright printed pattern
