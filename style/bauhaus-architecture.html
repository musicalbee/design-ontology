Bauhaus Architecture

architecture · 1919–1933 · also: Bauhaus building, Neues Bauen (Bauhaus strand)

A functional building of asymmetric wings, each wing sized by its use, joined by bridges and glazed on the work side. Gropius and Meyer taught building as the last step of a workshop education that joined art, craft and industry.

Core thesis

Form follows use: each function gets its own volume, and the plan is the generator of the building, not the facade.

Design principles

  • Divide the programme into separate wings by function, and size each wing by its use: workshop, studio, school, dwelling.
  • Arrange the wings in a pinwheel or an L around a pivot, with no central axis and no principal front.
  • Glaze the work side: a full-height curtain wall of 3 to 4 storeys on the workshop wing, and ribbon windows on the dwelling wing.
  • Keep the frame in concrete or steel, with infill walls in white render and a flat roof with an internal drain.
  • Set the module by use: a 1.1 to 1.25m grid for dwelling cells, and 5 to 6m bays for workshops.
  • Use primary colour only to mark function, in flat planes inside the building: red, yellow and blue, plus black, white and grey.

Anti-patterns

  • A symmetric front on one ceremonial axis
  • Pitched roofs, gables or dormers
  • Applied ornament, mouldings or decorative tile
  • Wings or rooms sized by the facade and not by use
  • Plan forms that hide the function of each volume
  • Rough, hand-textured or historicist surfaces
