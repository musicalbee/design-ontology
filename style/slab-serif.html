Slab serif

typeface · 1815–present · also: Egyptian, Mécane, Antique, Clarendon (bracketed slabs)

Heavy, square serifs on a sturdy letter with low contrast. Born on the poster in industrial Britain, it carries a loud, solid and practical voice, and modern text cuts serve screens and typewriters as well.

Core thesis

A letter for a loud and busy world must be heavy, square and hard to break.

Design principles

  • Make the serifs at least 0.6 of the stem thickness and square at the ends.
  • Keep the stroke contrast at 1.0–1.8:1, measured on the o.
  • Use no bracket (the Egyptian) or a small one (the Clarendon), and keep the choice the same in all letters.
  • Hold the x-height at 0.66–0.76 of the cap height.
  • Set titles in a bold or heavy weight; text cuts use a regular weight with serifs 0.6–0.8 of the stem.
  • Space the letters a little loose: the slabs need 0.01–0.02 em more room than a sans.

Anti-patterns

  • Hairline serifs or hairline strokes
  • Strong calligraphic stress
  • Tight negative tracking that makes the slabs touch
  • Delicate, light weights for display
  • Mixing bracketed and unbracketed slabs in one setting
