Grande cuisine

cuisine · 1815–1880 · also: Carême cuisine, Grand cuisine, High French cuisine of the 19th century

The cuisine of the great houses as Marie-Antoine Carême wrote it. Four grand sauces form the base of a system. Pastry and sugar make a pièce montée that looks like a building. Many dishes stand on the table at once.

Core thesis

Make cooking an art of order and show: a system of sauces on the stove, and architecture of sugar and pastry on the table.

Design principles

  • Derive each sauce from a small set of grand sauces: espagnole, velouté, allemande and béchamel.
  • Cook the stock first, and clarify it until it is clear as glass.
  • Build a pièce montée of sugar and pastry in the shape of a temple, a ruin or a fountain.
  • Dress each dish with hâtelets, a skewer with a truffle or a crayfish at the top.
  • Serve à la française: many dishes at once, in a pattern drawn on a plan.
  • Cover a cold dish with chaud-froid and aspic, and pipe a pattern on it.
  • Write each recipe in exact steps, and give each cut and each garnish its name.

Anti-patterns

  • A sauce made without a stock
  • A plain dish with no garnish
  • A dish for one guest on one plate
  • A table without a centrepiece
  • Chilli, soy or raw fish
  • Spice in the manner of the Middle Ages
