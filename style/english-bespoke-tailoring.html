English bespoke tailoring

fashion · 1800–present · also: Savile Row tailoring, London cut, The English drape

The cut of the London tailor: a plain, dark suit in fine worsted, drafted for one body and built by hand on hair canvas.

Core thesis

A man's suit is drafted for one body, built inside with canvas, and shown outside by a plain surface and a line with no fuss.

Design principles

  • Draft the pattern from 20 or more measures, and cut it by hand from a single length of cloth.
  • Baste a first fitting, then a forward fitting, then a finished fitting; allow 50 or more hours of work for a suit.
  • Build the chest on a canvas of horsehair and wool, pad-stitched by hand to hold the roll of the lapel.
  • Cut the coat with a clean chest and a gentle drape, and a waist that is lightly suppressed.
  • Set the sleeve head with a small roped or natural edge; sew the buttonholes by hand.
  • Choose a dark, plain cloth of 280 to 400 g per metre: navy, charcoal, grey.
  • Keep the trousers high at the waist, with pleats, a straight leg and a turn-up.

Anti-patterns

  • A boxy, ready-made block with no fitting
  • Glued fusible interlining in the chest
  • Loud, shiny or stretch suiting
  • A tight, short, cropped jacket
  • Padded, square shoulders with a hard edge
  • Visible logos or large buttons
