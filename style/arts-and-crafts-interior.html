Arts and Crafts

interior · 1861–1920 · also: Arts and Crafts interior, Craftsman interior, Mission style

The craft reform of the Victorian room. Handmade oak furniture shows its joints, a deep inglenook holds the hearth, and one stylised plant pattern runs on the walls or the textiles; low beams and built-in settles make the room honest and warm.

Core thesis

A room is honest when every piece is made by hand from local materials and shows how it is made.

Design principles

  • Show the construction: exposed joints, pegs, beams and hand-wrought hardware.
  • Use one stylised plant or bird pattern per room, on the paper or on the textiles, not on both.
  • Build furniture into the room: settles, window seats, dressers and bookcases.
  • Make the hearth a room in the room: an inglenook with built-in seats.
  • Keep colours from nature: oak brown, sage green, ochre and madder red.
  • Run a high picture rail or a plate shelf at about 2/3 of the wall height, so that the ceiling feels low.
  • Use hammered copper, leaded glass and hand-thrown pottery for the accents.

Anti-patterns

  • Machine-carved ornament
  • Veneer or gloss lacquer that hides the wood
  • Many patterns on one wall
  • Saturated synthetic dyes
  • Mass-produced reproduction furniture
