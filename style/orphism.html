Orphism

painting · 1912–1914 · also: Orphic Cubism, Simultanism

Cubist structure with the bright colour of the Fauves, pushed toward pure abstraction. Discs and arcs of pure colour set side by side make rhythm and light, and the subject melts away.

Core thesis

Colour alone can build the form and the movement of a picture, so the painter makes new wholes from pure colour and no longer shows the object.

Design principles

  • Build the picture from arcs and discs of pure colour, each 50–300mm across, in overlapping rings.
  • Set complementary colours side by side in each ring, so that the contrast makes the colour vibrate.
  • Break the object into planes as in Cubism, then drop the object when the colour is able to carry the form.
  • Repeat a circle or an arc 5–12 times, with the colour shifting by one step each time, to give the eye a beat.
  • Keep the space shallow, with 2–3 layers, and let the discs overlap so that they appear to move.
  • Use a bright, clear colour in the full spectrum, and keep black to a minimum.
  • Take the subject from the modern world, such as a tower, a window, a city or an aeroplane, and then dissolve it.

Anti-patterns

  • The grey and brown key of Analytic Cubism
  • A recognisable single viewpoint and a clear object
  • Deep perspective with a receding floor
  • Soft, atmospheric blending
  • Strict black outlines around flat shapes
  • A narrative scene with a story
