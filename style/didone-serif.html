Didone

typeface · 1784–1830 · also: Modern serif, Didone, Bodoni style, Fat face (heavy cuts)

The extreme of the roman: a vertical stress, hairline serifs with no bracket, and a strong contrast between stem and hairline. At display sizes it is the voice of fashion and luxury; in small text it sparkles and tires the eye.

Core thesis

A letter is a geometric figure of thick and thin, cut as fine as the metal and the paper allow.

Design principles

  • Keep the stroke contrast at 4:1 or more in text cuts and 8:1 or more in display cuts, measured on the o.
  • Set the stress exactly vertical: the thinnest parts of the o are at 12 and 6 o'clock.
  • Draw the serifs as flat hairlines with no bracket, at a right angle to the stem.
  • Use ball terminals on a, c, f, g, j, r and y.
  • Set the face at 18 pt or more; at text sizes, use an optical cut with heavier hairlines.
  • Give the page wide white space; the face needs air around it.

Anti-patterns

  • Bracketed or wedge serifs
  • A diagonal stress
  • Text below 10 pt in a display cut with 10:1 contrast
  • Low-resolution or low-contrast backgrounds that break the hairlines
  • Wide positive tracking on the lower case
  • Heavy, rounded corners on the stems
