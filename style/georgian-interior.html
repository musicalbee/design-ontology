Georgian

interior · 1714–1830 · also: Georgian interior, Palladian interior, Adam style

The British and colonial room of the four Georges. Walls follow a classical order of dado, field and cornice; a chimneypiece on the centre line, paired doors and sash windows set one axis; mahogany furniture stands against the walls, and the centre of the floor stays clear.

Core thesis

A room is good when its proportions obey the classical rules and each wall answers the wall opposite it.

Design principles

  • Divide each wall into dado, field and frieze in the proportions of a column: dado at about one fifth of the wall height.
  • Put the chimneypiece on the centre of the main wall and match doors, windows and pier glasses about that axis.
  • Give the room simple whole-number ratios: a single cube, a double cube or 2:3.
  • Stand furniture against the walls when the room is not in use; move chairs to the centre only for an event.
  • Use one main wall colour in a soft mineral tone and pick out mouldings in white or a lighter tint.
  • Keep ornament on the architecture: plaster ceiling, cornice, chimneypiece and door cases; keep the walls plain between them.
  • Hang pictures in pairs and in symmetric sets about the chimneypiece.

Anti-patterns

  • Furniture that floats at an angle in the room
  • Walls with no dado or cornice
  • A focal point off the centre line of a wall
  • Saturated synthetic colour
  • Open shelves full of small objects
  • Modern recessed downlights in a decorated ceiling
