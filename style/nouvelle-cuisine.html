Nouvelle cuisine

cuisine · 1965–1990 · also: New French cuisine, Cuisine du marché

A light French restaurant kitchen. The chef buys fresh produce at the market, cooks fish and vegetables for a short time, makes sauces without flour, and plates each dish in the kitchen. Gault and Millau gave it ten rules in 1973.

Core thesis

Take the weight out of Escoffier: fresh market produce, short cooking and a sauce without flour let the taste of the ingredient lead.

Design principles

  • Buy produce at the market on the day, and write the menu from what you bought.
  • Cook fish, shellfish and green vegetables for the shortest time: fish stays translucent at the centre, vegetables stay crisp.
  • Make each sauce from the cooking juices, a reduction and a little cream or butter; leave out the roux.
  • Keep the menu short: three or four dishes for each course, not a long list.
  • Plate each dish in the kitchen on a large white plate, one portion for one guest.
  • Leave about 40 per cent of the plate empty, and cut the garnish to two or three items.
  • Pair a savoury main ingredient with fruit, a flower or a herb, and keep every flavour clear.
  • Let the chef follow the season and invent: a new dish each week.

Anti-patterns

  • A heavy sauce thickened with flour and cooked for hours
  • Game or meat hung until it is high
  • A long menu with a dish for every taste
  • Fish and vegetables cooked until grey and soft
  • A garnish that covers the plate
  • A dish carved or finished at a trolley beside the table
