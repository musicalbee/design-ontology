Cuisine minceur

cuisine · 1975–1990 · also: Slimming cuisine, Spa gourmet cuisine

Michel Guérard's light cooking for a spa hotel. Each dish has few calories but the plate of a three-star kitchen: steam instead of butter, fromage blanc and vegetable purée instead of cream and flour, and a small portion.

Core thesis

A dish can be low in fat and sugar and still be haute cuisine, if the cook uses exact technique and fresh produce in place of butter and cream.

Design principles

  • Cook in steam, in a court-bouillon or in a covered pan with a spoon of water, not in butter.
  • Bind a sauce with fromage blanc, yoghurt, a vegetable purée or a reduced stock; leave out flour, cream and egg yolk.
  • Count the energy of each dish and keep a full menu at about 500 to 800 kcal.
  • Season with herbs, lemon, vinegar and spices so that the salt and the fat can stay low.
  • Use no sugar in a sauce; take the sweet taste from fruit and vegetables.
  • Serve each dish as a small portion on a large white plate, so the meal still has many courses.
  • Plate with the care of a three-star kitchen: a sauce pooled under the food, one herb on top.
  • Keep the taste of each ingredient clear; do not hide it behind a diet label.

Anti-patterns

  • A sauce thickened with flour or egg yolk
  • Butter or cream as the base of a sauce
  • A dessert of cream and sugar
  • A bare plate of boiled food with no seasoning
  • A large heap of food
  • A menu that names the calories on the plate
