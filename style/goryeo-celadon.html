Goryeo celadon

ceramics · 1100–1350 · also: Korean celadon, Inlaid celadon, Sanggam celadon

Korean celadon of the Goryeo kingdom. A thin, clear grey-green glaze covers a fine body, and potters inlay white and black slip into incised lines to make a pattern under the glaze.

Core thesis

A pot is a clear green lens over a drawing: the glaze is almost transparent and the inlaid slip line shows through it like ink in water.

Design principles

  • Fire a fine grey stoneware to a thin, translucent glaze of green-blue, about 0.3 to 0.5 mm thick.
  • Cut the pattern into the leather-hard clay with a fine tool.
  • Fill the cuts with white slip and black slip, then scrape the surface clean (sanggam inlay).
  • Use cranes, clouds, willows, chrysanthemums and lotus as the main motifs.
  • Shape the body as a graceful maebyeong with a high shoulder, or a gourd-shaped ewer.
  • Mould a lid or a spout as a lion, a duck or a turtle-dragon, for the finest pieces.
  • Fire at 1200 to 1250 °C in reduction on small clay spurs.

Anti-patterns

  • A thick opaque glaze that hides the inlay
  • Pattern painted on the glaze surface
  • A brown or yellow glaze from weak reduction
  • A heavy, thick-walled form with no curve
  • Plain geometric borders that replace the natural motifs
