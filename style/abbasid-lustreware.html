Abbasid lustreware

ceramics · 800–1050 · also: Early Islamic lustre, Basra lustre, Lustre-painted Abbasid ware

The first pottery with a metallic sheen. Potters in Abbasid Iraq painted copper and silver oxides over an opaque white tin glaze and fired them in smoke to a gold-brown shine.

Core thesis

A cheap clay pot can shine like metal when a white tin glaze and a thin film of reduced silver and copper turn into a mirror of gold.

Design principles

  • Cover the earthenware body with an opaque white tin glaze 0.5 to 1 mm thick.
  • Fire the glaze first, then paint metal oxides in vinegar or ochre over it.
  • Fire a second time at 600 to 700 °C in a smoky, reducing kiln, so that the metal film forms.
  • Polish the cooled surface to remove the red clay residue and show the gold-brown shine.
  • Fill the surface with geometric bands, birds, vegetal scrolls and Kufic-like script.
  • Leave dark ground with light motifs, or the reverse, in two clear tones.
  • Keep the shape simple: a flared bowl on a low foot ring, so the sheen catches the light.

Anti-patterns

  • A flat matt surface with no metallic reflection
  • Cobalt blue or a coloured glaze under the lustre in large amounts
  • Figurative scenes drawn with realistic perspective
  • A thick coarse wall with no clear foot
  • Gold leaf or applied gilding instead of true lustre
