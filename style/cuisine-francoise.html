Cuisine françoise

cuisine · 1651–1800 · also: La Varenne cuisine, French classical cuisine of the 17th century

The French kitchen after La Varenne. Le Cuisinier françois of 1651 sets down the roux, the stock, the bouquet garni and fines herbes. Spice falls to pepper and nutmeg, and butter replaces lard. The flavour of the main food leads.

Core thesis

Cook for the taste of the food itself: build each sauce on a stock and a roux, and use herbs where the medieval kitchen used spice.

Design principles

  • Make a stock first, and build each sauce and each soup on it.
  • Thicken a sauce with a roux of flour and butter, not with bread crumbs.
  • Use fines herbes, a bouquet garni and mushrooms in place of a heavy load of spice.
  • Cut the spice to pepper, nutmeg and clove, and use butter in place of lard.
  • Cook each dish with its own sauce, and give the soup and the entrée their own place.
  • Serve in the French manner: three services of dishes laid out in a pattern on the table.
  • Cook fresh vegetables and fish, and write each recipe with its method in short steps.

Anti-patterns

  • A sauce thickened with soaked bread and ground almond
  • A heavy load of ginger, cinnamon and grains of paradise
  • Sugar in each savoury dish
  • A dish of one flat colour with saffron
  • A plated course for one guest
  • Chilli, soy or tropical spice
