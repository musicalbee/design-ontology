Transitional serif

typeface · 1757–1800 · also: Réale, Baroque serif, Baskerville type

The Enlightenment roman between the old style and the Didone: the stress turns nearly vertical, the contrast rises, and the serifs become flatter and sharper. It is precise but still warm, and it reads well in text.

Core thesis

A letter can be drawn by reason and still serve the reader: the pen gives way to the engraver's line, but not yet to the extreme.

Design principles

  • Keep the stroke contrast between 2.5:1 and 4.5:1, measured on the lower-case o.
  • Set the stress vertical or within 10° of it.
  • Give the serifs a short bracket and flat, level head serifs on the lower case.
  • Make the round letters wide and the bowls full: the o is close to a circle in the lower case.
  • Hold the x-height at 0.60–0.70 of the cap height.
  • Use ball terminals on a, c, f, g and r, smaller than in a Didone.

Anti-patterns

  • A strong diagonal stress from a broad-nib pen
  • Hairlines finer than 0.15 of the stem in text sizes
  • Unbracketed, hairline serifs at a right angle to the stem
  • Monoline strokes with no contrast
  • Tracking tighter than −0.01 em in text
