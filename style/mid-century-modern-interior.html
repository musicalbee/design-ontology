Mid-century modern interior

interior · 1945–1969 · also: MCM interior, Mid-century, Atomic-age interior

The open-plan post-war home of the United States and Europe. Low teak and moulded-plywood furniture on tapered legs stands in front of a glass wall to the garden; a stone fireplace or the view is the focal point, and a few saturated accents warm a neutral room.

Core thesis

The modern home joins the room to the landscape, and good design made in series can be warm as well as rational.

Design principles

  • Keep furniture low: sofa backs at 70–75 cm and sideboards at 75 cm, so that one horizon line runs under the window sill.
  • Lift each piece on tapered splayed legs of 15–25 cm, so that the floor shows under it.
  • Make the glass wall to the garden or a stone fireplace the focal point, and face the main sofa toward it.
  • Group the seats on one area rug around a low table; the rug holds at least the front legs of each seat.
  • Repeat the structural bay of the house, near 240 cm, in the window mullions, the ceiling beams and the length of the sideboard.
  • Use warm wood for 25–35% of the visible surface, and set one rough plane of stone or brick against it.
  • Limit saturated colour to 10%: cushions, one chair, a lamp or a print in mustard, orange or teal.

Anti-patterns

  • Heavy furniture that sits on the floor without visible legs
  • Tall cabinets or high-back sofas in front of the windows
  • Historical mouldings, panelling or carved ornament
  • A formal mirrored layout about a centre line
  • Cold blue-white light above 4000 K
  • Gloss lacquer on large wood surfaces
