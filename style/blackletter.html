Blackletter

typeface · 1454–present · also: Gothic script, Fraktur, Textura, Old English, Broken script

The broken letter of the Gothic book hand: narrow, dark, with strokes that turn at sharp angles instead of curves. Gutenberg printed his Bible in textura; Fraktur stayed the German text face until 1941. Today it carries tradition, heraldry, metal and rebellion.

Core thesis

A letter written with a broad nib held at a steep angle breaks each curve into straight strokes and makes a dark, even texture on the page.

Design principles

  • Break the round strokes into straight segments that meet at angles; the bowl of the o is a hexagon or a lozenge in textura.
  • Hold the nib at 30–45°, so the vertical stems are heavy and the diagonals and joins are hairlines.
  • Keep the letters narrow and the space inside them about equal to the stem, so the line is a dark, even band.
  • Give the stems diamond feet and heads (textura) or a forked or curved top (Fraktur).
  • Make the capitals rich and round, with spurs and hairline flourishes, and never set a full word in them.
  • Use the long s (ſ) and the ligatures ch, ck, tz and ſt in Fraktur text.

Anti-patterns

  • Words set in all capitals
  • Wide letter spacing or positive tracking
  • Smooth round bowls as in a roman
  • A blackletter for long text on a screen for a general reader
  • Blackletter mixed with a script in one line
