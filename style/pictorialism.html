Pictorialism

photography · 1885–1914 · also: Photo-Secession look, Soft-focus art photography

Photography that borrows the look of drawing and etching. Soft-focus lenses, hand-worked pigment prints and warm toning blur the line between a photograph and a painting, to claim the status of art.

Core thesis

A photograph is art when the maker's hand shows in the image, so the print must look made and not recorded.

Design principles

  • Soften the image with a soft-focus lens or a diffusion filter so that edge sharpness falls to about 60% of a sharp lens.
  • Hold the tonal range narrow: lift the black point to 12–18% and roll the highlights off at 92–95%.
  • Tone the print warm: shadows near #3A2E26 and highlights near #E8DCC6, as in platinum or gum bichromate.
  • Light the subject from a large soft source, such as a north window or overcast sky, at a key-to-fill ratio of 2:1.
  • Compose like a picture: a few big tonal masses, a low horizon and a figure or object set slightly off centre.
  • Choose subjects with a mood, such as mist, evening, a pool, a woman in a loose dress or a bare tree, over a record of fact.

Anti-patterns

  • Crisp edge-to-edge sharpness and visible lens detail
  • Hard specular highlights and pure white paper
  • Saturated colour or a cool neutral grey print
  • Modern subjects shown without atmosphere, such as signs, cars and screens
  • Digital sharpening halos and clean clinical noise reduction
  • A flat documentary viewpoint at eye level with no tonal design
