Minimalism

painting · 1959–1975 · also: Minimal art, ABC art, Hard-edge minimalism

Flat, exact, repeated forms: stripes, grids and bare shapes in one or two colours. The picture is an object with no story and no hand, and the viewer sees what is there.

Core thesis

A picture is a plain object: what you see is what is there, so that form is reduced to the fewest parts and no illusion, gesture or story remains.

Design principles

  • Reduce the picture to 1–3 elements: a stripe, a grid, a bar or a single shape.
  • Repeat one unit, such as a stripe of 40–80mm, 8–40 times, in a strict order.
  • Paint flat, even coats with no stroke, and keep the edge sharp to under 1mm.
  • Limit the colour to 1–3 hues, or to black, grey and white, with no mixing and no gradient.
  • Let the shape of the canvas follow the pattern: a notched, shaped or very wide support is fine.
  • Fix the system before the painting starts, and follow it with no change by taste.
  • Give the picture no title that tells a story and no sign of the hand.

Anti-patterns

  • A gesture, a drip or a visible stroke
  • A figure, an object or a landscape
  • A soft, blended or glowing edge
  • A rich, many-hued or decorative palette
  • An off-centre, free, intuitive layout
  • A title that tells a story or a feeling
