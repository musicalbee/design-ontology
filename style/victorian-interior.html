Victorian

interior · 1837–1901 · also: Victorian interior, Victorian parlour, High Victorian

The middle-class room of the industrial century. Machine-made pattern covers walls, floors and furniture; dark, saturated colours, heavy curtains and many objects make a dense, enclosed parlour around the hearth.

Core thesis

A home shows the taste, the travels and the respectability of its family; more objects and more pattern mean more comfort.

Design principles

  • Divide the wall into dado, filling and frieze, each with its own paper or paint.
  • Use deep, saturated colours: crimson, bottle green, plum and brown, with gold.
  • Layer textiles: a fitted carpet, a rug on it, a tablecloth on the table and a fringed cover on the mantel.
  • Group chairs in several conversation circles, not one; the room holds many zones.
  • Display collections on the mantel, on whatnots and in cabinets.
  • Hang pictures close together on a picture rail, frame to frame.
  • Filter the daylight through three layers at the window: blind, lace and heavy drapes.

Anti-patterns

  • Bare walls with no pattern
  • White or pale walls in the main rooms
  • A single centred group of furniture with empty floor around it
  • Bright, unfiltered daylight
  • Thin, low modern furniture
