Liuli glazed roof tile

ceramics · 1420–1911 · also: Chinese glazed roof tile, Imperial yellow tile roof, Liuli

Glazed roof tiles in yellow, green and blue that the imperial kilns made for the palaces and temples of Beijing. Rank and meaning decide the colour: imperial yellow covers the main halls of the Forbidden City.

Core thesis

A roof tile is a sign of rank: its colour, its ridge figures and its curved line tell the viewer which hall is for the emperor.

Design principles

  • Glaze the main halls in yellow, the princes' houses in green and the Temple of Heaven in blue.
  • Cover the roof with half-round tiles and flat tiles in rows, and finish the eave with a round disc tile.
  • Place a row of glazed animals on each hip ridge; use up to 9 figures for the highest hall.
  • Mould each ridge piece, finial and dragon in a mould, and glaze it in the colour of the roof.
  • Curve the eaves up at the corner, so that the tile line follows a soft S shape.
  • Press the tile from a fine, pure clay and fire it twice: once bare, once with the lead glaze.
  • Build a glazed screen wall with moulded relief tiles, as in the Nine-Dragon Wall.

Anti-patterns

  • A matt grey or brown roof tile with no glaze
  • Random mixed colours with no rank
  • A flat roof or a roof of metal sheet
  • Ridge figures that copy a Western gargoyle
  • A thin, even glaze with no pooling at the edges
