Scandinavian interior photography

photography · 2005–today · also: Nordic home editorial, Soft daylight interior

Calm, bright pictures of rooms: pale wood, white walls, a window and a few objects. The light is soft, the lines are straight and the space is allowed to breathe.

Core thesis

A room looks livable when the light is honest and the objects are few, so the picture shows the light first.

Design principles

  • Shoot with a tilt-shift or a 24mm to 35mm lens at f/8 to f/11, with the camera level at 1.0m to 1.2m high.
  • Use window light only: turn off lamps, and add a white 1.2m reflector on the shadow side.
  • Keep a key-to-fill ratio of 2:1 to 3:1, so that shadows stay soft and open.
  • Correct all verticals to 0° and hold the horizon at 0°.
  • Set 3 to 5 objects in the room; leave 40–60% of the frame as wall, floor or air.
  • Grade to cool daylight, 5500–6000K, and a low saturation of −10 to −20% in a palette of white, pale wood and one green.

Anti-patterns

  • Overhead lamps, flash or mixed colour casts
  • Converging verticals from a tilted camera
  • Cluttered shelves and busy patterns
  • Heavy HDR with glowing windows
  • Saturated, warm yellow grades
  • Wide-angle stretch below 20mm
