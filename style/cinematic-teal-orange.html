Cinematic teal and orange

photography · 2000–today · also: Blockbuster grade, Orange and teal

A staged, shallow-focus frame with warm orange skin against cool teal shadows, graded like a film still. It is a colour recipe from the digital intermediate, and it is now a default look of films, ads and image prompts.

Core thesis

Two opposed hues, the warm skin and the cool environment, give a frame instant depth and a sense of cinema.

Design principles

  • Light with a warm key at 3200 to 3500K and a cool fill or ambient at 5600 to 6500K, at a key-to-fill ratio of 4:1.
  • Shoot at 35 to 85mm (35mm format), f/1.8 to f/2.8, so the subject separates from the background by at least 2 stops of blur.
  • Grade the shadows to teal (hue 185° to 195°) and the skin and highlights to orange (hue 20° to 30°).
  • Reduce the other hues by 20 to 40% in saturation, so that the pair carries the image.
  • Frame at 2.39:1 or 16:9 with a subject on a third line and negative space with a leading line.
  • Add atmosphere: haze, backlight, practical lights and a vignette of −15%.

Anti-patterns

  • Even, neutral white balance on skin and shadow
  • Saturated greens, magentas and yellows that compete with the pair
  • Flat front light and a deep-focus background
  • Casual snapshot framing with no staging
  • Teal skin or orange sky from a heavy-handed hue shift
  • A uniform tint over the whole frame, with no separation
