American pocket watch

fashion · 1850–1930 · also: Railroad watch, American factory watch, Waltham style

The plain, tough pocket watch that American factories made from 1850 with machine-cut interchangeable parts. It has a white enamel dial with black Arabic numerals and a gold-filled or silver case. The rail grade movement kept time for the railroads.

Core thesis

A watch is a public tool. Each part is made to a gauge, the face is clear and the case lasts for decades.

Design principles

  • Cut each plate and wheel to a gauge, so that a spare part fits any watch of the same model.
  • Print black Arabic numerals 1 to 12 on a white enamel dial, with a full minute track to 60.
  • Make the hands as blued spades or plain batons, so that the time shows at a glance.
  • Adjust a rail grade movement to keep time within 30 seconds a week in five positions.
  • Fit 21 jewels in a rail grade movement, and set them in gold settings.
  • Set the hands with a lever beside the crown, so that a knock cannot move them.
  • Make the case 10 to 14 mm thick, with a screw bezel and a snap back that keep out dust.
  • Mark the movement with the maker, the grade and a serial number.

Anti-patterns

  • A thin ornamental dress case that bends in a pocket
  • Script numerals or a crowded dial that slows the eye
  • A fashion colour dial
  • Parts filed by hand to fit one watch only
  • A plastic or steel case of the quartz era
  • A small dial that cannot be read at arm's length
