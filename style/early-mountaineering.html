Early mountaineering

fashion · 1920–1953 · also: Everest expedition dress, Alpine tweed and gabardine, Pre-nylon climbing dress

The dress of the climber before nylon: layers of wool, silk and flannel under a windproof gabardine smock, with tweed breeches, puttees and nailed boots.

Core thesis

Warmth comes from many thin layers, and wind is the enemy. The climber wears wool next to the skin and one tight cotton shell on top.

Design principles

  • Wear 4 to 6 thin layers, so that the climber can add or shed one at each rest.
  • Put wool or silk next to the skin, to hold heat when wet.
  • Close the outer smock with a deep hood and a drawcord at the hem.
  • Weave the outer cloth tight enough to stop a 40 km/h wind.
  • Cut the breeches full at the thigh and tight at the knee, over a wool puttee.
  • Cut the sleeve long, with a wrist cuff that closes over the glove.
  • Stitch the shoulder and the elbow with a double seam.

Anti-patterns

  • A single thick coat that cannot be shed
  • Cotton next to the skin that holds sweat
  • A loose hem that lets in the wind
  • Bright colour for show
  • A coat that has no hood
