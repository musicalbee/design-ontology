Emblem and badge

logo · 1900–now · also: Badge logo, Seal, Crest mark, Patch

A closed shape, a circle, a shield or a roundel, that holds a symbol and the name on a ring or a band. Agencies, clubs, transport lines and makers use it as a seal of membership or of origin.

Core thesis

A mark that is a closed seal says membership and authority: the frame tells the viewer that everything inside belongs together.

Design principles

  • Close the mark in one shape: a circle, a shield or a rounded rectangle; the frame is 4–10% of the mark width.
  • Set the name on an outer ring of text at 10–14% of the diameter, or on a straight band across the centre.
  • Put one central symbol at 40–60% of the inner area: a star, an animal, a tool or a vector arrow.
  • Use 2–4 flat colours, and give the outer ring a dark colour that holds the shape on any ground.
  • Make the mark work at 32 px as a solid disc with the central symbol, even if the ring text no longer reads.
  • Keep the mark symmetric on the vertical axis, except one dynamic element such as a swoosh or an orbit.

Anti-patterns

  • An open composition with no frame
  • Text on the ring smaller than 6% of the diameter
  • More than 5 colours
  • Two competing symbols inside the frame
  • Photographic texture inside the badge
