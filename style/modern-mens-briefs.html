Modern Men's Briefs

fashion · 1935–1969 · also: Y-front briefs, Jockey brief, Knitted briefs

The short, knitted, elastic-waist brief with a Y-shaped overlapping fly that Coopers, Inc. of Kenosha launched in 1935, and that replaced the loose drawers and the union suit.

Core thesis

A man's underwear gives support and freedom of movement. A short knitted brief with a sewn-in waist is better than a loose drawer or a long suit.

Design principles

  • Knit the body of combed cotton in a close, soft jersey, with a stretch in both directions.
  • Cut the leg short and high, with an edge at the upper thigh.
  • Sew an elastic waistband into the top edge, 3 to 4 cm wide.
  • Sew a Y-shaped seam at the front, so that two layers overlap at the fly.
  • Make the front pouch of a double layer of cloth for support.
  • Finish each seam flat, with no ridge against the skin.
  • Keep the colour to white, grey or a plain tone.

Anti-patterns

  • A long, loose drawer tied with a string
  • A one-piece union suit with long legs
  • A woven cloth with no stretch
  • A wide-cut boxer with a loose leg
  • A printed, patterned or logo waistband
