Documentary photography

photography · 1930s–today · also: Social documentary, Photojournalism

Photographs that bear witness to people, work and events. The maker gets close, earns trust and shows what the sitter needs the viewer to see.

Core thesis

A photograph can be evidence and an appeal at once, if the maker stays honest about what happened and with whom.

Design principles

  • Use a 35mm or 50mm lens at 1 to 2.5m, so that the sitter, the room and the work share the frame.
  • Shoot in available light at ISO 400 to 1600, with a shutter of 1/60s or faster, and do not add flash.
  • Show the face and the hands, and the place that explains them.
  • Make 100 or more frames for one story, and edit down to 8 to 20 pictures.
  • Print with a full but natural range: no crushed blacks and no clipped skies.
  • Caption with who, where and when, in plain words.

Anti-patterns

  • Staging a scene or rearranging people
  • Heavy HDR or composite skies
  • Cropping out context that changes the meaning
  • Victim pose and pity as the only note
  • Beauty filters or skin smoothing
  • Captions that invent facts
