Group f/64

photography · 1932–1935 · also: f/64 school, West Coast straight photography

Sharp, rich black-and-white contact prints of rocks, plants, shells and faces, made on large cameras at the smallest stop. The group named itself after that stop.

Core thesis

Pure photography needs no borrowed look from painting; the large camera, the small stop and the full tonal scale are enough.

Design principles

  • Use an 8×10 in view camera and a lens of 210mm to 300mm, stopped down to f/45 to f/64.
  • Expose for the shadows and develop for the highlights: place the darkest detail on Zone III and the brightest on Zone VII or VIII.
  • Make a contact print on glossy paper (grade 2) with no enlargement, so that the print is as sharp as the negative.
  • Show the whole scale: a pure black in the deepest shadow and a clean white in the highest light, with 9 or 10 zones between.
  • Take the subject as it is: a rock, a pepper, a dune or a face, framed by the shape of the plate.
  • Mount the print on white board with no frame decoration.

Anti-patterns

  • Soft focus, gum or bromoil handwork
  • Retouching the negative to change the scene
  • Cropping a print to repair the framing
  • Toning for mood
  • Staged allegory and costume
  • Enlarging a small negative
