Ulm and Braun functionalism

industrial · 1955–1995 · also: Ulm model, Rams functionalism, Good design (Ulm school)

Systematic household products of the Ulm school and the Braun firm: grey and white cases of aluminium and plastic, built on a grid, with controls that explain the object.

Core thesis

A product is a system: it is as little design as possible, it explains its use, and it fits with its neighbours on a common grid.

Design principles

  • Build each product on a grid; set all parts to a module of 5 or 10 mm.
  • Make the case a plain box with radius 1 to 4 mm, in white, grey or black.
  • Put the controls in a clear group, and mark each with a small, grey or white symbol.
  • Use aluminium, steel or matte plastic; no surface has a decorative texture.
  • Keep colour for one function only: a green, red or yellow key or a mark.
  • Make each object part of a family that shares size, grid and colour.
  • Draw for long use: choose a form that stays right after 10 years.
  • Test the object for use, noise and repair before the form is final.

Anti-patterns

  • Yearly changes of form that follow fashion
  • Fake wood and fake chrome
  • Round, soft pod forms and bubble shapes
  • Surface pattern and engraved decoration
  • Many colours with no function
  • Hidden screws that make repair hard
