Crayon lithograph

print · 1820–1900 · also: lithographie au crayon (French), Kreidelithographie (German)

The artist draws with greasy crayon directly on a grained limestone, and the grain catches the crayon as speckled black. The print keeps the drawing's pressure, from pale grain to dense black, with no engraved line.

Core thesis

A greasy crayon on a grained stone keeps the pressure of the hand, so that a drawing prints as drawn, with no engraver between.

Design principles

  • Draw in black crayon on a grained stone; press harder for a darker tone.
  • Let the grain of the stone speckle the pale tones, at about 80 grains an inch.
  • Scrape or scratch the whites out of the black with a needle or a blade.
  • Add the caption in lithographic script under the picture, in the same ink.
  • Print in one black ink on thin paper, so that a paper can run in thousands.
  • Keep the edges soft and smudged; do not trace a hard contour.

Anti-patterns

  • A hard engraved line
  • Halftone dots or a photographic tone
  • Plate mark or emboss
  • Bright flat colour
  • A digital line of even width
