Street photography

photography · 1930s–today · also: Candid street, Decisive moment

Unposed pictures of public life, made with a small camera, a wide lens and quick reflexes. The photographer finds the frame and waits for the moment when gesture, light and geometry agree.

Core thesis

Public life holds pictures that nobody can stage, and the photographer's job is to be in the right place and to see them in time.

Design principles

  • Use a 28mm or 35mm lens (35mm format) and stand 1 to 3m from the subject, so that context and person share the frame.
  • Zone-focus at f/8 to f/11 on a set distance of 2 to 3m, and keep the shutter at 1/250s or faster.
  • Shoot in found light: hard sun and deep shadow at a ratio of 8:1 or more, or the mixed light of a street at night.
  • Layer the frame: put at least three planes (foreground, subject, background) in it, and let edges and shadows cut figures.
  • Wait for the gesture: release when the hand, the gaze or the step is at its peak, within a 1/250s window.
  • Print with a hard grade: true black, bright highlights and visible grain.

Anti-patterns

  • Posed subjects who look at the lens and smile
  • Shallow depth of field that isolates a single face from the street
  • Telephoto compression from a hidden distance
  • Heavy retouching, cloning or removal of distracting people
  • Flat, even light with no shadow geometry
  • Staged scenes with props or hired models
