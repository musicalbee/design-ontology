Digital 3D-printed ceramics

ceramics · 2010–now · also: Additive ceramics, Printed clay vessels, Parametric ceramic vessels

Vessels that a machine builds layer by layer from a clay paste, after a digital model. The visible layer lines, the open lattice and the complex curves give the style its look.

Core thesis

When a machine builds the form from a file, the vessel can have a shape that no wheel or mould can make, and the layer line becomes the ornament.

Design principles

  • Draw the form as a digital model with a parametric rule: a twist, a wave, a lattice or a gradient.
  • Print the clay paste in layers of 0.8 to 2 mm with a nozzle of 1 to 4 mm.
  • Keep the layer lines as the surface texture; do not sand them away.
  • Vary the wall in one piece: a solid base, a thin wall and an open lattice at the top.
  • Print porcelain or stoneware paste that holds its shape, and dry it slowly for 3 to 7 days.
  • Fire the piece in one cycle at 1220 to 1300 °C, with a support of kiln setters where it overhangs.
  • Use a thin satin or clear glaze that keeps the ridges, or leave the surface bare.
  • Print a colour pattern with a ceramic inkjet if the piece needs a graphic surface.

Anti-patterns

  • A smooth, wheel-thrown surface with no layer line
  • A thick, glossy glaze that fills the ridges
  • A traditional folk or floral decoration
  • A form that a plain mould could make
  • A random mess of lines with no rule
