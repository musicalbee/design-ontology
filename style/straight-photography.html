Straight photography

photography · 1915–1950 · also: Pure photography, Group f/64 look

A sharp, unmanipulated print of a real thing, made on a large camera at a small aperture. The beauty comes from the medium itself: fine detail, a long tonal scale and exact focus from front to back.

Core thesis

Photography is an art of its own when it shows the world with the full detail and tonal range that only a camera can record.

Design principles

  • Stop down to f/32–f/64 on a view camera so that detail is sharp from the front to the horizon.
  • Previsualise the print: place the deepest shadow with detail on Zone III and the brightest textured highlight on Zone VII.
  • Fill the range: the print holds a true black (Dmax) and a clean paper white, with 8 to 10 steps between.
  • Choose a form that has its own pattern, such as a pepper, a dune or a shell, and fill the frame with it.
  • Print at full frame by contact, with no cropping, dodging beyond 1 stop or retouching.
  • Light the subject with clear, directional daylight at a ratio of 4:1 to 8:1 to model form.

Anti-patterns

  • Soft-focus lenses, diffusion or brushed pigment
  • Tricks of the darkroom that change the scene, such as montage
  • Cropping a negative to rescue a weak composition
  • Muddy mid-grey prints with no true black or white
  • Sentimental or anecdotal subjects
  • Warm sepia toning that hides the silver
