Arts and Crafts art pottery

ceramics · 1872–1920 · also: British art pottery, Lambeth art pottery, De Morgan lustreware

Hand-made British art pottery after 1872. The potters of Lambeth, Chelsea and Fulham used salt glaze and ruby lustre, and made each piece one by one with a visible hand.

Core thesis

A pot is a work of art when one person makes it from clay and glaze, and the hand is allowed to show.

Design principles

  • Make each piece by hand, with the maker's mark or initials incised in the base.
  • Throw or build the form with small irregularities of 1 to 3 mm; do not hide the throwing rings.
  • Fire a salt glaze at about 1200 °C and let it run to an orange-peel surface.
  • Fire a ruby or gold lustre in reduction at about 600 to 800 °C, so that the copper and silver show as a film.
  • Paint or incise plants, birds, fish and fantastic creatures, in a free line after Persian and Iznik models.
  • Use a limited palette of 3 or 4 colours: blue, turquoise, brown and ruby.
  • Keep the form plain and the surface rich.
  • Place the pot at home as a decorative object, not as a service.

Anti-patterns

  • Identical factory copies with a printed pattern
  • A thin, regular, white body with a clear glaze
  • Gold bands copied from porcelain
  • Heavy rococo scrolls
  • A surface so smooth that no hand shows
