Fauvism

painting · 1905–1908 · also: Les Fauves, The wild beasts

Colour at full strength and not true to nature: a green face, an orange sky. Flat patches, bold contours and quick strokes make the picture an arrangement of colour first.

Core thesis

Colour is free from the object: the painter chooses a hue for its force and its relations, and so builds the picture and the feeling by colour alone.

Design principles

  • Choose each hue for its force and not for its likeness: a green shadow, a red tree, a blue face.
  • Apply the colour pure, straight from the tube, in large flat patches of 30–200mm.
  • Set strong contrasts of complementary pairs at full saturation: red against green, orange against blue.
  • Outline the forms in a dark or coloured contour of 3–10mm, and leave the area inside flat.
  • Flatten the space: reduce the depth to 1–2 layers and let the colour carry the distance.
  • Paint fast, with a visible brush stroke, and leave bare ground between patches.
  • Take plain subjects, such as a landscape, a port, a portrait and a bather, and show them in bold, simple shapes.

Anti-patterns

  • True-to-life local colour and tone
  • Blended, graded modelling that makes a round form
  • Deep perspective and atmospheric haze
  • Muted, grey and brown palettes
  • Fine, fussy detail and a polished surface
  • Dots or a scientific system of colour
