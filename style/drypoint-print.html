Drypoint

print · 1480–1950 · also: pointe seche (French), Kaltnadelradierung (German)

A steel or diamond needle scratches the plate and pushes up a burr of metal along each furrow. The burr holds ink and prints as a soft velvety black line that wears down after a few dozen impressions.

Core thesis

A needle that scratches bare copper throws up a burr. The burr prints as a soft black line that no acid and no burin can make.

Design principles

  • Draw straight on the bare plate with a hard needle; no ground and no acid.
  • Keep the burr: do not scrape it off, because it holds the ink and makes the velvet edge.
  • Draw the darkest areas with dense, near-parallel lines, and keep the light areas open.
  • Wipe the plate with a thin film of ink left in the field, for a plate tone.
  • Expect the line to thin after 20–50 impressions, and number the edition on that count.
  • Combine with etching only when a firm line is also needed; keep the burr lines for the shadows.

Anti-patterns

  • A clean, hard line of even width
  • Halftone dots or a smooth gradient
  • Bright colour
  • Large flat solid areas
  • A long edition with no loss of the burr
