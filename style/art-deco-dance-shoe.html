Art Deco dance shoe

fashion · 1920–1939 · also: T-strap dance shoe, Charleston shoe, Jazz Age evening shoe

The strap shoe of the Jazz Age. It has a T-strap or a bar strap over the instep and a Louis or Cuban heel. It uses satin, kid or metallic leather with a cut-out or beaded pattern.

Core thesis

A dancing woman needs a shoe that stays on the foot, so the strap holds the instep and the Deco pattern shows in every step.

Design principles

  • Fasten the shoe with a T-strap or a bar strap over the instep; fix it with a button or a small buckle.
  • Set the heel at 5 to 7 cm, curved in a Louis or a Cuban shape.
  • Round the toe, and cut the vamp low so that the instep shows.
  • Cut a geometric pattern in the vamp: a sunburst, a chevron or a step.
  • Cover the shoe in satin, in kid, or in a metallic leather that catches the light.
  • Match the shoe to the dress in colour; dye the satin to a sample of the cloth.
  • Use a thin leather sole that slides on the floor.

Anti-patterns

  • A thick rubber sole that grips the floor
  • A bare, strapless slip-on court shoe
  • A square, heavy toe
  • A pastel and floral Victorian pattern
  • A platform or a stiletto heel
